# Cross-Test-Run Effort-Comparison Synthesis (10 topics)

**Date:** 2026-05-03
**Subject:** Themes across the ten `test-runs/{topic}/effort-comparison.md` documents (career-change, food-truck, habit-tracker, mobile-app, property-management, school-consolidation, space-party, tool-library, wearable-device, youth-mentorship), and what those themes imply for revisions to [`dev/2026-05-03_symphony-phase4/methodology.md`](2026-05-03_symphony-phase4/methodology.md) — particularly Phase 2 (Baseline / Ground-Truth Establishment) before BL1 executes med + high.
**Companion read:** [`dev/2026-04-27_effort-comparison.md`](2026-04-27_effort-comparison.md) — the four-topic precursor.

---

## 1. Headline judgment

Two findings. First, the four-topic synthesis from 2026-04-27 generalizes cleanly to ten: every topic preserves a 10–12 through-line skeleton from `min` to `high`, effort scales 3.4–4.3× in question count and 15–21× in persona-response count (Phase 3 carrying the marginal investment exactly as designed), and convergence-count phrasing (`"six of seven personas converged…"`) appears at `high` in 10 of 10 runs. The architecture is doing what it was supposed to do across far wider topic registers than the original four.

Second, the wider sample makes a structural feature visible that the four-topic synthesis only hinted at: **Symphony has two distinct effort-utility regimes, not four**. `min` and `low` both stay inside the user's framing of the REQUEST. `med` and `high` both interrogate the framing. The boundary lives between `low` and `med`, not between `med` and `high`. `high` is mostly a *robustness* upgrade over `med` (convergence quantification, foundational reversal of one or two `med` claims, panel-derived confidence) — it is not where the categorical reframes first arrive. They arrive at `med`, with one exception class noted in §3.4 below. This has direct consequences for the Phase 4 baseline ledger and for how FA1/LB1/RG1 should weight what counts as success.

The methodology is mostly sound. Phase 2 (BL1) needs eight specific revisions before launching the 40 med + high Opus subagents — most are sharpenings, two are corrections of latent assumptions that would have produced unreliable scoring later. They are catalogued in §4.

## 2. Themes that hold across all 10 topics

### 2.1. Through-line preservation is consistent and unidirectional

Every comparison reports 10–12 through-lines that appear in *every* effort level's BRAINSTORM.md. The fidelity verdict in §3 of every document is unanimous: a `min` reader has the operational skeleton; higher-effort readers add scaffolding, not different conclusions. Across all ten comparisons, no memo identifies a `min`-only insight that subsequent levels drop. **Loss runs in one direction.** This is the cleanest evidence that Phase 2 clustering and Phase 3 persona generation are producing genuinely overlapping insights from independent perspectives — not artefacts of shared scaffolding.

### 2.2. Effort scaling lives almost entirely in Phase 3

Question count grows 3.4–4.3× from `min` to `high`; persona-response count grows 15–21×. Across all 10 topics, none reports Phase 2 expansion outpacing Phase 3 — clustering does structural work (4–5 clusters at `min`, 8–10 at `low`/`med`, 11–13 at `high`) and the per-cluster persona work expands inside that. This pattern is now confirmed at scale (10 topics, four registers: life decisions, businesses, products, civic infrastructure), strengthening the prior conclusion that the *cost* of Symphony is concentrated where the *value* is — independent persona reasoning, not question authorship.

### 2.3. Productive drift lands in topic-shaped zones

Each comparison identifies 3–6 zones where higher-effort runs *sharpen* (rather than introduce) recommendations. The zones are topic-shaped, not mechanical: career-change drifts on financial planning, age, alternative paths, and emotional framing; tool-library on launch mechanic, inventory, damage response, and identity; wearable-device on scope, model architecture, privacy, and alert architecture. Phase 2's clustering and Phase 3's persona spread are reliably landing on the axes where the underlying question actually has tension — not on whatever axes the prompt happens to bias toward.

### 2.4. Convergence quantification is `high`-only and ubiquitous

All 10 `high` runs produce "N of M personas converged" phrasing in their BRAINSTORM exec summaries. Examples cited verbatim by the comparisons: career-change ("6/7 personas: same OS, different uniform"), food-truck ("six of seven personas converge"), school-consolidation ("strongest cross-topic convergence"), youth-mentorship ("seven independent perspectives converged"), wearable-device ("three of four perspectives converge on inverting the framing"), tool-library ("six perspectives independently raised the same red flag"). This phrasing is generated by Phase 3 (parallel personas), preserved by Phase 4's attributed/synthesis split, and surfaced in Phase 5's executive summary. Ten-of-ten propagation is strong evidence the attribution channel from `synthesis/attributed/{cluster}.md` through to BRAINSTORM.md is working at `high`. **It is the cleanest single propagation signal in the system.**

### 2.5. At least one foundational reversal per `high` run

Every `high` run produces a reversal that smaller runs do not reach:

| Topic | `high`-only foundational reversal |
|---|---|
| career-change | DOJ + MLP + MLS as a third path; law school as licensure-not-knowledge purchase |
| food-truck | The truck is a 12–18 month research instrument; premium pricing is the honest price |
| habit-tracker | Friction belongs at habit creation; recovery velocity replaces consistency |
| mobile-app | This is not a fitness app — it's a commitment-and-accountability product; 8 months ≠ 8 months |
| property-management | Visibility paradox; contractor capacity is the binding constraint; inflection at 15–20 properties |
| school-consolidation | Federated middle path; decouple smallest district's solvency from the merger vote |
| space-party | Mission Commander reframe; intersection-first menu becomes the cake; wave architecture |
| tool-library | Discovery-before-build; storefront as coordination hub for distributed network |
| wearable-device | Drop the NPU; arrhythmia-only descope is the marketable moat |
| youth-mentorship | Refuse the JJ case file; JJ teens are the strength signal; compliance as trust architecture |

This is structurally `high`-only — none of these appear at `med` (with the partial exceptions noted in §3.4). They are the strongest single argument for spending Opus on `high`.

### 2.6. The `min`–`low` boundary is mostly cosmetic; the `low`–`med` boundary is foundational

The boundary visible in every comparison: `min` and `low` accept the user's framing and optimize inside it. `med` and `high` interrogate the framing. Concretely:

- `min` accepts the REQUEST's frame and produces an operational plan inside it.
- `low` *names* the framing as questionable — surfaces survivorship bias, names "neither-lens gaps," tags risk-vs.-feasibility trade-offs — but the recommendations still live inside the framing.
- `med` introduces the **categorical reframe** — typically one sentence that redefines what the project actually is. Examples: tool-library ("not a tool-distribution project — a relationship-building project that uses tools as the legitimate excuse"); property-management ("not a property management business — a regulatory-legitimacy and community-relations business"); mobile-app ("three or four incompatible products fighting for one shell"); youth-mentorship ("the program cannot launch the way the grant pipeline implies").
- `high` extends the `med` reframe and surfaces convergence quantification + at least one further foundational reversal.

The implication for Phase 4 scoring: `med` is where the scoring stakes spike. A `med` run that fails to surface the categorical reframe is failing in a category-different way from a `low` run that does so. FA1's persona-distinctiveness scoring needs to account for this, not just count whether persona names survived (see §4.3).

### 2.7. The `low` "neither-lens gap" is a load-bearing artefact, not a deficiency report

Every `low` comparison flags structural gaps in DA × Pragmatist coverage. The flagged gaps cluster into recognizable categories:

- **Relational / social dynamics** — youth-mentorship's "post-report teen experience"; tool-library's renter–homeowner asymmetry; food-truck's founder-grief; mobile-app's "killing your darlings"
- **Equity / distributional** — school-consolidation's "the financial model treats all three communities symmetrically"; mobile-app's accessibility audit
- **Political-economy / gatekeeper** — tool-library's "landlord motives"; property-management's incumbent preferences; food-truck's politician asymmetry
- **Emotional / psychological architecture** — habit-tracker's shame-language audit; wearable-device's alert anxiety; space-party's authenticity probe of the birthday girl

Naming what the panel structurally cannot reach is itself an output, not a deficiency — and it does Phase 5 work (the gap categories surface what `med`/`high` personas need to be selected to fill). LB1 should explicitly score `low` summaries on whether the neither-lens gap is named *as a gap* and *substantively*, not flag-by-flag presence.

### 2.8. `min`'s "Conspicuous Absences" section is its distinctive contribution

Every `min` run's BRAINSTORM (or per-cluster summary) includes an explicit "Conspicuous Absences" section. The comparisons treat this as `min`'s structural compensation for single-voice generation. Some examples cited in the comparisons:

- mobile-app `min`: target retention number, paid-tier obligations
- property-management `min`: platform-ToS compliance, housing-affordability critique, pricing transparency
- food-truck `min`: founder burnout, food-safety certification, no competitor analysis
- school-consolidation `min`: equity package detail, post-vote facility decisions

In four of these cases, subsequent levels actually *fill* the flagged gap. In the others, the gap is real but isn't substantively addressed even at `high`. Either way, the conspicuous-absences artefact is `min`-distinctive prompt output that downstream scoring (LB1) should treat as a positive deliverable, not a deficiency report.

## 3. Themes that vary across topics — and what they reveal

### 3.1. Topic-register asymmetry: concrete operational vs. decisional

The 10 topics split roughly into two registers, which scale differently:

- **Concrete operational** (food-truck, tool-library, space-party, property-management): the `high` reframe is structural (research instrument, distributed network, Mission Commander, regulatory-legitimacy business) but does not reverse the basic direction. The session's job is to make the implicit decisions explicit and pre-commit them.
- **Decisional / first-principles** (career-change, school-consolidation, mobile-app, wearable-device, habit-tracker, youth-mentorship): the `high` reframe often *reverses* the question itself ("the binary you've framed is the wrong shape," "should we consolidate" is the wrong question, "this is not a fitness app," "drop the NPU"). The session's job is to generate the right question.

This asymmetry has implications for Phase 4 scoring: a `high` run on a concrete operational topic that *doesn't* reverse the framing is not necessarily failing — it may be that the right framing was already in the REQUEST. A `high` run on a decisional topic that doesn't reverse the framing is more suspect. FA1's persona-distinctiveness scoring should not penalize the absence of reversal universally.

### 3.2. The `med`-vs.-`high` value split varies by topic

For topics where `med` already produced a categorical reframe (mobile-app, youth-mentorship, property-management, food-truck, school-consolidation, tool-library), `high` is mainly investment in epistemic affordances — convergence quantification, panel robustness, foundational reversal of one or two `med` claims. The marginal value of `high` over `med` here is real but bounded.

For topics where `med` was still inside the REQUEST (career-change's DOJ + MLP + MLS stack, wearable-device's drop-NPU, habit-tracker's friction-at-creation), `high` is where the categorical reframe arrives at all. The marginal value is much larger.

The methodology's RG1 cross-effort regression should anchor against this split — within-effort variance across topics should track the topic-register asymmetry, not be smoothed into a single noise estimate.

### 3.3. `min` "Conspicuous Absences" quality varies sharply

Some `min` flags are load-bearing (skin-tone bias in wearable-device → fills at `low`; allergy ask in space-party → fills at `low`). Others are pro forma ("digital infrastructure," "parent role"). The variation suggests the generic `min` brainstormer is doing real work in some clusters and rote work in others. LB1 should score conspicuous-absence quality by whether higher-effort runs treat the same gap as substantive.

### 3.4. The `med`-categorical-reframe rule has exceptions

In three of ten topics, the categorical reframe arrives only at `high`, not at `med`:

- career-change: `med` is "diagnose burnout before deciding anything else" — sharper, but inside the JD frame. The DOJ + MLP + MLS substitute stack is `high`-only.
- wearable-device: `med` is "two products at once" — sharper, but assumes the NPU. The drop-the-NPU reversal is `high`-only.
- habit-tracker: `med` introduces the 72-hour window and four-state model — but the friction-at-creation reversal is `high`-only.

Notably, all three are decisional / first-principles topics where the REQUEST itself encodes a default the user may not have audited. **Pattern:** when the user's default has high inertia (a credentialing decision, a sensor architecture, a UX pattern inherited from a category), `med` doesn't break it, only `high` does. This is meaningful guidance for Phase 4: the most expensive prompt failures will likely be on `high` runs of decisional topics, where dropping the foundational reversal would silently make the run indistinguishable from `med`.

### 3.5. Property-management min's "80 (4 per question)" line is a memo error, not a structural anomaly

The property-management `effort-comparison.md` table reports `min` total persona responses as "80 (4 per question, single brainstormer)" against 4–5 in the other nine min runs. Direct inspection of `test-runs/property-management/min/responses/` confirms five clusters each containing a single `generic-response.md` — the standard `min` structure. The 80 is a memo error. No methodology change required, but BL1 subagents reading that memo as reference should be told to ignore the line. (See §4.5.)

## 4. Methodology revisions before Phase 2 (BL1) executes

The BL1 task spec is largely correct. The revisions below are pre-execution adjustments based on what the cross-comparison evidence actually shows. They are ordered by load-bearingness.

### 4.1. Replace "convergent ≥ 2 personas" binary with raw-count tracking + per-effort classification — load-bearing

The BL1 prompt currently says: *"Convergent: 2+ personas at low; 2+ personas at med; 2+ personas at high."* This treats convergence as binary across efforts, but the 10 comparisons demonstrate convergence is a graded signal that BRAINSTORM exec summaries quote verbatim ("six of seven," "five of eleven clusters"). FA1 will need to verify those quotes, which requires raw counts — not just a binary classifier.

**Change:** In Step 2 of the BL1 per-cluster prompt, record raw counts per through-line (e.g., `convergence: 5/7`) plus a derived classification per effort:

- **low (2 personas):** `dual` (2/2) | `single-lens` (1/2) — 2/2 is the baseline expectation, not strong convergence.
- **med (4 personas):** `strong-convergent` (3/4 or 4/4) | `weak-convergent` (2/4) | `single` (1/4).
- **high (7 personas):** `strong-convergent` (≥5/7) | `weak-convergent` (3–4/7) | `single` (1–2/7).

This costs little ledger ink and makes FA1's "every convergence-count claim must be checkable against the ledger" target operationally tractable.

### 4.2. Sequence the inputs to prevent cross-comparison memo contamination — load-bearing

The BL1 prompt lists `test-runs/{TOPIC}/effort-comparison.md` as input #5 with the warning "use as a reference only — do not seed the ledger from it." The warning is right in spirit, but the memos contain explicit through-line lists that are essentially ground-truth claims about which through-lines persist. The risk: a subagent reads the memo, sees "Through-line 7: validate before you borrow," then "discovers" exactly that through-line in the responses.

**Change:** Hard-sequence the reads in the prompt's Method section. Step 1 reads only `responses/` files and produces the raw inventory. Step 1.5 (new) reads the `effort-comparison.md` memo and produces an audit list — which of my through-lines correspond to memo-named ones; which memo through-lines did I miss; what does that suggest about my coverage. The memo's role is then *post hoc audit*, parallel in structure to the existing Step 6 `attributed/` cross-reference. Do not allow the memo to retroactively edit the ledger; flag discrepancies for FA1.

### 4.3. Split persona-distinctiveness into content vs. framing — load-bearing for FA1

The BL1 prompt currently asks for "distinctive contributions" without distinguishing axes. The 10 comparisons demonstrate two structurally different distinctiveness modes:

- **Content distinctiveness** — through-lines unique to one persona (the Lawyer's specific statutory issue; the Accountant's specific cost figure)
- **Framing distinctiveness** — a lens applied across through-lines (the Storyteller frames everything narratively; the Empath surfaces emotional architecture; the Politician surfaces gatekeeper dynamics)

A `med`/`high` synthesis can preserve content distinctiveness while flattening framing distinctiveness, and that is a Phase 4 failure FA1 needs to be able to detect.

**Change:** In Step 4 of the BL1 prompt, replace "name what they distinctively contributed to this cluster" with two sub-fields:

- **Distinctive through-lines** — TLs unique to this persona (existing).
- **Distinctive framing** — a 1-sentence characterization of the lens this persona applied across their response (new). Example from food-truck `high`: "The Visionary frames every operational decision as a product of identity transformation; the Accountant frames every operational decision as a unit-economics test."

FA1 should score both axes. A `_synthesis.md` that retains all named persona attributions but flattens the framing distinctions is partially failing.

### 4.4. Add a fifth Phase-5 signal: categorical-reframe candidate (med + high only)

Section 2.6 above demonstrates that the categorical reframe is the load-bearing artefact at `med` and `high`. The current Phase-5 signal candidates list (Central Tension, confidence tags, Neither-lens gaps, conspicuous absences) doesn't include it. Phase 5 will need to harvest categorical reframes to produce its exec-summary thesis.

**Change:** In Step 5 of the BL1 prompt, add a fifth signal candidate for `med` + `high` samples:

- **Categorical reframe candidate (med + high only):** if the cluster's responses collectively reframe the *subject matter* of the cluster (not just the answer to its questions), name the reframe in a single sentence. Skip if no reframe emerges. Speculative filler is worse than absence.

The BL1 cross-sample synthesis should then aggregate these into a "categorical reframes by topic" table that Phase 5's investigation can consume.

### 4.5. Add a memo-anomaly handling rule

Two of the ten effort-comparison memos contain identifiable anomalies:

- **property-management `min`:** "80 (4 per question)" line is a memo error (actual: 5 responses, one per cluster).
- **food-truck `high`:** "synth from 166, 3.15× compaction" — the compaction ratio is plausibly a different denominator than the other memos' ratios. Worth verifying before BL1 subagents read it as authoritative.

**Change:** Add a single sentence to the BL1 per-cluster prompt's Inputs to Read section, item 5:

> The `effort-comparison.md` memos are descriptive references, not authoritative inputs. If the memo's reported file/persona/response counts disagree with what you actually find in `responses/`, treat the filesystem as canonical and flag the discrepancy in your baseline file's verification section.

The cross-sample synthesis should compile a "memo-vs.-filesystem discrepancy log" so future memo regenerations can correct these.

### 4.6. Reduce or replace the Opus-vs.-Sonnet sanity check

The BL1 doc currently proposes: *"spawn 2 med samples on both Opus and Sonnet in parallel, diff the ledgers."* Two samples is too few — the convergence-call disagreement rate may be in the 5–15% range that 2 samples can't reliably detect, and the cross-comparison memos vary in convergence-detail specificity (food-truck explicitly says "six of seven... independently flag" multiple times; tool-library's high run says "six perspectives independently raised the same red flag" exactly once).

**Change:** Either (a) skip the sanity check and run all 40 med + high on Opus, accepting the bounded marginal cost; or (b) if running, use ≥4 samples (1 strategy-heavy + 1 tactical at `med`, repeat at `high`) and pre-register what counts as "substantive agreement" — specifically: convergence-call agreement on ≥85% of through-lines, and Central-Tension semantic agreement on all 4 samples. Anything looser is observation theatre.

### 4.7. Add a `low` neither-lens-gap taxonomy

Section 2.7 above derives four recurring categories of `low`-flagged neither-lens gaps from the 10 memos. Equipping BL1 subagents with this taxonomy upfront produces a structured aggregate the cross-sample synthesis can directly tabulate, instead of the current freeform "1–3 specific gaps."

**Change:** In Step 5 of the BL1 prompt (low only), provide the four-category taxonomy:

- Relational / social dynamics
- Equity / distributional
- Political-economy / gatekeeper
- Emotional / psychological architecture

With explicit instruction that any cluster may also flag a fifth, sample-specific category if nothing fits — but blank is preferred to forced filler.

### 4.8. Add a BL1 → SS1-revision feedback gate

If 2–3 samples turn out structurally unusable (e.g., the property-management `min` memo error proved on inspection to be a real anomaly, or a cluster has zero through-lines for some reason), FA1/LB1/RG1 will be scoring against a partly broken corpus. The methodology currently says "document the anomaly and proceed."

**Change:** Add a step between BL1's per-cluster phase and FA1/LB1/RG1 launch: the cross-sample synthesis subagent reviews anomaly flags and, if any sample is non-recoverable, recommends a manifest addendum (replacing 1–2 samples with backup samples from SS1). This is one extra step that prevents downstream waste. The bar for replacement should be high — only structural unusability, not "looks weird." Likely zero replacements needed; the gate is cheap insurance.

### 4.9. (Optional) Add Phase-4-signal propagation evidence to the cross-sample synthesis

The Phase 4 → Phase 5 fidelity question is partially in scope per FQ5. BL1's deliverables don't currently produce evidence about which raw counts and Central-Tension candidates actually propagated through `synthesis/{cluster}_synthesis.md` to the BRAINSTORM exec summary.

**Change (optional, scope-respectful):** In the cross-sample synthesis BL1_baselines.md, add a section: "Phase-4 signals consumed by Phase 5" — for each `high` sample, list which BL1-flagged Central Tension / convergence-count candidates appear as named claims in the corresponding `synthesis/{cluster}_synthesis.md`. This stays inside Phase 4 scope (does not modify Phase 5) but produces direct evidence for whether Phase 4's signal-emission is reliable enough for Phase 5 to consume — directly answering FQ5 with concrete examples instead of inference.

If this overshoots the methodology's scope discipline, omit. But the data is cheap to gather while the synthesis subagent is already reading 60 baselines.

## 5. The two-regime evaluation lens — what the prompt-design framing should look like

The 10-topic synthesis surfaces a structural feature the methodology evaluates along a single dimension ("preserve through-lines, preserve persona names") when it should evaluate along two job-specific ones. The data plumbing (BL1 → FA1/LB1/RG1 → RP1 → integration-spec) is correct; what needs sharpening is the *evaluation lens* and the success metrics.

### 5.1. Symphony has two regimes, not four

| Tier | Distinctive job | Primary failure mode | What to score for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **min** | Single-voice compression that surfaces *what one voice can't see* | Pro-forma conspicuous-absences (rote rather than load-bearing) | Through-line survival + **conspicuous-absences quality** |
| **low** | Two-voice compression that surfaces *what two lenses can't reach* | Smoothing the DA × Pragmatist tension; missing the neither-lens gap | Through-line survival + **tension preservation** + **neither-lens-gap substantiveness** |
| **med** | 4-voice synthesis surfacing the *categorical reframe* | Reframe-dropping; framing-flattening (names preserved, lenses smoothed) | Persona-name preservation + **framing fidelity** + **categorical-reframe presence** |
| **high** | 7-voice synthesis adding *convergence quantification* + at least one *foundational reversal* | Soft convergence math; reversal-dropping (run becomes indistinguishable from `med`) | Above + **raw-count traceability** + **foundational-reversal presence** |

The min↔low boundary is cosmetic; the med↔high boundary is mostly robustness. The load-bearing boundary lives between `low` and `med` — and PP1's contract analysis should treat that boundary as a *mandatory* divergence, not just a legitimate one.

### 5.2. Mandatory divergences (not just legitimate)

PP1 currently classifies divergences as "legitimate" or "drift." The 10-topic data shows several divergences are *mandatory* — collapsing them would break the prompt's job:

- **Conspicuous absences:** required at `min`, forbidden at `med`/`high` (the seven-persona panel doesn't have the same distinctive job)
- **Neither-lens gaps:** required at `low` (DA × Pragmatist structural gap), n/a above
- **Categorical reframe:** must surface at `med` and `high`, n/a at `min`/`low` (the prompts at lower tiers don't have the persona panel to support it)
- **Convergence-count phrasing:** must surface at `high` (10/10 in the data), optional at `med` (4 personas can support it but isn't always meaningful), n/a at `low`/`min`
- **Persona-name attribution in `attributed/`:** required at `med`/`high`, n/a below (no `attributed/` doc exists)

### 5.3. Mandatory similarities

Other surfaces should be uniform across all four:

- **Through-line preservation** — universal floor; LB1 measures it at min/low and FA1's Axis A3 measures it at med/high
- **Central Tension surfacing** — universal (per current PP1 finding); the load-bearing claim across all 10 high runs but missing from the min/med/high prompts' explicit output schema
- **No persona names in prose** of `_summary.md` and `_synthesis.md` — universal (per Discussion Q6); the portable-analysis principle

### 5.4. Concrete consequences for the test tracks

- **FA1** (med + high) needs two new failure-mode axes that current Axis A3 doesn't fully capture: **framing-flattening** (do persona lenses survive, even when content does?) and **categorical-reframe presence** (does the reframe make it through synthesis?). At `high` only, add **foundational-reversal presence**.
- **LB1** (min + low) is mostly correct as-is but should weight the regime-distinctive axes (Axis B tension preservation at low; Axis C conspicuous-absences quality at min) on equal footing with Axis A through-line survival. Currently the methodology's stop criteria treat survival as primary; the 10-topic data argues these are co-primary.
- **PP1** should explicitly enumerate mandatory divergences (§5.2) — currently flagged only as "legitimate divergences (preserve in future edits)," which is too weak a guarantee.
- **RG1's** intentional-vs-drift classifier needs a third bucket: **regime-mandated divergence** (e.g., conspicuous-absences appearing at min but not at high is *not* drift; it's the prompt's correct job). Today RG1 would correctly flag this as intentional, but it would lump it together with cluster-shape variance — losing diagnostic information for RP1.
- **RP1's** iteration goals should be tier-explicit. The methodology says "single-track refine" for full-synthesis vs. "2 variants" for summary-only (Q13 escalated full-synthesis to 2 variants), but doesn't specify *what* each iteration is optimizing for. Make it explicit: full-synthesis iterations optimize for reframe + framing preservation under aggregation pressure; summary-only iterations optimize for distinctive-output quality (conspicuous absences for min, neither-lens gaps for low) under compression pressure.

This is **not** an argument for two separate investigations. The shared infrastructure (BL1 baselines, RG1 cross-effort regression, RP1 integration spec) is correct; the costs of fracturing exceed the value. But the *evaluation lens* should be explicitly two-regime everywhere it surfaces.

## 7. Things the methodology already gets right (worth preserving)

Briefly, so revisions don't accidentally undo good decisions:
