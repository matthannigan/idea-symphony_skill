## Convergence Group 1
**Theme:** What is the "core value" — definition, identification, and discovery method
**Cluster:** B1 Cluster 1 (Core Value, Differentiation, and "What to Keep")
**Questions:**
- [Analyst Q1] Core feature isolation through data — quantitative framework for ranking 15 features by true value; correlation vs. causation; say-want vs. actually-use.
- [Questioner Q1] What does "core value" actually mean in practice? Features most people use vs. features that create most passionate engagement — what if those aren't the same?
- [Questioner Q2] How would we identify our true differentiator if we couldn't see any usage data? What does our product do that no other fitness app does?
- [Questioner Q3] What if the problem isn't which features to keep, but which job users are hiring us to do?
- [Systems Thinker Q1] Core value feedback loops — which features, isolated and executed exceptionally well, would create a self-reinforcing engagement loop with network effects?
**Merge recommendation:**
> "What does 'core value' actually mean for this app — the features most people use, the features that create the most passionate engagement, the job users hired us to do, or the features that would generate self-reinforcing engagement loops if executed exceptionally well — and what analytical framework (beyond current usage data) would help us identify it?"

Strong merge. Five personas, single shared concern (what IS core), distinct contributions (Analyst's quantitative lens, Questioner's three rhetorical reframings, Systems Thinker's feedback-loop lens) — the merge preserves all four angles in one compact question.

## Convergence Group 2
**Theme:** Core-value assumption itself may be wrong — the simplification premise challenged
**Cluster:** B1 Cluster 1 (Core Value) — distinct strand
**Questions:**
- [Devil's Advocate Q7] Assumption that a unified "core value" exists — what if retention is low because no single feature competes with dedicated alternatives? Could the real problem be execution quality across all features?
- [Questioner Q10] What if we're asking the wrong question entirely? Instead of "what do we cut," what if we asked "what would make users open this app every day even if it only did one thing"?
- [Appreciative Inquirer Q7] The "One Thing" Evolution — if this app became known for doing one thing exceptionally well, what would that be?
**Merge recommendation:** **Preserve all distinct.** These three questions all challenge the simplification premise but from genuinely different registers: Devil's Advocate (competitive-quality framing — "maybe the problem is execution, not scope"), Questioner (reframing the question — "what would make them open it daily"), Appreciative Inquirer (imagining the one-thing future — "what would it be known for"). Merging would collapse three distinct reframings into one less-sharp question. Keep at least 2 of these in synthesis output — recommend Devil's Advocate Q7 and Questioner Q10 as the most representative.

## Convergence Group 3
**Theme:** Feature interdependencies and cascading consequences of cuts
**Cluster:** B1 Cluster 2 (Feature Interdependencies and Cascade Risk)
**Questions:**
- [Analyst Q2] Feature interdependency mapping — which feature pairs/clusters create synergistic value that would be lost, and which create drag through complexity costs, notification fatigue, cognitive overload?
- [Analyst Q9] Feature cannibalization analysis — are features cannibalizing engagement from others? Would simplification actually increase total engagement?
- [Systems Thinker Q2] Feature interdependency mapping — complete causal chain of removal; data sharing, navigation pathways, reinforcing patterns, keystone features whose removal causes disproportionate system collapse.
- [Systems Thinker Q5] User adaptation and compensatory behavior — if you cut the social feed, will users flood challenge platform with social posts? What compensatory behaviors might undermine simplification?
**Merge recommendation:**
> "Before cutting any feature, what is the complete interdependency and cascade map — which features are synergistic (lost together), which cannibalize each other (net gain from cutting), which are 'keystone' features whose removal causes disproportionate system collapse, and what compensatory user behaviors might emerge that undermine simplification?"

Strong merge. Four personas' contributions (Analyst x2, Systems Thinker x2) all map the "what happens when you cut" territory with distinct angles: synergy/drag, cannibalization, keystone collapse, user compensation. The merge preserves all four.

## Convergence Group 4

Strong merge. Four personas, single concern (niche-user loss through cuts), distinct contributions (Devil's Advocate's cascading-churn warning, Analyst's valuation framework and churn-modeling rigor, Questioner's identification-method challenge).

## Convergence Group 5
**Theme:** Metrics redefinition — current rulers won't measure the new product
**Cluster:** B1 Cluster 4 (Metrics, Retention, Measurement)
**Questions:**
- [Analyst Q11] Success metric redefinition — leading indicators to replace lagging metrics (downloads, 30-day retention); micro-metrics signaling core-value discovery within first week.
- [Systems Thinker Q9] Success metric system realignment — current metrics were designed for bloated engagement-maximizing app; utility might have SHORTER sessions but HIGHER retention. Risks of measuring the new product with old rulers.
**Merge recommendation:**
> "What new metric system would create the right feedback loops for a simplified utility product — replacing current lagging metrics (downloads, 30-day retention, DAU/MAU, session length) with leading indicators that predict daily habit formation — and what are the risks of measuring the simplified product with the old rulers (e.g., shorter sessions misread as failure when they actually reflect efficient utility)?"

Two-persona tight convergence. Merge preserves Analyst's leading-indicator framing with Systems Thinker's "old rulers don't fit new product" insight — both essential.

## Convergence Group 6
**Theme:** Retention diagnostics — what the 8%/2%/47-second numbers actually reveal
**Cluster:** B1 Cluster 4 (Metrics, Retention, Measurement)
**Questions:**
- [Analyst Q4] Retention driver decomposition — cohort analysis within each feature; steepest drop-off points in onboarding-to-habit pipeline.
- [Analyst Q5] DAU/MAU ratio diagnostic — 47-second sessions not sticky; behavioral sequence analysis to understand failure-to-find vs. too-quick-completion vs. decision-paralysis.
- [Audience Advocate Q2] If we mapped the actual user journeys of the 8% who stayed beyond 30 days, what paths did they take? What patterns emerge vs. those who churn immediately?
- [Questioner Q5] What would happen if we interviewed the 8% who stayed past 30 days and asked what they'd stop using if we removed it?
**Merge recommendation:**
> "What would behavioral-sequence analysis, cohort retention curves per feature, and direct interviews with the 8% who stayed past 30 days reveal about (a) where in the onboarding-to-habit pipeline users drop off, (b) what the 47-second DAU sessions mean (failure-to-find, efficient utility, or decision paralysis), and (c) which features the retained users would actually miss?"

Strong merge. Four personas, single concern (diagnose what's happening through the retained cohort), distinct complementary methods (cohort decomposition, behavioral sequence, journey mapping, direct interview).

## Convergence Group 9
**Theme:** Research methodology — avoiding bias in core-value identification
**Cluster:** B1 Cluster 6 (Research Methodology)
**Questions:**
- [Devil's Advocate Q2] Self-selection bias in user research — survey respondents are the most engaged minority; how can we understand why the majority abandon without alienating the only users who still care?
- [Devil's Advocate Q9] One-person opinion is not "research" — with only one PM, who determines which user feedback matters? What guardrails ensure this isn't decided by one person's biases?
- [Questioner Q6] What if we tested feature removal before committing to it? Could we ship a version with one feature temporarily disabled and see who complains — or doesn't notice?
- [Audience Advocate Q8] What power dynamics exist when a product team decides which features matter, and how can we involve users in determining the app's core value proposition rather than making top-down assumptions?
**Merge recommendation:**
> "What research methodology — guarding against self-selection bias (only engaged users respond to surveys), single-PM bias (one person's opinion isn't research), and team-ownership bias — can produce reliable core-value signal? What role do real-world feature-removal A/B tests and structured user involvement play alongside interview-based methods?"

Strong merge. Four personas, shared concern (bias-resistant research), distinct contributions (Devil's Advocate's self-selection and one-PM warnings, Questioner's A/B-test proposal, Audience Advocate's power-dynamics and user-involvement framing).

## Convergence Group 10
**Theme:** Transition strategy — incremental vs. clean relaunch, 8-month runway
**Cluster:** B1 Cluster 7 (Transition Strategy)
**Questions:**
- [Analyst Q10] Incremental vs. clean slate methodology — framework for comparing risks of alienating users through abrupt change vs. opportunity costs of gradualism, given 8-month runway.
- [Devil's Advocate Q8] The relaunch assumption itself — "clean relaunch" assumes users give a second chance; app stores littered with 2.0 relaunches that failed. What evidence suggests existing user base returns?
- [Devil's Advocate Q11] The 8-month runway as false constraint — hidden dependencies, database migrations, user backlash could exhaust runway before launching anything viable. Realistic timeline with buffer? At what point pivot to incremental?
- [Questioner Q9] Why assume a clean relaunch is better than incremental simplification? What would happen if we removed one feature per month and measured the impact each time?
**Merge recommendation:**
> "Given the 8-month runway, what is the decision framework for choosing between incremental feature-by-feature simplification (lower risk, lower user alienation, longer time to focused product) vs. clean relaunch (clearer narrative, higher risk that users don't return given 2.0-relaunch failure patterns, potential runway exhaustion from hidden dependencies)? What evidence would justify each path, and at what point should signals trigger a pivot from one to the other?"

Strong merge. Four personas, shared decision-frame (process trade-off), distinct contributions (Analyst's comparative framework, Devil's Advocate's relaunch-failure warning and runway-realism, Questioner's incremental-as-default proposal).

## Convergence Group 11
**Theme:** Team morale, ownership, and burnout through the transition
**Cluster:** B1 Cluster 8 (Team Dynamics)
**Questions:**
- [Appreciative Inquirer Q3] Team's Brightest Moments — when felt most energized, proud, effective; conditions that made it possible; strengths to amplify in leaner environment.
- [Appreciative Inquirer Q6] Team Reimagined — team energized, creative, proud; day-to-day look; product culture they'd build.
- [Appreciative Inquirer Q10] Team-Aligned Product Vision — simplified product aligning with existing team strengths, making simplification feel like liberation rather than loss.
- [Devil's Advocate Q3] False consensus in team decision-making — 6 engineers may champion features they built; decision framework that avoids ownership bias.
- [Devil's Advocate Q10] Team morale as critical success factor — engineers who built features being deleted — how stay motivated? Mechanisms to rebuild psychological investment after "killing darlings."
- [Questioner Q15] What if we're underestimating the psychological impact on the team? How do we help engineers and designers feel proud of simplification rather than feeling like they "failed"?
**Merge recommendation:** **Preserve distinct — convert to 2-3 merged questions.** This is the densest human-centered convergence in the file and splits naturally into two merges plus one preserved-distinct question:

> **Merge A (team strengths and aspirational future):** "What are the team's existing strengths, best moments, and conditions for creative/proud work — and what simplified product would align with those strengths so the change feels like liberation rather than loss?" — anchored in AI Q3, AI Q6, AI Q10.

> **Merge B (morale and ownership through cuts):** "How do we execute the feature-cutting decision without letting team-ownership bias drive outcomes (each engineer championing what they built), while simultaneously rebuilding psychological investment among the engineers whose work is being deleted so they stay motivated through the relaunch?" — anchored in DA Q3, DA Q10, Q Q15.

Both merges are defensible. Two merges retain AI's aspirational voice separately from Devil's Advocate's morale warnings — collapsing further would silence one of the two registers.
