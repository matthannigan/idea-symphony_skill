# school-consolidation — low — Cluster Baseline

**Input file:** test-data/school-consolidation_low.md
**Total questions in input:** 97 (Synthesize: 69; Append: 28)
**Natural cluster count:** 10 cross-persona Synthesize clusters (plus 2 orphan clusters); see C1 section for flat-pool comparison

> Scope note: Clusters here are derived from the **Synthesize-group only** (69 questions across Analyst, Appreciative Inquirer, Audience Advocate, Devil's Advocate, Questioner, Systems Thinker). Append-group questions (Provocateur, Storyteller, Visionary, Connector) are catalogued separately in the B4 Append baseline and are mapped onto these clusters for round-robin selection. Including them in clustering would distort thematic centroids and violate the Phase 2C Synthesize/Append split.

## Cluster 1: Community Identity, Loss, and Grief When Schools Close
**Description:** Questions about what communities lose when "their school" closes — identity, grief, the school as town-hub/employer/gathering-place, the emotional and political weight of asking communities to vote for their own sacrifice. This cluster spans Audience Advocate's lived-impact framing, Systems Thinker's identity-dynamics framing, Questioner's town-cost accounting, and Appreciative Inquirer's identity-preservation reframing. Densest cross-persona cluster in the file alongside C2 Transportation.
**Question count in cluster:** 9
**Contributing personas:** Audience Advocate (3), Appreciative Inquirer (1), Questioner (2), Systems Thinker (2), Devil's Advocate (0*)
**Primary dimension:** Human-centered (with Strategic secondary)
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- "How might we understand the grief, loss, and identity disruption that communities experience when 'their school' closes—not just as political resistance, but as genuine human impact?" [Audience Advocate Q5]
- "What does it mean to ask communities to vote on losing something central to their identity—and how might we honor both the practical necessity and the genuine loss?" [Audience Advocate Q12]
- "What happens to a town when its school closes, and how do we measure that cost?" [Questioner Q9]
- "What happens to community identity when the school building becomes multipurpose space?" [Systems Thinker Q7]
- "Redefining 'our school' without losing community" [Appreciative Inquirer Q5]

*Devil's Advocate Q4 (deferred maintenance) touches this territory but primary home is C4 Facilities.

## C1 Comparison (Per-Persona vs. Flat Pooling)

### Per-persona clustering result
10 cross-persona clusters as enumerated above, plus 2 orphan clusters (AI aspirational, ST systems archetypes). Persona attribution is preserved by construction. The strongest clusters (C1 Identity, C3 Financial, C8 Equity, C9 Enrollment Decline, C10 Implementation) each have 3-4 contributing personas — these are robustly cross-persona. C4 Facilities is weaker cross-persona (2 strong contributors plus cross-cluster overlaps) and is a borderline orphan.

### Flat-pooling result (questions stripped of persona labels, shuffled, re-clustered)
I performed a parallel clustering treating the 69 Synthesize questions as an unattributed pool. Results:

- **Identical large clusters:** C1 Identity, C2 Transportation, C3 Financial, C5 Voter Approval, C8 Equity, C9 Enrollment Decline, and C10 Implementation all reform with essentially identical membership. These are content-driven clusters; persona structure neither reveals nor masks them.
- **C6 (Alternatives) grows under flat pooling.** Systems Thinker Q6 (managed bankruptcy vs. rushed consolidation) pulls into C6; Questioner Q13 (phased five-year) and Analyst Q8 (alternative service delivery) cohere even more tightly. Flat pooling adds 1-2 questions that per-persona clustering held in adjacent territory.
- **C4 Facilities dissolves under flat pooling.** The 3 Facilities questions (AI Q9, DA Q4, cross-referenced ST Q7) distribute into adjacent clusters — AI Q9 ("facility decisions that create community value") lands in C10 (Implementation), DA Q4 ("deferred maintenance") lands in C3 (Financial), and ST Q7 (building-as-multipurpose-space identity) lands solidly in C1. Per-persona view preserves C4 as a distinct thread; flat pooling reveals it as facets of three larger clusters.
- **ST orphan cluster partially fragments under flat pooling.** ST Q1 (self-amplifying closure) ties into C9 Enrollment Decline; ST Q3 (success-to-successful) ties into C1 Identity or C9; ST Q9 (fixes-that-fail) ties loosely into C10. ST Q5 (balancing feedback loops) and ST Q11 (stakeholder adaptation) remain orphans under flat pooling — they are distinctively meta-analytical archetype questions. The per-persona ST orphan cluster is partly an artifact of ST's vocabulary; content-wise, ~3 of 5 archetype questions distribute across existing clusters.
- **AI orphan cluster reduces under flat pooling.** AI Q1 (what makes districts special) ties into C10 (Implementation / existing-strengths); AI Q3 (hidden assets in decline) ties into C9 (Enrollment Decline reframe). AI Q4 (student-memory vision) and AI Q7 (learning from what works) remain AI-isolated even under flat pooling — the student-memory-future-vision and design-around-existing-strengths framings are genuinely distinctive. Flat pooling shrinks the AI orphan from 4 questions to ~2.

### Are the cluster sets substantially different?
**Mostly no, with two real exceptions.** Eight of ten primary clusters are stable across both approaches. The two meaningful differences:
1. C4 Facilities exists only under per-persona clustering. Flat pooling distributes its 3 questions across C1, C3, and C10.
2. ST's archetype orphan cluster shrinks from 5 to ~2 under flat pooling; the majority of its archetype questions distribute into content clusters.

### Does per-persona structure bias clustering toward persona-aligned clusters?
**Yes, moderately.** Per-persona structure preserves ST's named-archetype vocabulary as a coherent cluster rather than letting ST questions merge into content-adjacent clusters. It also elevates C4 Facilities to cross-persona-cluster status based on 2 contributors. **Both behaviors are arguably good** for Phase 2C — they preserve distinctive signal (ST archetypes) and ensure a recurring topical thread (facilities decisions) isn't dispersed. But they slightly under-count cross-persona convergence into C1, C3, C9, and C10.

### Recommendation for SP1's prompt — grouped (per-persona) vs. flat pooling

**Recommendation: per-persona structure with explicit cross-persona-cluster permission.** The synthesis prompt should present questions grouped by persona (preserving voice and aiding traceability) but instruct the synthesizer to actively look for cross-persona convergence and to note when a question could plausibly anchor in two clusters.

**For school-consolidation/low specifically, per-persona grouping is the safer default** because (a) the topic has genuinely distributed cluster ownership — no single persona dominates a cluster the way Audience Advocate dominated C1 in tool-library/low. C1 Identity is driven by Audience Advocate (3), Questioner (2), Systems Thinker (2), Appreciative Inquirer (1) — and that distribution needs to be *visible* to the synthesizer so it doesn't over-weight any single persona's framing. (b) ST's named-archetype vocabulary ("fixes that fail," "success-to-successful," "balancing feedback loops") is especially important for school-consolidation because the topic is rife with dynamic-system behavior; losing those labels would be a real voice-loss. (c) Flat pooling dissolves C4 Facilities, but facility decisions are a recurring policy thread in consolidation debates — keeping it as a distinct cluster in the output serves SP1 better than dispersing it. Per-persona structure plus cross-cluster permission is the hybrid that wins for this cell.
