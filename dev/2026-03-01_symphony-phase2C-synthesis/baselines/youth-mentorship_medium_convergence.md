# youth-mentorship — medium — Convergence Baseline

**Input:** 94 Synthesize-group questions from `test-data/youth-mentorship_medium.md`
**Convergence groups (P):** 17
**Questions in convergence (Q):** 66 of 94 (70%)
**Convergence density:** 70%
**Unique (non-convergent) Synthesize questions (U):** 28 of 94 (30%)

Convergence here means two or more Synthesize-persona questions address the same concrete concern such that synthesis can either (a) merge them into one richer question that anchors in all source texts, or (b) preserve all distinct voices when the framings differ enough that merging would strip persona-specific value. The introduction of Lawyer at medium effort raises convergence density vs. a hypothetical no-Lawyer baseline because Lawyer's regulatory specificity converges heavily with Devil's Advocate risk territory and Analyst's risk-framework scaffolding (especially on screening, mandatory reporting, insurance/liability, consent). youth-mentorship's ethical and legal density produces the highest convergence rate among sampled BL1 medium-effort cells so far (70% vs. food-truck/medium at 68%, tool-library/medium lower).

## Convergence Group 1: Volunteer screening scope, calibration & false-positive risk

**Theme:** The screening-architecture question — what registries to query, what offenses disqualify, FCRA-compliant procedures, calibration across multiple screening dimensions, and the false-positive/false-negative tradeoff that filters out qualified community mentors.
**Cluster:** C1 (Volunteer Screening)
**Questions:**
- [Lawyer Q1] Disqualifying offenses and registry checks — specific criminal offense categories permanently disqualifying; minor-serving registries; mid-program discovery protocols.
- [Lawyer Q2] Background check scope and renewal cycles — jurisdictional conflicts; point-in-time snapshot; renewal intervals.
- [Lawyer Q3] FCRA compliance — disclosure and consent requirements; adverse-action procedures if rejection results.
- [Analyst Q2] Volunteer screening calibration — background checks, reference interviews, behavioral assessments; false-positive/false-negative tradeoffs; not excluding qualified community mentors.
- [Systems Thinker Q10] Volunteer screening process creating unintended selection effects.
**Merge recommendation:** **Preserve partially distinct.** Two merge outputs:
- Question A (screening architecture — merge Lawyer Q1 + Lawyer Q2 + Lawyer Q3): "What specific criminal offense categories should permanently disqualify mentor applicants (and which may warrant case-by-case review given one-on-one minor-serving context), which specific registries and databases must be queried, what are the legal implications if a disqualifying record is discovered mid-program after a match has formed, how should the program determine which state's laws govern screening given multi-jurisdictional mentors/mentees, what background check renewal intervals (annual, biennial, or event-triggered re-checks) match the program's risk profile, and what specific FCRA disclosure, consent, and adverse-action procedures must the program implement when using third-party background check reports?"
- Question B (screening-as-selection-effect — merge Analyst Q2 + Systems Thinker Q10): "How should the volunteer screening process be calibrated across multiple dimensions — background checks, reference interviews, behavioral assessments — to identify genuinely unsuitable mentors while avoiding unintended selection effects where procedural burdens or disqualifying criteria disproportionately exclude qualified mentors from the community the program is meant to serve; where is the false-positive / false-negative trade-off on each screening component, and where should the program accept more statistical risk to maintain community trust and mentor-pool representativeness?"

Merging Lawyer into Analyst/ST would strip Lawyer's jurisdictional/FCRA/registry-specificity (the distinctive contribution Lawyer adds at medium effort). Keeping Lawyer's three as a merge preserves the regulatory-mechanism vocabulary; keeping Analyst + ST as a separate merge preserves the selection-effects-and-equity framing that operates at a different register.

- [Analyst Q3] Incident response and critical incident protocols — decision tree for mandatory reporting vs. relationship preservation; documentation and decision-rights.
- [Devil's Advocate Q7] Juvenile justice referral paradox — mandatory reporting undermining trust; preventing program from becoming carceral extension.

- [Questioner Q6] Safety protocols preventing authentic connection — rigid boundaries and mandatory reporting creating distrust.

**Merge recommendation:** **Preserve partially distinct.** Three merge outputs:
- Question A (mandatory-reporter architecture — merge Lawyer Q4 + Analyst Q3): "Under what specific state laws do volunteers working one-on-one with at-risk teens in a structured program qualify as mandatory reporters, what documentation must the program maintain to prove mentors received required training on recognizing and reporting abuse, neglect, or self-harm disclosures, what liability exposure does the organization face if a mentor fails to report something they should have, and what decision tree guides staff when mandatory reporting obligations conflict with relationship preservation — with documentation standards and decision-rights frameworks that protect both youth and mentors while supporting program continuity?"
- Question B (trust paradox and safe-harbor — merge Devil's Advocate Q7 + Questioner Q8 + Questioner Q6): "How does the program reconcile mandatory reporting obligations with creating a safe harbor for youth to discuss risky behaviors without fear of consequences — particularly for juvenile-justice-referred youth whose additional surveillance could undermine trust — and how do we navigate the specific moment when a teen discloses something that will breach confidentiality and possibly damage the relationship at its most vulnerable point, while preventing rigid boundaries and reporting protocols from creating the distrust that makes authentic connection impossible for teens who already mistrust authority systems?"
- Question C (justice-involved data privacy — merge Lawyer Q8 + Audience Advocate Q13): "When mentees are referred through juvenile justice channels, what specific legal restrictions govern how referral information, case status, and involvement with the justice system can be documented, stored, and shared within the program (and what are the consequences of mishandling juvenile records in the jurisdiction) — and from the teen's perspective, what promises can the program genuinely keep about what information is shared, with whom, and under what circumstances, in ways that protect their privacy while still enabling effective mentorship?"

Three merges preserve distinct registers: regulatory-mechanism (A), relational-paradox (B), and promises-to-youth (C). Merging all seven would collapse Lawyer's regulatory specificity, DA's carceral-extension critique, and AA's voice-of-youth framing into a single mega-question that serves none of the three.

## Convergence Group 17: Measurement architecture — logic model, burden, self-fulfilling outcomes & surveillance

**Theme:** The measurement-system design — logic model from inputs to impact, multi-stakeholder success definition, measurement-that-energizes vs. measurement-as-burden, outcome-measures-creating-self-fulfilling-prophecies, and mentor-satisfaction-concealing-problems.
**Cluster:** C9 (Program Design, Measurement)
**Questions:**
- [Analyst Q12] Logic model from inputs to long-term impact — theory of change; testable assumptions; measurement frequency; effect-size thresholds.
- [Analyst Q13] Success from multiple stakeholder perspectives — youth/mentor/parent/referring-partner/funder; quantitative vs. qualitative; privacy-honoring collection.
- [Appreciative Inquirer Q12] Measurement that energizes rather than burdens — strengths-based philosophy; mentors and teens observing their own growth.
- [Audience Advocate Q14] Measuring success without burdening participants — community perspective on success; not reducing complex lives to metrics.
- [Devil's Advocate Q10] Success measurement becoming a burden — mentors completing surveys instead of spending time; minimalist metrics avoiding perverse incentives.
- [Systems Thinker Q11] Outcome measures creating self-fulfilling prophecies.
- [Systems Thinker Q12] Mentor satisfaction data concealing emerging problems.
**Merge recommendation:** **Preserve partially distinct.** Three merge outputs:
- Question A (logic model and multi-stakeholder success definition — merge Analyst Q12 + Analyst Q13): "What logic model connects program inputs (mentor hours, training quality, supervision) through short-term outputs (meeting frequency, relationship quality) to medium-term outcomes (youth engagement, school attendance) to long-term impact (life trajectory) — with a theory of change containing testable assumptions, measurement frequency that yields meaningful data without survey fatigue, and effect-size thresholds justifying program expansion — and how should success be defined and measured from multiple stakeholder perspectives (youth, mentors, parents, referring partners, funders), balancing quantitative indicators (attendance, milestone completion) with qualitative assessment (relationship depth, youth voice), and honoring participant privacy while generating required reporting?"
- Question B (measurement-as-energizing-vs-burden — merge Appreciative Inquirer Q12 + Devil's Advocate Q10 + Audience Advocate Q14): "How could outcome measurement be designed to actually reinforce the program's strengths-based philosophy — helping mentors and teens notice and celebrate positive change as it unfolds, with mentors and teens playing an active role in observing their own growth — while specifically avoiding the failure modes where mentors complete surveys instead of spending time with mentees, youth feel reduced to data points, reporting requirements conflict with relationship-building, or complex community lives are reduced to metrics that exhaust participants, and what minimalist metrics capture genuine impact without creating perverse incentives?"
- Question C (measurement pathologies — merge Systems Thinker Q11 + Systems Thinker Q12): "What outcome measures create self-fulfilling prophecies about program success — where the act of measuring shapes mentor and mentee behavior toward the measured dimension at the cost of unmeasured dimensions — and how might mentor satisfaction data specifically conceal emerging problems (where satisfied mentors quietly report okay outcomes for relationships that are actually failing mentee-visible measures, masking the signal that would prompt intervention)?"

Three merges preserve distinct registers: measurement-architecture (A), burden-and-energizing (B), and measurement-pathology (C). Merging all seven would collapse the quantitative-architecture, strengths-based-design, and systems-pathology framings into a single "measure-impact" mega-question that serves none.
