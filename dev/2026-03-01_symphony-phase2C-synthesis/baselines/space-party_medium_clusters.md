## Orphan Questions (Not Cluster-Forming)

The Provocateur's 8 questions are best treated as orphans from a thematic-clustering standpoint: they deliberately invert the question frame (zero-budget parties, anti-social parties, deception-parties, parent-less parties, etc.) and their value is the *provocation itself*, not the underlying topic they happen to probe. They cross-pollinate multiple clusters (e.g., "no ending" touches Pacing; "zero budget" touches Budget; "20 years from now" touches Memory), but assigning them to a single cluster destroys their distinctive framing. Per D1, these are treated as Append-group and appended as-is rather than clustered.

## Ambiguous Assignments (documented, not forced)

Several questions sit on cluster boundaries. These are documented here for SP1 scoring tolerance.

1. **Analyst Q11 "How can we design a party experience that creates distinct, photo-documentable moments…"** — sits between Cluster 9 (Memory) and Cluster 10 (Measurement). Assigned to Cluster 9 because photo-documentability is framed as a memory artifact. Could equally serve Cluster 8 (Theme) if photography is read as narrative documentation.
2. **Analyst Q4 "What does a typical 5-year-old's attention arc look like during a 2-3 hour party…"** — sits between Cluster 2 (Activity Design) and Cluster 3 (Pacing). Assigned to Cluster 3 because the explicit subject is sequencing across the arc. Could sit in Cluster 2 because the answer informs activity selection.
3. **Analogist Q3 "Campfire storytelling traditions"** — sits between Cluster 8 (Theme/Narrative) and Cluster 9 (Memory/Legacy). Assigned to Cluster 8 because campfire/ritual analogies are narrative structure, but the "enduring tradition" framing also fits Cluster 9.
4. **Systems Thinker Q13 "Balancing parent-perceived success vs. child-experienced joy"** — sits between Cluster 6 (Birthday Girl Centering) and Cluster 9 (Memory/Meaning). Assigned to Cluster 6 because the core tension is "for whom" rather than "for how long."
5. **Audience Advocate Q8 "Food sensitivities and eating comfort"** — sits between Cluster 5 (Safety — allergens) and Cluster 7 (Social — feeling singled out). Assigned to Cluster 2 (Activity Design) because the Audience Advocate frame centers on participation-design; allergen severity lands in Cluster 5 only on Devil's Advocate Q3. Documented as tri-borderline.
6. **Systems Thinker Q15 "Creating an immersive narrative without over-scripting"** — sits between Cluster 3 (Pacing) and Cluster 8 (Theme). Assigned to Cluster 8 because narrative-coherence is the subject; the pacing dimension is secondary.

## C1 Comparison (Per-Persona vs. Flat Pooling)

**Per-persona clustering (as input):** Reading the test file in its delivered structure, each persona's output has internal cluster structure (e.g., Analyst's 15 have 5 internal sub-themes; Audience Advocate's 15 split clearly into sensory / participation / belonging / specific-populations groups). Per-persona reading *surfaces* intra-persona clusters cleanly but *buries* cross-persona thematic convergences (e.g., the sensory-safety cluster pulls 1 question from Analyst, 2 from Audience Advocate, 2 from Devil's Advocate, 2 from Questioner, 2 from Systems Thinker, 1 from Appreciative Inquirer — trivially visible in a flat view, requires cross-referencing six sections in per-persona view).

**Flat pooling (all questions de-personaed and shuffled):** Re-clustering in this mode produces the same 10 thematic clusters identified above, with roughly the same membership. One shift: the Systems Thinker's pacing-heavy block dissolves slightly, with 2–3 of its 7 Cluster-3 questions drifting toward Cluster 2 or Cluster 8 depending on the interpreter's read (this is not a problem — it matches the documented ambiguity above). Flat pooling does not *reveal* new cross-persona themes here; the themes are already cross-persona. But it *prevents* the synthesizer from under-weighting convergences by treating each persona's section as an independent silo.

**Bias check:** Per-persona structure carries a **single-persona-dominated-cluster risk** in two places:
- Cluster 10 (Measurement/Frameworks) is 100% Analyst — per-persona reading would preserve this as legitimate; flat reading would too, because no other persona addresses explicit frameworks. This is a real persona-specific cluster, not an artifact.
- Cluster 3 (Pacing) is 54% Systems Thinker — reasonable given that systems thinking *is* about flow/loops, but a synthesizer reading per-persona might treat this cluster as "a Systems Thinker section" rather than a general pacing theme that also draws Analyst, Appreciative Inquirer, Storyteller.

**SP1 recommendation: flat pooling for synthesis input, with persona tags preserved as metadata.**
- Rationale: the primary convergences here are cross-persona (Clusters 1, 2, 4, 5, 7 all draw ≥4 personas). Per-persona grouping forces the synthesizer to re-flatten before clustering, adding cognitive overhead. Retaining persona tags (rather than stripping them) preserves the attribution needed for B3's per-persona representation requirements and for D1's round-robin append logic.
- Caveat: Cluster 10 and the Provocateur orphan group are genuinely persona-bound; flat pooling doesn't harm these because the personas' distinctive voices are preserved in the tagging.
- Recommendation holds at this cell; will need cross-effort confirmation (the cross-topic synthesis subagent should check whether this is robust at low and high effort).
