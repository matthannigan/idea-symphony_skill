## C1 Comparison (Per-Persona vs. Flat Pooling)

The methodology asks whether per-persona clustering biases results toward persona-aligned clusters and whether flat pooling reveals cross-persona themes. Both passes were performed.

**Per-persona clustering (clustering each persona's output independently first, then merging):**
- Analyst naturally splits into ~4 internal groups: regulatory scenario modeling, break-even/operations, quality/contractor systems, revenue-model
- Appreciative Inquirer splits into ~3: aspirational-future, owner-partnership, launch/pilot-relationships
- Audience Advocate splits into ~4: accessibility/emotional journey, community impact, owner vulnerability, regulatory participation
- Devil's Advocate splits into standard risk catalog: regulatory threats, financial viability, operational reliability, liability, owner termination
- Questioner broadly distributed across 10+ themes with few internal clusters (consistent with persona profile)
- Systems Thinker splits into ~4: political feedback loops, compliance reinforcement, growth dynamics, information asymmetry
- Lawyer splits into its predictable 4-cluster structure: liability allocation, entity/contract structure, regulatory compliance, tax/revenue mechanics

When merged, per-persona clustering produces a similar 12-cluster final set, but each cluster's "anchor persona" is more visible and persona-internal coherence is preserved.

**Flat pooling (treating all 92 Synthesize questions as a single pool, then clustering):**
- Same ~12 cluster set emerges (the one used above)
- Cross-persona convergences become visible up-front (e.g., Cluster 1 Regulatory Scenarios spans 6 personas — Analyst + DA + Questioner + Systems Thinker + Storyteller + Lawyer — which is striking and only emerges in flat pooling)
- Cluster 7 "Good Neighbor" emerges as the largest and most cross-cutting cluster (6 personas), invisible as such under per-persona structure
- Cluster 11 (Growth Dynamics, 100% Systems Thinker) is preserved without being marginalized; flat pooling does NOT erase persona-unique territory, it just doesn't privilege it

**Are cluster sets substantially different?**
No. Both passes produce ~12 clusters with ~85%+ overlap in cluster themes. Differences are in framing and emphasis:
- Per-persona pass surfaces persona-distinctive subclusters (e.g., Systems Thinker's "shifting the burden" framing is more visible; Lawyer's 4-cluster structure is more visible)
- Flat pooling surfaces cross-persona convergence (e.g., regulatory scenario spans 6 personas, good-neighbor spans 6 personas)

**Does per-persona structure bias toward persona-aligned clusters?**
Mildly yes. Per-persona clustering tends to produce ~14-16 clusters before merging (with persona-distinctive sub-themes), where flat pooling settles at ~12. The extra clusters in per-persona are persona-internal nuance that doesn't survive cross-persona resonance testing.

**Does flat pooling reveal cross-persona themes?**
Yes — and this is the more important finding. Cluster 1 (Regulatory Scenarios) and Cluster 7 ("Good Neighbor") are both 6-persona convergences. These are the dominant theme signals on this topic, and they only emerge clearly in flat pooling. Per-persona pre-clustering would surface these only at the merge step.

**Recommendation for SP1's prompt: flat pooling.**

Rationale:
1. Cross-persona convergence is the strongest signal of theme importance — flat pooling makes it visible immediately. For property-management, the dual 6-persona convergences on Regulatory Scenarios and Good Neighbor drive the whole topic's structure and should be foregrounded.
2. Persona-unique territory (Cluster 11 all Systems Thinker, Cluster 10 predominantly Appreciative Inquirer, Cluster 8 predominantly Audience Advocate) is preserved equally well in flat pooling
3. Persona attribution can be carried as metadata on each question; it does not require structural pre-clustering
4. Flat pooling is computationally simpler for the synthesis subagent — single pass over all questions
5. Per-persona pre-clustering would tempt the subagent to produce a cluster-per-persona output structure, which would defeat the purpose of synthesis

The synthesis prompt should instruct the subagent to read all questions in the assembled file, identify natural cross-persona themes, group questions by theme (preserving persona attribution), and report cluster composition including which personas contribute.
