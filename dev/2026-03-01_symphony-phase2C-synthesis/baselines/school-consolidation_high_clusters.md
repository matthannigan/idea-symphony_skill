**Input file:** test-data/school-consolidation_high.md

The 12 clusters below are derived from the 130 Synthesize-group questions. Append-group questions are intentionally excluded from B1 because Append voices are orthogonal to the Synthesize topic space (per D1 rationale: "dimensional balancing against the Synthesize output would work against the purpose of appending"). ## Cluster 1: Fiscal Modeling, Break-Even & Unit Economics

**Description:** Quantitative modeling of the consolidation's financial case — one-time transition costs (legal, systems integration, union buyouts, closure expenses), multi-year break-even analysis against $1.2M administrative savings, unit economics at 10/25/50-year enrollment scenarios, forensic accounting of obligations from distressed districts, and the crossover point where enrollment decline erodes savings. Core Accountant/Analyst territory with DA skepticism bridging.

**Question count:** 13
**Contributing personas:** Accountant (4), Analyst (3), Devil's Advocate (2), Questioner (3), Systems Thinker (0), Audience Advocate (0), Appreciative Inquirer (0), Empath (0), First Principles Thinker (0), Futurist (0)
**Primary dimension:** Analytical

**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- [Analyst Q1] True break-even analysis — $1.2M admin savings vs. one-time transition costs (legal, systems integration, contract reconciliation, facility closure).
- [Accountant Q1] True break-even timeline — $3-5M transition costs producing 3-4 year net loss; contingency reserves; smallest district's 2-year fund balance.
- [Devil's Advocate Q1] Transitional expenses omitted from savings calculations — comprehensive 5-year cash flow model for true break-even.
- [Devil's Advocate Q2] Debt and obligations from insolvent districts burden consolidated entity — forensic accounting of obligations before proceeding.
- [Questioner Q5] What costs are we not calculating? — hidden costs beyond admin savings (transportation, legal, IT, curriculum, union-negotiated wages).

Also covers remote-work-migration sensitivity and the demographic-aging curve affecting voter support.

## C1 Comparison: Per-Persona vs. Flat Pooling

C1 (folded into B1 per methodology §"Relationship to Remaining Phase 2B Items") asks whether per-persona clustering helps or hinders synthesis vs. flat pooling.

### Method

- **Per-persona clustering pass:** read each persona's questions in isolation, identify internal clusters per persona, then attempt to align across personas.
- **Flat pooling pass:** treat all 130 Synthesize-group questions as a single unattributed pool, cluster by theme.

The 12 clusters above are the **flat-pooling result**. For comparison, the per-persona pass produced these distinct artifacts.

### Per-Persona Pass — Differences from Flat-Pooling Result

1. **Clusters 1 + 2 (Fiscal Modeling + Enrollment Trajectory) fragment in per-persona view** because Accountant, Analyst, and Devil's Advocate each address both break-even and enrollment-decline-scenario questions in their own sections. Per-persona pass treats these as "Accountant's financial analysis," "Analyst's analytical framework," "Devil's Advocate's fiscal skepticism" — three adjacent sections with heavy overlap. Flat pooling reveals the legitimate cross-persona convergence: all three personas are asking break-even-with-decline questions, and their merger (Accountant's quantified detail + Analyst's framework + DA's skepticism) produces stronger coverage than any persona alone.

2. **Cluster 8 (Community Identity/Town Loss) appears as "Empath's section" in per-persona view** when flat pooling reveals it's a cross-persona cluster: Empath (6 questions — grief, pride, won-lost, surrender), AA (3 questions — town identity, property values, invisible losses), AI (3 questions — what makes districts special, redefining our school, facility-value creation), DA (2 questions — facility closure winners/losers, social fabric rivals combining), ST (1 — school closures rippling), FPT (1 — buildings as anchors). The per-persona view misses that 5 personas are addressing community-identity-loss from different registers (emotional interior, equity frame, strengths-reimagination, structural risk, definitional). Flat pooling surfaces this as a cross-register cluster where Empath's 6-question density doesn't dominate but anchors.

3. **Cluster 11 (Systems Dynamics) is ST-dominated (10/13)** but flat pooling correctly identifies it as a systems-dynamics cluster where ST anchors with definitional systems archetypes ("Shifting the Burden," "Success to the Successful," "Eroding Goals," "Fixes that Fail," self-amplifying loops, delay effects) and 3 other personas bridge at the edges (DA exit-option absence, FPT first-year-vs-decade-three math, Analyst failure-modes, Accountant financial-resilience-modeling). Per-persona view would treat this as "ST's section" and miss the cross-register bridges.

4. **Cluster 5 (Governance) is cross-register (5 personas) on an institutional-design theme** that per-persona view fragments into Analyst's "optimal governance structure," AI's "governance that builds trust," Questioner's "why assume unified district," FPT's "district as unit of analysis," and Futurist's "governance structures for adaptability." Each is addressing governance alternatives through different registers (optimization, trust-building, paradigm-reframing, first-principles-reframing, trend-extrapolation). Flat pooling produces a single coherent cluster.

5. **Cluster 9 (Political Feasibility) is the densest cross-persona cluster (9 personas contributing 13 questions)** — Analyst (2), DA (3), AI (2), AA (1), Empath (2), Questioner (2), FPT (1), Futurist (1). Per-persona view would scatter political-coalition-building content across 9 separate sections, each with 1-3 questions about voter approval, coalition-building, messaging, or backlash. Flat pooling reveals the underlying shared concern: how to achieve voter approval in all three districts simultaneously, framed from 9 different angles. This is one of the most dramatic flat-pooling vs. per-persona divergences in the cell.

6. **Cluster 12 (Reimagination) is FPT + Futurist + Questioner-dominant (9/10)**, with DA providing one cross-over (alternative models without full consolidation). Per-persona view treats this as three separate "big-picture reframing" sections. Flat pooling reveals the shared theme (is consolidation the right frame at all?) across three registers: FPT definitional, Futurist trend-extrapolation, Questioner assumption-challenging. Even DA's Q14 (alternative partial-consolidation models) fits the reimagination theme through skeptical-rigor register.

### Bias Check

Per-persona clustering systematically **over-weights the persona with the largest contribution** to a theme and **under-counts cross-persona convergence**. In this high-effort universe:

- **Cluster 7 (Student Experience) has Audience Advocate at 7 of 14 (50%)** — per-persona view treats as "AA's section on student impacts" when flat pooling reveals 6 personas contributing distinct registers on student-lived-experience (equity, emotional-interior, operational, paradigm).
- **Cluster 8 (Community Identity) has Empath at 6 of 16 (37%)** — per-persona view treats as "Empath's section" when flat pooling reveals 5 personas on community-identity-loss.
- **Cluster 11 (Systems Dynamics) has ST at 10 of 13 (77%)** — genuine ST concentration. But DA+FPT+Analyst+Accountant+Questioner contribute bridges at the edges that per-persona view fragments across sections.
- **Cluster 1 (Fiscal Modeling) has Accountant at 4 of 13 (31%)** — Accountant anchors but doesn't dominate. Flat pooling correctly identifies the Accountant/Analyst/DA/Questioner convergence on break-even-analysis territory.
- **Cluster 9 (Political Feasibility) has no dominant persona** — 9 personas contributing 1-3 questions each, DA at 3 of 13 (23%). This is the most cross-persona-convergent cluster; per-persona view would maximally fragment it.
- **Cluster 6 (Labor/Union) has no dominant persona** — 6 personas contributing 1-2 questions each. Per-persona view fragments labor-contract-reconciliation into 6 sections.

### SP1 Recommendation: **Flat pooling for synthesis input, with persona tags preserved as metadata.**

Rationale:
- The primary convergences here are heavily cross-persona (Clusters 1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 9 each draw ≥5 personas).
- Per-persona grouping would force the synthesizer to re-flatten before clustering — with 10 Synthesize personas and 47 orphans (36%), the cross-referencing overhead is quadratic.
- Retaining persona tags preserves attribution needed for B3's per-persona representation requirements and for D1's round-robin append logic.
- The 36% orphan/bridging rate is the highest observed in the evaluation set — flat pooling correctly handles bridging questions through soft-assignment with documented ambiguity; per-persona view would force each into a single persona section with no bridging annotation.

**Caveats:**
- Cluster 11 (Systems Dynamics) is ST-dominant (77%) — per-persona view would produce a similar ST-dominant bucket for systems content, then miss DA/FPT/Accountant cross-register bridges.
- Cluster 6 (Labor/Union) is small (9 questions) and cross-persona — per-persona view would fragment it beyond the natural cluster size.
- Cluster 12 (Reimagination) is FPT+Futurist+Questioner-dominant (9/10) — flat pooling reveals the convergence across three personas on the shared "is this the right frame?" theme that per-persona view fragments into three "reimagination" sections.

**Consistency across effort levels:** Other high-effort cells analyzed (property-management/high, space-party/high, tool-library/high, food-truck/high, habit-tracker/high, youth-mentorship/high) all recommend flat pooling with high-to-very-high confidence. School-consolidation/high, with 10 Synthesize personas (same as youth-mentorship/high) and 36% orphan/bridging rate (highest observed), sits in the very-high-confidence flat-pooling zone — stronger case for flat pooling than any other cell observed due to the orphan/bridging density.

**Confidence: very high.** With 10 Synthesize personas and 36% orphan/bridging rate (highest in the set), the cross-persona convergence density is so high that per-persona clustering would actively obscure the primary thematic structure. The topic's dual Analytical/Human-centered character (no single dimension dominates) means no single persona carries any cluster alone — every cluster is a cross-register convergence.
