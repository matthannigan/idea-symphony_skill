## Cluster 1: Budget, Cost & Resource Strategy

**Description:** Financial scoping for a 5th-birthday space party — explicit cost categories, DIY-vs-purchased trade-offs, hidden expenses inherent to space themes (dry ice, blacklights, metallic supplies), scaling decisions, and the longer-arc concern that elaborate themed parties create escalating expense pressure across years and across the friend group.
**Question count:** 9
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (3), Devil's Advocate (4), Questioner (1), Appreciative Inquirer (1)
**Primary dimension:** Strategic
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- [Analyst Q1] What is the realistic budget range for a space-themed 5th birthday party serving 11 children, and which cost categories typically offer the highest return on investment...
- [Analyst Q3] What are the trade-offs between DIY versus purchased solutions across decoration, activity materials, and food...
- [Devil's Advocate Q5] Are we budgeting for the hidden costs of space-themed execution? (dry ice, blacklights, metallic paints)
- [Devil's Advocate Q11] Are we creating unnecessary birthday escalation for future years?
- [Questioner Q5] What does "budget-friendly" mean in this specific context? What are the hidden costs we haven't considered?

## Cluster 2: Activity Design, Pacing & Engagement

**Question count:** 11
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (3), Devil's Advocate (3), Questioner (3), Systems Thinker (2)
**Primary dimension:** Tactical
**Sample questions (up to 5):**

- [Systems Thinker Q1] [Activity pacing and energy buildup] How should the sequence of activities be structured to manage 5-year-olds' collective energy...
- [Systems Thinker Q2] [Parallel vs. sequential activity design] What activities can run simultaneously as stations without requiring adult supervision...

## C1 Comparison (Per-Persona vs. Flat Pooling)

C1 (from Phase 2B P1/C1 carryover into Phase 2C) asks whether per-persona clustering — each persona pre-organizing their own questions into clusters before synthesis sees them — helps or hurts cross-persona synthesis vs. flat pooling.

### Pass A: Per-persona clustering preserved

When persona-internal cluster structure is honored before cross-persona synthesis, the Synthesize personas in this dataset present roughly:

- **Analyst:** ~3 internal clusters (budget/scaling, activity-fit, success/measurement)
- **Appreciative Inquirer:** ~4 internal clusters (wonder/peak moments, existing strengths, rhythms/materials, traditions)
- **Audience Advocate:** ~4 internal clusters (entry/spotlight/expectations, sensory/accessibility/safety, withdrawal/exit, parent experience)
- **Devil's Advocate:** ~4 internal clusters (theme failure modes, capability/safety, budget/labor hidden costs, social dynamics)
- **Questioner:** No persona-supplied internal cluster headings — questions are a flat sequence
- **Systems Thinker:** All 11 questions are supplied with bracketed cluster labels

Synthesizing within these persona-supplied frames yields a 7-cluster cross-persona map closely matching the one above (Cluster 1-7), with the persona-internal labels acting as scaffolding.

### Pass B: Flat pooling

Treating all 72 questions as a flat list and clustering bottom-up yields the same 7 themes. The cluster boundaries shift slightly:

- "Birthday-Girl Agency" and "Memory & Wonder" merge more readily under flat pooling because both center the child's perspective; persona-aware clustering preserves the structural distinction (one is about *whose* vision, the other about *what lasts*).
- Devil's Advocate Q11/Q12 (escalation, arms race) attach more strongly to "Family & Group Dynamics" under flat pooling and to "Budget" under persona-aware (since DA's internal cluster groups them with hidden-cost questions).

### Difference, Bias Check & SP1 Recommendation

- **Difference:** Marginal — same 7 clusters, slight boundary differences on 3-4 borderline questions. Both passes agree on which questions are convergent and which are unique.
- **Bias check:** Per-persona clustering does *not* over-anchor to persona vocabulary in this dataset. Systems Thinker's bracketed labels and Audience Advocate's bolded titles act more as topic markers than as claims about cross-persona structure. No evidence of cluster inflation from persona-supplied headings.
- **SP1 recommendation:** **Flat pooling for the synthesis step.** With 72 questions and 6 personas, flat pooling produces equivalent cluster quality with a simpler prompt and fewer instructions to track. Per-persona clustering would add value if persona-supplied headings carried distinctive structural content (e.g., Systems Thinker's archetype names) that should survive into output — but that distinctive vocabulary lives in the question text itself, not in the cluster headers. SP1 prompt variants should not require the synthesizer to honor persona-internal cluster structure.
