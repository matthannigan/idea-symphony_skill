## Cluster 1: Financial Model, Capital Stack & Unit Economics

**Description:** The quantified viability core — break-even math across revenue streams, food-cost-ratio targets on a fusion menu with higher-cost ingredients (ube, adobo), contribution-margin engineering, debt-vs-bootstrap tradeoffs on the $40K SBA microloan, equipment TCO (finance vs. lease vs. flexible structures), and the full hidden-cost / replacement-cycle budget. Where Analyst creates analytical structure, DA stress-tests the capital runway, and Accountant fills in with concrete dollar ranges, benchmarks, and depreciation mechanics. The densest analytical cluster in the cell.
**Question count:** 17
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (5: Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4, Q10), Accountant (7: Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4, Q6, Q11, Q12), Devil's Advocate (3: Q2, Q3, Q10), Questioner (2: Q5, Q8)
**Primary dimension:** Strategic
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- [Analyst Q1] Food cost ratio optimization — component-level food costs for lumpia tacos / adobo burritos / ube horchata and price points achieving 25-30% food cost ratios while remaining competitive.
- [Accountant Q1] Target food cost ratio and pricing elasticity — fusion-ingredient cost structure; $12-16 for a lumpia taco plate without alienating price-sensitive lunch crowds.
- [Accountant Q4] Bootstrapping vs. SBA loan tradeoffs — $750-850/month debt service over 7 years changing break-even runway.
- [Devil's Advocate Q2] Cash flow fragility during startup — 12-18 month valley of death; contingencies if revenue runs 30% below projections for first 8 months.
- [Devil's Advocate Q10] Equipment ownership trap — maintenance, depreciation, reduced flexibility vs. lease-to-own preserving optionality.

### Pass B: Flat pooling

Treating all 93 Synthesize questions as a flat list and clustering bottom-up yields the same 9 themes with marginal boundary shifts:

- **C1 (Financial) and C8 (Expansion) merge less cleanly:** Under flat pooling, the brick-and-mortar and fleet-expansion financing questions (Analyst Q12, Accountant Q8/Q9, DA Q8) pull slightly toward a unified "capital deployment" super-cluster that combines C1 and C8 financing sub-themes. Persona-aware clustering preserves C1's unit-economics focus and C8's growth-decision axis as distinct.
- **C5 (Community/Accountability) and C6 (Brand/Loyalty) stay distinct under both passes:** Even under flat pooling, AA's accountability-structural questions don't collapse into AI's strengths-based-delight questions — the registers differ enough that flat pooling preserves the split.
- **Accountant's questions scatter under flat pooling:** Accountant's 12 questions cleanly distribute — 7 to C1 (Financial), 3 to C8 (Expansion), 1 to C7 (Operations), 1 to C9 (Resilience), 1 to C2 (Regulatory). Flat pooling doesn't concentrate them as a "financial-specialist" sub-cluster but integrates them into the analytical backbone of multiple clusters — which is exactly how the persona-selection-guide describes the Accountant/Analyst complementarity ("Analyst creates analytical structure, Accountant fills it with data").
- **Audience Advocate's C4/C5 split:** AA is the cell's most vocally-divided persona — its 14 questions split across CX/accessibility (C4, 7 questions) and community-accountability (C5, 4 questions) with ordinance-equity (C2, 2) and expansion-community (1). Flat pooling preserves this split clearly.

### Difference, Bias Check & SP1 Recommendation

- **Difference:** Marginal — same 9 clusters, boundary differences on 6-7 borderline questions (documented above). Both passes agree on convergent vs. unique question classification. Both identify C1 (Financial, 20 questions) and C8 (Expansion, 15 questions) as the densest convergence zones.
- **Bias check:** Per-persona clustering preserves Systems Thinker's archetype vocabulary as a genuine signal (not bias-inflating cluster count). AI's strengths-based framing is preserved under both passes because its cluster homes (C5, C6) are large enough to absorb AI's presence without the persona dominating a cluster. The only real bias risk is **Audience Advocate over-concentration in C4:** 7 of AA's 14 questions primary-assign to C4. Under per-persona reading, a synthesizer may treat C4 as "the AA section" and under-weight the Accountant Q1 cross-reference (pricing elasticity). Flat pooling reveals that even in C4, AA is the dominant voice but not the only voice, which is a correct signal.
- **SP1 recommendation: flat pooling for the synthesis step, with persona tags preserved as metadata.**
  - Rationale: Convergences here are heavily cross-persona (C1 pulls 4 personas, C2 pulls 5, C8 pulls 5).
