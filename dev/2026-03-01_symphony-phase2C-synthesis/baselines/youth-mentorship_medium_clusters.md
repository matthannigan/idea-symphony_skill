## Cluster 1: Volunteer Screening, Background Checks & Legal Compliance

**Description:** The formal gatekeeping apparatus — background check scope, FCRA disclosure and adverse-action procedures, disqualifying offense categories, registry/database queries specific to minor-serving programs, renewal cycles, point-in-time vs. event-triggered re-screening, and the selection-effect question of whose qualified mentors get filtered out. Where Lawyer's regulatory specificity meets Analyst's false-positive/false-negative modeling, Devil's Advocate's "screening-can't-detect-subtle-harms" critique, Questioner's "background-checks-give-false-confidence" challenge, and Systems Thinker's selection-effects archetype.
**Question count:** 10
**Contributing personas:** Lawyer (3: Q1, Q2, Q3), Analyst (1: Q2), Devil's Advocate (1: Q2), Questioner (1: Q7), Systems Thinker (1: Q10), Appreciative Inquirer (1: —), Audience Advocate (1: Q4 — cross-ref), plus cross-refs
**Primary dimension:** Strategic
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- [Lawyer Q1] Disqualifying offenses and registry checks — specific criminal offense categories permanently disqualifying mentor applicants; minor-serving registries; mid-program discovery protocols.
- [Lawyer Q2] Background check scope and renewal cycles — state-law jurisdictional conflicts; point-in-time snapshot vs. annual/biennial/event-triggered re-checks.
- [Lawyer Q3] FCRA compliance for volunteer screening — disclosure and consent requirements; adverse-action procedures if a background check leads to rejection.
- [Analyst Q2] Screening calibration across dimensions — background checks, reference interviews, behavioral assessments — false-positive/false-negative tradeoffs without excluding qualified community mentors.
- [Devil's Advocate Q2] Dangerous mismatching beyond background checks — subtle incompatibilities; real-time monitoring; early-warning indicators for deteriorating relationships.

### Pass B: Flat pooling

Treating all 94 Synthesize questions as a flat list and clustering bottom-up yields the same 9 themes with marginal boundary shifts:

- **C2 (Mandatory Reporting) and C6 (Risk/Liability) stay distinct under both passes:** Even when Lawyer questions about mandatory reporting, boundary policies, and insurance pool together, the mandatory-reporting-as-trust-breaking-event frame (C2) is register-distinct from the boundary-as-preventive-architecture frame (C6). Flat pooling preserves the split.
- **C7 (Power/Consent/Family) and C8 (Meeting Experience) stay distinct:** Audience Advocate spans both but her questions split register — consent-and-family-systems framing (C7) is structural; meeting-experience framing (C8) is experiential. Flat pooling reveals this split clearly.
- **Lawyer's compound questions scatter under flat pooling** — when each Lawyer question is unpacked, its sub-issues distribute: Q1 → C1 (disqualifying offenses), Q4 → C2 (mandatory reporting) + C4 (training documentation cross-ref), Q5 → C6 (insurance), Q6 → C6 (boundary policy), Q7 → C7 (consent), Q8 → C2 (data privacy for justice-involved), Q9 → C7 (FERPA), Q10 → C6 (data retention). Flat pooling integrates Lawyer into multiple clusters rather than concentrating her as a "legal-specialist" block — which is exactly how the persona-selection-guide describes the Lawyer: regulatory specificity across the full surface area.
