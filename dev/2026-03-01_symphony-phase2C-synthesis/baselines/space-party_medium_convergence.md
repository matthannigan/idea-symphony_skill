# space-party — medium — Convergence Baseline

**Input file:** test-data/space-party_medium.md
**Total questions in input:** 115
**Convergence groups:** 14
**Questions participating in convergence:** 58 (of 115, 50.4%)
**Convergence density:** 50.4%

**Convention:** A convergence group = 2+ questions from *different personas* addressing the same underlying concern. Within-persona thematic repetition is not counted. The Provocateur's 8 orphan questions are excluded from convergence counting because their value is provocation-framing, not topic-convergence — they are appended distinctively per D1.

## Convergence Group 1 — Sensory Overwhelm & Fear

**Theme:** The space theme's classic motifs (dim lighting, loud rockets, dark decor, "alien" imagery) risk frightening or overwhelming children, especially those with sensory sensitivities.
**Cluster:** B1 Cluster 1 (Sensory & Emotional Safety)
**Questions:**
- [Audience Advocate Q2] Sensory overload management — atmosphere exciting for sensory-seekers vs. overwhelming for darkness/noise-sensitive children
- [Devil's Advocate Q1] What space-themed elements could actually frighten 5-year-olds? Dark lighting, "alien" decorations, loud rocket sounds
- [Questioner Q2] How might the party overwhelm or frighten some children? Sensory sensitivities at this age
- [Systems Thinker Q3] Avoiding the "more is more" trap in decoration and stimulation — tipping point between magical immersion and chaotic overstimulation

**Merge recommendation:** **Merge into one question.** Source-anchored text:
> "Where is the tipping point between magical immersion and sensory overwhelm for 5-year-olds? Which space motifs (dim lighting, loud sounds, alien imagery) most risk frightening or overstimulating children with sensory sensitivities, and how can we design regulation-friendly spaces — quiet corners, softer lighting options, advance warnings — that let sensory-sensitive children participate meaningfully without watering down the theme for others?"

The four voices converge tightly on the same underlying concern and three of them explicitly name the same motifs (dim lighting, loud sounds, alien imagery). Merge is defensible.

## Convergence Group 2 — Arrival Transitions

**Theme:** The arrival/drop-off moment as a design challenge — building the transition from "regular world" to "party world" without triggering anxiety.
**Cluster:** B1 Cluster 1 (Sensory & Emotional Safety) + B1 Cluster 8 (Theme/Narrative)
**Questions:**
- [Audience Advocate Q1] First arrival jitters — arrival experience ensuring welcome rather than intimidation
- [Audience Advocate Q10] Parent separation anxiety — arrival/check-in process for nervous drop-offs
- [Storyteller Q1] The arrival story — sensory details transforming the approach into the beginning of a space adventure
- [Systems Thinker Q7] Managing parent-child dynamic shifts during drop-off/pick-up
- [Analogist Q2] Theme park narrative arcs — anticipation, immersion, arrival as narrative touchpoint

**Merge recommendation: Preserve all distinct.** The voices differ too much to merge. Audience Advocate focuses on *anxious children and anxious parents*; Storyteller focuses on *narrative seduction*; Systems Thinker focuses on *ripple effects between parents and children*; Analogist imports a *theme-park phase model*. Merging would collapse four distinct design lenses (equity, narrative, systems, analogy) into a generic arrival question. Keep all four; the synthesis output should have at minimum 2 (one equity-framed + one narrative-framed) to preserve the dual concern.

## Convergence Group 6 — Activity Failure Modes & Contingency

**Theme:** What to do when activities collapse mid-party — plan-B options, brittle points, activity-ending-unevenly problems.
**Cluster:** B1 Cluster 5 (Safety, Risk & Contingency)
**Questions:**
- [Analyst Q8] How can we structure the party timeline to minimize common failure modes — chaos during transitions, activities ending unevenly, overstimulation, logistical bottlenecks
- [Devil's Advocate Q4] What happens when the "perfect" space activity collapses under time pressure? Backup "plan B" activities in under 2 minutes
- [Questioner Q8] What if we planned for half the children but twice as many show up? What if weather changes outdoor plans? Where are brittle points in the plan?
- [Systems Thinker Q8] Handling unexpected system disruptions — injuries, meltdowns, spills

**Merge recommendation: Preserve all distinct** (partial merge). Merge Analyst Q8 + Devil's Advocate Q4 into:
> "Which activities have the highest collapse risk and what 2-minute-deployable plan-B activities can substitute, so the timeline absorbs uneven-ending or chaotic-transition failure modes without the whole party unraveling?"

Questioner Q8 (scale/weather contingency) and Systems Thinker Q8 (disruption-handling / emotional contagion) both introduce additional distinct concerns — plan-B is not the same as scale contingency and neither is the same as meltdown-containment. Keep Questioner Q8 and Systems Thinker Q8 separate.

## Convergence Density Interpretation

- **50.4% convergence density** — roughly half the questions participate in cross-persona convergence, half are uniquely-voiced. This feels appropriate for a medium-effort cell with 6 Synthesize personas whose territories are designed to overlap on core concerns but diverge on distinctive framings.
- **Highest-convergence clusters:** Cluster 1 (Sensory), Cluster 4 (Budget), Cluster 9 (Memory). Four+ personas converge in each.
- **Lowest-convergence cluster:** Cluster 10 (Measurement/Frameworks) — only Analyst addresses. This is a real persona-specific territory, not an artifact.
- **Append-group uniqueness:** Visionary's 7 are 100% unique; Storyteller's 8 are ~60% unique; Analogist's 8 are ~75% unique; Provocateur's 8 are all orphans. This supports D1's Append treatment — these voices would be stripped by synthesis.
