## C1 Comparison (Per-Persona vs. Flat Pooling)

**Per-persona clustering (as input):** Reading the test file in its delivered structure, each persona's output has clear internal cluster structure. Analyst's 14 split cleanly into ~4 internal sub-themes (economics, operational capacity, measurement, partnerships). Audience Advocate's 15 split into sensory/barrier/dignity/belonging/social groups. Devil's Advocate's 12 split into sustainability-risk / cost-risk / governance-risk groups. Per-persona reading *surfaces* intra-persona clusters cleanly but *buries* cross-persona thematic convergences — e.g., Cluster 5 (Access/Barriers) pulls 8 from Audience Advocate, 2 from Devil's Advocate, 3 from Questioner, 1 from Appreciative Inquirer, 1 from Analyst: trivially visible in flat view, requires cross-referencing five sections in per-persona view.

**Flat pooling (all questions de-personaed and shuffled):** Re-clustering in this mode produces the same 11 thematic clusters identified above, with roughly the same membership. Two shifts:
- The Audience Advocate's 8-question contribution to Cluster 5 dissolves slightly — 2-3 of those AA questions drift toward Cluster 6 (first-time dignity) depending on read (already documented as boundary cases).
- Systems Thinker's Cluster-11 block appears less cluster-dominant on flat view because its 5 questions get interspersed with Analyst and Questioner contributions and don't read as a "systems section."

Flat pooling does not *reveal* new cross-persona themes here; the themes are already cross-persona. It *prevents* the synthesizer from under-weighting convergences by treating each persona's section as an independent silo.

**Bias check:** Per-persona structure carries a **single-persona-dominated-cluster risk** in three places:
- Cluster 5 (Access/Equity Barriers) is 53% Audience Advocate — a synthesizer reading per-persona might treat this as "the Audience Advocate section" and under-weight the Devil's Advocate, Questioner, Analyst, and Appreciative Inquirer contributions to the same cluster. Flat pooling reveals that 5 different personas converge here.
- Cluster 8 (Volunteer Sustainability & Governance) is 38% Politician — the Politician's governance vocabulary dominates. Per-persona reading would surface this correctly (all 5 Politician governance questions are in this section). Flat reading would distribute them among Devil's Advocate, Questioner, Appreciative Inquirer, and Analogist contributions to the same cluster and make the Politician's distinct governance frame more visible.
- Cluster 11 (Long-term Dynamics) is 45% Systems Thinker — reasonable given that system dynamics *is* the persona's named territory. Per-persona reading would correctly surface this as a Systems-Thinker-anchored cluster, but might underweight the Analyst, Questioner, Appreciative Inquirer, and Storyteller voices that contribute too.

**SP1 recommendation: flat pooling for synthesis input, with persona tags preserved as metadata.**
- Rationale: the primary convergences here are cross-persona (Clusters 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 all draw ≥3 personas). Per-persona grouping forces the synthesizer to re-flatten before clustering, adding cognitive overhead. Retaining persona tags (rather than stripping them) preserves the attribution needed for B3's per-persona representation requirements and for D1's round-robin append logic.
- Caveat: Cluster 5's Audience Advocate concentration and Cluster 8's Politician concentration are legitimate persona-strength signals (AA is the equity persona; Politician is the governance persona). Flat pooling doesn't harm these because persona tags are preserved.
- Recommendation holds at this cell with **moderate confidence** — the Politician's Append-routed verbose questions (40-80 words) in Cluster 8 and Cluster 10 are structurally distinct from the other Synthesize questions, and mixing them in flat view may slightly confuse cluster-formation. Flat pooling with persona tags still wins, but scoring should tolerate minor cluster-boundary variation around the Politician contributions.
