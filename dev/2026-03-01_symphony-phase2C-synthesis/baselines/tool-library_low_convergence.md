## Convergence Group 1
**Theme:** Equitable access and structural barriers for marginalized community members
**Cluster:** B1 Cluster 1 (Equitable Access, Dignity, Inclusion)
**Questions:**
- [Audience Advocate Q9] How might the tool library inadvertently prioritize the needs of already-resourced homeowners while renters, low-income neighbors, and those facing the most barriers remain underserved? (Structural prioritization of the already-resourced.)
- [Audience Advocate Q2] How might the physical space, volunteer interactions, and policies accidentally signal "this space is not for you" to different community members? (Invisible-signal audit across race, language, ability, institutional-history.)
- [Audience Advocate Q5] What unspoken assumptions about tool literacy, physical ability, or access to resources might create invisible barriers to participation? (Assumptions embedded in ID, smartphones, tool vocabulary.)
- [Devil's Advocate Q10] What if the people who most need free tool access are exactly those who can't meet membership requirements? (Undocumented, transient, unstably-housed residents.)
- [Devil's Advocate Q11] How do we prevent the "early adopter capture" problem where the most organized and resourced residents dominate access? (Priority scheduling, checkout caps.)
- [Questioner Q5] What barriers might prevent different community members from using the library? (Language, ID, hours, cultural norms.)
- [Questioner Q7] What if early adopters don't reflect the neighborhood's diversity? (Proactive equity metrics.)

**Merge recommendation:**
> "How will the library design against the structural tendency to prioritize already-resourced residents (homeowners, English-speakers, the documented, those with stable housing) — including membership requirements, operating hours, tool literacy assumptions, and early-adopter capture dynamics — so that the neighbors facing the greatest obstacles are centered rather than treated as edge cases?"

Strongest and densest convergence in the file. Seven questions, four personas, one shared structural concern. Merge preserves each contributor's distinct angle (AA's "accidental signals," DA's "membership requirements," Q's "early-adopter capture"). **Adjacent but preserve separately:** Questioner Q6 ("How do we define 'community' and who gets to belong?") is boundary-definition rather than barrier-removal — same cluster, distinct question. Questioner Q10 ("What if our damage policies are too punitive for low-income users?") is damage-policy-specific — same cluster, distinct question.
