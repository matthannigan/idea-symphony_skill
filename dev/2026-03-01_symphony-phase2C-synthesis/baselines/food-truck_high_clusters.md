# food-truck — high — Cluster Baseline

**Input file:** test-data/food-truck_high.md
**Total questions in input:** 179
**Synthesize-group questions:** 132 (clustered below)
**Append-group questions:** 47 (Provocateur 7, Storyteller 7, Visionary 7, Constraint Flipper 8, Politician 10, Analogist 8 — clustered separately in B4)
**Natural cluster count (Synthesize universe):** 12

The 12 clusters reported here are derived from the 132 Synthesize-group questions. Append-group questions are intentionally excluded from B1 because Append voices are orthogonal to the Synthesize topic space and would distort cluster centroids (per D1 rationale). Append questions are clustered within their persona for round-robin selection in B4. Politician (Tier 3 Append) is included in the Append universe per persona-selection-guide_Phase2B §Append Group.

Clusters are listed in descending order of question count. Counts in parentheses next to a persona name indicate how many of that persona's questions fall in the cluster.

## Cluster 1: Regulatory/Ordinance Navigation & Political Engagement

**Description:** Questions about the pending city ordinance (restricted downtown vending hours, annual licensing fees), compliance economics, permit scarcity, commissary dependency, and the strategic stance toward the regulatory process (fight, comply, innovate, coalition-build). Spans scenario modeling of ordinance outcomes, reframing of regulation as design input, and the political/community-engagement angle of influencing ordinance design. Distinct from Cluster 4 (capital/debt) because the driver is external regulatory risk rather than internal financial structure.

**Question count:** 17
**Contributing personas:** Devil's Advocate (4), Questioner (3), Systems Thinker (3), Accountant (1), Analyst (1), Empath (2), FPT (1), Futurist (1), AA (1)
**Primary dimension:** Strategic (with analytical and human-centered overlay)

**Sample questions:**
- [Analyst Q8] Ordinance change scenario planning — best/worst/compromise financial impact modeling; contingency triggers.
- [Devil's Advocate Q2] Compliance cost projections — worst-case 50% higher or hours cut by one-third; viability under those conditions.
- [Questioner Q7] What if the proposed city ordinances pass in their current form? — restricted hours + fees impact on revenue model; alternative locations as blessing in disguise.
- [Systems Thinker Q1] Ordinances creating self-defeating cycles — restricted downtown pushes into residential, residential complaints drive stricter regulation; interrupting the feedback loop.

- [Empath Q10] The us-versus-them framing — engage with ordinance process as collaborative rather than adversarial.
- [FPT Q10] Regulatory constraint as design input — food truck designed FOR (not despite) current + future regulatory environment.
- [Audience Advocate Q11] Engaging the ordinance process to center community needs rather than just business interests.

## C1 Comparison: Per-Persona vs Flat Pooling

C1 (folded into B1 per methodology §"Relationship to Remaining Phase 2B Items") asks whether per-persona clustering helps or hinders synthesis vs flat pooling.

### Method

- **Per-persona clustering pass:** read each persona's questions in isolation, identify each persona's internal clusters, then attempt to align across personas.
- **Flat pooling pass:** treat all 132 Synthesize-group questions as a single unattributed pool, cluster by theme.

The 12 clusters above are the **flat-pooling result**. For comparison, the per-persona pass produced these distinct artifacts:

### Per-Persona Pass — Differences from Flat-Pooling Result

1. **Cluster 2 (Financial Model) and Cluster 4 (Capital Structure) merge** in per-persona view. Accountant, Analyst, and Devil's Advocate all treat financial concerns as a single bucket internally. Flat pooling separates them because the underlying decisions are different — Cluster 2 is about per-unit pricing/contribution-margin, Cluster 4 is about aggregate capital/debt architecture. Mergers in Phase 2C that collapse these lose the distinction between "is the menu profitable?" and "is the capital structure viable?"

2. **Cluster 5 (Fusion Validation) and Cluster 8 (Customer Acquisition) merge** in per-persona view for Questioner and Devil's Advocate. Flat pooling separates them because concept differentiation (why would someone choose you) is distinct from acquisition pipeline mechanics (how do strangers find and retain you). Devil's Advocate Q15 (CAC reality check) and Q6 (repeat vs novelty) are in different clusters by flat pool despite sharing DA's critical-framing voice.

3. **Cluster 1 (Regulatory) fragments persona-by-persona** in per-persona view. Devil's Advocate frames it as compliance risk; Systems Thinker as feedback loops; Questioner as assumption-challenge + pivoting; Empath as adversarial-vs-collaborative framing; FPT as design-input reframing; AA as community-impact. Flat pooling reveals the regulatory question as one cluster with 6 distinct angles — showing the cross-persona convergence that per-persona view fragments.

4. **Cluster 6 (Community) and Cluster 7 (Customer Experience) merge** in per-persona view because AI, AA, and Empath overlap in human-centered framings. Flat pooling separates them because Cluster 6 is structurally framed (equity, access, cultural authenticity, power) while Cluster 7 is experientially framed (atmosphere, ritual, delight). This mirrors tool-library/high's Cluster 8/9 distinction — both are human-centered but operate at different levels (structural architecture vs experiential feel).

5. **Cluster 12 (First Principles) is an FPT-and-Questioner-only cluster** in per-persona view. Flat pool confirms this but places adjacent Systems Thinker (Q5 catering-as-shifting-burden) as an orphan that touches Cluster 12 territory. The flat-pool finding aligns with tool-library: First Principles cluster is structurally dominated by FPT and Questioner.

6. **Cluster 11 (Emerging Trends) is Futurist-only** in per-persona view, and flat pool confirms this. This cell is a cleaner Futurist-anchored cluster than tool-library/high's Cluster 12 (External Positioning) because food-truck's Futurist questions are mostly direct-trend framings rather than strategic-positioning framings.

### Bias Check

Per-persona clustering systematically **over-weights the persona with the largest contribution** to a theme, and **under-counts cross-persona convergence**. In this universe:
- Audience Advocate contributes 9 of 12 Cluster 6 questions (~75%) — per-persona view would *isolate* this as an AA-only "equity/community" cluster and miss the AI (1) and Empath (2) contributions.
- AI contributes 5 of 11 Cluster 7 questions (~45%) — per-persona view would treat this as an AI-dominated cluster and lose the Empath (3) and AA (2) experiential-framing contributions.
- Futurist contributes 6 of 7 Cluster 11 questions (~86%) — per-persona view accurately represents Futurist-dominance here; flat pool does not gain much on this cluster vs per-persona.
