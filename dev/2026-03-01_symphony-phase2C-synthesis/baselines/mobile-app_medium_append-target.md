## Per-Persona Internal Cluster Identification

Inspection of the 4 Append personas' source questions yields the following internal cluster structures. None of the four personas supply explicit `##` cluster headings in the test data, so cluster labels here are inferred from question content and the universal-3-cluster heuristic noted in the persona-selection guide (Provocateur: "universal 3-cluster structure"; Connector: "exactly 3 clusters, zero filler — inherits Analogist's append mechanics cleanly"; Visionary: "universal 3-cluster structure; self-contained questions ideal for append"; Storyteller: "universal 3-cluster structure").

### Provocateur (7 questions → 3 internal clusters)

- **Cluster P-A: Feature/product destructive inversion** (3 questions) — inverting the premise "keep features" into "delete features" in various absurdist forms
  - Q1: What if you kept exactly ONE feature and made it absurdly worse? (useless button saying "I worked out"; data vs. ritual of acknowledgment)
  - Q2: What if the app did nothing but DELETE features for the next 8 months? (controlled demolition teaching what matters)
  - Q7: What if the 2% daily users are the problem, not the solution? (drive away most-engaged users; freak-user retention vs. normal humans)
- **Cluster P-B: Alternative product-category reframings** (2 questions) — inverting what-the-product-IS
  - Q3: What if you launched a "competitor" that was intentionally terrible? (all 15 features broken; which imperfections are dealbreakers?)
  - Q4: What if your 180K downloads were 180K different people who never opened twice? (single-use ceremony; perfect 47-second journey)
- **Cluster P-C: Fundamental category/time reframings** (2 questions) — inverting basic assumptions about what's possible
  - Q5: What if you explained this app to someone from 1995 who has never seen a mobile phone? (which features require digital existence to make sense)
  - Q6: What if the app was designed for a species that experiences time backwards? (motivation inverted; wrong direction of temporal motivation)

## Integration Notes (per D1 tagging convention)

Per D1's implementation description in the persona-selection-guide: "Append selected questions to the Phase 2C output under a persona-labeled section." The final Phase 2C output structure should be:

1. **Synthesized clusters** (29 questions across 9 clusters, per B3 Target) — each cluster labeled with its theme.
2. **Append section** (20 questions) — tagged by persona, in a distinct section of the output (or interleaved with clusters if the synthesis prompt design elects interleaving).

**Tagging style:** Each Append question carries the `[Perspective: persona-name]` tag so the reader can distinguish Append-group distinctive voice from synthesized Q&A. D1 is silent on interleaved-vs-segregated presentation; the default assumed here is a **segregated Append section at the end** (per the persona-selection-guide's "directly appended" language), with clear persona-labels. SP1 may test variants of this integration.

**Connector integration specifics:** Because Connector replaced Analogist for this cell (per GT1 Rev 3), the Append section reads as "Connector, Provocateur, Storyteller, Visionary" rather than the default "Analogist, Provocateur, Storyteller, Visionary." D1 is silent on naming/ordering under the Connector swap; the convention adopted here is alphabetical-by-persona-name with Connector in its natural alphabetical position. SP1 may test whether a "Connector is where Analogist would be" slot-convention is preferred; this is a formatting decision with no content impact.

**Cross-cluster tagging (optional):** Append questions can be secondarily tagged with the B1 thematic cluster(s) they most closely relate to (column 2 in the table above), so synthesized clusters have a "see also" pointer to relevant Append content. Example: Cluster 1 (Core Value Identification) has a "see also: Provocateur Q1 (one feature absurdly worse), Provocateur Q4 (180K different single-users), Provocateur Q5 (explaining to 1995-person), Visionary Q1 (fitness as emergence), Visionary Q3 (emotional-state destination), Visionary Q4 (identity transformation), Connector Q1 (feature pruning as ecosystem succession), Connector Q7 (retention-burnout feedback loop)" reference. This is a nice-to-have, not a D1 requirement.

**Provocateur treatment:** Provocateur questions inherently don't map cleanly to B1 thematic clusters — they're orphans in B1 because their value is the provocation itself (absurd inversion + "real insight"), not the underlying topic they happen to probe. Tagged as `[Perspective: Provocateur]` with optional cross-reference. The "real insight" italicized footers in each Provocateur question (e.g., *"The real insight: is the value in the data or in the ritual of acknowledgment?"*) are preserved as part of the question per D1.

**Connector vs. Analogist treatment note:** Per R7, Connector "inherits Analogist's append mechanics cleanly: same 3-cluster structure, same self-containment, same round-robin compatibility." For mobile-app specifically, R7 predicts Connector activates on inter-domain topics. Inspection of the 8 Connector source questions shows a mixed-mode distribution: 2 structural-isomorphism (retention-burnout feedback loop, notification-as-dependency-mapping), 2 cross-domain-import (IKEA effect, museum curation), 2 biological/linguistic isomorphism (ecosystem succession, language death), 1 paradigm-choice-between-metaphors (surgical triage vs. architectural restoration), 1 cross-domain-import-with-specific-theorist (entropy / Shannon). This is a 75% non-pure-import activation rate — validating the R7 swap rule for mobile-app (inter-domain topic where structural-isomorphism and recombination activate beyond simple import).

**Do not merge appended questions with synthesized questions even when they touch the same concern.** E.g., Visionary Q1 (fitness as emergence rather than tracking) touches C1 (Core Value) and overlaps conceptually with CG5 (what-job-are-users-hiring) and CG6 (one-thing-evolution), but D1 treats these as orthogonal — they preserve the persona's distinctive cognitive mode (paradigm-challenging reimagination) rather than filling a Synthesize gap (feature-identification via cohort-and-stickiness analysis).

**Preserve verbatim text.** Append-group questions are not edited, condensed, or reformatted. The "real insight" footers in Provocateur questions, Storyteller's named-character format (Sarah, Marcus, Priya), Visionary's paradigm-framing prose, and Connector's bracketed cluster-style prefixes (e.g., "[Feature pruning as ecosystem succession]") are preserved verbatim.
