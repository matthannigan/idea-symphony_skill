# habit-tracker — high — Cluster Baseline

**Input file:** test-data/habit-tracker_high.md
**Total questions in input:** 156
**Synthesize-group questions:** 121 (clustered below)
**Append-group questions:** 35 (Provocateur 7, Storyteller 7, Visionary 8, Constraint Flipper 7, Analogist 6 — clustered separately in B4)
**Natural cluster count (Synthesize universe):** 11

The 11 clusters reported here are derived from the 121 Synthesize-group questions. Append-group questions are intentionally excluded from B1 because Append voices are orthogonal to the Synthesize topic space and would distort cluster centroids (per D1 rationale: "dimensional balancing against the Synthesize output would work against the purpose of appending"). Append questions are clustered within their persona for round-robin selection in B4.

Clusters are listed in descending order of question count. Counts in parentheses next to a persona name indicate how many of that persona's questions fall in the cluster.

---

## Cluster 1: Failure States, Streak Breaks, and Recovery Design

**Description:** Questions about how the app should handle missed days, broken streaks, "what-the-hell" cascades, and the emotional/behavioral dynamics of returning after lapses. Spans both the felt experience of failure and the structural design of recovery paths.

**Question count:** 14
**Contributing personas:** Empath (4), Audience Advocate (3), Devil's Advocate (2), Systems Thinker (2), Questioner (2), Appreciative Inquirer (1)
**Primary dimension:** Human-centered (with tactical/structural overlay)

**Sample questions:**
- [Audience Advocate Q1] What does failure feel like? — designing for failure states that support recovery rather than shame.
- [Devil's Advocate Q4] Guilt-driven disengagement — sabbatical mode, reframing missed days as rest days, streak pausing without "cheating" feeling.
- [Empath Q1] How does the app feel during the inevitable failure days? — warmth toward missed days, not just successes.
- [Systems Thinker Q4] Failure cascades and recovery paths — interrupting the "what the hell" effect, early-warning indicators of disengagement spirals.
- [Questioner Q12] What if failure is the point? — reframing missed days as data points rather than breaks to avoid.

## C1 Comparison: Per-Persona vs Flat Pooling

C1 (folded into B1 per methodology §"Relationship to Remaining Phase 2B Items") asks whether per-persona clustering helps or hinders synthesis vs flat pooling.

### Method

- **Per-persona clustering pass:** read each persona's questions in isolation, identify each persona's internal clusters, then attempt to align across personas.
- **Flat pooling pass:** treat all 121 Synthesize-group questions as a single unattributed pool, cluster by theme.

The 11 clusters above are the **flat-pooling result**. For comparison, the per-persona pass produced these distinct artifacts:

### Per-Persona Pass — Differences from Flat-Pooling Result

1. **Cluster 4 (Gamification) and Cluster 7 (Behavioral Theory) merge** in per-persona view because individual personas (e.g., Systems Thinker, FPT) treat gamification critique and behavior-theory questioning as a single concern internally. The flat pool reveals that they are tactically distinct — gamification questions are about *what mechanisms to build*, theory questions are about *what assumptions underlie the product*.
2. **Cluster 5 (Privacy) and Cluster 11 (Architecture) merge** in per-persona view for Analyst, Systems Thinker, and Futurist (each treats privacy as an architectural concern). The flat pool separates them because Audience Advocate's privacy questions are about *user agency*, not architecture — a distinction lost when clustering per-persona.
3. **Cluster 9 (Emotional Tone) is invisible** in per-persona view because no single persona has a "tone" cluster — the cluster only emerges when Appreciative Inquirer's positive framing, Empath's emotional questions, and Audience Advocate's self-relationship questions are pooled together.
4. **Cluster 8 (Inclusion) appears as an Audience-Advocate-only cluster** in per-persona view, missing the contributions from Empath (life transitions) and Systems Thinker (habit evolution).

### Bias Check

Per-persona clustering systematically **over-weights the persona with the largest contribution** to a theme, and **under-counts cross-persona convergence**. In this universe:
- Audience Advocate dominates Cluster 8 in flat view (5/9) — per-persona view would isolate this further.
- Cluster 9 has no dominant persona in flat view (5/4/2 split is the largest persona contribution); per-persona view fails to find this cluster at all.

### SP1 Recommendation: **Use flat pooling.**

Flat pooling produces:
- More clusters (11 vs ~7 for per-persona)
- Better cross-persona convergence detection (Cluster 9 only emerges flat)
- Cleaner separability between adjacent themes (Cluster 4 vs 7, Cluster 5 vs 11)

For SP1 scoring, the synthesis prompt variants should be evaluated against the flat-pool cluster set (the 11 above). If a variant produces ~7 clusters that mirror the per-persona structure, score it as under-clustering rather than as a different-but-valid alternative.

**One caveat:** flat pooling may obscure persona representation. SQ3 scoring (persona representation) should *cross-check* the per-persona view: if flat-pool synthesis produces a Cluster 8 that mentions Empath's life-transition framing, persona representation is preserved; if it strips the Empath voice in favor of Audience Advocate phrasing, that's a representation failure even if the cluster theme is correct.
