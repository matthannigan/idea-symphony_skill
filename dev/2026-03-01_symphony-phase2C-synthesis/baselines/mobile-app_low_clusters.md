# mobile-app — low — Cluster Baseline

**Input file:** test-data/mobile-app_low.md
**Total questions in input:** 101 (Synthesize: 72; Append: 29)
**Natural cluster count:** 9 (Synthesize-group); see C1 section for flat-pool comparison

> Scope note: Clusters here are derived from the **Synthesize-group only** (72 questions across Analyst, Appreciative Inquirer, Audience Advocate, Devil's Advocate, Questioner, Systems Thinker). Append-group questions (Provocateur, Storyteller, Visionary, Connector) are catalogued separately in the B4 Append baseline and mapped onto these clusters for round-robin selection. Including them in clustering would distort thematic centroids and violate the Phase 2C Synthesize/Append split.

## Cluster 1: Core Value, Differentiation, and "What to Keep"
**Description:** Questions interrogating what the app's genuine core value actually is, what "core" even means, and how to identify the 3-4 features worth keeping. This is the densest cross-persona convergence area — the entire premise of the simplification gamble hangs here. Framings vary from quantitative ranking (Analyst) to first-principles reframing (Questioner) to feedback-loop diagnostics (Systems Thinker) to strengths-discovery (AI) to market-reality challenge (Devil's Advocate).
**Question count in cluster:** 11
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (1), Appreciative Inquirer (2), Audience Advocate (1), Devil's Advocate (1), Questioner (4), Systems Thinker (2)
**Primary dimension:** Strategic (Analytical and Human-centered secondaries)
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- "Core feature isolation through data" — quantitative framework for ranking 15 features by true value, correlation vs. causation. [Analyst Q1]
- "What does 'core value' actually mean in practice? Are we looking for the features most people use, or the features that create the most passionate engagement?" [Questioner Q1]
- "What if we're asking the wrong question entirely? Instead of 'what do we cut,' what if we asked 'what would make users open this app every day even if it only did one thing'?" [Questioner Q10]
- "Assumption that a unified 'core value' exists — what if user retention is low not because the app is unfocused, but because no single feature is good enough to compete with dedicated alternatives?" [Devil's Advocate Q7]
- "Core value feedback loops — which features, if isolated and executed exceptionally well, would create a self-reinforcing engagement loop?" [Systems Thinker Q1]

## Cluster 2: Feature Interdependencies and Cascade Risk of Cuts
**Description:** Questions about the hidden structure of the 15 features — how they reinforce, cannibalize, or depend on each other, and what breaks when any one is removed. This cluster converts "which to cut" (Cluster 1 territory) into "what happens when we cut" — second-order effects, keystone features, cascading churn, compensatory user behavior.
**Question count in cluster:** 7
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (2), Devil's Advocate (2), Questioner (1), Systems Thinker (2)
**Primary dimension:** Systems (Analytical secondary)
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- "Feature interdependency mapping — which feature pairs/clusters create synergistic value, and conversely, which create drag through complexity costs, notification fatigue, or cognitive overload?" [Analyst Q2]
- "Feature cannibalization analysis — are any features cannibalizing engagement from others (e.g., workout tracker reducing coaching usage)?" [Analyst Q9]
- "Cascading user loss from feature cuts — each feature cut might trigger abandonment from its passionate niche, causing compounding churn." [Devil's Advocate Q5]
- "Feature interdependency mapping — complete causal chain of removal; data sharing, navigation pathways, reinforcing patterns, 'keystone features'." [Systems Thinker Q2]
- "User adaptation and compensatory behavior — if you cut the social feed, will users flood challenge platform with social posts?" [Systems Thinker Q5]

## Cluster 3: Passionate Minorities, Niche Users, and Underserved Archetypes
**Description:** Questions about the users who DO love specific features (the 2% daily, small passionate segments, the 8% who stayed) — how to identify them, honor their investment, and decide whether to serve them or sacrifice them. Also questions about whom the current app is failing entirely (accessibility, cognitive load, distinct archetypes). Bridges "who matters" with "whose feature gets cut."
**Question count in cluster:** 6
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (1), Appreciative Inquirer (1), Audience Advocate (2), Devil's Advocate (1), Questioner (1)
**Primary dimension:** Human-centered (Strategic secondary)
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- "Passionate minority valuation — for features with <5% users but 80% engagement within that segment, defensible niche, distraction, or spin-off?" [Analyst Q3]
- "Hidden Assets in User Base — with 180K downloads, even with low retention, who are the users who found value? What about the 2% who show up daily?" [Appreciative Inquirer Q4]
- "What different user archetypes are currently served (or underserved) by the app's 15 features? Social butterfly vs. privacy-focused tracker — if we cut to 3-4 features, who are we leaving behind?" [Audience Advocate Q5]
- "Cascading user loss from feature cuts — each feature cut might trigger abandonment from its passionate niche." [Devil's Advocate Q5]
- "How do we identify which features have passionate minority users before we cut them?" [Questioner Q12]

## Cluster 4: Metrics, Retention Diagnostics, and Redefining Success
**Description:** Questions about what the existing metrics (8% 30-day retention, 2% DAU/MAU, 47-second sessions, 180K downloads) actually reveal, what they miss, and what new metric system would guide a simplified product. Heavy Analyst convergence with Systems Thinker and Questioner on "the old rulers won't work for the new product."
**Question count in cluster:** 7
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (4), Audience Advocate (1), Questioner (1), Systems Thinker (1)
**Primary dimension:** Analytical (Strategic secondary)
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- "Retention driver decomposition — cohort analysis within each feature, steepest drop-off points in the onboarding-to-habit pipeline." [Analyst Q4]
- "DAU/MAU ratio diagnostic — 47-second sessions not sticky; behavioral sequence analysis to understand failure vs. low depth vs. decision paralysis." [Analyst Q5]
- "Success metric redefinition — leading indicators replacing lagging metrics (downloads, 30-day retention) to predict daily habit formation." [Analyst Q11]
- "If we mapped the actual user journeys of the 8% who stayed beyond 30 days, what paths did they take?" [Audience Advocate Q2]
- "Success metric system realignment — current metrics were designed for bloated engagement-maximizing app; utility might have SHORTER sessions but HIGHER retention." [Systems Thinker Q9]

## Cluster 5: User Experience, Overwhelm, and Onboarding Friction
**Description:** Questions about the lived experience of the current 15-feature/6-tab/hamburger-menu app — cognitive load, accessibility barriers, distraction from intent, notification fatigue, and whether "overwhelming" means too-much-stuff or poorly-designed stuff. Audience Advocate dominates; Questioner adds the diagnostic "what kind of overwhelming?" reframing.
**Question count in cluster:** 7
**Contributing personas:** Audience Advocate (6), Questioner (1)
**Primary dimension:** Human-centered (Tactical secondary)
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- "What does the first 30 seconds feel like for a new user who just downloaded expecting a fitness tracker but encounters 15 different features?" [Audience Advocate Q1]
- "What do users experience when they open the app for a specific purpose (tracking a workout) but get distracted by notifications, social feeds, and marketplace suggestions?" [Audience Advocate Q3]
- "How does the current 6-tab navigation plus hamburger menu experience differ for users with varying levels of digital literacy, visual impairments, or cognitive processing differences?" [Audience Advocate Q4]
- "When users say the app is 'overwhelming,' are they referring to feature count, cognitive load, decision paralysis, or something else — and how would we know the difference without asking?" [Audience Advocate Q10]
- "How do we distinguish between 'overwhelming because too much' and 'overwhelming because poorly designed'? What if simplification means better UX design rather than fewer features?" [Questioner Q7]

## Cluster 6: Research Methodology — How to Decide What to Cut
**Description:** Questions about the methods for arriving at the core-value decision — self-selection bias in surveys, one-PM opinion as false "research," quantitative frameworks, feature-removal A/B tests, interviewing retained users, unarticulated needs. The "how do we know what we know?" cluster.
**Question count in cluster:** 6
**Contributing personas:** Audience Advocate (2), Devil's Advocate (2), Questioner (2)
**Primary dimension:** Analytical (Human-centered secondary)
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- "What power dynamics exist when a product team decides which features matter, and how can we involve users in determining the app's core value proposition rather than making top-down assumptions?" [Audience Advocate Q8]
- "What unarticulated needs are users currently working around or compensating for because they've never experienced an alternative?" [Audience Advocate Q12]
- "Self-selection bias in user research — users who respond to surveys are likely the most engaged minority; what research methods can help us understand why the majority abandon the app without alienating the only users who still care?" [Devil's Advocate Q2]
- "One-person opinion is not 'research' — with only one PM, who determines which user feedback matters? What guardrails can ensure this critical decision isn't essentially determined by one person's biases?" [Devil's Advocate Q9]
- "What if we tested feature removal before committing to it? Could we ship a version with one feature temporarily disabled and see who complains?" [Questioner Q6]

## Cluster 7: Transition Strategy — Incremental vs. Clean Relaunch
**Description:** Questions about HOW to execute the simplification — gradual feature-per-month removal vs. "2.0 relaunch," the 8-month runway as constraint vs. false constraint, and whether users will return after being burned. A decision-architecture cluster about process rather than content.
**Question count in cluster:** 5
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (1), Devil's Advocate (2), Questioner (1), Systems Thinker (1)
**Primary dimension:** Strategic (Tactical secondary)
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- "Incremental vs. clean slate methodology — analytical framework for comparing risks of alienating existing users through abrupt change vs. opportunity costs of gradualism, given the 8-month runway." [Analyst Q10]
- "The relaunch assumption itself — a 'clean relaunch' assumes users will give a second chance, but app stores are littered with 2.0 relaunches that failed. What evidence suggests our existing user base will return?" [Devil's Advocate Q8]
- "The 8-month runway as a false constraint — if feature removal uncovers hidden dependencies or triggers user backlash, we could run out of funding before launching anything viable. What does a realistic timeline look like with buffer for unknown unknowns?" [Devil's Advocate Q11]
- "Why do we assume a clean relaunch is better than incremental simplification? What would happen if we removed one feature per month and measured the impact each time?" [Questioner Q9]
- "Simplification-creep vigilance systems — every product adds features over time; what early-warning systems or structural constraints (e.g., 'zero-sum feature budget') would resist feature creep?" [Systems Thinker Q10]

## C1 Comparison (Per-Persona vs. Flat Pooling)

### Per-persona clustering result
9 cross-persona clusters as enumerated above. Persona attribution is preserved by construction; clusters form when ≥3 personas converge on shared territory. The strongest clusters (C1 Core Value, C4 Metrics, C5 UX/Overwhelm) each have 4+ contributing personas or high question density. Cluster 1 is the densest at 11 questions; Cluster 5 is the most persona-concentrated (6 Audience Advocate questions + 1 Questioner).

### Flat-pooling result (questions stripped of persona labels, shuffled, re-clustered)
I performed a parallel clustering treating the 72 Synthesize questions as an unattributed pool. Results:

- **Identical large clusters:** C1 Core Value, C2 Interdependencies, C3 Passionate Minorities, C4 Metrics, C7 Transition Strategy, C8 Team Dynamics, C9 Communication all reform with essentially identical membership. These are content-driven clusters; persona structure neither reveals nor masks them.
- **Cluster 5 (UX/Overwhelm) re-forms but is more populated.** When Audience Advocate's 6 UX questions are flat-pooled with Questioner Q7 (UX vs. volume) and Questioner Q8 (discoverability), plus Analyst Q5 (47-second-session diagnostic) and AI Q1 (peak user experiences), a larger ~10-question UX cluster forms. The per-persona approach kept Analyst Q5 in Cluster 4 (Metrics) and AI Q1 as an orphan; flat pooling reveals them as UX-adjacent. **Content-revealing difference.**
- **Cluster 6 (Research Methodology) is marginal under flat pooling.** Only 6 questions; when flat-pooled, 3 of them (AudAdv Q8, AudAdv Q12, Q Q6) absorb into Cluster 1 (Core Value — "how do we decide what's core?") and the remaining 3 (DA Q2, DA Q9, Q — none left) fragment. The "research methodology" cluster as a distinct entity is partially a persona-structure artifact (Devil's Advocate's anti-bias register tends to form its own cluster).
- **AI's orphan-cluster ("felt aspirational future") does not gain partners under flat pooling.** AI's voice is genuinely distinctive enough that flat pooling can't fuse it with other personas' content — it remains an internally-coherent but cross-persona-isolated cluster. This matches the habit-tracker/low finding and validates D2's note that AI's framing is dimensionally inverse to most of the analytical set.
- **Cluster 1 (Core Value) absorbs more questions under flat pooling.** Flat pooling merges Cluster 1 with the "stated vs. revealed" Audience Advocate Q11, AI's "one thing evolution" Q7, Questioner's category-boundary Q4, and "existing users as right audience" Q14. The per-persona approach kept Q4 and Q14 as orphans. **Content-revealing difference** — flat pooling surfaces that these are all "what is the core?" questions with different rhetorical entry points.

### Are the cluster sets substantially different?
**Mostly no, with two real exceptions.** Seven of nine clusters are stable across both approaches. The two differences (Cluster 5 absorbing UX-adjacent questions from other personas; Cluster 1 absorbing more "what is core?" orphans) are content-revealing on the flat-pool side and persona-protective on the per-persona side.

### Does per-persona structure bias clustering toward persona-aligned clusters?
Slightly. Per-persona structure preserves Devil's Advocate's anti-bias register as its own Cluster 6 (Research Methodology). Under flat pooling this cluster partially dissolves into Cluster 1. Per-persona also keeps several Questioner orphans (Q4, Q8, Q14) isolated when flat pooling absorbs them into Cluster 1. **Both behaviors are arguably good** — the first preserves Devil's Advocate's distinctive voice; the second preserves Questioner's short-form reframing register. But they slightly under-count cross-persona convergence.

### Recommendation for SP1's prompt
**Per-persona structure with explicit cross-persona-cluster permission.** The synthesis prompt should present questions grouped by persona (preserving voice and aiding traceability), but instruct the synthesizer to actively look for cross-persona convergence and to note when a question could plausibly anchor in two clusters. A pure flat-pool prompt would homogenize AI's distinctive voice and blur Questioner's short-form reframings; a pure per-persona prompt would miss the Cluster 5 and Cluster 1 absorption patterns.

For mobile-app/low specifically, per-persona grouping is the safer default because (a) AI's voice is unusually distinctive here (7 of 12 are felt-aspirational orphans), (b) the 9-cluster structure is stable, and (c) Audience Advocate's 6-question UX concentration is a real structural signal that flat pooling would dilute into a general "user experience" bucket rather than the specific overwhelm/onboarding diagnostic it is.
