## Per-Persona Internal Cluster Identification

### Provocateur (7 questions → 3 internal clusters)

Per the persona-selection guide, Provocateur has a "natural set point of ~7 questions with universal 3-cluster structure."

- **Cluster P-A: Unit-of-analysis / categorical inversion** (3 questions) — inverts what "school district" means as a construct
  - Q1: What if "school districts" were the wrong unit of analysis entirely? (students traveling to learning, teachers as contractors, learning-seasons)
  - Q3: What if every student attended a different "school" every week? (rotating through all 7 buildings; schools by interest not geography)
  - Q6: What if we planned for the district to be dissolved in 20 years? (expiration-date consolidation; transition-state not permanent)
- **Cluster P-B: Process/framing inversion** (2 questions) — inverts how consolidation is framed or executed
  - Q2: What if the goal was to make consolidation fail spectacularly? (disaster-planning as design-inversion)
  - Q4: What if districts deliberately made themselves LESS efficient before consolidating? (rescue-framing vs merger-framing)
- **Cluster P-C: Participation/equity inversion** (2 questions) — inverts who's affected and how
  - Q5: What if declining enrollment was framed as an educational asset rather than a crisis? (contraction-design)
  - Q7: What if consolidation required every student to change schools, not just some? (equitable disruption instead of selective protection)

Largest cluster: P-A (3). Next: P-B and P-C tied at 2; tiebreaker by source order → P-B first.

### Storyteller (7 questions → 3 internal clusters)

Per the guide, Storyteller has "universal 3-cluster structure."

- **Cluster S-A: Named-character journeys / lived experience** (3 questions) — individual characters navigating consolidation
  - Q2: Emma (single mother, two jobs) and Marcus (child thriving at small school) — infrastructure of daily life rebuilt
  - Q3: Javier (8th grader, third school transition) — scaffolding for stability-dependent students
  - Q4: Ms. Chen and Mr. Davis (two teachers from different districts) — professional collaboration vs zero-sum reorganization

### Visionary (6 questions → 3 internal clusters)

Per the guide, Visionary has "universal 3-cluster structure; self-contained questions ideal for append." Note: Visionary generated only 6 (within 5-8 validated range; natural undershoot pattern).

- **Cluster V-A: Re-imagining the fundamental unit** (2 questions) — what IS "a district" or "a school"?
  - Q1: Eliminate "school districts" entirely — replace with learning ecosystem; students/educators/resources flowing fluidly; project-based cohorts
  - Q5: Distributed learning cooperative instead of one-district-one-approach — three distinctive philosophies county-wide, students opting into different learning cultures
- **Cluster V-B: Redefining the enrollment-and-scale relationship** (2 questions) — smaller as feature, not bug
  - Q2: Declining enrollment as invitation to invent models that aren't predicated on economies of scale at all — intimacy as feature; "what becomes possible only when we have fewer students?"
  - Q3: Redefining town-identity-and-education such that losing a physical school could strengthen rather than diminish community connection — towns as "the place where X happens"
- **Cluster V-C: Time-horizon / paradigm reframings** (2 questions) — extending the temporal/framing horizon
  - Q4: Start from a 50-year horizon and work backward — "what should rural education look like in 2075?" paradigm-level shifts beyond budget-crisis-solving
  - Q6: Rural education as source of innovation rather than recipient — exporting approaches cities eventually adopt; rural-conditions-as-advantages framing

All three clusters at 2 questions (no ties). Round-robin with quota 5 of 6 means: 3 slots for Round 1 (one per cluster), 2 slots for Round 2. Round 2 needs tiebreaker since all clusters tied at 2 — source order: V-A → V-B → V-C.

### Connector (8 questions → 3 internal clusters)

Per R7, Connector has 3-cluster structure inherited from Analogist's universal structure. Connector operates in three sub-modes: cross-domain import (~35%), structural isomorphism (~30%), recombinant innovation (~35%). At this cell, where the topic is inter-domain reconciliation, the isomorphism and recombinant modes activate strongly.

- **Cluster C-A: Cross-domain imports** (3 questions) — borrowing from other domains metaphorically
  - Q2: Scandinavian multi-purpose rural community-hub model — co-located healthcare, library, senior programming; "gaining a community center" reframe
  - Q3: Mobile library services and rural healthcare outreach parallel — transportation as enrichment opportunity (rotating STEM labs, art studios, visiting educators)
  - Q6: Closed military bases and decommissioned hospitals — adaptive-reuse portfolio vs closure/keep binary
- **Cluster C-B: Structural isomorphism** (3 questions) — "X and Y are actually the same problem"
  - Q1: Town identity as distributed system — cloud-computing architecture parallel; identity across network of community nodes rather than single physical server
  - Q4: Watershed districts, conservation easements, regional transportation authorities — balancing local representation with resource efficiency
  - Q8: Shrinking cities "right-sizing" — leveraging smaller scale as quality-of-life improvement; contraction as feature not failure
- **Cluster C-C: Recombinant innovation** (2 questions) — "what if we combined X + Y?"
  - Q5: Rural healthcare's traveling-specialist and telemedicine solutions — position-sharing teachers, visiting-practitioner model as recruitment advantage
  - Q7: Hospital-merger "federated model" — local branding persists while back-office centralizes invisibly; HR/transportation first, then shared electives, then facilities

Largest clusters: C-A and C-B tied at 3. Tiebreaker by source order → C-A first.

## Integration Notes

Per D1's implementation description in the persona-selection-guide: "Append selected questions to the Phase 2C output under a persona-labeled section." The final Phase 2C output structure should be:

1. **Synthesized clusters** (30 questions across 12 clusters, per B3 Target) — each cluster labeled with its theme.
2. **Append section** (30 questions) — tagged by persona, in a distinct section of the output (or interleaved with clusters if the synthesis prompt design elects interleaving).

**Tagging style:** Each Append question should carry the `[Perspective: persona-name]` tag so the reader can distinguish Append-group distinctive voice from synthesized Q&A. D1 is silent on interleaved-vs-segregated presentation; the default assumed here is a **segregated Append section at the end** (per the persona-selection-guide's "directly appended" language), with clear persona-labels. SP1 may test variants of this integration.

**Cross-cluster tagging (optional):** Append questions can be secondarily tagged with the B1 thematic cluster they most closely relate to (column 2 in the table above), so synthesized clusters have a "see also" pointer to relevant Append content. Example: Cluster 5 (Governance) has a "see also: Connector Q1 (distributed system), Q4 (watershed model); Politician Q4 (board composition), Q5 (facility closure governance), Q6 (power centers)" reference. This is a nice-to-have, not a D1 requirement.

**Provocateur treatment:** Provocateur questions inherently don't map cleanly to B1 thematic clusters (see B1 Orphan section). They are tagged as `[Perspective: Provocateur]` with no thematic-cluster cross-reference; the provocation itself is the payload. Consistent with the persona-selection-guide: "Absurd inversion + 'real insight' format is the entire value. Synthesizing strips the provocation."

**Connector integration is high-salience at this cell:** Connector is prescribed by GT1 *because* school-consolidation is an inter-domain reconciliation topic, meaning Connector's distinctive recombinant-innovation and isomorphism modes activate (vs falling back to import-mode indistinguishable from Analogist). The 5 selected Connector questions span all three internal clusters (C-A, C-B, C-C) — cross-domain imports (Q2 Scandinavian hub, Q3 mobile-library-as-transportation), structural isomorphisms (Q1 distributed-system, Q4 watershed), and one recombinant innovation (Q5 rural-healthcare teacher-sharing). The recombinant-dropped Q7 (hospital-merger federated model) is arguably the highest-value Connector question for the topic — see the Drop section below for the flag.

**Politician integration is unusual:** Politician contributes 10 questions — the largest Append-persona contribution in this cell, and in Cluster 5 and Cluster 6 combined the Politician carries 7 of 10 questions (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4, Q6, Q7 in those two clusters). These cluster heavily into Governance + Voter Approval (7 questions) with 3 spilling into Identity/Union/Alternatives. The Politician's content is **organizational-political analysis** (coalition sequencing, veto players, legitimacy thresholds, resistance diagnosis, message tailoring) that would be stripped by synthesis. Readers should treat the Politician's 10 questions as the cell's governance-and-political-strategy layer, operating in parallel to the synthesized governance content in Clusters 5 and 6.

**Output arrangement for GT1 watchlist cell:** Because this is a GT1 Medium-confidence cell where Politician-vs-Accountant was a strong tiebreaker, the Append section's 10-question Politician block is the most visible signal of Tier 3 selection. If SP1 or downstream review later prefers an Accountant-centered Tier 3 at medium, the Append-block structure would shift dramatically (Accountant routes to Synthesize, not Append). This is a cell-specific sensitivity — document so that SP1 can re-run with the alternative selection if anomalies emerge.
