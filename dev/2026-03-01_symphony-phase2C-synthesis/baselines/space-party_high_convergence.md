# space-party — high — Convergence Baseline

**Input file:** test-data/space-party_high.md
**Total questions in input:** 161
**Synthesize-group questions:** 124 (this analysis operates on Synthesize universe only)
**Convergence groups (P):** 17
**Questions participating in convergence (Q-in-convergence):** 73 of 124 Synthesize-group = 58.9%
**Convergence density:** 58.9%

**Convention:** A convergence group = 2+ questions from *different personas* addressing the same underlying concern. Within-persona thematic repetition is not counted. The Provocateur's 8 questions (and other Append-group questions) are excluded from convergence counting because their value is framing, not topic-convergence — they are appended distinctively per D1.

**Higher convergence density at high effort (58.9% vs medium's 50.4%)** — expected behavior: more personas addressing overlapping territory. Convergence grows with persona count, which is the whole reason synthesis is needed.

## Convergence Group 1 — Sensory Overwhelm & Fear

**Theme:** The space theme's classic motifs (dim lighting, loud rockets, dark decor, "alien" imagery, flashing effects, enclosed spaces) risk frightening or overwhelming children, especially those with sensory sensitivities.
**Cluster link:** B1 Cluster 1 (Sensory, Emotional Safety & Fear)
**Questions:**
- [Audience Advocate Q2] Sensory overload management — exciting for sensory-seekers vs. regulation space for darkness/noise/visual-chaos-overwhelmed children
- [Devil's Advocate Q2] Physical sensory overload — darkness / flashing lights / loud sounds / enclosed spaces combinations triggering meltdowns
- [Questioner Q3] Sensory considerations — children overwhelmed by dark environments, loud noises, or chaotic visual effects
- [Systems Thinker Q3] "More is more" trap — tipping point between magical immersion and chaotic overstimulation
- [Empath Q4] Kids who find space scary or overwhelming — vast dark empty space or alien creatures as genuine fear triggers

**Merge recommendation:** **Merge all five** into:
> "Where is the tipping point between magical immersion and sensory overwhelm for 5-year-olds? Which combinations of space motifs (dim lighting, loud sounds, flashing effects, enclosed spaces, 'alien' imagery) most risk frightening or overstimulating children with sensory sensitivities, and how can we design regulation-friendly options — quiet corners, softer lighting zones, advance warnings, opt-out paths — that let sensory-sensitive children participate meaningfully without watering down the theme for others?"

All five converge tightly. AA Q2, DA Q2, and Q Q3 explicitly name the same motifs. ST Q3 names the structural tipping point. Empath Q4 names the emotional dimension (vast dark empty space as a felt fear, not just a sensory trigger). Merge is defensible.

## Convergence Density Interpretation

- **58.9% convergence density** — higher than medium's 50.4%, driven by the addition of Tier 2 personas (Empath, FPT, Futurist) that overlap with Tier 1 on core concerns. Expected behavior: more personas → more topical convergence on common concerns, more uniqueness at the persona-distinctive edges.
- **Highest-convergence clusters:** Cluster 1 (Sensory), Cluster 2 (Activity Design), Cluster 6 (Birthday Girl), Cluster 9 (Memory). Each draws ≥4 personas with tight thematic alignment.
- **Lowest-convergence clusters:** Cluster 10 (Measurement — Analyst + FPT share framework territory but with definitional-vs-operational differences), Cluster 11 (Futurist-dominated — trend-contextualization is single-persona territory).
- **Append-group uniqueness (not counted above):** Visionary 100% unique (7/7), Provocateur 100% orphan (8/8), Storyteller 75%+ unique (6/8 — narrative framing rarely converges with analytical/structural), Constraint Flipper 100% unique (6/6 — inversion register is exclusive), Analogist 75% unique (6/8 — cross-domain imports don't converge).
- **Persona representation signal:** Appreciative Inquirer (9/12 unique) and Futurist (8/12 unique) are the most at-risk in synthesis — their distinctive framings are easily domesticated. Audience Advocate's 8/15 unique questions include fine-grained equity dimensions that flatten quickly. SP1 scoring should track these three as priority-representation personas.
