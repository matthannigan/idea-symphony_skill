**Description:** Designing the space theme so dim lighting, dark decor, loud rocket sounds, and "alien" motifs excite sensory-seeking children without overwhelming or frightening those sensitive to darkness, noise, visual density, or unfamiliar imagery. Covers sensory regulation options, fear-prevention scans, developmental appropriateness of "real space dangers" framing, and arrival-anxiety handling.

**Question count:** 15
**Contributing personas:** Audience Advocate (4), Devil's Advocate (3), Questioner (2), Systems Thinker (1), Empath (2), Analyst (1), Appreciative Inquirer (0), First Principles Thinker (0), Futurist (0), plus 2 bridging questions
**Primary dimension:** Human-centered

**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- [Audience Advocate Q1] First arrival jitters — welcoming rather than intimidating entry for curious-but-cautious children.
- [Audience Advocate Q2] Sensory overload management — dim lighting / loud sounds / flashing effects for sensory-seekers vs. regulation space for overwhelmed children.
- [Devil's Advocate Q2] Physical sensory overload — darkness, flashing lights, loud sounds, enclosed spaces combinations triggering meltdowns.
- [Devil's Advocate Q1] Space exploration risks at age 5 — introducing real dangers (radiation, isolation, equipment failure) without creating anxiety.
- [Empath Q4] What about the kids who find space scary or overwhelming — vast dark empty space or alien creatures as genuine fear triggers.

## C1 Comparison: Per-Persona vs. Flat Pooling

C1 (folded into B1 per methodology §"Relationship to Remaining Phase 2B Items") asks whether per-persona clustering helps or hinders synthesis vs. flat pooling.

### Method

- **Per-persona clustering pass:** read each persona's questions in isolation, identify internal clusters per persona, then attempt to align across personas.
- **Flat pooling pass:** treat all 124 Synthesize-group questions as a single unattributed pool, cluster by theme.

The 11 clusters above are the **flat-pooling result**. For comparison, the per-persona pass produced these distinct artifacts.

### Per-Persona Pass — Differences from Flat-Pooling Result

1. **Cluster 6 (Birthday-Girl Centering) and Cluster 8 (Theme Immersion) merge** in per-persona view because Empath's 10 questions interleave centering concerns with theme-authenticity questions within the same internal cluster ("celebration-of-her"). Flat pooling separates these because the theme-coherence dimension (Analyst Q12, Systems Thinker Q15, FPT Q7) is about *theme design* regardless of who it's for, while Cluster 6 is specifically about *her centrality*.

2. **Cluster 9 (Memory/Meaning) is smaller and less visible** in per-persona view because no single persona owns it — the cluster only emerges when Analyst's measurement framings, Appreciative Inquirer's strengths-based framings, and FPT's "what persists" framings are pooled together. Per-persona view misses the Appreciative Inquirer + FPT convergence on "lasting meaning."

3. **Cluster 11 (Technology/Sustainability/Trend) appears as a "Futurist-only section"** in per-persona view, obscuring the one cross-bridge (Devil's Advocate Q15 on storage/reuse sustainability). This under-counts the structural convergence at ~5% but preserves Futurist's distinctive trend voice.

4. **Cluster 7 (Social Dynamics) loses its Systems Thinker contributions** in per-persona view because Systems Thinker treats social stratification as a subsystem of pacing/flow. Flat pooling reveals that "success-to-successful" (ST Q5) and social microclimates (ST Q6) are genuinely cross-persona with Audience Advocate's clique/engagement-style territory.

5. **Cluster 10 (Measurement/Frameworks) is 100% Analyst-dominated in per-persona** but becomes visibly shared with FPT in flat view (FPT's 3 framework questions about "what is a party," "what are we optimizing for," "what are we buying with the budget" are definitional-framework, not activity-framework, but pool cleanly with the Analyst's framework territory).

### Bias Check

Per-persona clustering systematically **over-weights the persona with the largest contribution** to a theme and **under-counts cross-persona convergence**. In this high-effort universe:

- **Cluster 11 is 100% Futurist** even in flat view — this is a real persona-specific cluster (Futurist is uniquely oriented to trend-contextualization). Not an artifact; both clustering methods correctly identify this as Futurist territory.
- **Cluster 3 (Pacing) is 57% Systems Thinker** (8/14) — reasonable given that systems thinking *is* about flow/loops. Per-persona reading would treat this cluster as "a Systems Thinker section"; flat reading reveals the cross-persona general pacing theme also drawn by Analyst, Appreciative Inquirer, Questioner, and Futurist.
- **Cluster 6 has no dominant persona** in flat view (Empath 4, AI 4, Questioner 3, others) — per-persona view fails to find this cross-persona cluster because no single persona centers on "birthday girl centering" as its own thematic block.

### SP1 Recommendation: **Flat pooling for synthesis input, with persona tags preserved as metadata.**

Rationale:
- The primary convergences here are cross-persona (Clusters 1, 2, 6, 8, 9 all draw ≥4 personas).
- Per-persona grouping forces the synthesizer to re-flatten before clustering, adding cognitive overhead.
- Retaining persona tags preserves attribution needed for B3's per-persona representation requirements and for D1's round-robin append logic.
- At high effort with 9 Synthesize personas, the volume of cross-persona convergence (~70% of input questions participate in multi-persona clusters) makes per-persona structure actively counterproductive.

**Caveats:**
- Cluster 10 (Measurement) is Analyst-heavy, and Cluster 11 (Futurist) is almost entirely Futurist — flat pooling doesn't harm these because the personas' distinctive voices are preserved in the tagging.
- Provocateur orphans (handled in B4) are *genuinely* persona-bound; the D1 append treatment honors this.

**Consistency with medium-effort B1 recommendation:** The space-party_medium cell also recommended flat pooling with tags preserved. Consistency across effort levels supports this as the robust cross-cell answer. The cross-topic synthesis subagent should verify this at low effort before generalizing.
