# tool-library — high — Cluster Baseline

**Input file:** test-data/tool-library_high.md
**Total questions in input:** 180
**Synthesize-group questions:** 132 (clustered below)
**Append-group questions:** 48 (Provocateur 7, Storyteller 8, Visionary 8, Constraint Flipper 7, Politician 10, Analogist 8 — clustered separately in B4)
**Natural cluster count (Synthesize universe):** 12

The 12 clusters reported here are derived from the 132 Synthesize-group questions. Append-group questions are intentionally excluded from B1 because Append voices are orthogonal to the Synthesize topic space and would distort cluster centroids (per D1 rationale: "dimensional balancing against the Synthesize output would work against the purpose of appending"). Append questions are clustered within their persona for round-robin selection in B4. Politician (Tier 3 Append) is included in the Append universe per persona-selection-guide_Phase2B §Append Group.

Clusters are listed in descending order of question count. Counts in parentheses next to a persona name indicate how many of that persona's questions fall in the cluster.

## Cluster 1: Access, Equity, and Barrier Design

**Description:** Questions about who is served vs. excluded by the library — ID/deposit/membership requirements, language/literacy/cultural barriers, undocumented residents, low-income members, and the tension between abuse prevention and equitable access. Spans both structural barrier identification and the design of alternatives (vouchering, trust-based systems, sliding scale).

**Question count:** 17
**Contributing personas:** Audience Advocate (5), Devil's Advocate (3), Questioner (3), Empath (2), Accountant (2), Analyst (1), Appreciative Inquirer (1)
**Primary dimension:** Human-centered (with strategic/structural overlay)

**Sample questions:**
- [Audience Advocate Q2] Invisible hurdles — requirements that feel simple to organizers but create genuine barriers (proof of address, credit cards, smartphone apps, literacy-assuming forms).
- [Devil's Advocate Q7] Access barriers masquerading as safeguards — deposits/ID/residency verification excluding undocumented, unhoused, or cash-poor residents.
- [Questioner Q1] Who defines "community member" eligibility? — criteria that unintentionally exclude new residents, undocumented, or those without traditional ID.
- [Audience Advocate Q4] Language and comfort — signals that a member of an institution-wary community is welcome as-is.
- [Empath Q4] How does requiring ID or payment feel to different community members — trust/distrust signals, surveillance anxiety.

## Cluster 2: Financial Sustainability, Revenue Model, and Funding

**Description:** Questions about how the library pays for itself long-term — membership fee structures, grants, sliding-scale pricing, corporate/municipal partnerships, replacement reserves, loss budgeting, and break-even analysis. Spans both the quantitative modeling and the strategic question of which revenue streams to pursue.

**Question count:** 15
**Contributing personas:** Accountant (7), Analyst (3), Devil's Advocate (1), Futurist (2), Questioner (2)
**Primary dimension:** Strategic (with analytical secondary)

**Sample questions:**
- [Accountant Q1] Membership fee structure that achieves self-sufficiency while maintaining equitable access — sliding scale / tiered / hybrid break-even analysis.
- [Accountant Q2] Grants, sponsorships, fiscal-sponsor arrangements to bridge the 2-3 year ramp to stable membership.
- [Analyst Q2] Unit economics per loan — staff time, space, depreciation, maintenance, overhead; utilization rate for sustainability.
- [Devil's Advocate Q3] Revenue reality check — realistic funding streams beyond one-time grants.
- [Futurist Q8] Alternative revenue diversification — corporate memberships, skills classes, event rental from day one.
- [Questioner Q6] Replacement cycle funding model — without constant external fundraising.

- [Devil's Advocate Q4] Single point of failure in tool expertise — distributed expertise via documentation, peer learning.
- [Analyst Q4] Volunteer capacity constraints — maximum sustainable transaction volume before needing paid staff.
- [Accountant Q8] Volunteer economics in sustainability model — dollar value of hours, transition planning.
- [Futurist Q7] Declining long-term volunteer commitment; resilience to episodic engagement shift.
- [Empath Q11] What keeps volunteers showing up — emotional sustenance, burnout anticipation.

## C1 Comparison: Per-Persona vs Flat Pooling

C1 (folded into B1 per methodology §"Relationship to Remaining Phase 2B Items") asks whether per-persona clustering helps or hinders synthesis vs flat pooling.

### Method

- **Per-persona clustering pass:** read each persona's questions in isolation, identify each persona's internal clusters, then attempt to align across personas.
- **Flat pooling pass:** treat all 132 Synthesize-group questions as a single unattributed pool, cluster by theme.

The 12 clusters above are the **flat-pooling result**. For comparison, the per-persona pass produced these distinct artifacts:

### Per-Persona Pass — Differences from Flat-Pooling Result

1. **Cluster 8 (Community Building) and Cluster 9 (Trust/Reciprocity) merge** in per-persona view. Appreciative Inquirer and Empath both treat "emotional warmth" and "relational trust" as one cluster internally. The flat pool separates them because Audience Advocate's "reciprocity vs charity" (Q11) and "borrowing without surveillance" (Q10) are about a *structural trust architecture*, which is distinct from Cluster 8's "does this space feel welcoming?" — a distinction that per-persona collapses.

2. **Cluster 1 (Access/Barriers) fragments persona-by-persona** in per-persona view. Devil's Advocate treats access barriers as a *risk-of-exclusion* cluster; Audience Advocate treats them as *design-for-excluded-users*; Questioner treats them as *assumption-challenging*; Empath treats them as *emotional-dignity*. Flat pooling reveals that all four framings address the same underlying design problem — merging them highlights the ~17 questions converging on barrier-design.

3. **Cluster 11 (First Principles) is an FPT-and-Questioner-only cluster in per-persona view.** Flat pool confirms it (it's structurally dominated by FPT and Questioner) but places adjacent Systems Thinker and AI questions as orphans that touch this territory. The flat-pool finding is: Cluster 11 is sharper when only FPT+Questioner are counted; AI's "small experiment" (Q10) is an adjacent-but-distinct concern.

4. **Cluster 10 (Ecosystem/Neighborhood) and Cluster 12 (External Positioning) merge** in per-persona view because Futurist is the primary contributor to Cluster 12 and treats neighborhood-ecosystem (10) and regional-positioning (12) as a single scale-up cluster. Flat pooling separates them because Systems Thinker's neighborhood-ecosystem questions (Q4, Q5, Q11) operate at a fundamentally different level of abstraction from Futurist's municipal-partnership / platform-positioning questions (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q6). This is one of the most consequential flat-pool gains for this cell.

5. **Cluster 4 (Liability) and Cluster 6 (Operations) partly merge** in per-persona view because Devil's Advocate and Accountant treat both as "operational failure modes." Flat pool separates them because Cluster 4 is about *external* exposure (injuries, legal consequences) while Cluster 6 is about *internal* system integrity (tracking, maintenance). This distinction matters for downstream Phase 3 — the audiences and solutions are different.

### Bias Check

Per-persona clustering systematically **over-weights the persona with the largest contribution** to a theme, and **under-counts cross-persona convergence**. In this universe:
- Accountant contributes 7 of 15 Cluster 2 questions (~47%) — per-persona view would *isolate* this further as an Accountant-only "finance" cluster and miss the Analyst (3), Futurist (2), and Questioner (2) contributions.
- FPT contributes 6 of 11 Cluster 11 questions (~55%) — per-persona view would treat this as an FPT-only cluster and lose the Questioner (4) structural-challenge contribution.
- Systems Thinker contributes 5 of 9 Cluster 10 questions (~56%) — per-persona view would produce an ST-only "ecosystem" cluster and lose the strategic-positioning contribution from Futurist that flat-pooling separates as Cluster 12.

### SP1 Recommendation: **Use flat pooling.**

Flat pooling produces:
- More clusters (12 vs ~8 for per-persona)
- Better separation of ecosystem-scale (10) from regional-scale (12) concerns
- Better separation of barrier-access (1) from community-belonging (8) from trust-architecture (9) — three conceptually distinct human-centered concerns that per-persona collapses into ~2 clusters
- Better cross-persona convergence detection (Cluster 1 barrier-design is the clearest — 7 personas contribute, and flat pooling makes the convergence visible)

For SP1 scoring, the synthesis prompt variants should be evaluated against the flat-pool cluster set (the 12 above). If a variant produces ~8 clusters that mirror the per-persona structure, score it as under-clustering — conflating distinct design concerns rather than a valid alternative organization.

**One caveat:** flat pooling may obscure persona representation. SQ3 scoring (persona representation) should *cross-check* the per-persona view: if flat-pool synthesis produces a Cluster 9 (Trust) that includes only AA framings and strips Empath's felt-experience framings or AI's strengths-based framings, persona representation is failing even if the cluster theme is correct.

**Topic-specific caveat:** Tool-library/high has unusually many cross-cluster-bridging orphans (~30%). Per-persona clustering naturally handles these by letting each persona keep its "signature" cluster (e.g., AA's "journey-mapping" cluster spans 1-8-9). Flat pooling forces an explicit orphan designation. SP1 should not penalize variants for placing these orphans in any of 2-3 defensible clusters — they are genuinely multi-cluster questions.
