## Convergence Group 1 — Permit Scarcity & Zone Strategy

**Theme:** New city STR ordinance limits permits per neighborhood zone. The company must map permit availability, plan zone concentration vs. diversification, handle HOA overlays, and prepare for permits-as-the-scarce-resource.
**Cluster link:** B1 Cluster 1 (Regulatory Landscape)
**Questions:**
- [Analyst Q2] Permit acquisition costs and timing — realistic timeline and cost structure across zones; city processing, availability, competitive bidding, grandfathering
- [Analyst Q3] HOA restriction navigation strategy — avoid entirely, accept higher legal costs, focus on ambiguous HOAs
- [Devil's Advocate Q1] Permit scarcity trap — mapped actual available permit capacity; what happens if you exhaust permits in best neighborhoods before year three
- [Devil's Advocate Q4] HOA restriction dead ends — working around HOA STR restrictions; strategy when HOA boards enforce fines
- [Lawyer Q4] Permit allocation across zoning restrictions — acquisition strategy; compliance tracking across 30-50 properties with varying expirations
- [Lawyer Q10] HOA restriction navigation and preemption strategy — avoid HOA properties / amend / challenge pending
- [Questioner Q5] HOAs become the primary battlefield — HOA board relationships positioning managed properties as assets

**Merge recommendation:** **Merge Devil's Advocate Q1 + Lawyer Q4 + Analyst Q2** into:
> "What is the realistic permit-availability map across target zones given the new ordinance's per-zone caps — and what acquisition strategy (competitive bidding on existing permits, grandfathering plays, zone diversification) maximizes permitted properties while preventing us from exhausting capacity in our best neighborhoods before reaching year-three targets?"

Merge Analyst Q3 + Devil's Advocate Q4 + Lawyer Q10 + Questioner Q5 into:
> "How do we handle HOA-level STR restrictions overlaying city permits — avoid HOA-governed properties entirely, accept higher legal costs to challenge restrictions, pursue HOA amendments, or build HOA board relationships that position managed properties as community assets rather than liabilities?"

Two mergers capture the two tight sub-convergences. Splitting preserves the "city permits" concern from the "HOA overlay" concern, which are operationally different even though topically linked.
