## Convergence Group 1
**Theme:** Failure / setbacks / broken streaks should be designed-for, not punished
**Cluster:** B1 Cluster 1 (Failure, Setbacks, and Resilience)
**Questions:**
- [Audience Advocate Q1] What does failure feel like, and how might the app respond to it with dignity? (Advocates "graceful failure as a core feature.")
- [Devil's Advocate Q5] What happens when users inevitably fail? (Reframe failure as "data to be learned from" rather than failure to be mourned; design recovery pathways.)
- [Systems Thinker Q5] The re-start penalty and habit discontinuity. (Streaks penalize resilience; design for the inevitable interruption as first-class.)
- [Questioner Q14] How might we design for failure? (Broken streaks and missed habits as valuable data.)
- [Appreciative Inquirer Q7] What would make streaks feel meaningful rather than fragile? (Reframe continuity to honor resilience.)
**Merge recommendation:**
> "How should the app design for inevitable failure — broken streaks, missed days, life disruptions — so that setbacks feel like resilient data points rather than punishing losses, and recovery pathways are first-class features rather than edge cases?"

This is the densest convergence in the file and the most defensible merge. All five questions converge on the same core insight (treat failure as designed-for) and the merged text faithfully reflects each contributor's framing.

## Convergence Group 2
**Theme:** Gamification / streaks / external motivation may undermine intrinsic motivation
**Cluster:** B1 Cluster 2 (Engagement Models)
**Questions:**
- [Analyst Q5] How can we quantify the trade-offs between gamification and sustainable intrinsic motivation? (Framework for when gamification supports vs. undermines.)
- [Devil's Advocate Q4] What if our engagement model backfires? (Streaks → unhealthy obsession; rewards undermine intrinsic motivation.)
- [Systems Thinker Q1] Motivation substitution risk. (External scaffolding becomes the primary driver; what strengthens internal regulation?)
- [Questioner Q5] What if gamification is the wrong path? (What would deliberately avoiding extrinsic motivation look like?)
- [Audience Advocate Q5] What does it mean to build a habit tracker that genuinely respects user autonomy rather than exploiting it? (Dark patterns vs. genuine support.)
**Merge recommendation:**
> "How do we evaluate the trade-off between extrinsic motivation tools (streaks, points, gamification, notifications) and durable intrinsic motivation — and what would an engagement model look like that builds internal regulation rather than dependence on the app's prompts?"

Strong merge candidate. Five personas, single shared concern. The merge preserves the analytical/evaluative framing while leaving room for the alternative-design question that Questioner and Audience Advocate raise.

## Convergence Group 5
