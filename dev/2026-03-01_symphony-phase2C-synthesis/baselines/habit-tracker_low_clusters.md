# habit-tracker — low — Cluster Baseline

**Input file:** test-data/habit-tracker_low.md
**Total questions in input:** 96 (Synthesize: 68; Append: 28)
**Natural cluster count:** 9 (Synthesize-group); see C1 section for flat-pool comparison

> Scope note: Clusters here are derived from the **Synthesize-group only** (68 questions across Analyst, Appreciative Inquirer, Audience Advocate, Devil's Advocate, Questioner, Systems Thinker). Append-group questions (Provocateur, Storyteller, Visionary, Analogist) are catalogued separately in the B4 Append baseline and are mapped onto these clusters for round-robin selection. Including them in clustering would distort thematic centroids and violate the Phase 2C Synthesize/Append split.

## Cluster 1: Failure, Setbacks, and Resilience
**Description:** Questions about what happens when users miss days, break streaks, or encounter life disruptions, and how the app should reframe failure as part of the process rather than a terminal event. This is the densest convergence area — nearly every Synthesize persona contributes.
**Question count in cluster:** 9
**Contributing personas:** Audience Advocate (2), Devil's Advocate (2), Systems Thinker (1), Questioner (1), Appreciative Inquirer (2), Analyst (1)
**Primary dimension:** Human-centered (with Strategic and Systems sub-strands)
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- "What does failure feel like, and how might the app respond to it with dignity?" [Audience Advocate Q1]
- "What happens when users inevitably fail?" [Devil's Advocate Q5]
- "The re-start penalty and habit discontinuity" [Systems Thinker Q5]
- "How might we design for failure?" [Questioner Q14]
- "What would make streaks feel meaningful rather than fragile?" [Appreciative Inquirer Q7]

## Orphan Questions (Not Cluster-Forming)

The following Synthesize-group questions did not coalesce into a cluster of three or more. They are real, distinct concerns that contribute lone insight:

- **Analyst Q2** — "How do user engagement patterns differ across different types of habits?" — habit-typology lens; touches Cluster 2 but is distinctively about heterogeneity.
- **Analyst Q3** — "What are the critical drop-off points in the user journey from sign-up to sustained habit formation?" — funnel/retention analytics; partial overlap with Cluster 1 but anchored in journey stages.
- **Appreciative Inquirer Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q8, Q10, Q11** — eight of AI's eleven questions are about felt qualities of a "beloved" tracker (Goldilocks calibration, generous gestures, simplicity-as-empowerment, first-3-day wins, daily ritual feel, self-compassion). These cluster among themselves into a tight "Aspirational Felt Quality" sub-theme but are largely unique to AI within the Synthesize group — they don't converge with other personas' questions even though they share thematic territory with B1 Cluster 1 (resilience) and Cluster 9 (lifecycle). **Ambiguity:** This is a real cluster *within* AI's output but it doesn't satisfy "cross-persona cluster" criteria. ## C1 Comparison (Per-Persona vs. Flat Pooling)

### Per-persona clustering result
9 cross-persona clusters as enumerated above. Persona attribution is preserved by construction; clusters form when ≥3 personas converge on shared territory. The strongest clusters (C1 Failure, C2 Engagement Models, C9 Lifecycle) each have 5+ contributing personas — these are robustly cross-persona.

### Flat-pooling result (questions stripped of persona labels, shuffled, re-clustered)
I performed a parallel clustering treating the 68 Synthesize questions as an unattributed pool. Results:

- **Identical large clusters:** C1 Failure, C2 Engagement Models, C5 Diversity, C6 Privacy, C7 Monetization, and C8 Scope/MVP all reform with essentially identical membership. These are content-driven clusters; persona structure neither reveals nor masks them.
- **Cluster 9 (Lifecycle) re-forms but absorbs more orphans.** When AI's "long arc" questions and Questioner's "graduation" questions are flat-pooled with Audience Advocate Q10 and Systems Thinker Q9, a slightly larger Lifecycle cluster of ~10 forms, pulling in 2-3 of AI's orphans. This is a real difference: per-persona clustering left these as AI-internal aspirational questions; flat pooling reveals them as cross-persona "lifecycle/graduation/identity" content.
- **Cluster 4 (First Principles / Behavioral Science) and Cluster 3 (Differentiation) blur into each other** under flat pooling. Both are about "what fundamental thing are we doing wrong vs. competitors?" and the persona attribution had been keeping them apart (Devil's Advocate's competitive framing vs. Questioner's first-principles framing). Flat pooling produces a single ~10-question cluster of "Existential / Mechanism / Differentiation" that fragments back into 2 sub-clusters only with deliberate effort.
- **AI's "felt quality" orphan-cluster does not gain partners under flat pooling.** AI's voice is genuinely distinctive enough that flat pooling can't fuse it with other personas' content — it remains an internally-coherent but cross-persona-isolated cluster. This validates D2's note that AI's framing is dimensionally inverse to most of the analytical set.

### Are the cluster sets substantially different?
**Mostly no, with two real exceptions.** Six of nine clusters are stable across both approaches. The two differences (Lifecycle absorption of AI orphans; First-Principles/Differentiation blur) are content-revealing on the flat-pool side and persona-protective on the per-persona side.

### Does per-persona structure bias clustering toward persona-aligned clusters?
Slightly. Per-persona structure preserves AI's distinct aspirational voice as an orphan-cluster rather than letting it merge into Lifecycle. It also keeps the Questioner's first-principles register separate from Devil's Advocate's market-competitive register. **Both behaviors are arguably good** — they preserve signal that synthesis would otherwise homogenize. But they slightly under-count cross-persona convergence.

### Recommendation for SP1's prompt
**Per-persona structure with explicit cross-cluster permission.** The synthesis prompt should present questions grouped by persona (preserving voice and aiding traceability), but instruct the synthesizer to actively look for cross-persona convergence and to note when a question could plausibly anchor in two clusters. A pure flat-pool prompt would homogenize AI's distinctive voice; a pure per-persona prompt would miss the Lifecycle expansion. Hybrid wins for this cell.

For habit-tracker/low specifically, per-persona grouping is the safer default because (a) AI's voice is unusually distinctive here, (b) the 9-cluster structure is stable, and (c) the orphan-cluster flag protects against losing AI's aspirational dimension.
