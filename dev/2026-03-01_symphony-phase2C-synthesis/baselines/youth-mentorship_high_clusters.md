# youth-mentorship — high — Cluster Baseline

**Input file:** test-data/youth-mentorship_high.md
**Total questions in input:** 179
**Synthesize-group questions:** 135 (clustered below)
**Append-group questions:** 44 (Provocateur 7, Storyteller 6, Visionary 7, Constraint Flipper 7, Politician 10, Connector 7 — clustered within their persona for B4 round-robin)
**Natural cluster count (Synthesize universe):** 12

The 12 clusters below are derived from the 135 Synthesize-group questions. Append-group questions are intentionally excluded from B1 because Append voices are orthogonal to the Synthesize topic space (per D1 rationale: "dimensional balancing against the Synthesize output would work against the purpose of appending"). Append questions are clustered within their persona for round-robin selection in B4.

- Synthesize: Analyst (15), Appreciative Inquirer (12), Audience Advocate (15), Devil's Advocate (14), Questioner (20), Systems Thinker (15), Empath (12), First Principles Thinker (10), Futurist (12), Lawyer (10) → 135 Synthesize.
- Append: Provocateur (7), Storyteller (6), Visionary (7), Constraint Flipper (7), Politician (10), Connector (7) → 44 Append.

**GT1 watchlist note (TDA1 §5.1):** This cell is on the GT1 Medium-confidence watchlist. ## Cluster 1: Screening, Background Checks & Mentor Qualification

**Description:** Mentor vetting architecture — disqualifying offense categories, multi-registry checks, FCRA compliance, probationary periods, reference interviews, behavioral screening, and the false-positive/false-negative trade-off. Includes the screening-as-gatekeeper question (does rigorous vetting exclude the adults best positioned to connect?) and renewal cycles. Core legal-operational risk management at the front door of the program.

**Question count:** 13
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (2), Devil's Advocate (2), Lawyer (3), Questioner (2), Systems Thinker (1), Audience Advocate (1), Appreciative Inquirer (0), Empath (0), First Principles Thinker (0), Futurist (1)
**Primary dimension:** Analytical

**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- [Analyst Q2] Volunteer screening calibration — multi-dimension (background, references, behavioral); false-positive/false-negative trade-off without disproportionately excluding qualified mentors.
- [Devil's Advocate Q1] Background check limitations — past convictions only; need for reference interviews, boundary-awareness behavioral questions, probationary pairing.
- [Lawyer Q1] Disqualifying offenses and registry checks — which offense categories permanently disqualify; case-by-case review; mid-program disqualifying-record discovery.
- [Lawyer Q3] FCRA compliance for volunteer screening — disclosure, consent, and adverse-action procedures for background checks.
- [Systems Thinker Q10] How might the volunteer screening process create unintended selection effects?

## Cluster 2: Mandatory Reporting, Legal Obligations & Reporter Training

**Description:** Mandatory reporter status and its downstream effects — legal determination of reporter status, documentation requirements, recognizing reportable versus non-reportable situations, the trust-vs-reporting tension, post-disclosure relationship preservation, decision-support tools for mentors. The Lawyer's specific regulatory-mechanics framing joined by DA/Analyst operational concerns and Questioner's "does reporting destroy trust" framing.

**Question count:** 8
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (1), Devil's Advocate (1), Lawyer (1), Questioner (2), Systems Thinker (0), Audience Advocate (0), Empath (0), First Principles Thinker (0), Futurist (0)
**Primary dimension:** Analytical

**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- [Analyst Q3] Incident response and critical-incident protocols — decision tree when mandatory reporting conflicts with relationship preservation.
- [Devil's Advocate Q6] Mandatory reporter confusion — failing to recognize reportable situations, over-reporting, decision-support tools.
- [Lawyer Q4] Mandatory reporter status and training requirements — training documentation; organizational liability if mentor fails to report.
- [Questioner Q5] Does safety training undermine trust? — how protocols designed to protect (background checks, mandatory reporting, boundaries) create barriers to authentic connection.
- [Questioner Q7] Who protects the mentors? — safeguards for adults encountering trauma disclosures, manipulation, or false accusations.

## Cluster 3: Liability, Insurance & Boundary Policies

**Description:** Liability structure for one-on-one mentoring activities — general vs. abuse-specific coverage gaps, transportation/non-owned-auto exposure, on-site vs. off-site activity tiering, waiver design, boundary policies mapping to legal standards for inappropriate conduct. Also covers the boundary-policy question (communication channels, physical contact, gifts, private meetings) as both misconduct prevention and false-allegation protection.

**Question count:** 7
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (1), Devil's Advocate (2), Lawyer (3), Questioner (0), Systems Thinker (0), Audience Advocate (0), Empath (0), First Principles Thinker (0), Futurist (0), Appreciative Inquirer (0)
**Primary dimension:** Analytical

**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- [Analyst Q4] Liability exposure quantification across activities — on-site, off-site, transportation, digital communication; frequency-severity matrices; activity risk tiers.
- [Devil's Advocate Q5] One-on-one isolation risks — structural safeguards (loggable locations, staff check-ins, graduated autonomy).
- [Devil's Advocate Q7] Liability coverage gaps — general liability may not cover abuse allegations during private meetings.
- [Lawyer Q5] Transportation liability and insurance coverage — non-owned auto, hired auto, vehicle safety requirements.
- [Lawyer Q6] Boundary policies and misconduct prevention — physical contact, communication, private meetings, gift-giving; protection from allegations.

## Cluster 4: Matching, Compatibility & Relationship Design

**Description:** Mentor-mentee pairing architecture — compatibility vs. complementarity factors, matching algorithms, demographic vs. emotional-chemistry matching, "wrong match as right match" possibilities, early warning indicators for relationship breakdown, re-matching mechanics, and the first-meeting trust moment. Spans analytical (matching metrics) and human-centered (emotional chemistry, power dynamics) registers.

**Question count:** 14
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (2), Appreciative Inquirer (2), Audience Advocate (1), Devil's Advocate (1), Questioner (3), Systems Thinker (2), Empath (2), First Principles Thinker (0), Futurist (1)
**Primary dimension:** Human-centered

**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- [Analyst Q5] Matching algorithm variables — compatibility (shared interests, demographics) vs. complementarity (different perspectives, skills).
- [Appreciative Inquirer Q11] Matching that creates potential chemistry — less visible elements of compatibility; unexpected pairings that worked brilliantly.
- [Devil's Advocate Q14] Matching process failure modes — poor matches causing harm, re-matching instability, exit processes minimizing harm.
- [Empath Q10] The matching process — setting up emotional mismatches in the name of demographic similarity; space for intuitive "click."
- [Futurist Q12] Algorithmic fairness in mentor matching — AI-driven matching replicating biases; build evidence-based framework vs. evaluate third-party tools.

**Question count:** 16
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (0), Appreciative Inquirer (1), Audience Advocate (4), Devil's Advocate (1), Questioner (2), Systems Thinker (0), Empath (6), First Principles Thinker (1), Futurist (0), Lawyer (0)
**Primary dimension:** Human-centered

- [Empath Q2] Where will shame live in this program? — "at-risk" label stigma, shame triggers in program design.
- [Empath Q9] What does it feel like to be "selected" because you're at-risk? — identity reinforcement vs. challenge.

## C1 Comparison: Per-Persona vs. Flat Pooling

C1 (folded into B1 per methodology §"Relationship to Remaining Phase 2B Items") asks whether per-persona clustering helps or hinders synthesis vs. flat pooling.

### Method

- **Per-persona clustering pass:** read each persona's questions in isolation, identify internal clusters per persona, then attempt to align across personas.
- **Flat pooling pass:** treat all 135 Synthesize-group questions as a single unattributed pool, cluster by theme.

The 12 clusters above are the **flat-pooling result**. For comparison, the per-persona pass produced these distinct artifacts.

### Per-Persona Pass — Differences from Flat-Pooling Result

1. **Cluster 5 (Safety/Climate) and Cluster 7 (Training/Support) appear as one "mentor-mentee dynamics" bucket** in per-persona view because Empath, AA, and AI all interleave emotional climate with training framings. Flat pooling separates because Cluster 5 is the *teen's felt experience* (safety signals, shame, dignity) while Cluster 7 is the *mentor's preparation and ongoing development* (skill building, boundary calibration, emotional-weight-bearing). The registers are genuinely different — mentor training is not the teen's felt experience, and teen safety is not mentor skill. Flat pooling honors that separation; per-persona view loses it because each persona addresses both sides.

2. **Cluster 2 (Mandatory Reporting) fragments in per-persona view** — Lawyer Q4 lives in "Lawyer's legal section," DA Q6 lives in "DA's risks section," Questioner Q5 and Q7 live in "Questioner's assumptions section." Flat pooling surfaces the convergence: all four are asking about the trust-vs-legal-obligation tension from different angles (regulatory mechanism, operational training failure, paradigm question, mentor protection). Per-persona view sees 4 separate "mandatory reporting mentions" in 4 different sections; flat pooling sees 1 coherent cluster with 4 angles.

3. **Cluster 6 (Family/Consent) has AA contributing 5 and Lawyer contributing 3** — per-persona view treats this as "AA's access/equity section plus Lawyer's legal section," when flat pooling reveals the actual concern: building genuine trust with families who have reasons to distrust institutions, with AA carrying the equity framing and Lawyer carrying the regulatory mechanism (FERPA, consent, minor data rights). Merging the equity framing with the legal framing produces a coherent family-trust cluster; per-persona view fragments it.

4. **Cluster 11 (Relationship Arc) is cross-persona in a particularly cross-register way.** Empath contributes emotional-interior framings (Q3 mentor doesn't like mentee; Q4 one-year-commitment emotional clock; Q12 end-of-program fallout), AA contributes process-design framings (Q7 re-matching dignity, Q10 handling rupture, Q15 graduation), DA contributes overcommitment challenges (Q2 graduated commitments), and Systems Thinker contributes structural framings (Q5 12-month turnover, Q7 feedback loops for troubled matches). Per-persona view sees 4 separate "relationship duration" framings; flat pooling reveals 4 registers on a shared theme.

5. **Cluster 12 (Reimagination) is 100% FPT + Futurist.** Both views agree on this cluster's composition. Per-persona view treats them as separate "FPT section" and "Futurist section"; flat pooling reveals the shared theme (is the conventional mentorship model the right one?). The convergence is real — both personas reach similar reimagination territory from different registers (definitional for FPT, trend-extrapolation for Futurist). Flat pooling makes this visible.

6. **Cluster 10 (Ecosystem) draws 7 personas** (Analyst, AI, Questioner, ST, Empath, FPT, Futurist) with relatively even contributions. Per-persona view would treat this as "organizational coordination" + "community positioning" + "trend context" as three separate discussions; flat pooling reveals the underlying question: how does this program situate itself in the broader youth-serving ecosystem?

### Bias Check

Per-persona clustering systematically **over-weights the persona with the largest contribution** to a theme and **under-counts cross-persona convergence**. In this high-effort universe:

- **Cluster 5 (Safety/Climate) has Empath at 6 of 16 (38%)** — per-persona view treats this as "Empath's section" when flat pooling shows 6 personas contributing distinct registers on teen felt-experience.
- **Cluster 6 (Family/Consent) has Audience Advocate at 5 of 13 (38%)** — per-persona view treats as "AA section plus Lawyer section"; flat pooling reveals the shared family-trust theme.
- **Cluster 12 has FPT + Futurist at 10 of 10 (100%)** — this is genuinely a 2-persona cluster. Flat pooling simply surfaces their convergence.
- **Cluster 1 (Screening) has Lawyer at 3 of 13 (23%)** with Analyst, DA, Questioner, AA, Futurist also contributing — per-persona view would fragment the shared screening-architecture concern into "legal compliance," "risk management," and "equity gatekeeping" sections.

### SP1 Recommendation: **Flat pooling for synthesis input, with persona tags preserved as metadata.**

Rationale:
- The primary convergences here are heavily cross-persona (Clusters 4, 5, 6, 10, 11 each draw ≥6 personas).
- Per-persona grouping would force the synthesizer to re-flatten before clustering, adding cognitive overhead with 10 Synthesize personas.
- Retaining persona tags preserves attribution needed for B3's per-persona representation requirements and for D1's round-robin append logic.
- The dense human-centered content (53/135 questions across 4 clusters) specifically benefits from flat pooling because Empath, AA, and AI cross-bridge all four human-centered clusters.

**Caveats:**
- Cluster 2 (Mandatory Reporting) is small (8 questions) but cross-persona-convergent on a tight theme; per-persona view would lose this cluster entirely by dispersing its questions.
- Cluster 3 (Liability/Insurance/Boundaries) is Lawyer-heavy (3 of 7) but still cross-persona; per-persona view would under-count DA's structural/insurance concerns bridging to Lawyer's regulatory specificity.
- Cluster 12 is FPT+Futurist-bound — flat pooling doesn't harm this because the personas' distinctive voices are preserved in the tagging.

**Consistency across effort levels:** Other high-effort cells analyzed so far (property-management/high, space-party/high, tool-library/high, food-truck/high, habit-tracker/high) all recommend flat pooling with high-to-very-high confidence. Youth-mentorship/high, with 10 Synthesize personas (one fewer than property-management/high's 11), sits in the same high-confidence flat-pooling zone.

**Confidence: very high.** With 10 Synthesize personas and ~30% orphan/bridging rate (36 orphans vs. property-management's 15%), the cross-persona convergence density is high enough that per-persona clustering would actively obscure the primary thematic structure.
