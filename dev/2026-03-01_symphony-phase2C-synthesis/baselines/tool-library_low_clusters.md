# tool-library — low — Cluster Baseline

**Input file:** test-data/tool-library_low.md
**Total questions in input:** 100 (Synthesize: 69; Append: 31)
**Natural cluster count:** 9 cross-persona Synthesize clusters (plus 2 orphan clusters); see C1 section for flat-pool comparison

> Scope note: Clusters here are derived from the **Synthesize-group only** (69 questions across Analyst, Appreciative Inquirer, Audience Advocate, Devil's Advocate, Questioner, Systems Thinker). Append-group questions (Provocateur, Storyteller, Visionary, Analogist) are catalogued separately in the B4 Append baseline and are mapped onto these clusters for round-robin selection. Including them in clustering would distort thematic centroids and violate the Phase 2C Synthesize/Append split.

## Cluster 1: Equitable Access, Dignity, and Inclusion
**Description:** Questions about who the tool library serves and excludes — renters vs. homeowners, undocumented residents, non-English speakers, users with mobility or cultural barriers, and the emotional dignity of borrowing "basic" tools. This is the densest convergence area in the file — 4 of 6 Synthesize personas contribute substantial content, with Audience Advocate dominating and Devil's Advocate, Questioner, Analyst reinforcing. Includes operational-fit sub-strand (hours, return grace periods) that serves the same equity frame.
**Question count in cluster:** 16
**Contributing personas:** Audience Advocate (9), Devil's Advocate (2), Questioner (4), Analyst (1)
**Primary dimension:** Human-centered (with Strategic secondary)
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- "What does the library feel like to walk into for someone who has never owned a power tool and feels deeply self-conscious about that fact?" [Audience Advocate Q1]
- "How might the tool library inadvertently prioritize the needs of already-resourced homeowners while renters, low-income neighbors, and those facing the most barriers remain underserved?" [Audience Advocate Q9]
- "What if the people who most need free tool access are exactly those who can't meet membership requirements?" [Devil's Advocate Q10]
- "How do we prevent the 'early adopter capture' problem where the most organized and resourced residents dominate access?" [Devil's Advocate Q11]
- "What barriers might prevent different community members from using the library?" [Questioner Q5]

## C1 Comparison (Per-Persona vs. Flat Pooling)

### Per-persona clustering result
9 cross-persona clusters as enumerated above, plus 2 orphan clusters (AI aspirational, ST systems dynamics). Persona attribution is preserved by construction. The strongest clusters (C1 Equity, C2 Volunteer Sustainability, C3 Financial) each have 2-5 contributing personas — these are robustly cross-persona. C9 (Community Co-Creation) is weaker cross-persona but is content-coherent.

### Flat-pooling result (questions stripped of persona labels, shuffled, re-clustered)
I performed a parallel clustering treating the 69 Synthesize questions as an unattributed pool. Results:

- **Identical large clusters:** C1 Equity, C2 Volunteer Sustainability, C3 Financial, C4 Problem Definition, C5 Liability, C6 Resilience, and C7 Trust/Commons all reform with essentially identical membership. These are content-driven clusters; persona structure neither reveals nor masks them.
- **Cluster 8 (Operational Design) gains orphans under flat pooling.** Systems Thinker Q8 (information flows) and several of ST's orphan-cluster questions (ST2 delay structures, ST7 small interventions) get pulled into a broader "Operational Learning & System Design" cluster of ~8 questions. This is a real difference: per-persona clustering preserved ST's named-archetype voice; flat pooling reveals these as operational-design territory shared with Analyst and Questioner.
- **Cluster 9 (Community Co-Creation) absorbs part of AI's orphan cluster.** When AI's "hidden assets" and "existing allies" questions flat-pool with AA Q8 (built with vs. for) and Q7 (early adopter diversity), a larger Co-Creation cluster of ~8 forms. The rest of AI's orphans (belonging, abundance, the storefront as magnet) remain AI-isolated even under flat pooling — AI's aspirational register is genuinely distinctive.
- **ST orphan cluster fragments under flat pooling.** ST1 (self-reinforcing growth) ties into C2; ST3 (success-to-successful) ties into C1; ST4 (behavioral adaptation) ties loosely into C4; ST9 (interactions with existing systems) and ST10 (boundary permeability) tie into a new "Inter-System / Community-Boundary" cluster of 3-4. The per-persona ST orphan cluster is partly an artifact of ST's vocabulary; content-wise, its questions distribute across existing clusters.
- **C1 Equity absorbs even more under flat pooling.** Already the largest cluster at 16, C1 grows to ~20 when ST3 (success-to-successful) and some Q-questions currently in C4 (Q4 typical user, Q8 individual-lending-default) flat-pool toward the equity lens.

### Are the cluster sets substantially different?
**Mostly no, with two real exceptions.** Seven of nine primary clusters are stable across both approaches. The two meaningful differences:
1. ST's systems-archetype orphan cluster exists in per-persona view but dissolves under flat pooling (its content distributes across C1, C2, C4, C8).
2. Part of AI's aspirational orphan cluster absorbs into C9 under flat pooling, but the core felt-quality questions (belonging, abundance, magnet) remain AI-isolated in both approaches.

### Does per-persona structure bias clustering toward persona-aligned clusters?
**Yes, moderately.** Per-persona structure preserves ST's named-archetype vocabulary as a coherent cluster rather than letting ST questions merge into content-adjacent clusters. It also preserves most of AI's aspirational voice as an orphan cluster. **Both behaviors are arguably good** for Phase 2C — they preserve distinctive signal that synthesis would otherwise homogenize. But they slightly under-count cross-persona convergence into C1, C2, C4, and C8.

### Recommendation for SP1's prompt — grouped (per-persona) vs. flat pooling

**Recommendation: per-persona structure with explicit cross-persona-cluster permission.** The synthesis prompt should present questions grouped by persona (preserving voice and aiding traceability) but instruct the synthesizer to actively look for cross-persona convergence and to note when a question could plausibly anchor in two clusters. A pure flat-pool prompt would homogenize AI's aspirational voice and ST's systems-archetype vocabulary; a pure per-persona prompt would miss the cross-persona convergence into C1 and C8.

**For tool-library/low specifically, per-persona grouping is the safer default** because (a) the C1 Equity cluster is already the dominant theme at 16 questions — flat pooling grows it to 20, which risks under-representing the other clusters by comparison; (b) ST's systems-archetype voice is an important distinctive contribution that flat pooling dissolves; (c) AI's aspirational orphan cluster is the persona's primary contribution to this cell — flat pooling partially absorbs it into C9 but would still require an explicit "preserve at least N AI questions" rule in B3 to protect the remainder. Per-persona structure plus cross-cluster permission is the hybrid that wins for this cell.
