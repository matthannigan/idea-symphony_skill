## Cluster 1: Financial Model, Capital & Unit Economics

**Description:** The quantified viability of the business model — break-even math across operating scenarios, food cost ratios on the fusion menu, cash flow through the startup "valley of death," SBA debt-vs-bootstrap framework, and equipment ownership total-cost-of-ownership. Spans quantitative rigor (Analyst) with risk/worst-case stress tests (DA) and founder-financial-value questions (AI).
**Question count:** 12
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (5), Devil's Advocate (3), Questioner (2), Appreciative Inquirer (1), Systems Thinker (1)
**Primary dimension:** Strategic
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- [Analyst Q1] Break-even analysis across scenarios — single truck daily vending, event-focused operations, catering-heavy approach — and how fixed costs vs. variable costs shift the economics of each scenario.
- [Analyst Q5] SBA loan versus bootstrapping tradeoffs — risk tolerance, growth timeline pressure, cash flow runway, opportunity costs of delayed expansion.
- [Devil's Advocate Q2] Cash flow fragility during startup phase — "valley of death" modeling between initial capital depletion and sustainable cash flow.
- [Devil's Advocate Q3] Debt service risk assessment — whether the business can service $40K SBA debt during worst-case seasonal scenarios.
- [Questioner Q8] Financing tradeoff analysis — restrictive covenants, personal guarantees, timeline changes under leaner bootstrap.

## Cluster 2: Regulatory Environment & Ordinance Response

**Description:** Active engagement with the pending city ordinances (downtown hours, annual fees), anticipatory compliance planning, regulatory coalition-building, and the broader question of whether the ordinance environment welcomes competition or protects entrenched interests. Combines scenario-modeling (Analyst, Questioner) with political-economy risk assessment (DA) and ecosystem adaptation dynamics (ST).
**Question count:** 7
**Contributing personas:** Questioner (3), Analyst (1), Devil's Advocate (2), Systems Thinker (2), Audience Advocate (1)
**Primary dimension:** Strategic
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- [Analyst Q6] Regulatory change impact modeling — scenario planning for passage/modification/rejection; quantitative thresholds where downtown vending becomes unviable.
- [Questioner Q1] Anticipatory compliance — how ordinance passage reshapes the model and which alternative revenue streams activate to offset constraints.
- [Questioner Q2] Regulatory engagement strategy — proactively shaping the regulatory landscape through coalitions and data-driven arguments.
- [Devil's Advocate Q4] Ordinance vulnerability blind spot — treating pending restrictions as existential rather than manageable; pre-developed adaptive pivots.
- [Devil's Advocate Q12] Regulatory capture by entrenched interests — whether the environment genuinely welcomes competition; early relationship-building to avoid marginalization.

## Cluster 3: Market Positioning, Competition & Fusion Concept Validation

**Description:** Whether Filipino-Mexican fusion has genuine sustainable appeal in this mid-size city beyond novelty, how to differentiate against 30 trucks / 4 fusion competitors, pricing strategy in a competitive landscape, and the defensibility of the fusion moat against better-capitalized imitators. Also covers the authentic-culture-vs-appropriation test as a market-positioning concern.
**Question count:** 8
**Contributing personas:** Analyst (1), Devil's Advocate (3), Questioner (3), Audience Advocate (1)
**Primary dimension:** Strategic
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- [Analyst Q4] Pricing strategy in competitive landscape — price positioning balancing perceived value against market rate sensitivity; price elasticity across contexts.
- [Devil's Advocate Q1] Market saturation vulnerability — what evidence supports demand for another entrant; interviewing failed food truck operators; minimum viable revenue stop-loss trigger.
- [Devil's Advocate Q5] First-mover disadvantage in emerging cuisine — market research validating that local palates will embrace the flavors versus treating them as novelty.
- [Devil's Advocate Q6] Competition escalation — what prevents better-capitalized competitors from copying the fusion concept; moat defensibility.
- [Questioner Q9] Fusion concept validation — evidence of sustainable appeal beyond novelty; genuine loyalty vs. curious one-time customers.

## Cluster 4: Customer Experience, Accessibility & Cultural Belonging

**Description:** The lived experience of approaching, ordering from, and eating at the truck — food access barriers (pricing, EBT, sliding scale), physical and sensory accessibility, first-time encounter with unfamiliar ingredients (ube, lumpia), dietary accommodations, cultural bridge-building vs. appropriation, and price-perception/value-communication across economic backgrounds. The equity-and-inclusion core of the brainstorm.
**Question count:** 10
**Contributing personas:** Audience Advocate (8), Appreciative Inquirer (1), Questioner (1)
**Primary dimension:** Human-centered
**Sample questions (up to 5):**
- [Audience Advocate Q1] Food access barriers — pricing and location choices creating/removing barriers; sliding scale, donation-based items, EBT acceptance.
- [Audience Advocate Q2] Physical accessibility — beyond basic ADA; truck design, ordering process accommodating mobility, sensory, communication differences.
- [Audience Advocate Q4] First-time customer psychology — mapping the emotional journey of curiosity/confusion/hesitation into delight.
- [Audience Advocate Q6] Dietary restrictions and belonging — naturally inclusive versus requiring special modifications.
- [Audience Advocate Q11] Price perception and value communication — what determines "worth it" vs. "overpriced" across economic backgrounds.

## C1 Comparison (Per-Persona vs. Flat Pooling)

C1 (from Phase 2B P1/C1 carryover into Phase 2C) asks whether per-persona clustering — each persona pre-organizing their own questions into clusters before synthesis sees them — helps or hurts cross-persona synthesis vs. flat pooling.

### Pass A: Per-persona clustering preserved

When persona-internal cluster structure is honored before cross-persona synthesis, the Synthesize personas in this dataset present roughly:

- **Analyst:** ~4 internal clusters (financial model, operational-measurement, regulatory-modeling, growth-decision)
- **Appreciative Inquirer:** ~4 internal clusters (peak moments / delight, community & relationships, strengths & financial practices, sustaining & resources)
- **Audience Advocate:** ~4 internal clusters (access barriers, bodies & needs, cultural belonging, community & equity)
- **Devil's Advocate:** ~4 internal clusters (market viability, financial fragility, regulatory/founder existential, operational/scale)
- **Questioner:** No persona-supplied cluster headings — questions flow as a flat sequence, though regulatory (Q1-Q4), financial (Q5-Q8), competitive (Q9-Q10), and continuity (Q6/Q13/Q14) threads are visible.
- **Systems Thinker:** All 12 questions are supplied with bolded-title headings that encode system archetypes (Success-to-Successful, Fixes that Fail, Tragedy of the Commons, Erosion of Goals).

Synthesizing within these persona-supplied frames yields an 8-cluster cross-persona map closely matching the one above (Cluster 1-8), with the persona-internal labels acting as scaffolding. Systems Thinker's archetype labels in particular are *distinctive content* that should survive into the output rather than being stripped as cluster-structural markup — they carry vocabulary no other persona generates.

### Pass B: Flat pooling

Treating all 72 questions as a flat list and clustering bottom-up yields the same 8 themes. The cluster boundaries shift slightly:

- "Market Positioning" and "Expansion/Growth Path" attach more strongly together under flat pooling (one "Strategic Direction" super-cluster) because both center strategic options; persona-aware clustering preserves the competition-vs-growth distinction.
- AA Q9 (staff-as-community-signal) and AA Q10 (supply chain transparency) are more likely to cluster with C6 (Operations) under flat pooling (operational practices) and with C5 (Community) under persona-aware (AA's internal equity-and-community frame).
- Systems Thinker Q6 (fixes-that-fail financing) sits in both C1 (financial) and C7 (growth) under flat; persona-aware keeps it in C1 because ST's internal framing emphasizes the capital-flexibility tradeoff rather than the growth decision itself.

### Difference, Bias Check & SP1 Recommendation

- **Difference:** Marginal — same 8 clusters, boundary differences on 4-5 borderline questions. Both passes agree on which questions are convergent and which are unique. Both passes identify the Regulatory, Accessibility/Belonging, and Community clusters as high-density convergence zones.
- **Bias check:** Per-persona clustering preserves Systems Thinker's distinctive archetype vocabulary as a genuine signal (not as bias inflating cluster count). Otherwise, persona-internal headings do not over-anchor cross-persona structure. No evidence of cluster inflation from persona-supplied structure.
- **SP1 recommendation:** **Flat pooling for the synthesis step.** With 72 questions and 6 personas, flat pooling produces equivalent cluster quality with a simpler prompt and fewer instructions to track. The one caveat: SP1 prompts should instruct the synthesizer to *preserve Systems Thinker's distinctive archetype vocabulary* (Success-to-Successful, Fixes that Fail, Tragedy of the Commons, Erosion of Goals) in output questions where it appears, because that vocabulary is distinctive content, not cluster-structural markup. This instruction is additive to flat-pooling and does not require per-persona clustering to implement.
