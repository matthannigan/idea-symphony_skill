# food-truck — medium — Convergence Baseline

**Input:** 93 Synthesize-group questions from `test-data/food-truck_medium.md`
**Convergence groups (P):** 16
**Questions in convergence (Q):** 63 of 93 (68%)
**Convergence density:** 68%
**Unique (non-convergent) Synthesize questions:** 30 of 93 (32%)

Convergence here means two or more Synthesize-persona questions address the same concrete concern such that synthesis can either (a) merge them into one richer question that anchors in all source texts, or (b) preserve all distinct voices when the framings differ enough that merging would strip persona-specific value. The introduction of Accountant raises convergence density meaningfully vs. food-truck/low (64% → 68%) because Accountant's quantitative layer frequently converges on Analyst and DA financial territory.

## Convergence Group 1: Ordinance impact, scenario planning & adaptive response

**Theme:** Pressure-testing the business model against pending downtown-hours/annual-fees/proximity-restrictions ordinance — quantitative threshold modeling, contingency activation, pre-developed pivots, and the ecosystem-level feedback loops that make ordinance response either self-reinforcing or self-defeating.
**Cluster:** C2 (Regulatory Environment & Ordinance Response)
**Questions:**
- [Analyst Q8] Ordinance change scenario planning — financial impact under best-case, worst-case, compromise scenarios; contingency metrics triggering strategic pivots.
- [Accountant Q10] Ordinance change impact modeling — $1,500 annual licensing fees + 30% downtown-hours restriction; revenue impact; break-even where secondary zones become viable.
- [Questioner Q1] Anticipatory compliance — how ordinance passage reshapes the business model; alternative revenue streams activating immediately.
- [Devil's Advocate Q4] Ordinance vulnerability blind spot — passes-in-most-restrictive-form scenario; pre-developed adaptive pivots.
- [Systems Thinker Q1] Self-defeating cycles — restrictions pushing trucks into residential areas creating complaints that trigger stricter regulations.
**Merge recommendation:** **Merged.** "How should the business model be pressure-tested and pre-adapted for the pending ordinance (restricted downtown hours, $1,500 annual fees, proximity-to-restaurant bans) across best-case / worst-case / compromise scenarios — including the quantitative break-even threshold where current downtown operations become unviable and secondary zones become more economically viable, the alternative revenue streams ready to activate immediately, and the structural positioning that avoids self-defeating feedback (residential pushback triggering stricter regulations) while pre-developing strategic pivots rather than reacting to constraints as they land?"
