# tool-library — medium — Brainstorming Persona Recommendations (Condition A)

## Topic Classification

The REQUEST describes a neighborhood nonprofit tool library (2,000 households, 500 sq ft donated storefront, 8-10 core volunteers, modest budget). The raw questions cluster heavily around two domains:

1. **User-Facing/Community** — walk-up experience, invisible hurdles (proof of address, smartphone apps, literacy), dignity in asking, language/comfort barriers, surveillance concerns, reciprocity vs. charity, first-time user journeys, "who gets left out."
2. **Implementation/Operations** — 500 sq ft space utilization, volunteer capacity bottlenecks, tool acquisition prioritization, unit economics per loan, membership vs. open-access trade-offs, replacement cycles.

Secondary dimensions include financial (budgeting, sustainability) and governance (board composition, landlord relationship), but these are supporting rather than dominant.

At medium effort, the rubric allocates 4 personas: Core (2) + Inner Ring topic-matched (2). I need to select 2 Inner Ring personas that cover the two dominant dimensions without cluster redundancy.

## Recommended Personas

| Persona | Circle | Topic Cluster | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Devil's Advocate | Core | Challenge Triad | Always included. Strong fit here — the REQUEST is rich with unchallenged assumptions: that 8-10 volunteers can sustain indefinitely, that the donated storefront will remain available, that tool lending is the right model, that demand exists. DA surfaces the "what if the landlord wants the space back," "what if volunteers burn out in year two," "what if this is a solution in search of a problem" pressure that this scope needs. |
| Pragmatist | Core | Analytical Core | Always included. Strong fit — the 500 sq ft / $modest budget / 8-10 volunteer constraint set demands minimum-viable thinking. Pragmatist asks "what's the smallest version that proves the model" (e.g., a single drill and ladder pilot) and prevents the team from designing a full-inventory operation before validating any demand. |
| Audience Advocate | Inner Ring | Human-Centered Triad | Strong fit for the user-facing dimension. The REQUEST is saturated with stakeholder-access concerns: renters vs. homeowners, undocumented residents without ID, limited-English speakers, people "juggling work, caregiving, and limited transportation," people with "negative experiences with institutional spaces," and the "user you never see" who never walks through the door. AA systematically maps which community segments the library's design choices include or exclude — central to the stated goal of "equitable access across different community segments." |
| Momentum Builder | Inner Ring | Progressive/Elaborative Pair | Strong fit for the implementation/ops dimension. The REQUEST is a launch-stage operational challenge — donated space, committed volunteers, starting inventory decisions, membership model choices, first 50 members establishing culture. MB's progressive elaboration (what's the first 30 days, what follows once a small tool set is circulating, how does the operation scale from pilot toward sustainability) directly addresses the "meaningful early win" / "first three months" framing present in the raw questions and keeps the plan action-oriented rather than purely analytical. |

## Selection Summary

- **Total personas:** 4
- **Circle distribution:** Inner 2, Middle 0, Outer 0 (medium effort allocates no Middle/Outer ring slots; this is correct per the rubric's effort-level mapping)
- **Cluster distribution:** Challenge Triad (1 — DA), Analytical Core (1 — Pragmatist), Human-Centered Triad (1 — AA), Progressive/Elaborative Pair (1 — MB). No cluster is doubled, maximizing diversity per the medium-effort algorithm step 5.
- **Dominant topic drivers:**
  - **Equity-of-access language throughout the raw questions** (ID requirements, language access, renters vs. homeowners, surveillance, dignity) drove the Audience Advocate selection over Empath. AA's stakeholder-mapping approach fits the REQUEST's explicit segment-analysis framing better than Empath's gut-feel emotional resonance, though either would have been defensible.
  - **Operational launch constraints** (500 sq ft, volunteer capacity, donated space, modest budget, "what does meaningful early win look like in first three months") drove the Momentum Builder selection over Technical Expert or Simplifier. This is a phased-rollout problem more than a subtraction problem.
- **Outer ring deliberately excluded:**
  - **Accountant** was considered (there are real financial sub-questions — unit economics, replacement cycles, membership models) but at medium effort the two Inner Ring slots are better spent on the dominant dimensions; Pragmatist covers basic cost-feasibility.
  - **Politician** was considered (landlord relationships, board governance, informal power dynamics appear in the raw data) but governance is secondary to access/launch concerns at this effort level.
  - **Lawyer** was not selected — liability questions exist but are not the dominant frame, and at medium effort outer-ring specialized lenses generally belong at high effort.
  - **Storyteller** was not selected despite the Human-Centered Triad relevance — AA covers the user-segment mapping more directly than Storyteller's named-character scenarios, and adding Storyteller would double up the Human-Centered cluster at the expense of the operational dimension.
