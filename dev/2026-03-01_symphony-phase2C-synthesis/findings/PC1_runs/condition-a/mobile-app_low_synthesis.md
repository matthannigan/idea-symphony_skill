# Brainstorming Questions: Mobile App Simplification (15-Feature Fitness App)

---

## Topic Cluster 01: Defining Core Value

1. **What "core value" actually means**: What framework should distinguish the features most people use from the features that create the most passionate engagement — and what if those aren't the same thing? Is "core" defined by frequency, intensity, differentiation from competitors, or the specific job users are hiring the app to do? Have we asked users directly what problem they thought they were solving when they downloaded it, and what would our true differentiator be if we couldn't see any usage data at all? [Questioner, Analyst]

2. **The "one thing" users cannot live without**: If the app became known for doing one thing exceptionally well — the thing that makes users think "I can't live without this" — what would that be, and how would that single focus shape onboarding, daily use, and word-of-mouth? Rather than "what do we cut," what would make users open this app every day even if it only did one thing? [Appreciative Inquirer, Questioner]

3. **Category reframe**: Why do we assume we need to be in the fitness app category at all? What if our actual value lies in an adjacent space (coaching, community, habit formation) we've accidentally stumbled into, and we should be marketing toward that audience rather than the fitness-app crowd? [Questioner]

## Topic Cluster 02: Measuring What Matters

4. **Retention-curve decomposition and leading indicators**: Breaking down the 8% 30-day retention and 2% DAU/MAU ratio, what would a cohort analysis reveal about where users drop off within each feature — and what leading micro-metrics (first-week return rate, task-completion rate, time-to-first-value) should replace lagging metrics like downloads and 30-day retention to better predict daily habit formation? [Analyst]

5. **Metrics-system realignment for a focused utility**: Your current metrics (30-day retention, DAU/MAU, 47-second session length) were designed for a bloated engagement-maximizing app; a focused utility that solves a problem quickly might have SHORTER session lengths but HIGHER long-term retention. What new success-metric system would create the right feedback loops for a simplified product, and what are the risks of measuring the new product with old rulers? [Systems Thinker]

6. **Distinguishing retention-drivers from on-ramps-to-nowhere**: The 8% retention rate makes usage data potentially misleading — features used early by churning users might appear "popular" while actually being on-ramps to nowhere. What behavioral-sequence analysis or experimental design would distinguish features that users say they want from features they actually use, and features that drive retention from those that merely attract initial engagement? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 03: Feature Interdependencies & Removal Risk

7. **Keystone features and cascade dynamics**: Before cutting any feature, what is the complete causal chain showing how its removal affects others — obvious dependencies (data sharing, navigation pathways) and subtle reinforcing patterns (on-ramps that introduce users to other features, data that powers recommendations)? Are there "keystone features" whose removal would cause disproportionate system collapse, and at what threshold does feature-cutting trigger a death spiral through loss of user-generated content, community moderation, or network effects? [Systems Thinker, Analyst] [R11 source-bound: ST uses systems-vocabulary — "keystone features," "death spiral" — not classical archetypes]

8. **Technical-debt explosion from removal**: Removing deeply integrated features could create more bugs and complexity than maintaining them, especially if the 15 features share code, data models, or infrastructure. What is the per-feature unit economics of removal (engineering person-hours to extract, break-even point in recovered velocity), and how do we assess whether the engineering cost might consume the entire 8-month runway without delivering a working product? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

9. **User adaptation and compensatory behavior**: How will existing users adapt their behavior when features disappear, and what unintended consequences might emerge — will they flood the challenge platform with social posts if the feed is cut, or clutter community forums with marketplace-style transaction requests? What compensatory behaviors should we anticipate, and how might they undermine the simplification we're trying to achieve? [Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 04: Distinguishing Real Value from Articulated Preferences

10. **"Overwhelming" — diagnosing the real complaint**: When users say the app is "overwhelming," are they referring to feature count, cognitive load, decision paralysis, poor information architecture, or simply bad discoverability — and how would we diagnose the real problem without asking leading questions? What if simplification means better UX design rather than fewer features, and "dormant gems" would thrive if liberated from interface clutter rather than eliminated? [Audience Advocate, Questioner, Systems Thinker]

11. **Stated vs. revealed preferences**: Where would we see mismatches between what users say they value and which features they actually engage with — and what unarticulated needs are users currently working around because they've normalized "all fitness apps are like this"? How do we distinguish features users genuinely need from features they think they should want? [Audience Advocate]

12. **Execution quality vs. scope as the real problem**: What if retention is low not because the app is unfocused, but because no single feature is good enough to compete with dedicated alternatives in the fitness space? Could the real issue be execution quality across all features rather than scope — and would simplification leave us with nothing compelling? [Devil's Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 05: Passionate Minorities & Niche Users

13. **Valuing the passionate minority**: For features with small but passionate user bases (e.g., <5% of users but 80% engagement within that segment), what analytical framework determines whether they represent a defensible niche worth preserving, a distraction to be cut, or an opportunity to spin off? How do we identify these niche user groups BEFORE cutting, given that removing a feature beloved by a small group may drive away our most vocal advocates and take their friends, their user-generated content, and their community contributions with them? [Analyst, Questioner, Systems Thinker]

14. **Graceful migration for loyalists**: Considering the small but passionate user bases for certain features, how might we honor their commitment while simplifying — what communication strategies, transition timelines, or product choices would turn potential churn into deeper engagement rather than leaving users feeling abandoned or betrayed? [Appreciative Inquirer, Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 06: Research Methods & Decision Process

15. **Avoiding self-selection and one-person bias**: Users who respond to surveys are likely the most engaged minority, and with only one PM on the team, critical decisions could be driven by one person's biases. What research methods would help us understand why the 92% abandon (without alienating the minority who still care), and what decision framework could objectively identify core value without letting ownership bias, team politics, or limited perspective dominate? [Devil's Advocate, Audience Advocate]

16. **Testing removal before committing**: Could we ship a version with one feature temporarily disabled and measure who complains — or who doesn't notice? What small, low-risk experiments could the team try this month to test which features truly represent the app's core value, and how would we engage users in ways that make them feel part of the product's evolution rather than subjects of it? [Questioner, Appreciative Inquirer]

17. **Interviewing the 8% who stayed**: What would happen if we interviewed the users who stayed past 30 days and asked them specifically what they'd stop using if removed — would their answers surprise us, and what patterns emerge from tracing their actual user journeys versus those of users who churn immediately? [Questioner, Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 07: Transition Strategy (Incremental vs. Clean Relaunch)

18. **Incremental vs. clean-slate methodology**: How should we structure the decision between incremental simplification (remove one feature per month, measure impact each time) versus clean relaunch — what analytical framework compares the risks of alienating existing users through abrupt change against the opportunity costs of gradualism, given the 8-month runway? At what point does feature removal uncover so many hidden dependencies that we should pivot to incremental simplification? [Analyst, Questioner, Devil's Advocate]

19. **The "2.0 relaunch" assumption**: A clean relaunch assumes users will give us a second chance after we've burned them with a confusing first experience, but app stores are littered with failed 2.0s because users had already moved on. What evidence suggests our existing user base will return, are we over-indexing on retention versus acquisition of net-new users, and what is the maximum acceptable user loss we can sustain if simplification backfires? [Devil's Advocate]

20. **Framing the change as focus, not loss**: What would happen if we framed simplification as "we're focusing on what matters most" rather than "we're taking things away" — what transparency, timeline, and communication approach would preserve trust with existing users while signaling a bold focus to new ones? [Questioner, Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 08: User Experience & Attention

21. **The first-30-seconds experience**: What does the first 30 seconds feel like for a new user who downloaded expecting a fitness tracker but encounters 15 features across 6 tabs and a hamburger menu — from excitement ("I'm going to get fit!") to overwhelm ("Where do I even start?") — and how does that experience differ for users with varying digital literacy, visual impairments, or cognitive-processing differences, or for users with limited time, attention, or mental bandwidth who are dipping in for 47 seconds of value? [Audience Advocate]

22. **Intent vs. distraction friction**: What do users experience when they open the app for a specific purpose (tracking a workout) but get pulled into notifications, social feeds, and marketplace suggestions — how often do they successfully complete the task they came for versus abandon because the app pulled them in too many directions? What notification philosophy would align with respecting users' attention rather than demanding it? [Audience Advocate]

23. **User archetypes served or abandoned by simplification**: What different user archetypes are currently served (or underserved) by the 15 features — the social butterfly who loves challenges, the privacy-focused person who just wants to track workouts — and if we cut to 3-4 features, who are we leaving behind, and is that acceptable? [Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 09: Team, Runway & Amplifying Strengths

24. **Team-resource ROI of simplification**: With 8 months of runway and a burned-out team of 6 engineers, 1 designer, and 1 PM maintaining 15 half-built features, what is the ROI calculation for refactoring — how much engineering capacity would be freed by cutting to 3-4 features, what is the break-even in recovered development velocity on the remaining features, and how does redeploying engineers toward depth (vs. reducing headcount) either reinforce simplicity or naturally drift back toward complexity? [Analyst, Systems Thinker]

25. **Team morale through the cull**: If engineers have spent months or years building features now being deleted, how will they stay motivated to execute the relaunch — and how do we rebuild psychological investment after a round of "killing darlings" while helping the team feel proud of simplification rather than feeling they "failed" by building features that are now being cut? [Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

26. **Team-aligned product vision and brightest moments**: Think about a time when this team felt most energized, proud, or effective — what conditions made that moment possible, what strengths does each member bring, and what kind of simplified product (aligned with their existing technical capabilities, design sensibilities, and domain expertise) would allow them to do their best work so the simplification feels like liberation rather than loss? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 10: Aspirational Vision & Existing Assets

27. **Peak user experiences and features that sparked joy**: Tell me about a moment when a user genuinely loved this app — a session that went beyond 47 seconds, a time someone told a friend about it, or a feature that elicited the most positive feedback and sustained engagement. What exactly were they doing, what made that feature resonate (utility, experience, social connection), and how might we design the entire product to create more of those specific moments? [Appreciative Inquirer]

28. **The three-years-from-now champion user**: Imagine it's three years from now and this app has become something users genuinely champion — not just use, but actively recommend to friends. What would they say about it, what specific feeling or outcome would they describe, and how would it fit into their daily routine in a way that feels indispensable rather than overwhelming? [Appreciative Inquirer]

29. **Hidden assets and self-reinforcing loops**: With 180K downloads and the 2% who show up daily, who are the users already finding value, what specific use case keeps them coming back, and what existing assets (technical infrastructure, user insights, team skills, community relationships, learnings from the current feature set) could accelerate the move toward a focused product? Which features, if isolated and executed exceptionally well, would create a self-reinforcing loop where users' successful outcomes attract more users, whose collective outcomes generate network effects that further reinforce the value proposition? [Appreciative Inquirer, Systems Thinker]

30. **Simplification-creep vigilance**: Every product adds features over time through a reinforcing loop of competitive pressure, user requests, and the urge to grow. What early-warning systems or structural constraints could we design now — a zero-sum feature budget where adding anything requires removing something else, for example — that would naturally resist feature creep and maintain simplicity? [Systems Thinker]
