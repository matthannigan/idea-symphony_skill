# Brainstorming Questions: Filipino-Mexican Fusion Food Truck

---

## Topic Cluster 01: Vision, Purpose, and the Three-Year Horizon

1. **Three-year anniversary vision and customer-described signature**: Imagine it's three years from now and you're celebrating the business's anniversary with loyal customers, team members, and community supporters — what would people say the business has contributed to the city's food culture, what would the atmosphere feel like, and if customers described this truck to friends as an experience they genuinely loved (not just for the food, but for how it made them feel), what words, moments, and emotional responses would recur? [Appreciative Inquirer]

2. **Peak culinary moments and sustaining purpose**: Tell me about a time when you created a dish that completely delighted people — what made it work so well, how might we design the truck experience to create more of those moments regularly, and what daily or weekly practices would keep you connected to the joy and purpose that drew you to this business (especially during challenging periods) while also communicating your values to customers and team members? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 02: Existing Strengths and Assets to Activate

3. **Founder strengths, network assets, and learning from what works**: What unique strengths, resources, or capabilities do you already bring to this venture that other food truck startups might lack — who in your existing network has specific gifts (skills, connections, resources) that could help launch through mutual benefit rather than transactional exchange — and what aspects of the existing food truck scene in this city are working exceptionally well that we might build on? [Appreciative Inquirer]

4. **Centering Filipino-Mexican culinary tradition**: What specific menu items, flavor combinations, or culinary traditions from Filipino and Mexican cuisine should we center to create both authenticity and broad appeal, and how might we structure the menu to educate and delight customers unfamiliar with these cuisines? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 03: Financial Architecture and Capital Strategy

5. **Break-even scenarios and the "valley of death"**: What are the realistic break-even points under single-truck daily vending, event-focused operations, and catering-heavy approaches, and how do fixed costs (permitting, insurance, commissary fees) versus variable costs (COGS, labor, fuel) shift the economics of each scenario — including the 12–18 month "valley of death" ramp where revenue may run 30% below projections for the first 8 months and which revenue stream provides the most predictable cash flow foundation? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate]

6. **SBA loan vs. bootstrapping — a Fixes-that-Fail pattern**: What decision framework should guide the choice between $40K in SBA debt versus conservative bootstrapping — and how does this map to the Fixes-that-Fail archetype where immediate capital accelerates startup and validates the decision, but fixed monthly obligations reduce flexibility to weather worst-case seasonal scenarios (winter months, rainy seasons, event cancellations), while potential covenants or personal guarantees beyond comfort could shift the timeline entirely? What specific equipment or operational advantages does the loan actually buy that bootstrapping cannot replicate through creativity or time? [Analyst, Systems Thinker, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

7. **Equipment ownership trap — financing vs. leasing**: For truck acquisition, kitchen equipment, and POS systems, what is the quantified tradeoff between financing (ownership, depreciation, higher upfront cash impact) and leasing (lower initial capital, tax deductibility, long-term cost premium) — including maintenance responsibility, downtime, resale value, and lease-to-own structures that preserve optionality as the concept evolves? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate]

8. **Sustainable financial practices and seasonality buffer**: What sustainable financial practices from other successful small food businesses could we adapt — particularly pricing that values both customer experience and business viability, revenue streams that create stability, and cash flow management that respects seasonal rhythms — and how many months of operating reserves are truly necessary given weather-driven revenue interruption, with $100K sufficient to weather year one? [Appreciative Inquirer, Questioner]

## Topic Cluster 04: Market Validation and Competitive Differentiation

9. **Fusion concept validation vs. novelty trap**: With 30 active food trucks and 4 direct fusion competitors, what evidence validates that local palates will embrace Filipino-Mexican flavors beyond novelty curiosity — how would you pressure-test revenue projections by interviewing failed food truck operators, test demand with minimal capital before full buildout, and distinguish genuine loyalty from one-time curiosity? What minimum viable revenue number triggers a "stop loss" decision? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

10. **Defensibility, differentiation, and curbside communication**: Given 4 similar fusion concepts already operating, what pricing strategy and distinctive positioning would make this truck meaningfully different in customers' minds beyond menu items — how would it be communicated instantly at curbside — and what "moat" prevents better-capitalized competitors from copying the concept and out-executing with superior locations, marketing, or economies of scale? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

## Topic Cluster 05: Regulatory Environment and Political Strategy

11. **Ordinance as existential threat and anticipatory pivots**: The pending ordinances restricting downtown hours and adding annual fees may be existential rather than a manageable annoyance — what quantitative thresholds (reduced operating hours, fee increases) would make downtown vending unviable, what adaptive pivots have you pre-developed if the ordinance passes in its most restrictive form, and what alternative revenue streams could be activated immediately to offset those constraints? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

12. **Regulatory engagement as a double-edged feedback loop**: Rather than reacting to ordinances, what would it look like to proactively shape the regulatory landscape by building coalitions with other food truck owners and presenting data-driven arguments to city council — while calibrating engagement to avoid becoming marked as a visible operator inviting closer scrutiny, and recognizing that regulatory capture by entrenched interests may disadvantage newcomers in permit allocation, prime location assignments, and ordinance negotiations? [Systems Thinker, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

13. **Equity-centered regulatory advocacy**: How do the pending ordinances differentially affect food truck owners who are women, people of color, or immigrants with less capital and political access — and how can the business engage in advocacy that centers equity and marginalized-stakeholder voice rather than just protecting its own interests? [Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 06: Growth Pathway and Systemic Scaling Dynamics

14. **Fleet expansion decision gates and coordination ceiling**: What measurable milestones (revenue per hour, repeat customer rate, catering demand growth) should trigger fleet vs. brick-and-mortar vs. single-truck decisions — and at what scale does coordination overhead (inventory across locations, overlapping shifts, maintenance, commissary contention) begin eating margins faster than expansion adds revenue, given that food truck economics often suffer from diseconomies of scale? What indicators suggest remaining single-truck indefinitely? [Analyst, Systems Thinker, Devil's Advocate]

15. **Brick-and-mortar: constraint or amplifier?** Why assume brick-and-mortar is the natural evolution rather than staying fleet-focused, developing a packaged product line, or becoming primarily catering — and at what point does a restaurant shift from constraint (fixed costs skyrocket, flexibility disappears) to amplifier (stabilizing anchor for prep, storage, and brand that supports fleet operations more efficiently than separate commissary arrangements)? [Systems Thinker, Questioner]

16. **Success-to-the-Successful: catering cannibalizing daily vending**: As catering revenue grows, it creates a reinforcing Success-to-the-Successful dynamic — catering pays better, requires less staffing complexity, builds repeat-booking relationships — pulling attention from street vending, weakening public visibility and brand presence, which reduces walk-up demand. How do you prevent catering from cannibalizing the public presence that makes your brand valuable, guard against client concentration and feast-or-famine volatility, and maintain consistent daily truck revenue while pursuing sporadic high-value bookings? [Systems Thinker, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

## Topic Cluster 07: Operations, Commissary, and Systemic Fragility

17. **Commissary as Tragedy-of-the-Commons bottleneck**: Given the mandatory commissary requirement, what analysis should drive kitchen selection beyond base cost (location relative to prime vending zones, storage capacity, prep-window efficiency, potential sublet revenue) — and recognizing the Tragedy-of-the-Commons dynamic where each added truck competes with itself for prep space, cooler storage, and peak-hour equipment access, at what scale do alternative structures (staggered schedules, negotiated exclusive hours, partial ownership) become necessary, and what contingencies exist if the partnership becomes unavailable or prohibitively expensive? [Analyst, Systems Thinker, Questioner]

18. **Founder-dependency existential risk and operational redundancy**: As a solo operator with no business ownership experience, what happens to revenue during illness, family emergencies, or burnout — what systems allow the business to generate income during your absence, what is the realistic timeline to hire a first reliable backup operator, and what operational redundancies can be built from day one to prevent single-point failure? [Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

19. **Menu innovation trap and complexity erosion of goals**: How should the menu be structured to optimize food cost ratios and cross-utilization potential across lumpia tacos, adobo burritos, and ube horchata — while managing the innovation trap where each new item adds compounding complexity to inventory, prep, and service speed (an "erosion of goals" dynamic), and what system adds and removes items to maintain novelty without accumulating complexity? [Analyst, Systems Thinker]

20. **Location performance, seasonality oscillation, and weather dependency**: What systematic approach should guide location selection and rotation (sales per hour, customer demographics, repeat rate, weather sensitivity, statistically significant visit counts) — and what percentage of annual revenue needs to come from non-truck sources (meal kits, corporate contracts, prepared foods) to smooth the seasonal oscillation where summer boom-time decisions (hiring, equipment, marketing spend) create painful winter contractions through delayed feedback? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Questioner, Systems Thinker]

21. **Equipment reliability as reinforcing loop**: Cheap equipment breaks frequently, causing service interruptions, which frustrate customers and reduce revenue, making quality replacements harder to afford — a self-reinforcing fragility loop; high-quality equipment has the opposite reinforcing dynamic on delay. How do you evaluate this tradeoff with finite startup capital, and which specific equipment items have the highest leverage in shifting the system toward reliability? [Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 08: Customer Access, Belonging, and Cultural Stewardship

22. **Food access, pricing perception, and equity barriers**: How might pricing strategy and location choices create or remove barriers for community members with limited disposable income — what mechanisms (sliding scale, donation-based items, EBT acceptance) ensure the truck serves as genuine community resource rather than premium-only — and when customers see a $12–15 fusion taco, how is the value (quality ingredients, fair wages, cultural authenticity, portion size) communicated across different economic backgrounds? [Audience Advocate]

23. **Accessibility, dietary belonging, and first-time customer psychology**: Beyond basic ADA compliance, how does the truck's physical design, ordering process, and service experience accommodate diverse mobility challenges, sensory sensitivities, non-English speakers, hearing loss, social anxiety, and dietary restrictions (vegetarian, gluten-free, allergies, religious dietary laws) — and how can the ordering experience, menu descriptions, and staff interactions transform first-time uncertainty about unfamiliar ingredients like ube or lumpia into delight while streamlining time-pressured lunch flow without sacrificing human connection? [Audience Advocate]

24. **Cultural bridge vs. appropriation**: Filipino-Mexican fusion sits at the intersection of two rich culinary traditions — how can the business honor and elevate both cultures authentically while avoiding appropriation, and what does genuine community collaboration with Filipino and Mexican communities in the city look like? [Audience Advocate]

25. **Neighborhood good-neighbor, supply-chain transparency, and staff treatment**: What does it mean to be a good neighbor in the locations where you park (supporting local events, hiring locally, sourcing from nearby producers), what would supply chain transparency (local sourcing, ethical meat, fair trade) build with customers who have experienced industrial food systems as exploitative or opaque, and what signals do wages, working conditions, and opportunities for growth send to customers about whether this is a business they want to support? [Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 09: Relationships, Loyalty, and Word-of-Mouth Dynamics

26. **Relationship-first operating practices**: How might we design intentional practices for building relationships with city officials, fellow food truck owners, and neighborhood communities from the very beginning — what would a "relationship-first" approach look like in daily operations, and what structures would make that sustainable even during busy periods? [Appreciative Inquirer]

27. **Location loyalty vs. follower loyalty and the word-of-mouth pricing loop**: Some customers find you wherever you park; others come because you're at their office or brewery — the follower model is portable and resilient, while the location model builds micro-community density through self-reinforcing clusters. Which model scales better when adding trucks, and how does pricing set the word-of-mouth loop in motion (price determines who tries you, who talks about you, what reputation you build, what customer you attract, what price they'll accept — operating over months rather than days)? [Systems Thinker, Analyst]

## Topic Cluster 10: Early Experiments and Measurement

28. **Small concrete experiment to try now**: What small, concrete experiment could you try this month or next that would both test your concept and begin building the kind of community relationships and customer loyalty you want to see three years from now — and what would you learn from that experiment? [Appreciative Inquirer]

29. **Critical KPIs and price-sensitivity discovery**: What 5–7 key performance indicators provide the most actionable intelligence without creating measurement paralysis — how should they be weighted across daily operations optimization, financial health, customer satisfaction, and growth trajectory, and what testing methods reveal the exact price point where customers begin choosing alternatives before permanent pricing is locked in? [Analyst, Questioner]

## Appended Questions (Perspective, Provocation, and Distinctive-Register Personas)

### Provocateur (Append)

1. **[What if the food disappeared, but the line remained?]**: Imagine a food truck where people pay specifically to wait in line — the line itself is the product, and the food is just a free bonus that happens at the end. What would you design into that waiting experience that people would actually pay for? What does this reveal about the hidden value in your concept that you're not currently capturing? *The real insight: what if community, anticipation, and social experience are the actual product, not just a side effect of hunger?*

2. **[What if success meant putting yourself out of business?]**: Imagine your explicit goal is to be so successful that you're forced to close within 18 months — either because the city bans you, you can't keep up with demand, or you create a movement that makes your entire concept obsolete. How would you operate differently if runaway success was actually failure? What "safe, reasonable" growth strategies are actually just playing small? *The real insight: what if we're optimizing for sustainability when we should be optimizing for impact — and what does "too successful" even look like?*

3. **[What if you had to start with zero capital and $100K in debt?]**: Flip the entire financial premise: instead of $60K savings + $40K optional loan, you begin with -$100K and no assets. You cannot spend money to make money — you can only use relationships, creativity, and other people's resources. How does the business model change when every dollar you spend is a dollar you don't have? *The real insight: what if the financial cushion is actually masking a much more interesting opportunity to design a capital-light model?*

### Storyteller (Append)

1. **First Bite Discovery**: Imagine it's 11:45 AM on a Tuesday—Maria, an office worker who packed the same sad lunch for years, walks past your truck. What does she see, smell, and hear in those first 10 seconds that compels her to abandon her familiar routine and try something entirely new? How does that first encounter with Filipino-Mexican fusion become a story she tells her coworkers that afternoon?

2. **Solo Founder's Threshold**: Envision the founder, Elena, working alone on the truck during the lunch rush when three separate corporate catering inquiries come in simultaneously—how does she handle that moment of decision between saying "yes to growth" and "no, I need to protect the experience I can actually deliver"? What does that choice reveal about the kind of business owner she's becoming, and how does it shape the next chapter of her expansion plans?

3. **Regulatory Crossroads**: Paint the scene where Elena attends a city council meeting about the proposed food truck restrictions—what does she say in her three minutes of public comment that transforms her from "just another food truck owner complaining" into "the culinary entrepreneur whose testimony makes council members reconsider the human impact of these ordinances"? How does that moment of advocacy ripple through her relationships with other truck owners and city officials?

### Visionary (Append)

1. **What if a food truck functioned less like a portable restaurant and more like a traveling cultural ambassador that creates pop-up community experiences?** Instead of thinking about how to sell Filipino-Mexican fusion from a truck, imagine if the business was fundamentally about creating temporary third spaces — micro-gatherings that transform parking lots, office parks, and neighborhood streets into destinations where food is the catalyst for connection, storytelling, and cultural exchange. What would the business model look like if revenue came from experiences, partnerships, and community-building rather than transactional food sales alone? How might this change conversations with city officials about the truck's "public value"?

2. **What would this business look like if it were designed from the ground up to NEVER become a traditional restaurant chain, even while achieving the founder's economic and creative goals?** Instead of the conventional trajectory (truck → fleet → brick-and-mortar), imagine entirely new organizational forms: a networked federation of independent owner-operators sharing a unified brand and backend; a guild-style collective that trains and launches new food entrepreneurs under a shared umbrella; a platform business that provides infrastructure, financing, and market access to culinary creators while remaining fundamentally decentralized. What if "scaling" meant multiplying access to opportunity rather than multiplying outlets?

3. **What if we designed the business to be antifragile — becoming stronger through volatility, regulatory changes, and competitive pressure — rather than merely resilient to them?** Instead of viewing pending city ordinances, competition from similar fusion concepts, or seasonal fluctuations as threats to manage, imagine a business architecture that actually *feeds* on disruption: a modular model that can rapidly reconfigure based on regulatory conditions; a guerrilla logistics network that can pivot operations overnight; a community-political power base that gives the business influence over the very regulations that constrain it. How could constraints become the engine of innovation rather than brakes on growth?

### Analogist (Append)

1. **Migratory patterns and resource distribution**: How do migratory bird species optimize their routes and timing to maximize food availability while minimizing competition, and what patterns from their adaptive movement strategies could inform a food truck's approach to location selection, seasonal shifts, and avoiding direct competition with similar trucks in the same geographic zones?

2. **Craft brewery expansion playbook**: The craft brewing industry went through a similar phase where successful local breweries navigated the transition from single taproom to regional distribution, with many failing by expanding too quickly into brick-and-mortar before establishing sustainable economics—what specific expansion mistakes and successful patterns from that industry's growth cycle should inform your 3-year transition plan from truck to potential storefront?

3. **Hub-and-spoke vs. distributed network models**: Logistics companies like FedEx explicitly chose hub-and-spoke systems over point-to-point delivery for efficiency advantages—does your planned expansion (truck → catering → potential fleet → brick-and-mortar) map better to a hub-and-spoke model with a central kitchen or a distributed model, and what are the operational economics and break-even points where each architecture becomes superior?
