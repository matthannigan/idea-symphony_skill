# Brainstorming Questions: Filipino-Mexican Fusion Food Truck

---

## Topic Cluster 01: Vision, Values, and Cultural Foundation

1. **Three-year legacy and peak moments**: What would people — loyal customers, team members, community supporters — say the business contributed to the city's food culture three years from now, and which peak culinary and relational moments from early operations reveal the hidden assets and aspirational strengths the business should design to reproduce regularly? [Appreciative Inquirer]

2. **Cultural bridge vs. appropriation**: Filipino-Mexican fusion sits at the intersection of two rich traditions — what does genuine collaboration with Filipino and Mexican communities in the city look like, and how do we honor and elevate both cuisines authentically rather than commodifying them, given that Filipino-Mexican fusion is trendy in coastal markets but unproven in this mid-size city and may be treated as a novelty that fades? [Audience Advocate, Devil's Advocate, Appreciative Inquirer]

3. **Is the fusion concept itself the trap?**: What evidence suggests Filipino-Mexican fusion has sustainable appeal beyond novelty — building genuine loyalty rather than curious one-time customers — and how might we test demand with minimal capital commitment before full vehicle buildout, while amplifying specific flavor combinations and culinary traditions that create both authenticity and broad appeal? [Questioner, Devil's Advocate, Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 02: Unit Economics, Pricing, and Financial Foundation

4. **Break-even economics across operating models**: What are the realistic break-even points under single-truck daily vending, event-focused operations, and catering-heavy approaches — and how do fixed costs (permitting, insurance, commissary fees) vs. variable costs (COGS, labor, fuel) shift the economics, including what happens if food costs run 5-8% higher than projected and which menu items become unsustainable first? [Analyst, Questioner]

5. **Pricing strategy and reputation feedback loop**: Given 30 active food trucks and 4 direct fusion competitors, what price positioning balances perceived value against market rate sensitivity for a $12–15 fusion taco — how does price elasticity differ across lunch crowds, dinner, and catering, and how does the initial price point kick off a word-of-mouth loop (bargain-seekers vs. quality-seekers) that locks in brand positioning and customer base over months? [Analyst, Audience Advocate, Systems Thinker]

6. **SBA loan vs. bootstrapping — a "Fixes-that-Fail" dynamic**: What decision framework should guide the choice between $40K SBA debt and conservative bootstrapping, given that the short-term fix of more capital creates fixed monthly obligations that mask longer-term loss of flexibility — a classic Fixes-that-Fail pattern — and how does this break down across risk tolerance, seasonal debt-service viability, runway, and worst-case trigger metrics that would shift the business from growth investment to pure survival mode? [Analyst, Systems Thinker, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

7. **Menu food cost and supply-chain values alignment**: How should the fusion menu be structured to optimize food cost ratios while preserving the concept — where are core ingredients (enabling cross-utilization across lumpia tacos, adobo burritos, ube horchata) most vulnerable to price volatility, and how might making the supply chain transparent (local sourcing, ethical meat, fair trade) simultaneously build trust with customers who have experienced industrial food systems as exploitative? [Analyst, Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 03: Regulatory Environment and Advocacy

8. **Ordinance vulnerability and adaptive pivots**: The pending ordinance restricting downtown hours and raising fees may be treated as a manageable annoyance but could be an existential threat — what quantitative thresholds (reduced hours, fee increases) would make the downtown model unviable, what adaptive pivots have been pre-developed for the most restrictive passage scenario, and what alternative revenue streams could be activated immediately? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

9. **Equity-centered regulatory engagement**: Rather than reacting, what would it look like to proactively shape the regulatory landscape — building coalitions with other truck owners and presenting data-driven arguments to city council — in ways that center how these changes differentially affect owners who are women, people of color, or immigrants with less capital and political access, rather than just protecting the business's own interests? [Questioner, Audience Advocate]

10. **Regulatory relationship feedback loop**: Engagement with city officials gives advance warning and credibility, but marks you as a visible operator inviting closer scrutiny and consumes operational time — and the existing food truck association may have relationships that disadvantage newcomers. How do you calibrate engagement to get the benefits without becoming dependent on political access, and how do early relationship-building efforts prevent marginalization in permits, location assignments, and ordinance negotiations? [Systems Thinker, Devil's Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 04: Competition, Differentiation, and Market Position

11. **Differentiation and defensibility in a saturated market**: With 4 similar fusion concepts already operating and 30 total trucks, what makes this truck meaningfully distinct beyond menu items, what "moat" exists beyond being first to the niche (preventing better-capitalized imitators from out-executing on locations and marketing), and how can that differentiation be communicated instantly at curbside? [Questioner, Devil's Advocate]

12. **Learning from what already works**: What aspects of the existing food truck scene are working exceptionally well — what are local trucks doing that customers clearly love — and how might we build on those proven patterns while still offering something distinctive? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 05: Customer Experience, Access, and Belonging

13. **First-time customer journey and streamlined lunch experience**: Mapping the emotional journey of someone encountering Filipino-Mexican fusion for the first time (curiosity, confusion about ube or lumpia, hesitation) — how do ordering experience, menu descriptions, and staff interactions transform uncertainty into delight, and how can the time-pressured lunch customer's frictions in ordering, payment, and pickup be streamlined without sacrificing the human connection that makes food trucks special? [Audience Advocate, Appreciative Inquirer]

14. **Inclusive access — economic, physical, and dietary**: How might pricing and location choices create or remove barriers for community members with limited disposable income (mechanisms like sliding scale, donation items, EBT) — while the physical design, ordering process, and service also accommodate diverse mobility challenges, sensory sensitivities, non-English speakers, and dietary restrictions (vegetarian, gluten-free, allergies, religious laws) so people feel welcomed rather than an afterthought? [Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 06: Operations, Capacity, and Systems

15. **Commissary as a Tragedy-of-the-Commons bottleneck**: Given the mandatory commissary requirement, what analysis should drive kitchen selection (location, storage capacity, prep-window efficiency, potential sublet revenue), and how does shared-kitchen use become a Tragedy-of-the-Commons dynamic as fleet grows — each truck optimizing its own prep schedule while collectively creating congestion — such that at some scale staggered schedules, negotiated exclusive hours, or partial ownership change the equation entirely? Also: what if the commissary partnership becomes unavailable or prohibitively expensive? [Analyst, Systems Thinker, Questioner]

16. **Solo-founder dependency and operational redundancy**: As a solo operator with no business ownership experience, the entire operation depends on continuous presence — what happens to revenue during illness, burnout, or family emergencies, what operational redundancies can be built from day one to prevent single-point failure, and what's the realistic timeline to hire a first reliable backup operator? [Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

17. **Equipment quality reliability loop and ownership tradeoffs**: Cheap equipment creates a reinforcing reliability failure loop (breakdowns → service interruptions → lost revenue → harder to afford quality), while the financing-vs-leasing decision involves not just financial tradeoffs but maintenance responsibility, depreciation, and upgrade flexibility — have you modeled total cost of ownership (repairs, downtime, resale) and identified which specific equipment items have the highest leverage for shifting the system toward reliability? [Systems Thinker, Analyst, Devil's Advocate]

18. **Menu innovation trap**: Introducing new items creates a virtuous novelty cycle, but each addition compounds complexity in inventory, prep, and service speed — a Success-to-the-Successful dynamic where the very innovation attracting customers eventually slows service and drives them away. What system for adding and removing items maintains novelty without accumulating complexity? [Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 07: Cash Flow, Seasonality, and Survival

19. **Valley-of-death cash flow through seasonal oscillation**: Food truck businesses often need 12–18 months to reach consistent profitability, and revenue mix across daily vending, catering, and events must sustain positive cash flow through seasonal dips — but summer revenue justifies fixed commitments that persist through winter, creating an oscillation pattern of over-expansion then painful contraction. How have you modeled the gap between capital depletion and sustainable cash flow (including 30%-below-projection scenarios for 8 months), how many months of reserves are truly necessary, and what leading (not trailing) indicators should inform expansion timing? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Questioner, Systems Thinker]

20. **Weather and non-truck revenue diversification**: Food trucks face extreme revenue variation from weather, and indoor dining captures business during harsh seasons — how would you develop revenue streams not weather-dependent (meal kits, corporate contracts, prepared foods), and what percentage of annual revenue needs to come from non-truck sources to smooth cash flow? [Devil's Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 08: Growth Pathways and Long-Term Trajectory

21. **Expansion decision gates and brick-and-mortar assumption**: What measurable milestones (revenue per hour, repeat rate, catering demand growth) should trigger fleet expansion vs. brick-and-mortar vs. staying single-truck — and why assume brick-and-mortar is the natural evolution rather than staying fleet-focused, developing a packaged product line, or becoming primarily catering? The restaurant shifts from constraint to amplifier at some point in the trajectory — what early decisions make that transition smooth rather than disruptive? [Analyst, Questioner, Systems Thinker]

22. **Fleet expansion: coordination overhead and Success-to-the-Successful catering drift**: Adding a second truck can quadruple coordination complexity (multi-location inventory, scheduling, maintenance, commissary competition) faster than revenue doubles, while simultaneously catering creates a Success-to-the-Successful reinforcing dynamic that pulls attention from street vending, weakens public visibility, and further pushes toward catering — but catering revenue also concentrates risk in feast-or-famine cycles and large client dependence. At what scale does coordination overhead eat margins, and how do you prevent catering from cannibalizing the public brand presence and from over-reliance on a few contracts? [Systems Thinker, Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

23. **Location loyalty vs. follower loyalty as scaling architecture**: Some customers find you wherever you park; others come because you're at their office or brewery. The follower model is portable; the location model builds self-reinforcing micro-community density. Which scales better when adding trucks — and how do you design presence from day one to build the kind of loyalty that supports multi-unit expansion rather than recreating coordination problems or competing with yourself for mobile customers? [Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 09: Measurement, Metrics, and Learning

24. **KPI dashboard and location performance framework**: What 5–7 KPIs provide actionable intelligence without measurement paralysis, weighted differently across daily operations, financial health, customer satisfaction, and growth — and what systematic approach guides location rotation (sales per hour, demographics, repeat rate, weather sensitivity), including how many visits yield statistically significant data and what triggers prompt keeping, rotating, or dropping a location? [Analyst]

25. **Concept-validation early experiment**: What small, concrete experiment could be tried this month or next that would both test the fusion concept and begin building the community relationships and customer loyalty envisioned three years out — and what would you learn from it? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 10: Community, Relationships, and Staff as Legacy

26. **Relationship-first operating practice**: How might we design intentional practices for building relationships with city officials, fellow food truck owners, and neighborhood communities from the very beginning — what would a "relationship-first" approach look like in daily operations, what structures keep it sustainable during busy periods, and who already in the existing network brings specific gifts that could be invited into the process from day one for mutual rather than transactional benefit? [Appreciative Inquirer]

27. **Good neighbor beyond transactions**: Food trucks occupy public space and become part of neighborhood fabric — what does it mean to be a good neighbor in the locations you park, and how might the business contribute to community wellbeing beyond selling food (supporting local events, hiring locally, sourcing from nearby producers)? [Audience Advocate]

28. **Staff treatment as visible community impact**: From the community's perspective, how the business treats employees is visible in every interaction — what signals do wages, working conditions, and growth opportunities send to customers about whether this is a business worth supporting, and how might investing in staff wellbeing become a competitive advantage? [Audience Advocate]

29. **Sustaining founder purpose**: What daily or weekly practices would keep the founder connected to the joy and purpose that drew them to the business — especially during challenging periods — and how might those practices also communicate values to customers and team members? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Appended Questions (Perspective, Provocation, and Distinctive-Register Personas)

### Provocateur (Append)

1. **[What if the food disappeared, but the line remained?]** Imagine a food truck where people pay specifically to wait in line — the line itself is the product, and the food is just a free bonus that happens at the end. What would you design into that waiting experience that people would actually pay for? What does this reveal about the hidden value in your concept that you're not currently capturing? *The real insight: what if community, anticipation, and social experience are the actual product, not just a side effect of hunger?*

2. **[What if success meant putting yourself out of business?]** Imagine your explicit goal is to be so successful that you're forced to close within 18 months — either because the city bans you, you can't keep up with demand, or you create a movement that makes your entire concept obsolete. How would you operate differently if runaway success was actually failure? What "safe, reasonable" growth strategies are actually just playing small? *The real insight: what if we're optimizing for sustainability when we should be optimizing for impact — and what does "too successful" even look like?*

3. **[What if the city passed the worst possible ordinance, and it was the best thing that ever happened to you?]** The new restrictions pass: downtown vending hours eliminated, annual fees tripled, food trucks banned within 500 feet of restaurants. But somehow this becomes the catalyst that transforms your business model entirely. What does that transformed business look like, and why is it actually better than the original plan? *The real insight: what if the pending regulatory threat is actually a gift you haven't unwrapped yet?*

### Storyteller (Append)

1. **First Bite Discovery** Imagine it's 11:45 AM on a Tuesday—Maria, an office worker who packed the same sad lunch for years, walks past your truck. What does she see, smell, and hear in those first 10 seconds that compels her to abandon her familiar routine and try something entirely new? How does that first encounter with Filipino-Mexican fusion become a story she tells her coworkers that afternoon?

2. **Solo Founder's Threshold** Envision the founder, Elena, working alone on the truck during the lunch rush when three separate corporate catering inquiries come in simultaneously—how does she handle that moment of decision between saying "yes to growth" and "no, I need to protect the experience I can actually deliver"? What does that choice reveal about the kind of business owner she's becoming, and how does it shape the next chapter of her expansion plans?

3. **Cultural Bridge Building** Follow the journey of a Filipino-American family who discovers your truck at a street festival—how does seeing familiar flavors reimagined through Mexican street food format make them feel both seen and surprised in ways that keep them coming back not just for the food, but for what your truck represents about cultural fusion and identity? How do you harness that emotional connection to build a community that advocates for your business through regulatory challenges and competition?

### Visionary (Append)

1. **What if a food truck functioned less like a portable restaurant and more like a traveling cultural ambassador that creates pop-up community experiences?** Instead of thinking about how to sell Filipino-Mexican fusion from a truck, imagine if the business was fundamentally about creating temporary third spaces — micro-gatherings that transform parking lots, office parks, and neighborhood streets into destinations where food is the catalyst for connection, storytelling, and cultural exchange. What would the business model look like if revenue came from experiences, partnerships, and community-building rather than transactional food sales alone? How might this change conversations with city officials about the truck's "public value"?

2. **What would this business look like if it were designed from the ground up to NEVER become a traditional restaurant chain, even while achieving the founder's economic and creative goals?** Instead of the conventional trajectory (truck → fleet → brick-and-mortar), imagine entirely new organizational forms: a networked federation of independent owner-operators sharing a unified brand and backend; a guild-style collective that trains and launches new food entrepreneurs under a shared umbrella; a platform business that provides infrastructure, financing, and market access to culinary creators while remaining fundamentally decentralized. What if "scaling" meant multiplying access to opportunity rather than multiplying outlets?

3. **What if we designed the business to be antifragile — becoming stronger through volatility, regulatory changes, and competitive pressure — rather than merely resilient to them?** Instead of viewing pending city ordinances, competition from similar fusion concepts, or seasonal fluctuations as threats to manage, imagine a business architecture that actually *feeds* on disruption: a modular model that can rapidly reconfigure based on regulatory conditions; a guerrilla logistics network that can pivot operations overnight; a community-political power base that gives the business influence over the very regulations that constrain it. How could constraints become the engine of innovation rather than brakes on growth?

### Analogist (Append)

1. **Migratory patterns and resource distribution** How do migratory bird species optimize their routes and timing to maximize food availability while minimizing competition, and what patterns from their adaptive movement strategies could inform a food truck's approach to location selection, seasonal shifts, and avoiding direct competition with similar trucks in the same geographic zones?

2. **Craft brewery expansion playbook** The craft brewing industry went through a similar phase where successful local breweries navigated the transition from single taproom to regional distribution, with many failing by expanding too quickly into brick-and-mortar before establishing sustainable economics—what specific expansion mistakes and successful patterns from that industry's growth cycle should inform your 3-year transition plan from truck to potential storefront?

3. **Hub-and-spoke vs. distributed network models** Logistics companies like FedEx explicitly chose hub-and-spoke systems over point-to-point delivery for efficiency advantages—does your planned expansion (truck → catering → potential fleet → brick-and-mortar) map better to a hub-and-spoke model with a central kitchen or a distributed model, and what are the operational economics and break-even points where each architecture becomes superior?
