# Brainstorming Questions: Mobile App Simplification

---

## Topic Cluster 01: Core Value & Job-to-be-Done

1. **Defining "core value" in practice**: What does "core value" actually mean for this product — the features most people use, the features that create the most passionate engagement, or the underlying job users are hiring the app to do when they download it? How do we reconcile stated user preferences with revealed behaviors, and what if our true differentiator lies in an adjacent space we've accidentally stumbled into rather than in the crowded fitness-app category itself? [Questioner, Analyst, Audience Advocate, Devil's Advocate]
2. **Risk that no unified core exists**: What if user retention is low not because the app is unfocused but because no single feature is good enough to compete with dedicated alternatives — i.e., the real problem is execution quality across all features, not scope? How do we stress-test the assumption that a simplified product built from existing parts would compel daily use, rather than leaving us with nothing compelling? [Devil's Advocate, Questioner]
3. **Peak user moments as a design compass** (AI orphan register): Tell us about a moment when a user genuinely loved this app — a session that went beyond 47 seconds, or a time someone told a friend about it. What exactly were they doing, what problem did the app solve so well that they felt grateful, and how might we design the entire product to create more of those specific moments? [Appreciative Inquirer]
4. **The "one thing" aspiration** (AI orphan register): If this app became known for doing one thing exceptionally well — the thing that makes users think "I can't live without this" — what would that be, and how would that single focus shape onboarding, daily use, and word-of-mouth? What would users accomplish or feel with this app that they couldn't elsewhere? [Appreciative Inquirer, Questioner]

## Topic Cluster 02: Feature Interdependencies & Cascade Risk

1. **Keystone features and self-reinforcing loops**: Before cutting any feature, what is the complete causal chain showing how its removal affects other features — both obvious dependencies (data sharing, navigation) and subtle reinforcing patterns (on-ramps that introduce users to other features, data powering recommendations)? Which features are "keystone features" whose removal would cause disproportionate system collapse, and which create a self-reinforcing engagement loop versus being merely additive? [Systems Thinker, Analyst, Questioner]
2. **Cascade dynamics and death-spiral thresholds**: When passionate niche users leave after their features are cut, what second- and third-order consequences emerge — do they take their friends, user-generated content, and community moderation with them? At what threshold does feature-cutting trigger a death spiral, and how do we measure compounding churn that could far exceed current attrition when multiple features are cut simultaneously? [Systems Thinker, Devil's Advocate, Analyst]
3. **Technical-debt balancing loop**: Removing deeply integrated features could create more bugs and complexity than maintaining them, especially if 15 features share code, data models, or infrastructure. What feedback loops does "surgical extraction" (expensive now, cleaner long-term) versus "abandon in place" (cheap now, accumulating cruft) create, and how do we assess whether the engineering cost of removal might consume the 8-month runway without delivering a working product? [Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker, Analyst]

## Topic Cluster 03: Retention Diagnostics & Behavioral Signals

1. **Decomposing the 8% retention and 2% DAU/MAU**: What does a cohort-level behavioral-sequence analysis reveal about the 47-second session — are users failing to find what they need, completing tasks too quickly (indicating low depth), or bouncing due to decision paralysis, and where are the steepest drop-off points in the onboarding-to-habit-formation pipeline? How does the answer vary by user segment? [Analyst, Audience Advocate]
2. **Popularity vs. value among churning users**: The 8% retention rate means usage data is potentially misleading — features used early by churning users might appear "popular" while actually being on-ramps to nowhere. How do we distinguish features that drive retention from features that merely attract initial engagement before users quit, and how do we trace the actual journeys of the 8% who stayed beyond 30 days? [Devil's Advocate, Audience Advocate, Analyst]
3. **Unarticulated needs and daily-return triggers**: What fundamental needs are users working around or compensating for because they've normalized "all fitness apps are like this," and what micro-metrics in the first week would signal users are finding the core value proposition and returning daily — rather than relying on lagging metrics (downloads, 30-day retention) designed for a bloated engagement-maximizing app? [Audience Advocate, Analyst, Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 04: Simplification Approach & Validation

1. **"Overwhelming" — diagnosis before prescription**: When users say the app is "overwhelming," are they referring to feature count, cognitive load, decision paralysis, poor information architecture, or discoverability — and how would we know the difference without asking? How can we validate that simplification will actually improve retention before irreversibly removing features, given the risk that the true issue is UX design rather than feature count? [Audience Advocate, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]
2. **Incremental vs. clean relaunch**: How should we structure the decision between incremental simplification (remove one feature per month, measure impact) versus clean relaunch, given app stores are littered with "2.0" relaunches that failed because users had already moved on? What is the analytical framework for comparing risks of alienating existing users through abrupt change against opportunity costs of gradualism, and what evidence suggests our base will return? [Analyst, Questioner, Devil's Advocate]
3. **Testing removal before committing**: Could we ship a version with one feature temporarily disabled and see who complains — or who doesn't notice? What low-risk experiments this month could test which features truly represent core value, engaging users as co-investigators rather than subjects? [Questioner, Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 05: User Research & Decision Rigor

1. **Self-selection bias in user research**: Users who respond to surveys or interviews are likely the most engaged minority, so we risk overoptimizing for power users while ignoring the 92% who leave. What research methods help us understand why the majority abandon the app without alienating the users who still care, and how do we interview the 8% who stayed about what they'd stop using if we removed it? [Devil's Advocate, Questioner]
2. **False consensus and one-person "research"**: A 6-engineer team has 6 opinions about "core," each unconsciously championing features they personally built, while a single PM decides which user feedback matters. What decision framework and guardrails objectively identify core value without letting ownership bias, team politics, or one person's limited perspective drive the outcome? [Devil's Advocate]
3. **Power dynamics in choosing what matters**: What power dynamics exist when a product team decides which features matter, and how can we involve users (surveys, interviews, beta testing, advisory boards) in determining the app's core value proposition rather than making top-down assumptions — designing simplification *with* users rather than *for* them? [Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 06: Passionate Minorities, Migration & User Compensation

1. **Valuing small-but-passionate user bases**: For features with small but passionate user bases (e.g., <5% of users but 80% engagement within that segment), what is the analytical framework for determining whether they represent a defensible niche worth preserving, a distraction, or an opportunity to spin off? How do we identify these users before we cut their features, so we don't drive away our most vocal advocates? [Analyst, Questioner]
2. **Migration with grace**: Considering passionate users for certain features, how might we honor their commitment while simplifying — what would a graceful transition look like that acknowledges loyalty while guiding users toward the core value, and what communication, timeline, and support would turn potential churn into deeper engagement? [Appreciative Inquirer, Audience Advocate]
3. **Compensatory behaviors after removal**: How will existing users adapt when features are removed, and what unintended consequences emerge — if the social feed is cut, do users flood the challenge platform with social posts? If the marketplace is eliminated, do they clutter community forums with transaction requests? What compensatory behaviors should we anticipate, and how might they undermine the simplification we're attempting? [Systems Thinker, Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 07: Runway, Team Economics & Metric Realignment

1. **ROI of refactoring and the realistic timeline**: With 8 months of runway and a team maintaining 15 half-built features, what is the ROI of refactoring — how much engineering capacity would be freed by cutting to 3-4 features, what is the break-even point in development velocity on remaining features, and what does a realistic timeline look like with buffer for hidden dependencies, database migrations, and unknown unknowns? At what point do we pivot to incremental simplification if feature removal uncovers more than anticipated? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate]
2. **Team capacity-reinforcement dynamics** (balancing loop): When you eliminate features, do you redeploy engineers to deepen remaining features (potentially creating new bloat) or reduce headcount (undermining iteration capacity)? What system are you creating with team-structure decisions, and how does that system either reinforce simplicity or naturally drift back toward complexity as a balancing loop over time? [Systems Thinker]
3. **New metric system for a focused utility**: Current metrics (30-day retention, DAU/MAU, session length) were designed for engagement-maximizing; a focused utility that solves a problem quickly might have shorter sessions but higher long-term retention. What leading-indicator metric system creates the right feedback loops for a simplified product, and what are the risks of measuring the new product with old rulers? [Systems Thinker, Analyst]

## Topic Cluster 08: User Experience, Access & Communication

1. **The first 30 seconds and the intent-vs-app-behavior friction**: What does the first 30 seconds feel like for a new user who downloaded expecting a fitness tracker but encounters 15 features and 6 tabs plus a hamburger menu? When users open the app for a specific purpose (like tracking a workout) but get distracted by notifications, social feeds, and marketplace suggestions, how often do they successfully complete the task they came for, and how do notifications affect their sense of agency rather than respecting their attention? [Audience Advocate]
2. **Accessibility, digital literacy, and underserved archetypes**: How does the current navigation differ for users with varying digital literacy, visual impairments, or cognitive processing differences, and which user archetypes (the social-challenge-lover, the privacy-focused workout tracker, the time-constrained burnout) are served or abandoned by simplification to 3-4 features? Does "do one thing well" actually make the app more accessible for people dealing with stress or competing priorities, or does it leave some segments behind? [Audience Advocate]
3. **Communication that honors investment**: How will users who built routines around specific features experience their removal, and what communication approaches (transparency, timeline, support, framing as "focusing on what matters most" rather than "taking things away") preserve trust without making users feel abandoned or betrayed? How do we tell the story of simplification as innovation rather than retreat? [Audience Advocate, Questioner, Storyteller]
4. **Communication-fragmentation feedback loop** (leverage points): The simplification announcement creates its own system — how early adopters react, how sentiment spreads through reviews and social media, how that sentiment influences fence-sitters, and how resulting retention numbers shape future product decisions. What are the leverage points in this communication system where small messaging changes could dramatically shift user perception? [Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 09: Team Morale & Strengths Amplification

1. **Psychological impact of "killing darlings"**: If engineers have spent months or years building features now being deleted, how will they stay motivated through the relaunch — especially if "core" features require working on code they didn't originally write and may not feel ownership of? What mechanisms rebuild psychological investment, and how do we help engineers and designers feel proud of simplification rather than feeling like they "failed"? [Devil's Advocate, Questioner, Appreciative Inquirer]
2. **Team's brightest moments and team-aligned vision** (AI orphan register): Think about a time when this team of six engineers, one designer, and one PM felt most energized, proud, or effective — perhaps shipping something meaningful or seeing user feedback validate their work. What conditions made that moment possible, what unique strengths does each team member bring that a leaner environment could amplify, and what simplified product focus would align with what they're already good at — making simplification feel like liberation rather than loss? [Appreciative Inquirer]
3. **Amplifying what works and existing assets** (AI orphan register): Based on features that already show promise and resources already in place (technical infrastructure, user insights, team skills, community relationships, learnings from the current feature set), what structures, practices, or product principles would reliably amplify those strengths — making the MVP reset feel like evolution rather than restart? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Appended Questions (Perspective, Provocation, and Distinctive-Register Personas)

### Provocateur (Append)

1. **What if you kept exactly ONE feature and made it absurdly worse?** Imagine you stripped the app down to just workout tracking, but then removed all logging, history, progress visualization, and data—leaving only the immediate act of pressing a button that says "I worked out" before the app closes itself. What would this useless button reveal about what users actually crave from the tracking experience? *The real insight: is the value in the data or in the ritual of acknowledgment?*
2. **What if your 180K downloads were 180K different people who never opened the app twice?** Imagine the retention disaster is actually a feature—your app is designed for single-use transformation, like a ceremonial rite of passage after which users are "graduated" and should never return. How would you redesign the onboarding experience to complete someone's entire fitness journey in 47 seconds? *The real insight: what if the lifetime value model is wrong for this problem and the real opportunity is in perfect, self-contained moments?*
3. **What if the 2% who actually use the app daily are the problem, not the solution?** Imagine your power users are actually tumors—pathological attachments to features that represent the app's worst impulses, and their retention is preventing the product from evolving into something healthy for normal humans. What would happen if you actively tried to drive away your most engaged users? *The real insight: what if your retention metrics are optimized for a tiny minority of freak users while everyone else is correctly recognizing that the product doesn't serve them?*

### Storyteller (Append)

1. **What would the "first five minutes" experience look like for a returning user who opens the simplified app for the first time, and what emotional state should we be designing toward?** Imagine it's Tuesday morning and Sarah, who previously faced 15 features and 6 navigation tabs, opens the new version. Walk through what she sees, what she feels, and what she can accomplish in those critical first minutes. How do we ensure the transition from confusion to clarity happens within a single session? What does "success" look like in that first interaction—should she feel relief, excitement, curiosity, or something else entirely?
2. **How might the engineering team's daily work life transform when they shift from maintaining 15 half-built features to perfecting 3-4 world-class experiences, and what "micro-moments" of professional satisfaction will replace the current burnout cycle?** Walk us through the standup meeting six months from now. What language are engineers using to describe their work? How do conversations about quality, testing, and user feedback change when they're not spread thin across a feature graveyard? What moments of pride—seeing a user review, watching usage metrics, shipping a polished experience—replace the current reality of constant bug fixes and technical debt management? How does the team's relationship with the codebase itself evolve when they're not afraid to touch it?
3. **How might the press release, app store description, and launch announcement tell a story that frames simplification as innovation rather than retreat, and what metaphors or analogies would help users and investors see this as a bold step forward?** Consider the difference between "we're removing features because we couldn't make them work" versus "we've discovered what users actually need, and we're cutting away everything else." What story positions this as the company's bravest decision rather than its most desperate? How do we communicate to new users that this simplified version isn't a downgrade, but rather the first version of the product that actually works? What cultural references or parallels—from other companies, from product design history, from everyday life—could make this pivot feel obvious and inevitable in retrospect?

### Visionary (Append)

1. **[What if fitness wasn't something you track, but something that emerges naturally through a fundamentally different relationship with your body?]** Instead of asking which features to keep or cut, what if we completely reimagined the category: What would a fitness product look like if it were designed around joy, emergence, and organic behavior change rather than metrics, tracking, and discipline? What if the core value isn't "helping people work out" but something entirely different that doesn't have a current analog in the fitness app space?
2. **[What if user retention isn't about features at all, but about identity transformation — and what if we designed for that psychological shift instead of for engagement?]** Consider that the 2% DAU/MAU ratio might not be a feature problem but an identity problem: users don't see themselves as "people who use this app." What if we completely rethought onboarding, not as a tutorial, but as an identity-forming ritual that fundamentally reshapes how users view themselves? What would an app look like if its primary function was identity construction rather than fitness tracking?
3. **[What if the 47-second average session isn't a problem to be solved, but the actual product — and what if we leaned into that rather than trying to extend engagement?]** What would it mean to embrace the micro-session pattern and design something that delivers maximum value in under a minute? Could this become a fundamentally new product category — the "instant fitness intervention" that exists in the interstitial moments of daily life? What if the app's role isn't to occupy attention but to punctuate it with brief, transformative experiences?

### Connector (Append)

1. **Feature pruning as ecosystem succession** The challenge of cutting features mirrors ecological succession, where crowded ecosystems self-thin to let dominant species thrive. In forest ecology, "self-thinning" creates space for the strongest organisms to flourish. What if we viewed the 15 features as competing species in an ecosystem — how would we identify which features are the "keystone species" whose presence enables the entire ecosystem to function, and which are the invasive species crowding out the core value?
2. **UI complexity as information entropy** Information theory uses entropy to measure uncertainty and complexity in communication systems. The current app's 6 navigation tabs plus hamburger menu represents high interface entropy — users must process too much information before finding value. Claude Shannon's work showed that reducing noise increases signal transmission. If we treated every UI element as adding "noise" to the user's cognitive load, what entropy-reduction principles from information theory could guide which elements are signal (essential) versus noise (eliminable)?
3. **Surgical triage vs. architectural restoration** There are two different structural parallels for feature removal: surgical triage (emergency room — save what can be saved, let go of what's too far gone) and architectural restoration (preserve historical integrity while modernizing function). Triage is fast but destructive; restoration is slow but honors the original vision. The 8-month runway creates urgency, but the "jack of all trades" problem suggests vision clarity is needed. Which metaphor better serves this situation — and how does that choice change the feature selection criteria?
