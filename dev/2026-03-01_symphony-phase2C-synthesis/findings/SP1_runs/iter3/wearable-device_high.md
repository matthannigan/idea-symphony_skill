# Brainstorming Questions: On-Device ML Wearable Health Device

---

## Topic Cluster 01: Foundational Framing and First Principles

1. **Why a wearable at all, and is anomaly detection the right primitive?**: Setting aside the wrist form-factor assumption and the "deviation from personal baseline" framing, what IS this product fundamentally — is the core problem continuous private health monitoring, pattern recognition for known pathological signatures, or something else entirely? What are the necessary and sufficient conditions such that a bedside device, smart-home integration, or smartphone could achieve the same goals, and how does reframing the ML primitive (population-level signatures vs. personal anomaly) change the architecture? [First Principles Thinker, Questioner]

2. **What does on-device ML actually solve, stripped of its justifications?**: If privacy and latency can be achieved through cryptographic techniques, secure enclaves, or differential privacy, what is the first-order benefit of on-device inference — personalization, connectivity independence, or the visceral feeling of containment? How would system design diverge if we were explicit about which is load-bearing, and how does the privacy requirement decompose (raw biometric data never leaving vs. preventing any PII health inference from being transmitted)? [First Principles Thinker, Empath]

3. **Irreducible minimum sensor set vs. multi-modal fusion**: For the specific conditions of interest (cardiac arrhythmias, sleep apnea), what is the minimum information-theoretic signal required — could a single carefully-chosen sensor achieve 80% of the clinical value, and what analytical framework decides when the marginal benefit of adding PPG, accelerometer, temperature, or microphone fails to justify the computational, calibration, and privacy costs (early vs. late vs. hybrid fusion with attention gating)? [First Principles Thinker, Analyst, Technical Expert, Questioner]

4. **Inverting the power-budget paradigm**: Rather than treating 7-day battery as a target to fit features into, what if we started with "we have X milliwatts continuous — what is the maximum clinical value we can deliver?" What is the first-principles argument for 7 days specifically (vs. nightly or weekly charging rhythms), and how does this inversion reshape sensing frequency, NPU utilization, and the systematic power allocation across PPG driver, accelerometer, mic, BLE, NPU vs. MCU, and flash writes? [First Principles Thinker, Analyst, Technical Expert]

## Topic Cluster 02: Visioning and Strengths-Based Foundations

5. **Peak moments of health awareness as design inspiration**: Tell me about a time when someone had a moment of genuine insight about their health that changed how they lived — what conditions made that insight possible, how did it feel, and how might the device be designed to create more such moments routinely rather than merely delivering alerts? [Appreciative Inquirer]

6. **Technologies that become invisible partners**: What device or tool have you used that became so seamlessly helpful it felt like a natural extension of your awareness — what subtle design choices created that relationship, and how might our device cultivate that quality of embodied, trusted presence on the wrist? [Appreciative Inquirer]

7. **Three-year user testimonial and the smallest meaningful experiment**: Imagine users three years from now describing not features but how the device changed their relationship to their body — what are they saying, what moments do they recount with gratitude, and what is the smallest human-reaction prototype we could run this month (not a technical feasibility test) that would give genuine insight into whether we are building something people will care about? [Appreciative Inquirer]

8. **Existing assets and cross-functional superpowers the 12-engineer team already has**: Looking at the team's firmware / ML / hardware / mobile distribution, the timeframe, and the technical approach, what strengths are we taking for granted — what advantages do competitors envy, and what meeting structures, communication patterns, or decision rituals would make this team's diversity a daily superpower rather than a coordination challenge? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 03: Privacy Architecture as Philosophy and Experience

9. **Privacy as a feeling, not just a feature — designed as user experience**: Beyond HIPAA legal checkboxes and encryption guarantees, what is the visceral difference between knowing your data stays on-device versus being told it is "secure"? When have you seen a product make privacy feel like a benefit rather than a burden, and how do we design interface, communication, and transparency patterns so users can viscerally feel their data is safe from day one? [Empath, Appreciative Inquirer, Devil's Advocate]

10. **Re-identification risk and the data-minimization paradox**: Even with raw biometric data never leaving the device, could the combination of processed alerts, aggregated summaries, and usage patterns create a unique fingerprint enabling cross-platform identification — particularly given the rare, high-impact, distinctive temporal patterns of cardiac arrhythmia events? How do we reconcile the tension between needing sufficient training data for accuracy and the data-minimization principle, and what specific technical and organizational measures ensure aggregated summaries cannot be reverse-engineered? [Questioner, Lawyer]

11. **HIPAA PHI boundary and the on-device/sync architecture paradox**: Your architecture creates ambiguity about what constitutes PHI under HIPAA when raw data stays on-device but processed alerts sync to the app. How specifically do we architect the data pipeline to clearly delineate PHI from de-identified data, what legal review process validates this before clinical partnerships, and what systematic approach determines what constitutes "raw" versus "processed" at each pipeline stage? [Devil's Advocate, Analyst, Lawyer]

12. **Compelled disclosure, third-party ecosystem, and data subject rights without cloud storage**: If law enforcement demands access under legal compulsion, what should remain technically inaccessible versus retrievable? How do we design the companion app APIs to prevent third-party integrations (health platforms, insurers) from indirectly reconstructing the sensitive data we are committed to protecting, and what technical mechanisms allow GDPR/CCPA access, deletion, and portability rights when data exists only on hardware the user possesses? [Questioner, Lawyer]

13. **State-level consumer health privacy laws and jurisdictional portability**: HIPAA is increasingly a floor rather than ceiling — California's CMIA, Washington's My Health My Data Act, and proliferating international data-residency/localization laws create a patchwork of stricter requirements. Which state laws should you design for as baseline given nationwide distribution, and how does our on-device-only approach position us for international expansion versus cloud-dependent competitors? [Lawyer, Futurist]

## Topic Cluster 04: Privacy-Performance Lock-In and Systems Dynamics

14. **Privacy-performance delayed feedback as a Success-to-the-Successful lock-in**: Processing everything on-device creates a Success-to-the-Successful pattern where early privacy commitments lock you out of later capabilities that cloud-first competitors can easily add, yet cloud dependence creates its own lock-in. How do we architect the pipeline to keep future options open — on-device first but with clean paths to hybrid approaches if clinical utility demands it — and where is the personalization boundary we should traverse incrementally as trust develops? [Systems Thinker, Questioner]

## Topic Cluster 05: Alert Design, Trust, and Psychological Dynamics

15. **Alert-fatigue calibration and false-positive burden**: If the ML model generates frequent false positives, users in the 40+ cardiac-conscious demographic may experience unnecessary stress, ER visits, and medical-system strain — and as trust erodes, they disable notifications or stop wearing the device, eliminating protective benefit. How do we design the alert-threshold system to navigate the "better safe than sorry" vs. "boy who cried wolf" tradeoff, and how do we quantify the costs of missed detections versus alert fatigue across stakeholders (users wanting peace of mind, clinicians wanting diagnostic utility, company wanting to avoid liability)? [Devil's Advocate, Analyst, Systems Thinker, Questioner]

16. **Alert framing Shifting-the-Burden dynamic**: When a consumer wellness device provides health alerts, users naturally interpret them as medical guidance — creating liability when the device misses something, yet over-disclaiming ("consult a doctor") causes users to ignore warnings. This is a classic Shifting-the-Burden archetype where the device creates a dependency such that users rely on alerts rather than developing body awareness or clinical relationships; the short-term convenience erodes long-term capacity for informed health decision-making. How do we design the alert UX and user education to support rather than replace clinical relationships, and what duty-to-warn vs. user-responsibility design patterns (require acknowledgment, log dismissals) create an evidentiary record while preserving user autonomy? [Systems Thinker, Lawyer, Devil's Advocate]

17. **Health anxiety reinforcement loop and embodied awareness**: Continuous monitoring creates a self-amplifying pattern where more data visibility leads to more attention to minor fluctuations, leading to more anxiety and more checking — hypervigilance masquerading as empowerment. What design elements break this compounding dynamic (normalizing variation, smoothing displays, framing uncertainty), and what if we centered the experience on cultivating embodied somatic literacy rather than notification delivery? [Systems Thinker, Empath, Visionary]

18. **False alarms, emotional whiplash, and trust recovery**: What is the emotional toll of a 3 AM false positive — the adrenaline spike, the scramble, the lingering "it was nothing" — and when the device inevitably misses something after a user has trusted it, what happens in the days and weeks after that trust-breaking event? What design patterns and agency-preserving transparency (visibility into what triggered an alert, ability to flag false positives) would rebuild trust when the device gets it wrong and preserve user agency when they cannot verify the underlying data? [Empath, Audience Advocate, Storyteller-adjacent framing, Devil's Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 06: Model Accuracy, Drift, and Validation

19. **Model drift, update cadence, and synchronous deployment**: On-device ML will face drift from sensor degradation, user physiology changes, and population-level distribution shifts — and firmware updates require user action, creating uneven deployment where multiple model versions exist simultaneously, making it harder to attribute detection-quality changes to improvements vs. population changes. How do we detect when model performance degrades, what monitoring infrastructure (phased rollouts, embedded model versioning in alert data, A/B infrastructure within firmware constraints) maintains clear signal, and how do we preserve personalized adaptations across updates? [Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker, Questioner]

20. **On-device personalization and concept drift in baselines**: Given that a user's "normal" evolves over months due to aging, lifestyle changes, or medical interventions, how does the model distinguish benign baseline shifts from early warning signs? What technical approaches (online learning with tiny updates, periodic recalibration, user-supplied ground-truth labels) are viable within 128KB SRAM constraints, and what is the feasibility window for federated learning given heterogeneity, hardware constraints, and communication costs? [Questioner, Technical Expert, Futurist]

21. **Clinical validation self-selection bias as Fixes-that-Fail**: Users motivated to participate in clinical studies are already symptomatic or anxious — not representative of mainstream users. This creates a potential Fixes-that-Fail pattern: you optimize for the high-risk study population, performance differs in the wild, and you face unexpected false positive/negative rates and liability exposure. How do you design validation and ML training to account for this shift (stratifying studies, weighting training data, explicitly modeling concerned vs. casual users), and without labeled clinical data for training, how do we establish that our detection catches the conditions we claim rather than correlated noise? [Systems Thinker, Questioner, Analyst]

22. **False positive/false negative calibration protocol under liability constraints**: For health alerts with liability implications, what systematic approach determines acceptable threshold balances? What specific accuracy thresholds are you targeting for different event types (arrhythmia vs. sleep apnea), what evidence establishes these thresholds, and what product positioning, user-agreement language, and clinical validation strategy mitigate wrongful-death or failure-to-warn claims when targeting users with family history of cardiac events? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Lawyer, Technical Expert]

## Topic Cluster 07: Hardware, Power, and Sensor Architecture

23. **7-day battery reality: power budget decomposition, subsystem contingencies, and graceful degradation**: Seven-day battery with continuous multi-sensor sensing, on-device ML, display, and Bluetooth is aggressive for a wrist form factor. What is the defensible sub-5mW average allocation across sensing (PPG, accelerometer, mic, temperature), compute (NPU vs. MCU), storage (flash writes), and communication (BLE advertising vs. connection events)? What contingency plans exist if NPU or sensors consume more than spec, and given that battery capacity degrades 15-25% over 2-3 years (while marketing promised 7 days), what graceful-degradation modes (adaptive sampling, model throttling) preserve core detection capability rather than creating sudden capability loss? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Technical Expert, Systems Thinker, Futurist]

24. **NPU architecture, quantization, and vendor lock-in**: What NPU features (INT8 acceleration, sparse compute, zero-point quantization) should drive model architecture, and how do we maximize utilization while maintaining fallback paths? What percentage of theoretical capacity can we realistically sustain given thermal constraints? Designing for a specific NPU creates vendor dependency — what happens if the vendor discontinues the chip or raises prices, and is the NPU assumption even solving the right problem (could carefully-optimized traditional ML or hand-crafted signal processing achieve equivalent clinical utility at orders-of-magnitude lower power)? [Technical Expert, Devil's Advocate, First Principles Thinker, Questioner]

25. **Sensor fusion fragility, signal quality, and graceful single-sensor fallback**: Your anomaly detection combines PPG, accelerometer, temperature, and microphone — but if PPG contact becomes unreliable (poor wrist fit, skin tone, tattoos), does the model over-weight remaining sensors producing false confidence, or detect reduced data quality and flag uncertainty? What real-time signal-quality assessment (motion-artifact detection, PPG perfusion validation, sensor-fault detection) gates inference inputs, and what training approaches (artificial sensor dropout, per-sensor confidence modeling, single-sensor fallback modes) ensure individual sensor failures do not cascade catastrophically? [Systems Thinker, Questioner, Technical Expert]

26. **Deterministic inference latency and real-time constraints**: For cardiac event detection requiring <5 second response, what is the acceptable latency window, at what point does further latency optimization degrade detection accuracy, and how do we architect the system to guarantee worst-case latency even during background operations (firmware OTA, sync, log compression)? [Analyst, Technical Expert]

27. **Fault tolerance, manufacturing variability, and multi-year reliability**: What technical mechanisms ensure reliability over multi-year lifetimes — watchdog recovery from NPU hangs, CRC validation of model weights in flash, detection of sensor drift requiring recalibration, safe-mode behavior when inference produces statistically impossible outputs? Given hardware tolerances and calibration variations across thousands of units, how do we ensure consistent model performance without per-device calibration procedures? [Technical Expert, Questioner]

28. **Privacy-preserving telemetry for debugging ML failures**: For debugging and model improvement without violating HIPAA, what technical approaches (minimally hashed diagnostic payloads, opt-in crisis-dump telemetry, differential noise injection for aggregate metrics) allow gathering actionable data while preserving privacy, and how do opt-out flows remain technically useful? [Technical Expert]

## Topic Cluster 08: Regulatory Strategy and Wellness-to-Clinical Path

29. **Regulatory path-dependency: day-one FDA vs. wellness-first trajectory**: Launching as wellness creates a specific trajectory — user expectations and data practices established in an unregulated space, then retrofitted for clinical evidence. Consumer-grade data collection does not meet clinical requirements, yet pursuing FDA from day one delays time-to-market and over-engineers initial product. What framework guides this choice, how do we quantify opportunity costs of delayed entry vs. competitive advantage and clinical partnerships, and what is the minimal architectural and data-collection decision set we must make now to design "regulatory optionality" into the system rather than choosing one path permanently? [Analyst, Systems Thinker]

30. **Wellness-to-clinical boundary, premature categorization, and the Wellness-to-Clinical Gap**: Launching as consumer wellness while planning FDA 510(k) creates significant risk — users may treat wellness alerts as medical diagnoses regardless of disclaimers. Where specifically in marketing, UI, and documentation could language cross from wellness into "diagnosis" or "treatment" claims triggering medical-device classification? What product, marketing, and legal safeguards prevent reliance on unvalidated clinical claims, and how does the dual-path strategy affect your 18-month runway given that FDA clearance typically requires additional clinical studies? [Devil's Advocate, Lawyer]

31. **Clinical evidence generation and 510(k) preparatory activities**: What structured breakdown of evidence generation is needed for both consumer wellness positioning and future 510(k) submission? At what stage does analytical validation of model performance transition to requiring clinical validation studies, and what specific preparatory activities (quality system records, design history files, software validation protocols, QMS elements proportionate to a 12-person startup) should the team document prospectively that would accelerate an eventual submission versus being reconstructed retrospectively? [Analyst, Lawyer]

32. **Regulatory convergence window, clinical integration, and payer coverage trajectory**: The FDA's digital-health framework is expanding rapidly around SaMD and AI/ML algorithms; healthcare systems are accelerating RPM and CCM reimbursement adoption; and insurance coverage for preventive cardiovascular monitoring is expanding. How should we architect today to make future 510(k) submission straightforward without over-constraining the wellness launch, what data export capabilities and integration hooks position us for hospital partnerships within 2-3 years, and what evidence-generation investments now position us for future coverage decisions? [Futurist, Analyst]

33. **Regulatory success as collaborative enabler, not obstacle**: Imagine the FDA 510(k) process not as a hurdle but as a collaboration that makes the device demonstrably more valuable to users — what would that process look like, and how might clinical partnerships enhance rather than restrict what we deliver to consumers? [Appreciative Inquirer]

34. **BAA structure, insurance coverage, and hybrid liability allocation**: If a hospital system uses your device for post-discharge remote monitoring, what BAA provisions govern data flow when raw data stays on-device but processed alerts trigger clinical workflows — who is covered entity versus business associate, and where do HIPAA's access/transmission controls apply? As a hardware startup with planned clinical partnerships, what product-liability, cyber-liability, and professional-liability/E&O coverage and limits should you carry, and how do policy exclusions for "medical devices" affect coverage during your pre-clearance wellness phase? [Lawyer]

## Topic Cluster 09: Equity, Accessibility, and Population Fit

35. **Training-data distribution mismatch and silent workarounds**: ML models trained on skewed demographics may perform poorly for users whose skin tone, wrist size, HRV, or movement patterns are underrepresented — how might these users silently work around inaccurate readings without ever reporting the problem, and how do we design to detect and close this feedback gap? [Audience Advocate]

36. **Accessibility barriers for users with disabilities**: For users with tremors, limited dexterity, visual impairments, or sensory-processing differences, what frustrations emerge in daily wear, charging, alert acknowledgement, and companion-app interaction? How might our design inadvertently assume "ideal" users with full mobility, vision, and fine motor control? [Audience Advocate]

37. **Battery-life interaction with non-standard routines**: How does the 7-day battery target interact with users who travel frequently, have unreliable charging access, work long shifts, or have cognitive differences making regular charging routines challenging — and what graceful-degradation patterns preserve dignity and utility when batteries run low? [Audience Advocate]

38. **Wellness vs. clinical interpretation across populations**: Different populations may not distinguish between consumer wellness guidance and medical advice — especially when language around cardiac arrhythmias and sleep apnea sounds clinical. What legal, ethical, and experience-design considerations emerge when users act on alerts as if they were medical diagnoses, and how does this interact with health literacy across demographics? [Audience Advocate]

39. **Unarticulated privacy needs beyond HIPAA**: From the community's perspective, even if raw data never leaves the device, users have concerns about who in their household sees alerts, what happens during repair/replacement, and whether employers could infer health status from usage patterns — what unarticulated privacy needs exist beyond legal compliance? [Audience Advocate]

40. **Power dynamics, bodily autonomy, and challenging the device**: What power dynamics exist when users receive alerts they cannot verify or challenge — being told "your heart rhythm was abnormal" without access to the underlying data or ability to flag it as a false positive — and how does this affect bodily autonomy and the user's relationship with their own health signals? [Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 12: Operational Edge Cases and Usage Gaps

52. **Wrist compliance, charging gaps, and alert-actionability loop closure**: Wrist-worn devices face compliance challenges — users remove them for sleep discomfort, exercise, water, or forget — and 7-day battery still creates weekly charging windows where monitoring drops, potentially missing events. What design features maximize continuous wear, how do we handle charging-time gaps (hot-swappable batteries, alternative charging, user education), how do we handle technical edge cases (sensor dislodgement during sleep, battery depletion causing intermittent sensing, firmware mid-update, thermal throttling), and how do we close the loop between detection and care so alerts lead to meaningful health actions rather than anxiety (partnerships, integrations with treatment pathways)? [Devil's Advocate, Technical Expert]

53. **Consumer privacy expectations and alert-fatigue retention dynamics**: Public concern about health-data commercialization is growing, with increasing scrutiny of data brokerage and secondary use — how do we communicate and operationalize "data minimization" as a core value proposition rather than a technical constraint? User research across health and safety apps shows declining engagement with notification-based interventions over time, with measurable alert fatigue — how do we design our alert strategy and communication to sustain engagement without contributing to notification burnout? [Futurist]

54. **Regulatory sandbox experimentation and off-label use implications**: If users or clinicians begin relying on the device for clinical purposes beyond its wellness scope (monitoring known conditions rather than screening), how do architecture and messaging encourage or discourage this evolution? What technical architecture allows experimenting with more advanced features in limited "research cohorts" while maintaining clear separation from the general consumer product for regulatory purposes? [Questioner]

55. **Companion app bidirectional influence loop**: The app receives aggregated summaries and alerts while users input context (symptom logs, medication, activity). This creates a bidirectional influence: app-provided context improves detection, but device alerts shape what users choose to log — if users only log symptoms after alerts, the system never learns to predict events users did not already notice. How do you structure the app-device flow for useful context augmentation without reinforcing existing detection blind spots? [Systems Thinker]

## Appended Questions (Perspective, Provocation, and Distinctive-Register Personas)

### Provocateur (Append)

1. **What if the device was designed to actively ignore your health data?** Imagine a wellness wearable that deliberately obscures your metrics — showing only vague impressions, encouraging body attunement over quantification. What changes when the product is designed to make you *less* dependent on numbers, not more? *The real insight: what if our obsession with measurement is part of the problem, not the solution?*

2. **What if the wearable monitored everyone except the wearer?** Picture a device designed for "community health surveillance" — tracking patterns across households, workplaces, friend groups — but explicitly forbidden from giving individuals their own data. What if privacy was achieved by making individual data literally inaccessible to the person it came from? *The real insight: what if the most valuable health insights live between people, not within them?*

3. **What if HIPAA compliance required *more* data sharing, not less?** Turn the privacy framework on its head: imagine a regulatory regime where the *only* way to achieve medical-grade protection was to pool all data into an anonymized commons — with individual access deliberately blocked. What if "your data stays on device" is the *opposite* of true privacy? *The real insight: what if individual data privacy is impossible, and collective data stewardship is the only real protection?*

4. **What if FDA 510(k) clearance required the device to *fail* for some users?** Imagine a regulatory requirement that any approved health monitor must be contraindicated for specific populations — explicitly documented as ineffective or harmful for some groups. What if "works for everyone" was treated as medically suspicious? *The real insight: what if our universal design aspirations prevent us from acknowledging that no health solution works equally well across all bodies?*

5. **What if the business model charged you for *every detected anomaly*?** Invert the revenue model: the device is free, but each health alert costs money — creating a direct financial incentive for the device to be *less* sensitive. What happens when profit aligns with calm, not vigilance? *The real insight: what if our current model profits from anxiety, and we've never questioned whether that's the right alignment?*

6. **What if the ML model trained on *only* the wearer's data and then deleted itself?** Picture a device that learns your personal baseline, detects anomalies relative to *you alone*, then purges its training data — leaving no trace, no federated learning, no model updates. What if personalization meant total isolation, not continuous improvement? *The real insight: what if the most accurate health model is one that knows nothing about anyone except you — and then forgets what it knows?*

7. **What if the device's ML model was deliberately worse at detecting anomalies?** Consider a device that intentionally introduces randomness into its predictions — refusing to give you the comfort of certainty. What would it mean for a health device to embrace uncertainty rather than claim precision? *The real insight: what if false confidence in medical algorithms is more dangerous than acknowledged uncertainty?*

8. **What if the device required NO battery and died every 24 hours?** Imagine a wearable deliberately engineered for daily mortality — it exhausts its power, goes dark, and you must "bury" it and start fresh tomorrow. What would this forced daily reset change about how users relate to their health data? *The real insight: what if continuous monitoring creates unhealthy attachment, and periodic disconnection is a feature, not a bug?*

### Storyteller (Append)

1. **The middle-of-the-night moment** Imagine it's 3 AM and Marcus, a 52-year-old user with a family history of heart disease, wakes to a gentle vibration on his wrist. The device has detected something unusual. Walk through the emotional and practical journey of what happens next—from that first alert to his decision (or not) to seek medical care. How do we design this critical moment to balance appropriate urgency without inducing panic, especially when the device itself acknowledges uncertainty in its detection?

2. **The dinner table conversation** Picture a holiday gathering where the wearer notices everyone glancing at their wrist. "Is that the heart thing?" someone asks. How does this device become part of someone's identity and social narrative—for better and worse? What design choices influence whether wearers feel empowered, protected, or defined by their device, and how might this differ across personality types and health anxiety levels?

3. **From wellness device to medical tool** Imagine the journey of someone who bought this as a consumer wellness product, then two years later receives an FDA-cleared software update that transforms it into a clinical-grade diagnostic tool. How does their relationship with the device evolve? What expectations, responsibilities, and emotional weight shift when a fitness accessory becomes a medical instrument—and how do we prepare users for this transition?

4. **Living with the "watchful shadow"** Consider Elena, who wears this device daily for six months without a single alert. Then one Tuesday, it notifies her of a potential anomaly. How does the prolonged period of normal "silence" shape her reaction when an alert finally comes? How do we design the ongoing user experience to maintain engagement and trust during those quiet months, while preparing users for the possibility that one day, the device might change their life?

5. **Handing down a health narrative** Consider the moment when a parent who has worn this device for five years passes it—or the insights accumulated from it—to their adult child, who shares the same genetic risk factors. What would it mean to inherit not just a device, but a personalized health baseline and detection model shaped by a parent's physiology? How might this create new forms of intergenerational health awareness—and new ethical complexities?

6. **The "boy who cried wolf" problem in reverse** When the device misses something significant—or produces a false positive that leads to an unnecessary ER visit—how does that moment reshape the user's relationship with the technology? Walk through the recovery narrative: what happens in the days and weeks after a trust-breaking event, and what systems or interactions could rebuild (or deepen) that relationship?

7. **When the battery dies** Think about the moment after 7 days when the device needs charging—a routine interruption, except that James had been relying on continuous monitoring during a high-stress week he knew was risky for his heart. How do we design around the "naked wrist" periods when protection is temporarily unavailable? What is the emotional arc of vulnerability during charging windows, and how might the device design and companion app transform this from anxiety source to intentional health ritual?

8. **The data diary no one else can see** Since raw biometric data never leaves the device, users accumulate a rich, private health history that even they cannot fully access or interpret. What is the psychological experience of carrying intimate knowledge about your body that remains opaque—data you "own" but cannot truly see or understand?
