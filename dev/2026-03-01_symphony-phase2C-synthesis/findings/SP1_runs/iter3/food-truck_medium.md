# Brainstorming Questions: Filipino-Mexican Fusion Food Truck

---

## Topic Cluster 01: Concept Validation and Cultural Authenticity

1. **Fusion concept durability beyond novelty**: What evidence — beyond coastal-market trend data — validates that local palates will build sustained loyalty to Filipino-Mexican fusion rather than treating it as a curiosity that fades, and what minimal-capital demand tests (pop-ups, residencies, tasting events) could surface leading indicators of repeat intent before full vehicle buildout? What measurable factors would distinguish genuine loyalty from one-time novelty attraction? [Questioner, Devil's Advocate, Analyst]

2. **Cultural co-creation versus appropriation**: Given that fusion cuisine can feel inauthentic or exploitative, how might you meaningfully engage Filipino and Mexican community members — through menu co-development, elder consultation, cultural storytelling, or collaborative recipe refinement — to ensure the concept honors both traditions, and what specific practices would distinguish genuine community co-creation from performative representation? [Audience Advocate]

3. **Amplifying signature dishes through authenticity-plus-appeal**: Which specific menu items, flavor combinations, or culinary traditions (lumpia tacos, adobo burritos, ube horchata, and beyond) should we center to create both authenticity and broad appeal, and how might the menu structure educate and delight customers unfamiliar with either cuisine while honoring what has worked beautifully in successful Filipino-Mexican fusion elsewhere? [Appreciative Inquirer]

4. **Menu profitability mix and contribution margins**: With fusion ingredients like ube and adobo potentially carrying higher input costs, what component-level food costs apply to signature dishes, which price points hit the 25–30% target food cost ratio while remaining competitive, and how do you engineer a menu where the top 3 sellers cover at least 60% of daily fixed costs through their combined contribution margins without alienating price-sensitive lunch crowds at a $12–16 plate price? [Analyst, Accountant]

## Topic Cluster 02: Community Relationship and Equity Access

5. **Designing for community access, not just convenience**: Who are the potential customers never considered in food truck business plans — people experiencing food insecurity, seniors with limited mobility, families in underserved neighborhoods — and how might truck height and wheelchair accessibility, payment methods and digital divide, language barriers, dietary restrictions, sensory sensitivities, and the dignity of the ordering experience itself be deliberately designed for rather than treated as afterthoughts? [Audience Advocate]

6. **Cultural and generational difference in street-food meaning**: How does the community's relationship with street food differ across cultural backgrounds and generations — where older residents from certain cultures may associate street vending with economic necessity while younger residents see it as artisanal — and how do you navigate these different perspectives and potential class assumptions without alienating any group? [Audience Advocate — AI orphan of register distinct from convergence]

7. **Pricing strategy grounded in real economic tradeoffs**: What would it look like to genuinely understand what different community members can and will pay, where they see value, and what "affordable" actually means to them — not as abstract market research but as real people making real tradeoffs with food budgets — and how does this inform a pricing and portion strategy that reflects genuine awareness of community economic realities? [Audience Advocate]

8. **Relationship-first operating practices**: How might we design intentional practices for building lasting relationships with city officials, fellow food truck owners, and neighborhood communities from day one — looking at what has worked when local food businesses built genuine, lasting community connections — and what structures would make "relationship-first" daily operations sustainable even during busy periods? [Appreciative Inquirer]

9. **Community accountability beyond market success**: What would it mean for the business to be accountable to the community beyond revenue and customer satisfaction — employing locally, sourcing regionally, contributing to food security, supporting other food entrepreneurs — and how might invisible aspects of business practice (sourcing ethics, labor practices, profit distribution) be made visible in ways that feel genuine rather than performative? [Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 03: Regulatory Environment and Ordinance Response

10. **Ordinance scenario modeling and pre-developed pivots**: Under best-case, worst-case, and compromise outcomes of the pending ordinance on downtown hours and annual fees (including the estimate of +$1,500 annual fees and 30% downtown-hour restriction), what is the financial impact on highest-margin locations, at what point do secondary zones become economically viable, and what pre-developed adaptive pivots exist if the current business model becomes illegal or unprofitable? Which contingency metrics would trigger strategic pivots? [Analyst, Accountant, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

11. **Proactive regulatory engagement and coalition-building**: Rather than reacting to proposed ordinances, what would it look like to proactively shape the regulatory landscape — building coalitions with other food truck owners, presenting data-driven arguments to city council, engaging in permit-allocation conversations — and how might this advocacy center community needs (low-income workers, evening service workers, those dependent on downtown food-truck gatherings) rather than just business interests? [Questioner, Audience Advocate]

12. **Ordinance feedback loop as self-defeating cycle**: If new restrictions push trucks into residential or less-regulated areas, how could complaints about noise, parking, or waste create a political feedback loop leading to even stricter regulations, and what engagement strategies could interrupt this reinforcing cycle before it accelerates? Additionally, what distinguishes genuine collective action through the food truck association from regulatory capture in which incumbents use "quality standards" (permit caps, zoning, licensing) to block newcomers? [Systems Thinker, Devil's Advocate]

13. **Compounding regulatory cost structure**: Annual licensing fees establish a precedent for incremental increases; once the city council discovers food trucks as a reliable revenue source, what prevents the fee structure from becoming the primary constraint on growth, and what leading indicators would let you detect that tipping point early enough to adapt? [Systems Thinker]

14. **Commissary dependency and alternatives**: With commissary kitchen fees typically $800–1,500/month, what happens if the required commissary partnership becomes unavailable, prohibitively expensive, or burdensome — and how do shared-use commercial kitchens, restaurant off-hour partnerships, or eventually building out a small prep facility compare economically as alternative compliance arrangements? [Questioner, Accountant]

## Topic Cluster 04: Capital Structure, Debt, and Financial Resilience

15. **SBA loan as risk-adjusted decision**: What specific revenue projections, confidence intervals, and worst-case seasonal scenarios (winter months, rainy seasons, event cancellations) justify the $40K SBA microloan's roughly $750–850/month debt service burden versus a leaner bootstrap approach, what early-warning indicators would trigger shifting from growth investment to pure survival mode, and what optionality does the debt remove even as it provides leverage? [Analyst, Accountant, Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker]

16. **Option value of staying undercapitalized**: What advantages emerge from capital scarcity — forced resource constraints driving innovation, prevention of premature scaling — that excess capital would obscure, and what does a $60K bootstrap path make visible that a $100K path would hide? [Systems Thinker]

17. **Startup valley-of-death cash flow modeling**: Food truck businesses often require 12–18 months to reach consistent profitability and face 40–60% revenue swings between peak and winter months — how have you modeled the gap between initial capital depletion and sustainable cash flow, what working capital reserve must be built during peak months, and what contingencies exist if revenue runs 30% below projections for the first 8 months without compromising ingredient quality or staffing? [Devil's Advocate, Analyst, Accountant]

18. **Break-even and daily revenue threshold**: Given fixed costs (truck depreciation/financing, insurance, permits, commissary fees, labor), what daily revenue threshold and service-window hours are required to break even, and how does that break-even runway change under different capital structures (full savings, partial loan, maximum loan)? [Accountant]

19. **Equipment ownership vs. leasing across time horizons**: What is the total cost of ownership comparison between purchasing used truck and kitchen equipment versus leasing — factoring depreciation, maintenance, resale value, repair cycles (generators last 2–3 years under heavy use), lease-to-own flexibility, and tax implications — and how does that decision reshape risk across short, medium, and long time horizons as the concept evolves? [Analyst, Accountant, Systems Thinker, Devil's Advocate]

20. **Hidden and replacement-cycle costs**: Beyond obvious operating costs, what less-visible expenses should be budgeted for — equipment replacement cycles, ingredient spoilage during weather closures, emergency repairs, parking tickets in unclear-signage zones — and how much should these contingencies add to the monthly burn rate estimate? [Accountant]

## Topic Cluster 05: Revenue Streams, Catering, and Weather Resilience

21. **Catering vs. daily vending economics and opportunity cost**: What is the true profitability per labor-hour for catering versus daily vending after accounting for setup, transportation, equipment wear, and opportunity cost of missed street vending locations — including the concrete tradeoff of an $800–1,200 three-hour catering gig against a $300–500 vending window — and how does that framework weigh immediate revenue against brand visibility, repeat customer acquisition, and presence-at-regular-location? [Analyst, Accountant]

22. **Catering concentration risk and Shifting-the-Burden dynamic**: Catering provides reliable revenue that buffers daily-vending volatility, but over-reliance creates a Shifting-the-Burden dynamic where the stabilizer becomes the primary business — eroding street presence and brand visibility, and introducing feast-or-famine client concentration. What safeguards prevent over-reliance on a few large contracts, and when does the pattern signal a genuine pivot rather than a problem? [Systems Thinker, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

23. **Revenue stream correlation and weather-independent diversification**: To what extent are daily vending, catering, and event revenues correlated or countercyclical (do weekday lunch sales decline when weekend weddings increase?), and what weather-independent revenue streams (meal kits, corporate contracts, prepared foods) would smooth cash flow — specifically what percentage of annual revenue needs to come from non-truck sources? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate]

24. **Location-volatility vs. habit formation**: Regular customers become repeat customers through predictable availability, but food truck schedules are inherently unstable due to weather, competition, regulations, and private events. What predictable anchors can you create amidst the volatility, and how do you design communication systems that convert unpredictability into anticipation rather than frustration? [Systems Thinker]

25. **Customer acquisition economics and word-of-mouth amplification**: What are the most effective customer acquisition channels — social media, foot traffic, word-of-referral, catering partnerships — and what is the estimated CAC and LTV ratio for each? Additionally, where does the virtuous cycle of referrals amplify (delighted customer → socially relevant recommendation occasion → trust transfer) and where does it naturally attenuate, so that interventions strengthen amplification without triggering resistance? [Analyst, Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 06: Competition, Differentiation, and Market Position

26. **Differentiation in a saturated, imitative market**: With 30 active food trucks, 4 direct fusion competitors, and the likelihood of competitive convergence as execution techniques aren't proprietary — what measurable factors actually correlate with sustained success (location strategy, menu innovation frequency, social media engagement, pricing) and what defensible moats exist around brand, experience, and relationships that go beyond being first to this niche and can be communicated instantly at curbside? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker, Questioner]

27. **Existing assets and distinctive strengths**: What unique strengths, resources, capabilities, or network connections does the founder already bring that other food truck startups might lack, and how might these be leveraged from day one — and already-existing network members with skills or resources invited into the process through mutual-benefit rather than transactional structures — to create early momentum? [Appreciative Inquirer]

28. **Learning from peak moments and existing scene**: What aspects of the existing food truck scene are working exceptionally well — what are local trucks doing that customers clearly love, and recall a time when you created a dish that completely delighted people — and how might we design experiences and operational patterns to reproduce those peak moments regularly? [Appreciative Inquirer — AI orphan of strengths-based register]

## Topic Cluster 07: Operations, Founder Capacity, and Throughput

29. **Solo founder single-point-of-failure risk**: As a solo operator with no business-ownership experience, the entire operation depends on continuous founder presence — what happens to revenue during illness, family emergencies, or burnout, what operational redundancies could be built from day one, and what is the realistic timeline to hire your first reliable backup operator? [Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

30. **Operational bottlenecks in single-truck mode**: Where do actual throughput bottlenecks emerge in a single-truck solo operation — prep time, service speed, inventory management — and what measurements would identify these constraints before they impact revenue? [Analyst]

31. **Unit economics pressure testing under cost shock**: What happens to profitability if food costs run 5–8% higher than projected, and which menu items become unsustainable first under those conditions? Which variable costs (food, labor, fuel, commissary, maintenance) have the greatest impact on profitability at scale, and how do they shift as the business grows? [Questioner, Analyst]

32. **Sustaining founder vision through challenging periods**: What daily or weekly practices would keep the founder connected to the joy and purpose that drew them to this business — especially during challenging periods — and how might those practices also communicate your values to customers and team members? Additionally, what small, concrete experiment could you try this month that tests the concept while beginning to build community relationships, and what would you learn from it? [Appreciative Inquirer — AI orphan of aspirational/interiority register]

## Topic Cluster 08: Growth, Fleet Expansion, and Brick-and-Mortar

33. **Fleet expansion criteria and incremental unit economics**: What are the quantified prerequisites for adding a second truck — monthly revenue consistency, profit margin stability, customer repeat rate — recognizing that a second truck typically generates 70–80% of the first's revenue due to management overhead and prime-location scarcity, and at what scale do coordination costs (scheduling, inventory, maintenance, staff coordination) grow faster than marginal revenue so you can detect a Limits-to-Growth ceiling before growth stalls? What indicators would suggest remaining a single-truck operation indefinitely? [Analyst, Accountant, Systems Thinker, Devil's Advocate]

34. **Brick-and-mortar as assumed destination vs. alternative paths**: Why assume brick-and-mortar is the natural evolution rather than staying fleet-focused, developing a packaged product line, or becoming primarily a catering operation? How does unit economics (rent, build-out costs, higher staffing, weather independence, throughput) compare, how does the brick-and-mortar goal create decision distortion that leads to premature scaling investments the truck doesn't yet need, and could the truck continue operating as a satellite revenue stream? [Questioner, Analyst, Accountant, Systems Thinker]

35. **Fleet expansion and ecosystem-level effects**: How does your growth from one truck to a fleet or brick-and-mortar change your relationship with the community and the existing food ecosystem — consolidating market share, potentially pressuring marginal operators, attracting new entrants, influencing regulation — and what would it look like to grow in ways that strengthen rather than displace existing food vendors while maintaining the informal, personal connections that make food trucks special? [Audience Advocate, Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 09: Three-Year Vision and Success Definition

36. **Three-year anniversary vision**: Imagine three years from now celebrating the business's anniversary with loyal customers, team members, and community supporters — what would people say the business has contributed to the city's food culture, what would the atmosphere feel like, and what specific achievements would make you most proud? Additionally, if customers described the truck to friends as an experience they genuinely loved — not just for the food but for how it made them feel — what words and moments would they use? [Appreciative Inquirer — AI orphan of aspirational register]

37. **Cooperation vs. competitive leakage among trucks**: Information sharing, joint marketing, and sub-leased commissary space create value in a fragmented market — but at what point does transparency about locations, pricing, and strategies begin eroding your competitive position? What is the boundary between healthy collaboration and competitive leakage, especially within a food truck association that may drift toward incumbent protection? [Systems Thinker]

## Appended Questions (Perspective, Provocation, and Distinctive-Register Personas)

### Provocateur (Append)

1. **[What if the food disappeared, but the line remained?]** Imagine a food truck where people pay specifically to wait in line — the line itself is the product, and the food is just a free bonus that happens at the end. What would you design into that waiting experience that people would actually pay for? What does this reveal about the hidden value in your concept that you're not currently capturing? *The real insight: what if community, anticipation, and social experience are the actual product, not just a side effect of hunger?*

2. **[What if success meant putting yourself out of business?]** Imagine your explicit goal is to be so successful that you're forced to close within 18 months — either because the city bans you, you can't keep up with demand, or you create a movement that makes your entire concept obsolete. How would you operate differently if runaway success was actually failure? What "safe, reasonable" growth strategies are actually just playing small? *The real insight: what if we're optimizing for sustainability when we should be optimizing for impact — and what does "too successful" even look like?*

3. **[What if the city passed the worst possible ordinance, and it was the best thing that ever happened to you?]** The new restrictions pass: downtown vending hours eliminated, annual fees tripled, food trucks banned within 500 feet of restaurants. But somehow this becomes the catalyst that transforms your business model entirely. What does that transformed business look like, and why is it actually better than the original plan? *The real insight: what if the pending regulatory threat is actually a gift you haven't unwrapped yet?*

4. **[What if you had to start with zero capital and $100K in debt?]** Flip the entire financial premise: instead of $60K savings + $40K optional loan, you begin with -$100K and no assets. You cannot spend money to make money — you can only use relationships, creativity, and other people's resources. How does the business model change when every dollar you spend is a dollar you don't have? *The real insight: what if the financial cushion is actually masking a much more interesting opportunity to design a capital-light model?*

5. **[What if you had to give away the food, but charged for everything else?]** The lumpia tacos are free, but you charge for the stories behind them, the recipes, the packaging, the recommendation of which bite to take first, the music pairing with your meal, the permission to take photos. What would people actually pay for when the food itself costs nothing? *The real insight: what if the intellectual property, experience design, and cultural storytelling are worth more than the physical calories?*

### Storyteller (Append)

1. **First Bite Discovery** Imagine it's 11:45 AM on a Tuesday—Maria, an office worker who packed the same sad lunch for years, walks past your truck. What does she see, smell, and hear in those first 10 seconds that compels her to abandon her familiar routine and try something entirely new? How does that first encounter with Filipino-Mexican fusion become a story she tells her coworkers that afternoon?

2. **The Catering Memory** Walk through the moment when a wedding guest tastes your lumpia tacos for the first time during the reception toast—what makes that bite memorable enough that six months later, when they're planning their own corporate event, they search specifically for your truck rather than scrolling through twenty other catering options? What narrative thread connects that single bite to becoming their go-to recommendation?

3. **Regulatory Crossroads** Paint the scene where Elena attends a city council meeting about the proposed food truck restrictions—what does she say in her three minutes of public comment that transforms her from "just another food truck owner complaining" into "the culinary entrepreneur whose testimony makes council members reconsider the human impact of these ordinances"? How does that moment of advocacy ripple through her relationships with other truck owners and city officials?

4. **Cultural Bridge Building** Follow the journey of a Filipino-American family who discovers your truck at a street festival—how does seeing familiar flavors reimagined through Mexican street food format make them feel both seen and surprised in ways that keep them coming back not just for the food, but for what your truck represents about cultural fusion and identity? How do you harness that emotional connection to build a community that advocates for your business through regulatory challenges and competition?

5. **Rainy Day Loyalty** Picture Javier, your regular customer who orders adobo burritos every Thursday, approaching your truck during a torrential downpour with wind gusting at 30 mph. What does his experience look like—how do you serve him while keeping both your food and staff safe, and how does that difficult weather day transform him from a "customer who likes the food" into "someone who tells everyone this truck is worth supporting no matter what"?

### Visionary (Append)

1. **What if a food truck functioned less like a portable restaurant and more like a traveling cultural ambassador that creates pop-up community experiences?** Instead of thinking about how to sell Filipino-Mexican fusion from a truck, imagine if the business was fundamentally about creating temporary third spaces — micro-gatherings that transform parking lots, office parks, and neighborhood streets into destinations where food is the catalyst for connection, storytelling, and cultural exchange. What would the business model look like if revenue came from experiences, partnerships, and community-building rather than transactional food sales alone? How might this change conversations with city officials about the truck's "public value"?

2. **How could we completely reimagine the relationship between a mobile food business and the communities it serves — creating something that doesn't exist in the current restaurant or food truck paradigm?** Imagine if the truck operated on a model where neighborhoods "adopt" it as a shared community asset, with residents having input on menu evolution, scheduling, and even profit-sharing in exchange for hosting commitments. What if the business wasn't owned by a single founder but existed as a cooperative ecosystem where customers, suppliers, and the city all have stakes in its success? Could this become a new category of civic infrastructure — mobile, participatory, and collectively governed?

3. **What would this business look like if it were designed from the ground up to NEVER become a traditional restaurant chain, even while achieving the founder's economic and creative goals?** Instead of the conventional trajectory (truck → fleet → brick-and-mortar), imagine entirely new organizational forms: a networked federation of independent owner-operators sharing a unified brand and backend; a guild-style collective that trains and launches new food entrepreneurs under a shared umbrella; a platform business that provides infrastructure, financing, and market access to culinary creators while remaining fundamentally decentralized. What if "scaling" meant multiplying access to opportunity rather than multiplying outlets?

4. **What if we designed the business to be antifragile — becoming stronger through volatility, regulatory changes, and competitive pressure — rather than merely resilient to them?** Instead of viewing pending city ordinances, competition from similar fusion concepts, or seasonal fluctuations as threats to manage, imagine a business architecture that actually *feeds* on disruption: a modular model that can rapidly reconfigure based on regulatory conditions; a guerrilla logistics network that can pivot operations overnight; a community-political power base that gives the business influence over the very regulations that constrain it. How could constraints become the engine of innovation rather than brakes on growth?

5. **What if the physical truck was merely the visible tip of a much deeper, invisible network of cultural exchange, culinary education, and cross-generational storytelling?** Beyond serving fusion cuisine, imagine the business as a platform for preserving and evolving Filipino and Mexican food traditions through live cooking demos, oral history documentation, collaborative recipe development with community elders, and educational partnerships with schools. Could the primary value proposition shift from "feeding people efficiently" to "keeping living food cultures vital and accessible" — and how would that transformation reshape revenue streams, growth strategy, and the very definition of success?

### Analogist (Append)

1. **Migratory patterns and resource distribution** How do migratory bird species optimize their routes and timing to maximize food availability while minimizing competition, and what patterns from their adaptive movement strategies could inform a food truck's approach to location selection, seasonal shifts, and avoiding direct competition with similar trucks in the same geographic zones?

2. **Craft brewery expansion playbook** The craft brewing industry went through a similar phase where successful local breweries navigated the transition from single taproom to regional distribution, with many failing by expanding too quickly into brick-and-mortar before establishing sustainable economics—what specific expansion mistakes and successful patterns from that industry's growth cycle should inform your 3-year transition plan from truck to potential storefront?

3. **Hub-and-spoke vs. distributed network models** Logistics companies like FedEx explicitly chose hub-and-spoke systems over point-to-point delivery for efficiency advantages—does your planned expansion (truck → catering → potential fleet → brick-and-mortar) map better to a hub-and-spoke model with a central kitchen or a distributed model, and what are the operational economics and break-even points where each architecture becomes superior?

4. **Regulatory capture and collective action** Historical examples from taxi medallion systems, liquor licensing, and food cart regulations show that industries facing restrictive regulations either succumb to barriers or organize collectively to influence policy—what strategies from other industries' successful collective action against unfavorable regulatory changes could inform your engagement with the city council process and the local food truck association?

5. **Niche differentiation in ecosystems** In mature ecosystems, competing species evolve specialized niches to coexist rather than compete directly—how might this concept of resource partitioning apply to differentiating your Filipino-Mexican fusion concept from the four existing similar trucks, and what underserved dayparts, locations, or customer segments could represent your equivalent of an unoccupied ecological niche?
