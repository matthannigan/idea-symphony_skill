# Brainstorming Questions: Wearable Device

---

## Topic Cluster 01: Privacy Architecture and Trust

1. **What "privacy" means across user segments and how trust is earned**: How might a 45-year-old with a family history of heart conditions conceptualize privacy differently from a tech-savvy early adopter, and what visible transparency mechanisms (visual indicators, audit trails, third-party verification) would make "data stays on-device" viscerally credible rather than an abstract claim — especially when users may not distinguish between "wellness" and "clinical" data usage? [Questioner, Audience Advocate, Appreciative Inquirer]
2. **Unintended leakage through aggregated summaries and sync-protocol threat surface**: Given that only "processed alerts and aggregated summaries" sync to the companion app, what sensitive health patterns might be reverse-engineered from timing metadata, alert frequency, and summary statistics, what threat modeling has been done on the sync protocol, and how are these risks communicated to users transparently? [Questioner, Devil's Advocate]
3. **Domestic coercion, stigma, and right to be forgotten**: What protections exist for users in abusive relationships who could be monitored or coerced through the companion app, how does the device's appearance and public notifications avoid inadvertently disclosing a health condition, and what mechanisms guarantee complete data deletion across device, app, and cloud services when a user discontinues use? [Audience Advocate]
4. **Privacy as felt experience, not compliance checklist**: When have you seen a product make security or privacy feel like a genuine benefit rather than a legal burden, and how might interface design, communication patterns, and transparency make users viscerally feel their data is safe from day one? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 02: False Alarms, Alert Fatigue, and the Shifting-the-Burden Trap

1. **The false positive-negative trade-off space and who decides the balance**: Given the clinical liability implications of missed arrhythmia detection versus the user-experience cost of false alarms, what analytical framework should guide the optimal precision-recall balance at different model confidence thresholds, what false-positive rate will different user segments (known heart conditions vs. prevention-focused) tolerate before they ignore alerts, and who decides the acceptable balance? [Analyst, Audience Advocate, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]
2. **Adaptive sensitivity tuning without silencing real anomalies**: How does the system adapt its sensitivity threshold when users repeatedly dismiss alerts — creating a reinforcing loop (more false alarms → more dismissals → model learns "user doesn't want alerts" → potential missed detections) — and what prevents this self-correcting mechanism from drifting into dangerous territory or oscillating between too-noisy and too-quiet states? [Systems Thinker, Devil's Advocate]
3. **Liability, ambiguous findings, and the Shifting-the-Burden pattern**: What happens at the boundary where the device detects something concerning but not definitive — where a Shifting-the-Burden dynamic lets users offload interpretation onto their doctors, creating cascades of unnecessary clinical visits — and how do we disentangle device failure from user inattention when a genuine cardiac event occurs shortly after a false alert created alert fatigue? [Systems Thinker, Devil's Advocate]
4. **Context-aware notification delivery that acts without inducing panic**: When the device detects a potentially serious anomaly, what notification protocol accounts for the user's immediate context (driving, sleeping, in a meeting), balances urgency with calm, and avoids the constant low-level hypervigilance that 40+ adults with heightened health awareness may already experience? [Audience Advocate]
5. **"Nothing to report" as reassurance**: How might the system communicate value during long stretches of normal readings so users don't interpret "no alerts" as "device not working" — and how does prolonged silence shape trust when an alert finally does come? [Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 03: Sensor Accuracy, Bias, and Physiological Diversity

1. **Skin-tone bias, calibration verification, and the "standard body" problem**: How does the PPG sensor design account for documented optical-heart-rate biases across skin tones, what validation approach ensures accuracy for melanin-rich skin, and how is the ML model trained and validated on diverse physiological patterns (arrhythmias that present differently across genders, ages, body compositions, comorbidities) rather than the "typical" male physiology dominating cardiac research? [Audience Advocate, Devil's Advocate]
2. **Graceful failure under physiological edge cases**: What happens when users have conditions that affect sensor reliability — poor circulation, skin pigmentation affecting PPG, tremors affecting motion sensing, motion artifacts during exercise, poor skin contact — and how does the system degrade gracefully rather than fail silently? [Questioner, Analyst]

## Topic Cluster 04: Sensor Fusion, Failure Modes, and Fixes-that-Fail

1. **Sensor fusion architecture and partial-failure detection (Fixes-that-Fail)**: What systematic evaluation criteria should determine early- vs. intermediate- vs. late-fusion for multi-signal anomaly detection, how does the system detect when individual sensors fail partially (PPG giving noisy but not null readings, temperature stuck at one value), and how do we avoid a Fixes-that-Fail pattern where compensating models silently mask degrading sensors until the pipeline collapses catastrophically? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker]
2. **Model drift detection and baseline recalibration**: How will the system detect when on-device ML performance has degraded due to sensor drift, firmware updates, or population shift, and how does it distinguish genuine anomalies from gradual baseline shifts as users age, change medications, or develop new conditions — without normalizing dangerous decline or flagging normal aging as pathology? [Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 05: Power Budget, Battery Life, and Detection-Quality Cascades

1. **Power budget allocation across the ML pipeline and inference latency constraints**: How does power consumption break down across sensor sampling, preprocessing, inference, and post-processing at different model complexity levels, what is the marginal accuracy gain per additional milliwatt, and what is the maximum acceptable inference latency that still enables clinically meaningful intervention given the target NPU? [Analyst]
2. **Battery-detection death spiral and transparent power modes**: How do power-allocation decisions create second-order effects on detection quality, what self-reinforcing patterns emerge when battery-conserving modes sacrifice sensor resolution (battery low → reduce sensing → lower resolution → more false positives/negatives → more processing to compensate → faster drain), and would users prefer explicit "accuracy vs. battery life" tradeoffs over invisible automatic degradation — including the hypothesis that a 3-day battery with medical-grade detection could beat a 7-day battery with consumer-grade detection? [Systems Thinker, Questioner]
3. **Anomaly-detection power cliff and battery degradation over time**: How will the system handle a worst-case scenario where the ML model enters a high-frequency false-positive state, continuously triggering inference and communication bursts that drain the battery within hours rather than days, and how does the power budget remain viable when battery capacity degrades to 70% of original over 2–3 years? [Devil's Advocate]
4. **Charging windows, data continuity, and the "naked wrist" ritual**: How does weekly charging affect data continuity and risk becoming a confounding variable in anomaly detection, and what design choices would make battery life contribute to peace of mind — turning charging windows from anxiety sources into intentional health rituals rather than monitoring gaps? [Devil's Advocate, Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 06: Model Updates, Personalization, and Tragedy-of-the-Commons

1. **Update strategy trade-offs: on-device, federated, firmware**: Breaking down on-device learning, federated learning, and periodic firmware updates, what are the incremental engineering costs, privacy implications, and model performance gains for each approach, and how might over-the-air model updates work within medical device constraints or push the architecture toward federated learning? [Analyst, Questioner]
2. **Federated learning opt-in and the Tragedy-of-the-Commons**: How does the system encourage opt-in to federated learning updates when users receive no direct benefit — a Tragedy-of-the-Commons variant where free-riding weakens the federated signal — and what self-amplifying patterns emerge if early adopters experience better model performance while non-participants stagnate? [Systems Thinker]
3. **Clinical validation feedback without breaking privacy**: If early users share anomaly reports with their physicians and receive validation that the device correctly detected events, how does that medical confirmation flow back into the model (perhaps via a privacy-preserving "true-positive/false-positive" tagging mechanism that never accesses raw biometric data) to close the learning loop without violating on-device data residency? [Systems Thinker]
4. **Phased rollout and user recalibration after model updates**: When models are updated to improve accuracy or reduce false positives, how does the system manage the transition period where users have calibrated to the old alert behavior, and what prevents whiplash from sudden sensitivity shifts retraining users to distrust alerts — arguing for phased rollouts with user-controlled update timing? [Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 07: Wellness-to-Clinical Pathway and the Fixes-that-Fail of Technical Debt

1. **Designing today for FDA 510(k) tomorrow (avoiding a Fixes-that-Fail regulatory pattern)**: What architectural decisions made for the initial consumer wellness launch would create technical debt or require rework when pursuing FDA 510(k) clearance — a Fixes-that-Fail pattern where consumer shortcuts in documentation, validation data, and traceability become rework that delays clinical entry — and what design patterns treat the wellness version as a "clinical device in waiting" from day one? [Analyst, Systems Thinker]
2. **Clinical validation data: feasibility against the 18-month runway**: What is the minimum viable clinical dataset size and composition for validating arrhythmia and sleep apnea detection accuracy, and how does that requirement map against the 18-month funding runway, available resources, and the contingency if the 510(k) pathway proves infeasible due to predicate device changes or new guidance? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate]
3. **Alternative regulatory pathways and the wellness-clinical boundary**: Why assume FDA 510(k) is the right path — could De Novo classification, partnering with established medical device companies, or staying in wellness with clinical research partnerships achieve credibility faster or with different tradeoffs, and how do marketing and UI design prevent users (and their physicians) from treating a wellness device as clinical-grade diagnostic equipment? [Questioner, Devil's Advocate]
4. **Reimbursement and the Success-to-the-Successful fracture**: If the device transitions to clinical use and insurance reimbursement enters the picture, how does that shift product priorities from user-centric to payer-centric — a Success-to-the-Successful dynamic where clinical users get better features while wellness users are deprioritized — and what prevents the user base from fracturing into two-tier experiences? [Systems Thinker]
5. **Regulatory success as collaboration, not obstacle**: Imagine the FDA 510(k) process not as a hurdle but as a collaboration that makes the device demonstrably more valuable — how might clinical partnerships enhance rather than restrict what we deliver to consumers, and what could the wellness version borrow from clinical standards (and vice versa) to make each version stronger? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 08: Accessibility, Equity, and Inclusive Design

1. **Disability, sensory, and digital-literacy accommodations**: For users with hearing, visual, or motor impairments, what alternative alert modalities and companion-app interfaces ensure independence without forcing caregiver dependency, and what accommodations support 40+ users with limited technical comfort when interpreting anomalies, managing firmware updates, or understanding privacy controls? [Audience Advocate]
2. **Anxiety induction, anthropomorphization, and appropriate trust**: How does the device balance alerting users to genuine concerns without cultivating constant hypervigilance, and what design elements mitigate the risk that users treat the wearable as infallible medical advice rather than wellness information? [Audience Advocate, Questioner]

## Topic Cluster 09: Business Model, Unit Economics, and End-of-Life

1. **Hardware cost structure, recurring revenue, and the 18-month runway**: Breaking down bill of materials, manufacturing, and per-unit software development costs, what are the unit economics at different production volumes, where is the break-even point, and given the medical device regulatory constraints and funding runway, what analysis supports a hardware-only purchase model versus a subscription model for ongoing monitoring? [Analyst]
2. **Data lock-in temptation and platform power**: What prevents the companion-app sync protocol from becoming a data moat that traps users, and how does the system design anticipate the temptation to monetize aggregated health insights (more users → more data → more insights → more monetization pressure → privacy erosion) in ways that undermine the privacy-first value proposition — arguing for user-owned exportability and open sync standards as structural guarantees? [Systems Thinker]
3. **End-of-life, device transitions, and the Success-to-the-Successful upgrade trap**: What happens when hardware reaches end-of-life or a user upgrades to a new generation, and how does the system preserve a user's health baseline and model personalization — avoiding a Success-to-the-Successful dynamic where new devices get better models while old ones are deliberately degraded via updates to force upgrades — through open export formats and baseline portability across hardware generations? [Systems Thinker]
4. **Monitoring vacations and opt-in alerting paradigms**: Why assume users want continuous monitoring at all — what would "dormancy modes," "monitoring vacations," or opt-in "check your status when you choose" paradigms look like, and how might periodic disengagement improve long-term adherence without defeating the purpose of continuous monitoring? [Questioner]

## Topic Cluster 10: Team, Experiments, and Latent Strengths (Appreciative Inquiry)

1. **Peak moments of health insight the device might create**: Tell me about a time when you or someone you know had a moment of genuine insight about their health — a discovery that changed how they lived. What conditions made that insight possible, how did it feel, and what if our device could create more such moments routinely? [Appreciative Inquirer]
2. **Devices that become invisible partners**: What tool have you used that became so seamlessly helpful it felt like a natural extension of your awareness — not something you had to think about, but something that made you smarter about your daily life — and what subtle design choices created that relationship? [Appreciative Inquirer]
3. **Three years later: what users celebrate**: Imagine it's three years from now and users are telling their friends about this wearable — not describing features but describing how it changed their relationship to their body and their health. What are they saying, and what specific moments do they recount with gratitude? [Appreciative Inquirer]
4. **The earned-trust alert moment**: Picture an experience where the device alerts you to something important, gets it right, and the intervention genuinely matters. What would that moment feel like, and how would the device have earned your trust so that when it speaks, you listen? [Appreciative Inquirer]
5. **What this 12-engineer team already does well**: This team has a specific distribution of expertise — firmware, ML, hardware, mobile. When has this kind of cross-functional collaboration produced something extraordinary, and what meeting structures, communication patterns, or decision rituals would make the team's diversity a daily superpower rather than a coordination challenge? [Appreciative Inquirer]
6. **The smallest meaningful human-reaction experiment**: What's the smallest prototype or user test we could run this month — not a technical feasibility test, a human reaction test — that would give us genuine insight into whether we're building something people will actually care about, and what would make that experiment trustworthy? [Appreciative Inquirer]
7. **Existing assets we haven't fully leveraged**: Looking at the team, the timeframe, and the technical approach, what strengths are we taking for granted, what advantages do we already have that competitors would envy, and how might we design the project to amplify those natural advantages from day one? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Appended Questions (Perspective, Provocation, and Distinctive-Register Personas)

### Provocateur (Append)

1. **What if the device was designed to actively ignore your health data?** Imagine a wellness wearable that deliberately obscures your metrics — showing only vague impressions, encouraging body attunement over quantification. What changes when the product is designed to make you *less* dependent on numbers, not more? *The real insight: what if our obsession with measurement is part of the problem, not the solution?*
2. **What if the wearable monitored everyone except the wearer?** Picture a device designed for "community health surveillance" — tracking patterns across households, workplaces, friend groups — but explicitly forbidden from giving individuals their own data. What if privacy was achieved by making individual data literally inaccessible to the person it came from? *The real insight: what if the most valuable health insights live between people, not within them?*
3. **What if the device required NO battery and died every 24 hours?** Imagine a wearable deliberately engineered for daily mortality — it exhausts its power, goes dark, and you must "bury" it and start fresh tomorrow. What would this forced daily reset change about how users relate to their health data? *The real insight: what if continuous monitoring creates unhealthy attachment, and periodic disconnection is a feature, not a bug?*

### Storyteller (Append)

1. **The middle-of-the-night moment** Imagine it's 3 AM and Marcus, a 52-year-old user with a family history of heart disease, wakes to a gentle vibration on his wrist. The device has detected something unusual. Walk through the emotional and practical journey of what happens next—from that first alert to his decision (or not) to seek medical care. How do we design this critical moment to balance appropriate urgency without inducing panic, especially when the device itself acknowledges uncertainty in its detection?
2. **The dinner table conversation** Picture a holiday gathering where the wearer notices everyone glancing at their wrist. "Is that the heart thing?" someone asks. How does this device become part of someone's identity and social narrative—for better and worse? What design choices influence whether wearers feel empowered, protected, or defined by their device, and how might this differ across personality types and health anxiety levels?
3. **From wellness device to medical tool** Imagine the journey of someone who bought this as a consumer wellness product, then two years later receives an FDA-cleared software update that transforms it into a clinical-grade diagnostic tool. How does their relationship with the device evolve? What expectations, responsibilities, and emotional weight shift when a fitness accessory becomes a medical instrument—and how do we prepare users for this transition?

### Visionary (Append)

1. **What if health monitoring wasn't about detection but about conversation?** Instead of a device that passively collects data and issues alerts, what if we designed a system that engaged in an ongoing dialogue with the wearer's body — not metaphorically, but through a feedback architecture where the device's sensing modalities actively respond to physiological state changes, creating a bidirectional relationship between wearer and technology that fundamentally reimagines what "monitoring" means?
2. **What would a device look like if it treated privacy not as a constraint but as a design philosophy that created entirely new capabilities?** Instead of asking "how do we meet HIPAA requirements?" what if we asked "what becomes possible when health data genuinely never leaves the body, not even as processed outputs?" Could we create an architecture where the device itself becomes the primary interface — where insights, patterns, and even health guidance emerge locally through a new kind of intelligence that lives entirely on the wrist?
3. **How might we design for a 10-year relationship rather than a 2-year product cycle?** Most wearables are designed around replacement cycles and feature roadmaps. What if we completely reimagined this — creating a device that grows and evolves with its wearer, where model updates, sensor capabilities, and even the core value proposition shift over time as the user's health journey changes? What would a "lifelong companion" architecture look like, and how would it transform the regulatory and business landscape?

### Connector (Append)

1. **Biological nervous system parallel** The human nervous system processes most sensory signals locally (reflex arcs) before sending summaries to the brain — how could we structure our on-device ML pipeline to mirror this hierarchy, and what does this biological parallel suggest about which anomalies need immediate local processing versus which can be aggregated for periodic sync?
2. **Automotive safety-critical systems** Modern vehicles have multiple redundant safety systems that run independently with different complexity levels (ABS, traction control, stability control) — how might we apply this layered redundancy philosophy to create fail-safe anomaly detection that conserves power while maintaining safety-critical reliability?
3. **Privacy-accuracy symbiosis** The HIPAA constraint (no raw data leaves device) and the power constraint (7-day battery) seem like separate challenges, but they both push toward edge-compute efficiency — how might solving one actually accelerate solutions for the other, and what techniques from privacy-preserving ML (like differential privacy during federated learning) create power-efficient architectures?
