# Brainstorming Questions: Food Truck

---

## Topic Cluster 01: Vision, Aspiration, and Cultural Identity

1. **Three-year aspirational moment**: Imagine it's three years from now and you're celebrating the business's anniversary with loyal customers, team members, and community supporters — what would people say about what this business has contributed to the city's food culture, what atmosphere would fill the room, and what specific achievements would you be most proud of? [Appreciative Inquirer]

2. **Peak culinary moments as design blueprint**: Tell me about a time when you created a dish that completely delighted people — what was happening in that moment, what made it work so well, and how might we design the food truck experience to create more of those moments regularly? [Appreciative Inquirer]

3. **Cultural bridge vs. cultural appropriation**: Filipino-Mexican fusion sits at an intersection of two rich culinary traditions — how can the business honor and elevate both cultures authentically while avoiding appropriation, what does genuine community collaboration with Filipino and Mexican communities in the city look like, and how might we center specific menu items, flavor combinations, or traditions (lumpia tacos, adobo burritos, ube horchata) to create both authenticity and broad appeal? [Appreciative Inquirer, Audience Advocate]

4. **Customer experience aspiration**: If customers described this food truck to their friends as an experience they genuinely loved — not just for the food, but for how it made them feel — what words would they use, what moments would they describe, and what emotional responses would they consistently have? [Appreciative Inquirer]

5. **Sustaining the vision through hard seasons**: What daily or weekly practices would keep you connected to the joy and purpose that drew you to this business — especially during challenging periods — and how might those practices also communicate your values to customers and team members? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 02: Market Validation and Differentiation

6. **Fusion concept validation under market saturation**: With 30 existing food trucks and 4 direct fusion competitors, what evidence supports sustainable appeal for Filipino-Mexican fusion beyond novelty — how would you pressure-test demand by interviewing failed food truck operators to understand their actual obstacles, what first-mover disadvantage risks apply since this cuisine is unproven in a mid-size city, and what indicators would distinguish genuine loyalty from one-time curiosity? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

7. **Differentiation and defensive moat**: With four similar fusion concepts already operating, what would make this food truck meaningfully distinct in customers' minds beyond menu items, how could that differentiation be communicated instantly at curbside, and what prevents better-capitalized competitors from copying the concept and out-executing on locations, marketing, or economies of scale? [Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

8. **Pricing strategy and price-sensitivity discovery**: Given the 30 active food trucks with 4 direct fusion competitors, what price positioning strategy balances perceived value for unique fusion offerings against market rate sensitivity — how might elasticity differ between lunch crowds, dinner service, and event catering, and what testing methods could reveal the exact price point where customers switch to alternatives before pricing decisions are locked in? [Analyst, Questioner]

## Topic Cluster 03: Financial Model, Capital, and Unit Economics

9. **Break-even scenarios and revenue-mix seasonality**: What are realistic break-even points across operating models — single truck daily vending, event-focused operations, catering-heavy approach — and how do fixed costs (permitting, insurance, commissary fees) versus variable costs (COGS, labor, fuel) shift the economics, with what revenue mix month-over-month across daily vending, catering, and events needed to maintain positive cash flow through seasonal dips? [Analyst]

10. **Food cost optimization and unit-economics pressure test**: How should the menu be structured to optimize food cost ratios while maintaining the fusion concept — which core ingredients provide highest cross-utilization across lumpia tacos, adobo burritos, and ube horchata, where is cost structure most vulnerable to ingredient price volatility, and what would happen to profitability if food costs ran 5–8% higher than projected? [Analyst, Questioner]

11. **SBA loan vs. bootstrapping — the Fixes-that-Fail pattern**: The SBA loan provides immediate capital that accelerates startup speed and equipment quality, generating better early revenue and validating the decision — but fixed monthly loan payments reduce the ability to weather seasonal fluctuations, a classic Fixes-that-Fail dynamic where the short-term fix masks reduced flexibility. What decision framework should guide the choice against a conservative bootstrapping approach (risk tolerance, growth timeline pressure, cash flow runway, restrictive covenants, personal guarantees), and what's the minimum viable startup that reaches positive cash flow without loan payments? [Analyst, Systems Thinker, Questioner]

12. **Cash-flow fragility, seasonality buffer, and valley of death**: The plan commits $100K capital, but food trucks often require 12–18 months to reach consistent profitability — how have you modeled the "valley of death" between initial capital depletion and sustainable cash flow, are operating reserves sufficient given weather-driven revenue interruptions, and what contingencies exist if revenue runs 30% below projections for the first 8 months? [Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

13. **Debt service stress test under worst-case seasons**: Taking a $40K SBA microloan creates fixed obligations regardless of performance — have you calculated whether the business can service debt during winter months, rainy seasons, or event cancellations, and what specific metrics would trigger shifting from growth investment to pure survival mode? [Devil's Advocate]

14. **Equipment financing vs. leasing and the ownership trap**: For truck acquisition, kitchen equipment, and POS systems, what's the quantified tradeoff between financing (ownership, depreciation, higher upfront cash) and leasing (lower initial capital, tax deductibility, long-term premium) — at what usage duration does ownership become economical, and have you modeled total cost of ownership including repairs, downtime, resale value, and lease-to-own structures that preserve optionality? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate]

15. **Financial strengths from proven small-food-business patterns**: What sustainable financial practices from other successful small food businesses could we adapt — particularly around pricing that values both customer experience and business viability, revenue streams that create stability, and cash flow management that respects seasonal rhythms? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 04: Regulatory Environment and Political Engagement

16. **Ordinance vulnerability and anticipatory compliance**: The pending restrictions on downtown vending hours and annual licensing fees could eliminate highest-traffic locations — what scenario planning should model ordinance passage, modification, or rejection, what quantitative thresholds would make the downtown model unviable, and what alternative revenue streams could be activated immediately to offset constraints if the ordinance passes in its most restrictive form? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

17. **Regulatory engagement and equity-centered advocacy**: Rather than reacting to proposed ordinances, what would it look like to proactively shape the regulatory landscape by building coalitions with other food truck owners and presenting data-driven arguments to city council — and how do these changes differentially affect food truck owners who are women, people of color, or immigrants with less capital and political access, so that advocacy centers equity rather than just protecting the business's own interests? [Audience Advocate, Questioner]

18. **Regulatory-capture assessment and political access**: The existing food truck association and city officials may have relationships that disadvantage newcomers — how would you assess whether the regulatory environment genuinely welcomes competition versus creating barriers to entry, what early relationship-building efforts prevent marginalization in permit allocation, prime location assignments, or ordinance negotiations? [Devil's Advocate]

19. **Relationship-building with officials as a double-edged feedback loop**: Engagement gives advance warning of regulatory changes, influence in the process, and credibility for permits or variances — but that same engagement marks you as a visible operator, inviting closer scrutiny and consuming operational time. How do you calibrate regulatory engagement to get the benefits without becoming dependent on political access, and what would a "relationship-first" approach look like in daily operations so it's sustainable even during busy periods? [Appreciative Inquirer, Systems Thinker]

20. **Commissary dependency and permit scarcity contingencies**: What if the required commissary partnership becomes unavailable or prohibitively expensive, or vending permits are capped or downtown zones restricted — what alternative compliance arrangements, partnerships, private property agreements, or suburban expansion options maintain visibility and customer access? [Questioner]

## Topic Cluster 05: Equity, Access, and Community Belonging

21. **Food access barriers and pricing equity**: How might pricing strategy and location choices create or remove barriers for community members with limited disposable income, what mechanisms (sliding scale, donation-based items, EBT acceptance) could ensure the truck serves as genuine community resource, and when customers see a $12–15 fusion taco, what determines whether they perceive it as worth it or overpriced — how can the business communicate value (quality ingredients, fair wages, cultural authenticity, portion size) across different economic backgrounds? [Audience Advocate]

22. **Physical accessibility and communication inclusion**: Beyond basic ADA compliance, how does the physical design of the truck, ordering process, and service experience accommodate people with diverse mobility challenges, sensory sensitivities, or communication needs (non-English speakers, those with hearing loss, social anxiety)? [Audience Advocate]

23. **First-time customer psychology and dietary belonging**: Mapping the emotional journey of someone encountering Filipino-Mexican fusion for the first time — what might they feel (curiosity, confusion about ube or lumpia, hesitation), how can ordering experience and menu descriptions transform uncertainty into delight, and when someone with dietary restrictions (vegetarian, gluten-free, allergies, religious dietary laws) approaches, how do they feel welcomed rather than an afterthought? [Audience Advocate]

24. **Supply chain transparency and neighborhood stewardship**: What would it look like to make the supply chain transparent (sourcing locally, ethical meat, fair trade ingredients) to build trust with customers who've experienced industrial food systems as opaque, and what does it mean to be a good neighbor in locations where you park — supporting local events, hiring locally, sourcing from nearby producers? [Audience Advocate]

25. **Staff treatment as visible community signal**: From the community's perspective, how the business treats employees is visible in every interaction — what signals do wages, working conditions, and growth opportunities send to customers about whether this is a business they want to support, and how might investing in staff wellbeing become a competitive advantage? [Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 06: Operations, Systems, and Delivery Experience

26. **Location performance measurement and time-pressure lunch flow**: What systematic approach should guide location selection and rotation — what data points per location (sales per hour, customer demographics, repeat rate, weather sensitivity), how many visits provide statistically significant performance data, and for the daytime worker with a limited lunch break, what subtle frictions in ordering, payment, or pickup cause abandonment and how can the experience be streamlined without sacrificing the human connection that makes food trucks special? [Analyst, Audience Advocate]

27. **Critical metrics dashboard without measurement paralysis**: What 5–7 KPIs provide the most actionable intelligence without creating measurement paralysis, and how should these metrics be weighted across daily operations optimization, financial health, customer satisfaction, and growth trajectory? [Analyst]

28. **Commissary kitchen optimization as Tragedy-of-the-Commons**: The commissary bottleneck intensifies with fleet expansion — add a second truck and you compete with yourself for prep space and peak-hour equipment, a classic Tragedy-of-the-Commons dynamic where each truck independently optimizes its prep schedule but collectively creates congestion that slows everyone down. Beyond base cost, what analysis should drive kitchen selection (location relative to prime vending zones, storage capacity, prep windows, revenue-sharing through subletting unused slots), and at what scale does the shared-kitchen model become a constraint rather than cost-saver? [Analyst, Systems Thinker]

29. **Menu innovation complexity trap**: Introducing new items attracts attention and gives regulars reasons to return, but each addition adds complexity to inventory, prep, and service speed — eventually the menu becomes unwieldy, service slows, and quality consistency drops. What's your system for adding and removing menu items that maintains novelty without accumulating complexity, and how do you know when you've reached the right balance? [Systems Thinker]

30. **Equipment quality as a reliability feedback loop**: Cheap equipment breaks frequently, causing service interruptions that frustrate customers, reducing revenue and making quality replacements harder — while reliable equipment builds trust and revenue that enables further investment. The reinforcing loop operates on delay: payoff appears months later while upfront cost is immediate. How do you evaluate this tradeoff with finite startup capital, and which specific items have highest leverage in shifting the system toward reliability? [Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 07: Growth Pathway, Expansion, and Scaling Dynamics

31. **Expansion pathway decision criteria and brick-and-mortar assumption**: What measurable milestones and decision gates should determine when to pursue fleet expansion vs. brick-and-mortar vs. staying single-truck — what leading indicators (revenue per hour, repeat customer rate, catering demand growth) trigger each path, why assume brick-and-mortar is the natural evolution rather than staying fleet-focused, developing packaged products, or becoming primarily catering, and at what point does the restaurant shift from constraint to stabilizing amplifier? [Analyst, Systems Thinker, Questioner]

32. **Fleet expansion coordination overhead and unit economics**: Adding a second truck can quadruple coordination problems (inventory across locations, overlapping shifts, maintenance, commissary overlap) — at what scale does coordination overhead eat the margins that made expansion attractive, what specific unit economics must the first truck achieve before expansion makes financial sense, and what indicators suggest remaining single-truck indefinitely? [Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker]

33. **Catering vs. daily vending — the Success-to-the-Successful dynamic**: As catering revenue grows it creates a Success-to-the-Successful reinforcing dynamic: catering pays better and builds repeat-booking relationships, pulling resources from street vending, weakening public visibility, which reduces walk-up demand and pushes further into catering. What safeguards prevent over-reliance on a few large catering contracts, how would you maintain consistent daily truck revenue while pursuing sporadic high-value bookings, and how do you design the business to prevent catering from cannibalizing the public presence that makes the brand valuable? [Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker, Questioner]

34. **Seasonality-driven oscillation and weather-independent revenue**: Summer revenue justifies expansion (hiring, equipment, marketing), but fixed costs persist through winter when demand drops — most operators don't see the loop until they're in contraction with commitments they can't unwind. How would you develop revenue streams that aren't weather-dependent (meal kits, corporate contracts, prepared foods), what percentage of annual revenue needs to come from non-truck sources to smooth cash flow, and what leading indicators (not trailing) should inform expansion timing? [Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker]

35. **Location loyalty vs. follower loyalty for multi-unit scaling**: Some customers find you wherever you park; others come because you're at their office, brewery, or event space. The follower model is portable and resilient; the location model builds density and word-of-mouth within specific micro-communities. Which scales better when you add trucks — and how do you design your presence from day one to build the kind of loyalty that supports multi-unit expansion rather than complicating it? [Systems Thinker]

36. **Pricing signals and the reputation feedback loop**: Set prices too low and you attract bargain-seekers rather than quality-seekers; raising prices later feels like a betrayal. The real system effect is word-of-mouth: price determines who tries, who talks, what reputation builds, and what customer the brand attracts — a loop operating over months. What reputation do you want to build, and what pricing strategy creates that specific reinforcing loop rather than leaving it to chance? [Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 08: Founder Capacity, Resources, and Early Relationships

37. **Founder dependency and solo-operator capacity limits**: As a solo operator with no ownership experience, the operation depends on your continuous presence — what happens during illness, family emergencies, or burnout, how would you build systems that generate income during your absence, what's the realistic timeline to hire reliable backup operators, and what operational redundancies prevent single-point failure from day one? [Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

38. **Existing assets and resource activation**: What unique strengths, resources, or capabilities do you already bring that other food truck startups might lack, who in your existing network or community has skills, connections, or resources that could help launch, what specific gifts or capacities do they bring, and how might inviting them into the process from the beginning create mutual benefit rather than transactional exchanges? [Appreciative Inquirer]

39. **Learning from local success patterns**: What aspects of the existing food truck scene are working exceptionally well, what are local trucks doing that customers clearly love, and how might we build on those proven patterns while offering something distinctive? [Appreciative Inquirer]

40. **Immediate low-cost experiment**: What small, concrete experiment could you try this month or next that would both test your concept and begin building the community relationships and customer loyalty you want to see three years from now — and what would you learn from it? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Appended Questions (Perspective, Provocation, and Distinctive-Register Personas)

### Provocateur (Append)

1. **[What if the food disappeared, but the line remained?]** Imagine a food truck where people pay specifically to wait in line — the line itself is the product, and the food is just a free bonus that happens at the end. What would you design into that waiting experience that people would actually pay for? What does this reveal about the hidden value in your concept that you're not currently capturing? *The real insight: what if community, anticipation, and social experience are the actual product, not just a side effect of hunger?*

2. **[What if success meant putting yourself out of business?]** Imagine your explicit goal is to be so successful that you're forced to close within 18 months — either because the city bans you, you can't keep up with demand, or you create a movement that makes your entire concept obsolete. How would you operate differently if runaway success was actually failure? What "safe, reasonable" growth strategies are actually just playing small? *The real insight: what if we're optimizing for sustainability when we should be optimizing for impact — and what does "too successful" even look like?*

3. **[What if the city passed the worst possible ordinance, and it was the best thing that ever happened to you?]** The new restrictions pass: downtown vending hours eliminated, annual fees tripled, food trucks banned within 500 feet of restaurants. But somehow this becomes the catalyst that transforms your business model entirely. What does that transformed business look like, and why is it actually better than the original plan? *The real insight: what if the pending regulatory threat is actually a gift you haven't unwrapped yet?*

### Storyteller (Append)

1. **First Bite Discovery** Imagine it's 11:45 AM on a Tuesday—Maria, an office worker who packed the same sad lunch for years, walks past your truck. What does she see, smell, and hear in those first 10 seconds that compels her to abandon her familiar routine and try something entirely new? How does that first encounter with Filipino-Mexican fusion become a story she tells her coworkers that afternoon?

2. **The Catering Memory** Walk through the moment when a wedding guest tastes your lumpia tacos for the first time during the reception toast—what makes that bite memorable enough that six months later, when they're planning their own corporate event, they search specifically for your truck rather than scrolling through twenty other catering options? What narrative thread connects that single bite to becoming their go-to recommendation?

3. **Regulatory Crossroads** Paint the scene where Elena attends a city council meeting about the proposed food truck restrictions—what does she say in her three minutes of public comment that transforms her from "just another food truck owner complaining" into "the culinary entrepreneur whose testimony makes council members reconsider the human impact of these ordinances"? How does that moment of advocacy ripple through her relationships with other truck owners and city officials?

### Visionary (Append)

1. **What if a food truck functioned less like a portable restaurant and more like a traveling cultural ambassador that creates pop-up community experiences?** Instead of thinking about how to sell Filipino-Mexican fusion from a truck, imagine if the business was fundamentally about creating temporary third spaces — micro-gatherings that transform parking lots, office parks, and neighborhood streets into destinations where food is the catalyst for connection, storytelling, and cultural exchange. What would the business model look like if revenue came from experiences, partnerships, and community-building rather than transactional food sales alone? How might this change conversations with city officials about the truck's "public value"?

2. **How could we completely reimagine the relationship between a mobile food business and the communities it serves — creating something that doesn't exist in the current restaurant or food truck paradigm?** Imagine if the truck operated on a model where neighborhoods "adopt" it as a shared community asset, with residents having input on menu evolution, scheduling, and even profit-sharing in exchange for hosting commitments. What if the business wasn't owned by a single founder but existed as a cooperative ecosystem where customers, suppliers, and the city all have stakes in its success? Could this become a new category of civic infrastructure — mobile, participatory, and collectively governed?

3. **What would this business look like if it were designed from the ground up to NEVER become a traditional restaurant chain, even while achieving the founder's economic and creative goals?** Instead of the conventional trajectory (truck → fleet → brick-and-mortar), imagine entirely new organizational forms: a networked federation of independent owner-operators sharing a unified brand and backend; a guild-style collective that trains and launches new food entrepreneurs under a shared umbrella; a platform business that provides infrastructure, financing, and market access to culinary creators while remaining fundamentally decentralized. What if "scaling" meant multiplying access to opportunity rather than multiplying outlets?

### Analogist (Append)

1. **Migratory patterns and resource distribution** How do migratory bird species optimize their routes and timing to maximize food availability while minimizing competition, and what patterns from their adaptive movement strategies could inform a food truck's approach to location selection, seasonal shifts, and avoiding direct competition with similar trucks in the same geographic zones?

2. **Craft brewery expansion playbook** The craft brewing industry went through a similar phase where successful local breweries navigated the transition from single taproom to regional distribution, with many failing by expanding too quickly into brick-and-mortar before establishing sustainable economics—what specific expansion mistakes and successful patterns from that industry's growth cycle should inform your 3-year transition plan from truck to potential storefront?

3. **Hub-and-spoke vs. distributed network models** Logistics companies like FedEx explicitly chose hub-and-spoke systems over point-to-point delivery for efficiency advantages—does your planned expansion (truck → catering → potential fleet → brick-and-mortar) map better to a hub-and-spoke model with a central kitchen or a distributed model, and what are the operational economics and break-even points where each architecture becomes superior?
