# Brainstorming Questions: Filipino-Mexican Fusion Food Truck

---

## Topic Cluster 01: Vision, Purpose, and Aspirational Foundation

1. **Three-year success picture and peak moments**: Imagine the three-year anniversary — what would loyal customers, team members, and community supporters say the business has contributed to the city's food culture, what hidden assets and peak culinary moments (dishes that delighted people) would you most want to build the experience around, and what daily practices would keep you connected to that purpose during hard seasons? [Appreciative Inquirer]

2. **Existing assets and network activation**: What unique strengths, capabilities, and relationships do you already bring that other food truck startups lack, and who in your existing network has skills, connections, or resources that could be invited into the launch as mutual-benefit collaborators rather than transactional vendors? [Appreciative Inquirer]

3. **Learning from what works in the local scene**: Which specific practices of the existing local food trucks and other successful small food businesses are clearly working — beloved menu patterns, pricing that honors both customer and viability, cash-flow rhythms that respect seasonality — and how might we amplify those proven patterns while offering something distinctive? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 02: Fusion Concept Validation and Differentiation

4. **Fusion concept validation vs. novelty trap**: What evidence suggests Filipino-Mexican fusion has sustainable appeal in this mid-size market beyond initial curiosity, what market research validates that local palates will embrace it rather than treat it as a fading novelty (as Devil's Advocate warns has happened outside coastal markets), and what minimum-capital test (pop-up, farmers market, limited menu) would let you distinguish genuine loyalty from one-time curiosity before full buildout? [Questioner, Devil's Advocate]

5. **Authentic fusion vs. appropriation**: Because fusion cuisine can feel inauthentic or exploitative, what would meaningful co-creation with local Filipino and Mexican communities look like — which signature dishes, flavor combinations, and culinary traditions should we center to honor both traditions, and how might the menu educate customers unfamiliar with these cuisines? [Audience Advocate, Appreciative Inquirer]

6. **Differentiation in a saturated fusion field**: With 4 direct fusion competitors and 30 trucks overall, what makes this concept meaningfully distinct beyond menu items (location strategy, menu-innovation frequency, social engagement, pricing, or brand experience), what measurable factors actually correlate with sustained success among those 30, and how can the differentiation be communicated instantly at curbside? [Analyst, Questioner]

## Topic Cluster 03: Unit Economics, Pricing, and Menu Profitability

7. **Food cost ratio and pricing elasticity**: Breaking core menu items (lumpia tacos, adobo burritos, ube horchata) to component-level costs, what target food-cost percentage (25–35%) should guide pricing given that fusion ingredients like ube and adobo may run higher than standard taco fare, and how do you validate whether the local market will support $12–16 plate pricing without alienating price-sensitive lunch crowds? [Analyst, Accountant]

8. **Menu profitability mix and contribution margins**: Which items should serve as thin-margin volume drivers versus high-margin signature items, how do you engineer the menu so the top 3 sellers cover at least 60% of daily fixed costs, and what price-discovery testing methods would reveal the threshold where customers begin choosing alternatives before pricing is locked in? [Accountant, Questioner]

9. **Variable cost sensitivity and unit-economics pressure testing**: Which variable costs (food, labor, fuel, commissary, maintenance) have the greatest impact on profitability at scale, what happens to margins if food costs run 5–8% higher than projected, and which menu items become unsustainable first under that pressure? [Analyst, Questioner]

10. **Break-even analysis across revenue streams**: Given fixed costs (truck depreciation/financing, insurance, permits, commissary fees, labor) and the $100K starting capital, what daily revenue threshold must you clear, what are the unit economics and break-even points for each stream (daily vending, catering, events), and which combination minimizes cash-flow risk in the first 12 months? [Analyst, Accountant]

## Topic Cluster 04: Capital Structure, Debt, and Adaptive Capacity

11. **SBA loan risk-adjusted decision (Shifting-the-Burden risk)**: Under what revenue projections and confidence intervals does the $40K SBA microloan (~$750–850/month debt service for 7 years) become rational versus reckless, how does that fixed obligation constrain adaptive capacity during seasonal downturns or worst-case scenarios (winter, rainy seasons, event cancellations), and what early-warning indicators would trigger shifting from growth investment to survival mode before debt service becomes a Shifting-the-Burden dynamic where short-term cash obligations crowd out long-term strategy? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker, Accountant]

12. **Option value of staying undercapitalized**: What innovation, resource-discipline, and resilience advantages emerge from bootstrapping with $60K instead of $100K (what Systems Thinker calls the option value of capital scarcity), how would a leaner launch prioritize or defer key investments, and how does that compare against the restrictive-covenant risks of the SBA loan? [Systems Thinker, Questioner]

13. **Equipment ownership vs. leasing across time horizons**: What is the total-cost-of-ownership comparison between purchasing a used outfitted truck versus leasing — factoring depreciation, maintenance, tax implications, resale value, repairs, and downtime — and how does each option reshape your risk profile and flexibility to pivot or exit over short, medium, and long horizons (including toward potential brick-and-mortar)? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker, Accountant]

## Topic Cluster 05: Cash Flow, Seasonality, and Weather Resilience

14. **Seasonal cash-flow modeling and reserves**: Given food truck revenue typically fluctuates 40–60% between peak (May–Oct) and winter, what working-capital buffer and cash reserves are truly required to cover fixed costs through off-season months without compromising ingredient quality or staffing, and is $100K sufficient capital to weather year one? [Analyst, Accountant, Questioner]

15. **Valley-of-death and downside contingency**: Food trucks often need 12–18 months to reach consistent profitability — how have you modeled the "valley of death" between initial capital depletion and sustainable cash flow, and what contingencies activate if revenue runs 30% below projections for the first 8 months? [Devil's Advocate]

16. **Weather-independent revenue streams**: How would you develop revenue that isn't weather-dependent (meal kits, corporate contracts, prepared-food lines), and what percentage of annual revenue needs to come from non-truck sources to smooth cash flow? [Devil's Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 06: Regulatory Environment and Ordinance Strategy

17. **Ordinance scenario modeling and financial impact**: Model best-case, worst-case, and compromise scenarios for the pending ordinance (restricted downtown hours, new annual licensing fees around $1,500): what is the revenue impact on your highest-margin location, what contingency metrics would trigger strategic pivots, and at what point does operating in secondary zones become more economically viable despite lower foot traffic? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Accountant]

18. **Anticipatory compliance and pre-developed pivots**: If the ordinance passes in its most restrictive form — effectively eliminating highest-traffic locations — what adaptive pivots have you pre-developed (private property arrangements, suburban expansion, catering-heavy model), and what alternative revenue streams could be activated immediately? [Questioner, Devil's Advocate]

19. **Proactive regulatory engagement centering community voice**: Rather than reacting to ordinances, what would it look like to build coalitions with other truck owners and present data-driven arguments to city council — and to advocate in a way that centers the low-income workers, evening service workers, and transit-dependent residents most affected, rather than just business interests? [Questioner, Audience Advocate]

20. **Self-defeating feedback loops in regulation (Shifting the Burden)**: If new restrictions push trucks into residential or less-regulated areas and that generates noise/parking/waste complaints, how could that political feedback create a Shifting-the-Burden dynamic where each quick regulatory fix masks the underlying issue and leads to even stricter rules — and what engagement strategies would interrupt the cycle before it accelerates? [Systems Thinker]

21. **Regulatory capture and association dynamics**: At what point does the food truck association shift from advocacy to competitive capture that protects incumbents against newcomers through permit caps or expensive "quality standards," how do you assess whether the regulatory environment genuinely welcomes competition, and what early relationship-building prevents marginalization in permit allocation and ordinance negotiations? [Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker]

22. **Commissary dependency**: If the required commissary kitchen becomes unavailable, prohibitively expensive, or burdensome (typical fees $800–1,500/month), what alternative compliance arrangements exist — shared-use commercial kitchens, restaurant off-hour partnerships, or eventually building your own prep facility — and how do the economics compare? [Questioner, Accountant]

## Topic Cluster 07: Competition, Market Saturation, and Defensibility

23. **Market saturation and failure-mode evidence**: With 30 food trucks and 4 fusion competitors already operating, what evidence beyond general industry growth supports demand for another entrant, how would you pressure-test revenue projections by interviewing failed operators to learn their actual obstacles, and what minimum viable revenue would trigger a "stop loss" decision point? [Devil's Advocate]

24. **Competitive convergence and defensibility (Fixes-that-Fail)**: Filipino-Mexican fusion feels distinctive now, but success attracts imitation — what prevents better-capitalized competitors from copying the concept and out-executing on location, marketing, or scale (a Fixes-that-Fail pattern where menu-innovation-as-moat produces only short-lived distinction)? How do you build durable moats around brand, experience, and relationships rather than just menu innovation? [Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 08: Catering Strategy and Revenue Diversification

25. **Catering economics, concentration risk, and opportunity cost**: What is true profitability per labor-hour for catering versus daily vending after setup/breakdown, transportation, equipment wear, and opportunity cost of a missed prime location (e.g., an $800–1,200 catering gig versus $300–500 at a vending spot in the same window), what safeguards prevent over-reliance on a few large contracts (with feast-or-famine volatility), and how do you maintain consistent daily revenue while pursuing sporadic high-value bookings? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Accountant]

26. **Revenue-stream correlation and "Shifting the Burden" to catering**: To what extent are daily vending, catering, and event revenues correlated or countercyclical, and how might early success with catering create a Shifting-the-Burden dynamic where the stabilizer gradually erodes street presence and brand visibility until catering becomes the main business — a pivot that may be strategic or self-defeating? [Analyst, Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 09: Growth Path — Fleet, Brick-and-Mortar, and Premature Scaling

27. **Fleet expansion prerequisites and diseconomies**: What quantified prerequisites (monthly revenue consistency, margin stability, repeat-customer rate) should gate adding a second truck, what capital ($50–70K) and incremental revenue (often only 70–80% of first truck) should be modeled, and at what scale do coordination costs (scheduling, inventory, QC, staff) grow faster than marginal revenue — the leading indicator that would tell you you've hit the ceiling? [Analyst, Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker, Accountant]

28. **Brick-and-mortar crossover and premature scaling distortion**: What does the unit-economics comparison look like between truck and small storefront (rent, labor efficiency, throughput, weather dependency, build-out costs), and how might the 3-year brick-and-mortar goal be distorting current decisions by forcing premature investment in systems a single truck doesn't need? What evidence would support staying fleet-focused, developing a packaged product line, or becoming primarily catering instead? [Analyst, Questioner, Systems Thinker, Accountant]

## Topic Cluster 10: Operations, Founder Capacity, and Systems

29. **Solo-founder existential risk and operational redundancies**: As a solo operator with no ownership experience, what happens to revenue during illness, family emergency, or burnout; what operational redundancies can be built from day one to prevent single-point failure; and what is a realistic timeline to hire the first reliable backup operator? [Devil's Advocate, Questioner]

30. **Operational bottlenecks and throughput limits**: In a single-truck operation with solo-founder constraints, where do throughput bottlenecks actually emerge (prep time, service speed, inventory), and what measurements would identify these constraints before they impact revenue? [Analyst]

31. **Hidden costs and replacement budgeting**: Beyond obvious operating costs, what less-visible expenses should be budgeted — generator replacement (2–3 years under heavy use), ingredient spoilage during weather closures, emergency repairs, parking tickets in unclear zones — and how much contingency should they add to monthly burn? [Accountant]

## Topic Cluster 11: Customer Experience, Access, and Community Equity

32. **Customer acquisition channels and CAC/LTV**: What are the most effective customer acquisition channels (social media, foot traffic, word-of-referral, catering partnerships), and what is the estimated CAC and LTV ratio for each? [Analyst]

33. **Structural access barriers across the customer journey**: At each step of the customer journey (approaching, reading the menu, asking, ordering, paying, waiting, eating, disposing), what physical, economic, and social barriers might prevent different community members from participating — truck height and wheelchair accessibility, language access and menu comprehension, payment methods and digital divide, dietary and cultural food practices, sensory sensitivities, and the dignity of the ordering experience — and what concrete design choices would shift who feels welcomed? [Audience Advocate]

34. **Customers traditionally excluded from food-truck plans**: Traditional food-truck marketing focuses on office workers, event attendees, and bar crowds — what about people experiencing food insecurity, seniors with limited mobility, or families in neighborhoods underserved by restaurants? How might the business model include or exclude them, and what would designing for accessibility rather than just convenience look like? [Audience Advocate]

35. **Pricing strategy grounded in community economic realities**: What would it look like to genuinely understand what different community members can and will pay, where they perceive value, and what "affordable" actually means — not as abstract market research but as real tradeoffs with real food budgets — and how might portion and pricing strategy reflect that awareness? [Audience Advocate]

36. **Trust, cultural memory, and invisible values**: How do past negative experiences with food trucks (food poisoning, cultural appropriation, opportunistic "authenticity," broken community promises) shape initial trust, and how might invisible practices (sourcing ethics, labor conditions, environmental impact, profit distribution, reinvestment) be made visible in ways that feel genuine rather than performative? [Audience Advocate]

37. **Differential generational and cultural relationships with street food**: Older residents from certain cultures may associate street vending with economic necessity, while younger residents see it as trendy — how do you navigate these perspectives and class assumptions without alienating any group? [Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 12: Brand, Location, and Word-of-Mouth Dynamics

38. **Short-term revenue vs. long-term brand building**: Maximizing immediate revenue means chasing high-traffic events and festivals, but consistent locations build regular customers and word-of-mouth — how does short-term revenue focus undermine long-term asset value, and what balance point prevents sacrificing future growth for today's cash flow? [Systems Thinker]

39. **Location volatility and habit formation**: Regular customers form through predictable availability, but food truck schedules are inherently unstable (weather, competition, regulations, private events) — what predictable anchors can you create amidst the volatility, and how do you design communication that converts unpredictability into anticipation rather than frustration? [Systems Thinker]

40. **Word-of-mouth amplification and its limits**: Every delighted customer is a potential referral, but only when they have socially relevant occasions, remember you at the right moment, and their trust transfers — where does the virtuous cycle amplify, where does it naturally attenuate, and what design interventions strengthen amplification without triggering resistance? [Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 13: Community Accountability and Ecosystem Impact

41. **Accountability beyond market success**: If the business thrives financially but fails to employ locally, source regionally, contribute to food security, or support other food entrepreneurs — is that enough? How might you define and measure success in terms of community impact rather than just revenue and growth, and meaningfully share power through menu development, location, hiring, or reinvestment (rather than the gentrifying-entrepreneur pattern where profits rise while long-term residents are displaced)? [Audience Advocate]

42. **Scaling effects on the local food ecosystem**: How might expansion from one truck to fleet or brick-and-mortar change your relationship with the community and existing food ecosystem — potentially consolidating market share, pressuring marginal operators, attracting new entrants, and influencing how the city regulates the industry — and how do you grow in ways that strengthen rather than displace existing vendors? [Audience Advocate, Systems Thinker]

43. **Cooperation-to-competition boundary with peer trucks**: Information sharing, joint marketing, and sub-leased commissary space create value in a fragmented market, but at what point does transparency about locations, pricing, and strategies begin eroding your competitive position? [Systems Thinker]

## Appended Questions (Perspective, Provocation, and Distinctive-Register Personas)

### Provocateur (Append)

1. **[What if the food disappeared, but the line remained?]** Imagine a food truck where people pay specifically to wait in line — the line itself is the product, and the food is just a free bonus that happens at the end. What would you design into that waiting experience that people would actually pay for? What does this reveal about the hidden value in your concept that you're not currently capturing? *The real insight: what if community, anticipation, and social experience are the actual product, not just a side effect of hunger?*

2. **[What if success meant putting yourself out of business?]** Imagine your explicit goal is to be so successful that you're forced to close within 18 months — either because the city bans you, you can't keep up with demand, or you create a movement that makes your entire concept obsolete. How would you operate differently if runaway success was actually failure? What "safe, reasonable" growth strategies are actually just playing small? *The real insight: what if we're optimizing for sustainability when we should be optimizing for impact — and what does "too successful" even look like?*

3. **[What if the city passed the worst possible ordinance, and it was the best thing that ever happened to you?]** The new restrictions pass: downtown vending hours eliminated, annual fees tripled, food trucks banned within 500 feet of restaurants. But somehow this becomes the catalyst that transforms your business model entirely. What does that transformed business look like, and why is it actually better than the original plan? *The real insight: what if the pending regulatory threat is actually a gift you haven't unwrapped yet?*

4. **[What if you had to start with zero capital and $100K in debt?]** Flip the entire financial premise: instead of $60K savings + $40K optional loan, you begin with -$100K and no assets. You cannot spend money to make money — you can only use relationships, creativity, and other people's resources. How does the business model change when every dollar you spend is a dollar you don't have? *The real insight: what if the financial cushion is actually masking a much more interesting opportunity to design a capital-light model?*

5. **[What if the fusion concept itself was the problem?]** Everyone says Filipino-Mexican fusion is your differentiator. What if it's actually a trap — what if the most obvious, headline concept is preventing you from discovering the thing that actually makes you irreplaceable? What would this business look like if fusion was off the table entirely? *The real insight: are you building around a genuine competitive advantage, or just around the first idea that sounded clever?*

### Storyteller (Append)

1. **First Bite Discovery** Imagine it's 11:45 AM on a Tuesday—Maria, an office worker who packed the same sad lunch for years, walks past your truck. What does she see, smell, and hear in those first 10 seconds that compels her to abandon her familiar routine and try something entirely new? How does that first encounter with Filipino-Mexican fusion become a story she tells her coworkers that afternoon?

2. **The Catering Memory** Walk through the moment when a wedding guest tastes your lumpia tacos for the first time during the reception toast—what makes that bite memorable enough that six months later, when they're planning their own corporate event, they search specifically for your truck rather than scrolling through twenty other catering options? What narrative thread connects that single bite to becoming their go-to recommendation?

3. **Solo Founder's Threshold** Envision the founder, Elena, working alone on the truck during the lunch rush when three separate corporate catering inquiries come in simultaneously—how does she handle that moment of decision between saying "yes to growth" and "no, I need to protect the experience I can actually deliver"? What does that choice reveal about the kind of business owner she's becoming, and how does it shape the next chapter of her expansion plans?

4. **Regulatory Crossroads** Paint the scene where Elena attends a city council meeting about the proposed food truck restrictions—what does she say in her three minutes of public comment that transforms her from "just another food truck owner complaining" into "the culinary entrepreneur whose testimony makes council members reconsider the human impact of these ordinances"? How does that moment of advocacy ripple through her relationships with other truck owners and city officials?

5. **Brick-and-Mortar Dream vs. Reality** Let me walk you through the day Elena signs papers on her first physical location, imagining both her excitement about having a permanent kitchen and her quiet anxiety about losing the mobility and flexibility that defined her brand identity—how does she reconcile these conflicting emotions, and what does that reveal about whether expansion should mean abandoning trucks entirely, operating them alongside a restaurant, or finding a different growth path altogether?

### Visionary (Append)

1. **What if a food truck functioned less like a portable restaurant and more like a traveling cultural ambassador that creates pop-up community experiences?** Instead of thinking about how to sell Filipino-Mexican fusion from a truck, imagine if the business was fundamentally about creating temporary third spaces — micro-gatherings that transform parking lots, office parks, and neighborhood streets into destinations where food is the catalyst for connection, storytelling, and cultural exchange. What would the business model look like if revenue came from experiences, partnerships, and community-building rather than transactional food sales alone? How might this change conversations with city officials about the truck's "public value"?

2. **How could we completely reimagine the relationship between a mobile food business and the communities it serves — creating something that doesn't exist in the current restaurant or food truck paradigm?** Imagine if the truck operated on a model where neighborhoods "adopt" it as a shared community asset, with residents having input on menu evolution, scheduling, and even profit-sharing in exchange for hosting commitments. What if the business wasn't owned by a single founder but existed as a cooperative ecosystem where customers, suppliers, and the city all have stakes in its success? Could this become a new category of civic infrastructure — mobile, participatory, and collectively governed?

3. **What would this business look like if it were designed from the ground up to NEVER become a traditional restaurant chain, even while achieving the founder's economic and creative goals?** Instead of the conventional trajectory (truck → fleet → brick-and-mortar), imagine entirely new organizational forms: a networked federation of independent owner-operators sharing a unified brand and backend; a guild-style collective that trains and launches new food entrepreneurs under a shared umbrella; a platform business that provides infrastructure, financing, and market access to culinary creators while remaining fundamentally decentralized. What if "scaling" meant multiplying access to opportunity rather than multiplying outlets?

4. **What if we designed the business to be antifragile — becoming stronger through volatility, regulatory changes, and competitive pressure — rather than merely resilient to them?** Instead of viewing pending city ordinances, competition from similar fusion concepts, or seasonal fluctuations as threats to manage, imagine a business architecture that actually *feeds* on disruption: a modular model that can rapidly reconfigure based on regulatory conditions; a guerrilla logistics network that can pivot operations overnight; a community-political power base that gives the business influence over the very regulations that constrain it. How could constraints become the engine of innovation rather than brakes on growth?

5. **What if the ultimate legacy of this business wasn't its survival as a restaurant but its role as a launchpad for dozens of other food entrepreneurs — a "phoenix model" where the parent vehicle dissolves to seed new entities?** Imagine deliberately designing the truck as a temporary, incubatory platform: each year, it spawns 1-2 spinoff businesses (bricks-and-mortar, new trucks, packaged products, catering concepts) while the original truck might eventually sunset, transform, or be sold. What if success wasn't measured by longevity of the founding entity but by the ecosystem of businesses it catalyzes — and how would that fundamentally alter financial projections, founder psychology, and the very definition of "growth"?

### Analogist (Append)

1. **Migratory patterns and resource distribution** How do migratory bird species optimize their routes and timing to maximize food availability while minimizing competition, and what patterns from their adaptive movement strategies could inform a food truck's approach to location selection, seasonal shifts, and avoiding direct competition with similar trucks in the same geographic zones?

2. **Craft brewery expansion playbook** The craft brewing industry went through a similar phase where successful local breweries navigated the transition from single taproom to regional distribution, with many failing by expanding too quickly into brick-and-mortar before establishing sustainable economics—what specific expansion mistakes and successful patterns from that industry's growth cycle should inform your 3-year transition plan from truck to potential storefront?

3. **Hub-and-spoke vs. distributed network models** Logistics companies like FedEx explicitly chose hub-and-spoke systems over point-to-point delivery for efficiency advantages—does your planned expansion (truck → catering → potential fleet → brick-and-mortar) map better to a hub-and-spoke model with a central kitchen or a distributed model, and what are the operational economics and break-even points where each architecture becomes superior?

4. **Regulatory capture and collective action** Historical examples from taxi medallion systems, liquor licensing, and food cart regulations show that industries facing restrictive regulations either succumb to barriers or organize collectively to influence policy—what strategies from other industries' successful collective action against unfavorable regulatory changes could inform your engagement with the city council process and the local food truck association?

5. **Niche differentiation in ecosystems** In mature ecosystems, competing species evolve specialized niches to coexist rather than compete directly—how might this concept of resource partitioning apply to differentiating your Filipino-Mexican fusion concept from the four existing similar trucks, and what underserved dayparts, locations, or customer segments could represent your equivalent of an unoccupied ecological niche?
