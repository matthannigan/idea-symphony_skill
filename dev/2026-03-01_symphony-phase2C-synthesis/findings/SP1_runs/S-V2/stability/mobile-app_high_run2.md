# Brainstorming Questions: Mobile App Simplification (MVP Reset)

---

## Topic Cluster 01: Defining Core Value and Fundamental Purpose

1. **What problem do users actually hire this app to solve?**: At the atomic level, what is the single core problem a "social fitness app" exists to solve — is it motivation, accountability, knowledge, connection, or something else entirely? The 47-second session suggests users open with a specific intent; the 15 features suggest 15 competing assumptions about need. How many of those assumptions have been tested against fundamental human behavior rather than competitive benchmarking, and how would we know if we're wrong? [Questioner, First Principles Thinker]

2. **The emotional core value beneath the metrics**: Beyond usage data and retention metrics, what's the emotional state users are actually seeking when they open this app — motivation, accountability, calm, connection, achievement — and what unspoken emotional need does feature bloat itself serve (the reassurance of potential, the safety blanket of options)? When we strip features away, what happens to that felt sense of security? [Empath, Questioner]

3. **What fundamental unit of value drives engagement?**: Is it a workout logged, a meal planned, a friend found, a challenge completed? Without understanding the atomic unit of value, we cannot make principled decisions about what to keep. The current 15-feature bundle may be trying to be everything — perhaps "social fitness" isn't one problem but five unrelated problems masquerading as a single product. Should we be simplifying or splitting? [First Principles Thinker, Analyst]

4. **What unique combination could only exist in a single app?**: Rather than competing with dedicated tools on their own terms, what feature combination might create emergent value greater than the sum of its parts — and is that combination present in the current feature set? Users have Strava for tracking, MyFitnessPal for meals, Calm for meditation. What is the first-principles argument for why a single app should combine these, and are we solving a real integration problem or just delivering a bundle of mediocrity? [Questioner, First Principles Thinker]

5. **Redefining the North Star metric**: The current metrics (2% DAU/MAU, 47-second sessions, 8% 30-day retention) signal product-market-fit failure — but engagement isn't the same as value delivered. A simpler app might have shorter sessions because users accomplish what they need faster. What leading indicators should we target instead, and what success metric would actually capture core value delivery rather than the perverse incentive where bloat makes users wander longer? [Analyst, Systems Thinker]

6. **The "one thing" evolution**: If this app became known for doing one thing exceptionally well — the thing that makes users think "I can't live without this" — what would that be? What peak user experiences, moments that went beyond 47 seconds, reveal about where real value already lives, and how would that single focus shape every aspect of the product from onboarding to word-of-mouth? [Appreciative Inquirer, Questioner]

## Topic Cluster 02: Diagnosing Retention and Engagement Signals

1. **Causation versus correlation in retention**: The 8% 30-day retention and 2% DAU/MAU ratio indicate almost no one finds lasting value — but we cannot conclude which features drive retention without controlled experiments. This is a causal inference problem: what would happen if we removed each feature one by one and measured isolated impact? Are we looking for features that cause retention, or features used by the tiny subset who were going to stick around regardless? [First Principles Thinker, Devil's Advocate, Analyst]

2. **What does the 8% who stay actually do differently?**: The retention differential between the 92% who leave and the 8% who remain is a massive signal — but there's a reinforcing dynamic where the most engaged users are likely using a tiny subset of features while the confused majority bounce. What specific behaviors or feature combinations distinguish these groups, and are these survivors of a broken system the users we should optimize for, or the wrong segment entirely? [Questioner, Systems Thinker]

3. **Power users as skewed signal — or as the problem itself**: What if our data is misleading because power users are skewing engagement metrics — the passionate minorities using niche features represent 80% of engagement while the majority bounced immediately? Or inversely, what if the 2% who use the app daily are not the solution but pathological attachments — freak users whose retention is preventing the product from evolving into something healthy for normal humans? [Devil's Advocate, Analyst]

4. **What does "overwhelming" actually mean across segments?**: When users say the app is overwhelming, are they describing cognitive load, decision paralysis, navigation confusion, something else — and how does this differ between a 25-year-old fitness enthusiast and a 55-year-old trying to get healthy? The emotional texture of that 47-second session matters: is it frustration from being lost, overwhelm from choices, guilt from underuse, or something users can't articulate but feel every time? [Empath, Audience Advocate, Questioner]

5. **Cohort segmentation framework**: The key variables for feature prioritization may be user segments rather than aggregate metrics. What distinct cohorts exist (social users, solo trackers, content consumers), and which features serve as "gateway drugs" versus "retention anchors" for each cohort? Before cuts, we should cluster user interviews into distinct personas and test whether a stripped-down MVP serves any of them well. [Analyst, Devil's Advocate]

6. **What gaps exist between what users say and what they do?**: The 47-second session and 2% DAU/MAU suggest users aren't engaging as intended. How might users' expressed preferences differ from their revealed behaviors, what frustrations have they normalized as "just how apps work," and what experiments could reveal the gap between stated desire and actual need? [Audience Advocate, Questioner]

7. **Sessions as destination, not deficit**: What if the 47-second average session isn't a problem to solve but the product itself — a micro-intervention that delivers maximum value in under a minute, built for the interstitial moments of daily life? [User Q where relevant] [Analyst, Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 03: Feature Prioritization and Dependency Mapping

1. **Feature interaction and keystone features**: If we remove certain features, what's the structural impact on remaining functionality? Some features might be "keystone species" whose removal disproportionately harms the ecosystem, while others may be "invasive species" crowding out core value. How do we map hidden dependencies, and how would we detect if the core value is actually an unexpected combination of 2-3 features working together — rather than treating each feature as an independent silo? [Analyst, Systems Thinker, Connector]

2. **Dependencies versus dependents**: Some features might exist primarily to support others rather than provide standalone value. How would mapping these dependency relationships change what we consider "core" versus "ancillary," and what does notification architecture reveal as a dependency map in disguise — since features that don't generate meaningful notifications may be features users don't actually value? [Questioner, Connector]

3. **Success-to-the-Successful archetype in current feature distribution**: The current 15-feature structure creates a "Success to the Successful" dynamic where popular features get more attention and investment, making them more popular, while neglected features atrophy — this self-reinforcing pattern may be amplifying the wrong things. How do we interrupt the archetype so signals about emerging niche value aren't drowned out? [Systems Thinker, Analyst]

4. **Small but passionate bases as future core signal**: A feature with 2% adoption but 80% retention among those users could be a foothold into an underserved niche — or a dead-end. What leading indicators distinguish "early adopters of the future core" from "enthusiastic users of a peripheral feature"? And what might we learn from deeply understanding these micro-community users whose experiences might reveal the app's true north? [Systems Thinker, Audience Advocate, Devil's Advocate]

5. **Features That Sparked Joy — and what made them resonate**: Among the 15 features, at least one must have elicited enthusiasm, delight, sustained engagement, or strong loyalty. Which feature produced the most positive feedback, and what made it resonate — the utility, the experience, the social connection, or something else entirely? What structures or principles could amplify those strengths if the entire app were built around that core? [Appreciative Inquirer]

6. **Poor execution versus wrong concept**: How do we distinguish between features that are poorly executed versus features users simply don't want? A feature might have low engagement because it's bad, not because the concept is wrong. Would A/B testing existing features with UX improvements reveal different answers than we'd get by cutting them outright? [Devil's Advocate, First Principles Thinker]

7. **Is the problem interface, not feature count?**: We're framing this as removing features, but the fundamental UX problem might be cognitive load. Could we solve the core problem by radically simplifying the interface and onboarding without removing any features — showing each user only the 2-3 relevant to their goals? Applying information-theory entropy principles, which UI elements are signal (essential) versus noise (eliminable)? [First Principles Thinker, Connector, Constraint Flipper]

8. **One feature done exceptionally, not 3-4 done adequately**: The assumption that 3-4 features represents the right scope might itself be wrong. What would a true single-feature version look like, and how would we test whether that's too radical? [Questioner, First Principles Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 04: Equity, Access, and Whose Needs Define "Essential"

1. **Structural barriers across tech literacy, language, and accessibility**: An app with 15 features and complex navigation likely presents significant barriers. How might feature prioritization change if we centered users with limited digital literacy, non-English speakers, or users who rely on assistive technologies? Which features create disproportionate documentation and language barriers, and whose experiences have been erased by aggregate usage data? [Audience Advocate]

2. **"Essential" from the perspective of scarcity of time and bandwidth**: The team is thinking about essential features from a product strategy perspective, but what would essential mean to a single parent with 5 minutes to spare, or someone managing chronic illness who needs simple tracking without cognitive overload? [Audience Advocate]

3. **Power dynamics in feature prioritization**: Feature prioritization often reflects the perspectives of those with the loudest voices or most data visibility. Whose needs and use cases might be systematically overlooked in determining what's "essential," and what marginalized-stakeholder perspectives are being filtered out? [Audience Advocate]

4. **Disproportionate impact of notification and feature overload**: Users report "too many notifications," but which communities are most impacted? Are we driving away the very users who might benefit most from a simplified, focused experience — and is the cultural-inclusion dimension of notification design being overlooked? [Audience Advocate]

5. **Understanding users' contexts beyond the app**: The data shows what users do in the app, but what might we learn about their broader lives — their constraints, motivations, environments — that would inform which features actually matter in their real, messy lives? How do cultural and socioeconomic contexts shape what "essential" means? [Audience Advocate]

## Topic Cluster 05: User Emotional Experience of Loss, Transition, and Identity

1. **Grief as a design signal**: Which feature cuts, if announced tomorrow, would trigger genuine emotional loss in users rather than just annoyance or inconvenience — and what does that tell us about which features actually form emotional bonds versus functional utility? How might users experience a feature reduction — as simplification or as loss, and what's the emotional reality of being a user who deeply relied on a now-removed feature? [Empath, Audience Advocate]

2. **Identity disruption when the "all-in-one" becomes "just one thing"**: When users have built their fitness identity around this app being their all-in-one solution, what happens to their sense of themselves when we say "actually, we're just this one focused thing now"? What identity have existing users built around the current app, and could removing features feel like a betrayal of that identity regardless of usage metrics? [Empath, Questioner]

3. **Betrayal or relief in the moment of discovery**: When users open the simplified app and find their favorite feature gone, what's the visceral split-second reaction — and how does that differ between the 2% who actually use it daily versus the 92% who've never touched it? Are we assuming existing users will tolerate radical simplification without warning or migration support? [Empath, Devil's Advocate]

4. **The belonging paradox**: If we simplify to the 3-4 features that represent our true core, what happens to the users who found belonging and community in the features we cut — and is that a necessary sacrifice or a failure of imagination? [Empath]

5. **Repairing a broken relationship**: How might the team rebuild trust with users who feel burned by the app's current state? Users who stuck around despite poor experience may feel betrayed or skeptical — what would it take to demonstrate genuine commitment to their needs rather than just business survival, and could the relaunch be an opportunity to acknowledge past missteps and invite users into a new chapter? [Audience Advocate]

6. **Hidden assets in the existing user base**: With 180K downloads, even with low retention, there are users who found value. Who are they? What specific use case or demographic found this app worthwhile, and what might we learn from the 2% who show up daily — what are they doing that keeps them coming back? [Appreciative Inquirer]

## Topic Cluster 06: Team Dynamics, Morale, and Capacity

1. **Burnout as symptom of emotional weight, not just workload**: Is the team's burnout actually from maintaining 15 features, or from the emotional weight of knowing none of them are good enough? Would simplification feel like relief or like admitting defeat — and what's the hidden resistance to building something simple (fear of being bored, seeming less ambitious, having nowhere to hide if it fails)? [Empath]

2. **The grief of killed darlings**: What's the emotional reality for the engineer who spent months building the community forum or the designer who crafted 50 meditation screen flows — and how do we honor that investment without letting it hijack the simplification conversation? How might the team's emotional attachments to features mirror users', creating blind spots that prevent separating ego from evidence? [Empath, Questioner]

3. **The "defensive attachment to proactive pruning" leverage point**: The team has invested emotionally in building these 15 features; cutting them feels like admitting failure. If people feel ownership over "their" features, they resist cuts, which maintains bloat, which increases maintenance burden, which lowers morale further. What's the smallest intervention that could flip this self-reinforcing dynamic from defensive attachment to proactive pruning? [Systems Thinker]

4. **Team's brightest moments and strengths to amplify**: This team of six engineers, one designer, and one PM has been through a lot. When did the team feel most energized, proud, or effective, and what conditions made that moment possible? What strengths does each team member bring that could be amplified in a leaner, more focused environment — what simplified product would align with what they're already good at, making simplification feel like liberation rather than loss? [Appreciative Inquirer]

5. **Maintaining morale when engineers must delete their work**: How do we maintain team morale when engineers have spent years building features they're now asked to delete? The team might feel their work was wasted, leading to disengagement or attrition precisely when we need them most. Could we frame cuts as strategic pivots, celebrate learning from each feature, and tie bonuses to the relaunch's success rather than lines of code removed? [Devil's Advocate]

6. **Capacity surplus and the slow-return-to-bloat risk**: Six engineers maintaining 15 features are stretched thin, but six engineers maintaining 3 features suddenly have excess capacity — creating a self-amplifying opportunity for each remaining feature to become dramatically better. But excess capacity can also lead to scope creep, where the team slowly adds features back until they're bloated again. What structural constraints or processes would prevent the "slow return to bloat" pattern? [Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 07: Unit Economics and Financial Viability

1. **Unit economics per feature (LTV:CAC)**: What's the lifetime value and acquisition cost for users of each feature cluster, and which features are currently burning cash without any path to positive unit economics? With 180K downloads and only 2% DAU/MAU, most features are likely costing more to maintain than they generate — but which specific features have demonstrable LTV:CAC ratios that justify existence, and which are economic dead ends? [Accountant]

2. **Revenue attribution across the feature set**: If the app has revenue streams (subscriptions, in-app purchases, marketplace fees, coaching commissions), how does revenue attribute across the 15 features, and what percentage of total revenue comes from the top 3 features versus the long tail? The economic reality is likely that 80% of revenue comes from 20% of features — what's the opportunity cost of maintaining the revenue-negative features? [Accountant]

3. **Maintenance cost per active user**: What's the annual maintenance cost per active user for each feature, factoring engineering time, server costs, third-party APIs, and support overhead? With 6 engineers maintaining 15 features (roughly 2.5 features per engineer), features like marketplace, supplement store, and coaching service likely have dramatically higher cost structures than progress journal or habit tracker. Does that cost align with value delivered? [Accountant]

4. **Churn cost versus elimination savings**: What's the economic trade-off between revenue lost from churning passionate users of cut features versus ongoing maintenance savings and potential retention gains? If removing the meditation feature loses 1,000 dedicated users but improves 30-day retention from 8% to 15% for the remaining base, what's the break-even point in terms of user LTV and acquisition cost recovery? [Accountant]

5. **Retention improvement economics**: What's the revenue impact of improving 30-day retention from 8% to industry benchmarks (20-30% for fitness apps), and how does that compare to the revenue loss from eliminating features? If simplification increases retention to 20%, that's a 2.5x increase in user lifetime, which could justify losing significant portions of the current base. [Accountant]

6. **Unit economics of feature simplification**: What does the cost structure look like per feature — engineering maintenance load, customer support burden, server costs, opportunity cost — and how do we calculate the ROI of cutting features in terms of resource freed versus revenue/user impact risk? [Analyst]

7. **Funding scenario modeling against 8-month runway**: Under different funding scenarios (extending current runway, raising a bridge round, cutting to breakeven), what's the minimum viable feature set that achieves sustainability, and what's the feature set that maximizes growth potential? The 8-month runway forces an economic decision: is the goal to survive as long as possible or demonstrate growth potential for a raise? [Accountant]

8. **Post-simplification operating budget and break-even**: What's the target monthly burn rate for the simplified product, and how does that change the break-even point in terms of paying users or ad revenue? Fewer features should drop server costs and raise engineering efficiency — but by how much, and what's the new economic model (e.g., 5,000 paying users at $5/month versus 20,000 active users at $0.50 ARPU)? [Accountant]

## Topic Cluster 08: Runway, Timing, and Transition Strategy

1. **Runway optimization scenarios**: With 8 months of funding remaining and a team of 8, what's the break-even analysis for different simplification strategies — how much does reducing feature surface area lower burn rate versus extending runway, and at what point does simplification trade long-term viability for short-term survival? [Analyst]

2. **Limits-to-Growth and the runway-versus-rigor trade**: There's a dangerous delay in this system — we won't know if the simplified product works until 3-6 months after launch, but we only have 8 months of runway total. Move too fast, we may cut the wrong features; move too slow, we run out of money. This time pressure can force "Shifting-the-Burden" decisions (cutting corners now that create bigger problems later). What's the minimum viable timeline for doing this right, and what do we trade if we compress it? [Systems Thinker]

3. **Incremental simplification versus clean relaunch**: What are the comparative risk profiles and economics of gradual simplification versus clean relaunch — considering user confusion, technical complexity, market perception, team bandwidth, and acquisition costs? Incremental feels safer but creates a self-reinforcing pattern of compromise where "just one more feature" erodes goals; clean relaunch risks shocking 180K users at once. [Analyst, Systems Thinker, Accountant]

4. **Phased rollout as risk mitigator**: What if we can't ship a simplified product within 8 months because the remaining features are more complex than estimated? Removing 11 features doesn't automatically mean less work — the remaining 3-4 might need complete rebuilds to stand alone. Could we scope the relaunch as a phased rollout where we cut features incrementally rather than all at once? [Devil's Advocate]

5. **Feature team reallocation economics**: With 8 months of runway and a burned-out team, what's the ROI of reallocating engineers from feature maintenance to growth initiatives (acquisition, retention optimization, monetization), and what's the timeline to seeing that ROI materialize within remaining runway? [Accountant]

6. **The withdrawal period after cuts**: What happens to user behavior during the 3-6 month period after feature removal when habits formed around removed features are still present but the features are gone? Users who built routines around the meal planner or meditation guide will experience withdrawal — a balancing loop where frustration drives them away — but if the simplified experience is genuinely better, frustration may transition to relief, creating a virtuous cycle. What determines which loop dominates? [Systems Thinker]

## Topic Cluster 09: Technical Architecture and Debt

1. **Technical debt quantification per feature**: How do we measure the maintenance cost of technical debt embedded in each feature — what's the difference between "delete-and-forget" features versus "excise-carefully" features with deep system integration, and what's the engineering effort distribution across the feature portfolio? [Analyst]

2. **Cutting features may INCREASE technical debt ("Fixes-that-Fail")**: Fifteen deeply integrated features likely share authentication, databases, APIs, analytics — removing features could break dependencies across the codebase or leave ghost code that still runs (maintenance burden without user value). This is a classic Fixes-that-Fail pattern: we cut features to simplify, but technical complexity remains, and the team maintains hidden code for features users can't even access. Have we audited the architecture and built a removal dependency graph rather than assuming clean cuts? [Devil's Advocate, Systems Thinker]

3. **When does rebuild beat refactor?**: If the 15 features are deeply integrated, removing them piecemeal may be more complex and risky than rebuilding from scratch. At what level of coupling does "simplification by subtraction" become more expensive than "simplification by reconstruction"? What is the threshold where the latter becomes the rational choice? [First Principles Thinker]

4. **Technical debt paydown ROI**: What's the cost-benefit analysis of paying down technical debt from removed features versus living with a "ghost" codebase, and how does the maintenance cost curve change over 12-24 months depending on which approach you take? What's the break-even point where engineering savings from removal exceed the cost of extraction? [Accountant]

5. **Zero-based budget for features**: Imagine we started from scratch today, knowing everything we know about user behavior and market dynamics, but with no legacy code, no existing features, no sunk costs. What would we build? If the answer is "something completely different from what we have," what does that tell us about whether simplification is even the right frame — or whether we have the wrong features built on outdated assumptions? [First Principles Thinker]

3. **First experiments the team could run this month**: What small, low-risk experiments could the team try this month to test which features truly represent the app's core value? 5. **Framing: focus versus simplification, cuts versus refinement**: The language we use to describe changes may dramatically affect user reception — how would different frames (focusing, refining, specializing, doubling down) alter the emotional impact of losing features, and is one narrative actually true, or are we choosing the story that serves us? 7. **Co-creation rather than announcement**: How could we involve users in the simplification decisions rather than announcing them? 8. **Graceful migration that honors loyalty**: Considering the small but passionate user bases for certain features, how might we honor their commitment while simplifying? 9. **Sunset rather than sudden removal**: What if we gradually hid features rather than removing them outright? A sunset approach where features become progressively harder to access might provide softer transitions and better data — but would this undermine the clarity of a focused product? [Questioner]

10. **Anger as data, not threat**: How do we avoid angering passionate minorities without drowning in feature requests? Users who love the supplement store, meditation guide, or friend finder might leave en masse and leave negative reviews — but their reactions may be the most valuable research available. Which of our 15 features would be most compelling in an AR-first interface? [Futurist]

11. **What would competitors do if we simplified successfully?**: A focused, successful relaunch might provoke competitive responses — how might single-purpose apps react to our newfound clarity, and what defensive or offensive opportunities does that create? ## Appended Questions (Perspective, Provocation, and Distinctive-Register Personas)

### Provocateur (Append)

1. **What if you kept exactly ONE feature and made it absurdly worse?** Imagine you stripped the app down to just workout tracking, but then removed all logging, history, progress visualization, and data—leaving only the immediate act of pressing a button that says "I worked out" before the app closes itself. What would this useless button reveal about what users actually crave from the tracking experience? *The real insight: is the value in the data or in the ritual of acknowledgment?*

2. **What if the app did nothing but DELETE features for the next 8 months?** Every week, one feature disappears permanently with no replacement, no explanation, and no user choice—until month 9 when the app becomes entirely blank. What would user behavior and retention patterns in this controlled demolition reveal about which features create genuine dependence versus which create mere noise? *The real insight: what if feature removal itself—rather than the final simplified state—was the product experience that taught you what matters?*
