### Selection Rationale

**Connector/Analogist decision:** Analogist (default)
- Swap rationale: The REQUEST describes a single business (one food truck operation) navigating its operating environment — permits, pricing, competition, a pending city ordinance, and a potential growth path. This is the explicit negative-swap example ("single-domain business strategy — one business navigating permits, pricing, competition"). Criterion (b) does not apply: there are stakeholders (city council, food truck association, customers, lender) but only one institutional decision-making system is being designed — the founder's business — not distinct institutional systems that must be reconciled with each other. Effort-invariant: the same call holds at medium.

**Tier 3 selections — structured rationale per candidate persona:**

- Persona: Accountant
- Trigger strength: strong
- Topic citation: "Pricing strategy and unit economics (food cost ratios, break-even analysis)… $60K savings + potential $40K SBA microloan… Cash flow management through seasonal fluctuations… Equipment financing vs. leasing tradeoffs… Whether to pursue the SBA loan or bootstrap more conservatively"
- Decision: include
  - Q1b gating test satisfied: the financial dimension IS the core decision axis, not a background bullet. If all financial content (unit economics, pricing, capital structure, cash flow, SBA decision) were removed from the REQUEST, the central brainstorming question — how to build a sustainable multi-revenue-stream business from a single truck — would collapse. The REQUEST names specific financial mechanisms (dollar amounts, loan size, food cost ratios, break-even analysis, leasing-vs-financing).

- Persona: Lawyer
- Trigger strength: none
- Topic citation: "city requires food truck permits, commissary kitchen use, and designated vending zones; city council is currently debating new food truck ordinances that would restrict downtown vending hours and add annual licensing fees"
- Decision: exclude
  - Operating-environment-permits trap applies: permits, commissary requirements, vending zones, and licensing fees are routine operating constraints for a single small business to comply with, not a compliance architecture to design. No named statutory regime (HIPAA, FDA, STR ordinance) is in play. The strategic dimension of the pending ordinance is stakeholder-political (engagement with city council and the food truck association), which Politician covers; the financial dimension (annual licensing fees, vending-hour restrictions on revenue) is covered by Accountant.

- Persona: Politician
- Trigger strength: strong
- Topic citation: "city council is currently debating new food truck ordinances… how to engage with the regulatory process… Building relationships with city officials and the local food truck association"
- Decision: include
  - The political/governance mechanism is central, not ambient: the decision on how to shape and respond to the pending ordinance hinges on coalition-building with the local food truck association and direct engagement with city council. This is stakeholder politics and institutional-change coalition assembly, explicitly named as a key consideration, not mere "team morale" or "user communication."

- Persona: Technical Expert
- Trigger strength: none
- Topic citation: "Equipment financing vs. leasing tradeoffs"
- Decision: exclude
  - No hardware/ML/systems-architecture decisions. Equipment choice is an operational/financial question, not engineering architecture.
