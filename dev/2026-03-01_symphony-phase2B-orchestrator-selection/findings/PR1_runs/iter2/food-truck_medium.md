### Selection Rationale

**Connector/Analogist decision:** Analogist (default)
- Swap rationale: Food-truck startup is single-domain business strategy — one solo founder operating one business within one city's operating environment. No distinct institutional systems must be reconciled; permits, pricing, and competition are facets of a single business navigating its market. Explicitly listed as an Analogist-keep example (single-business strategy). Effort-invariance confirmed: this decision would be the same at high effort because it is a topic-structure decision, not an effort-level decision.

**Tier 3 selections — structured rationale per candidate persona:**

- Persona: Accountant
  - Trigger strength: strong
  - Topic citation: "Develop a sustainable food truck business with clear pricing strategy, unit economics, and a realistic path from single truck to multi-revenue-stream operation"; "Pricing strategy and unit economics (food cost ratios, break-even analysis)"; "Starting Capital: $60K savings + potential $40K SBA microloan"; "Equipment financing vs. leasing tradeoffs"
  - Decision: include
  - Q1b gating note: Passes — financial structure is a core decision axis, not a background bullet. Removing the financial content (pricing, unit economics, break-even, SBA loan, equipment financing, cash flow) would collapse the central brainstorming question; the Goals statement explicitly names pricing strategy and unit economics as the outcome to develop. Specific dollar figures ($60K, $40K) and named financial mechanisms (food cost ratios, break-even analysis, equipment financing vs. leasing) clear the moderate bar and reach strong.

- Persona: Lawyer
  - Trigger strength: none
  - Topic citation: "city requires food truck permits, commissary kitchen use, and designated vending zones; city council is currently debating new food truck ordinances that would restrict downtown vending hours and add annual licensing fees"
  - Decision: exclude
  - Rationale: Operating-environment-permits trap applies directly. The permit regime and pending ordinance are routine local operating-environment constraints for a single small business, not a multi-jurisdiction regulatory strategy or named statutory regime (HIPAA, FDA, STR ordinance) the founder must design around. The brainstorming need is to navigate and engage with these constraints, not to architect compliance. Accountant (financial modeling of licensing fees) and Politician (engagement with city council, if triggered) cover the strategic dimensions.

- Persona: Politician
  - Trigger strength: moderate
  - Topic citation: "Building relationships with city officials and the local food truck association"; "The pending city ordinance changes and how to engage with the regulatory process"
  - Decision: exclude
  - Rationale: Real stakeholder-political content (coalition with food truck association, engaging city council on pending ordinance) but not central to the brainstorming goal. The stated goal is a sustainable food truck business with pricing strategy, unit economics, and growth path — political engagement is one listed consideration among many, ambient rather than the decision hinge. Moderate trigger; at medium effort the threshold is strong-only.

- Persona: Technical Expert
  - Trigger strength: none
  - Topic citation: "—" (no engineering, hardware, ML/AI, or architectural content in REQUEST)
  - Decision: exclude
  - Rationale: No technical architecture dimension. Equipment financing is a financial question, not an engineering tradeoff.

**Notes:** Politician (moderate) deferred — would qualify at high effort where moderate-or-better triggers are admitted. Lawyer is not deferred; it is a clean exclude under the operating-environment-permits trap and would remain excluded at high effort.
