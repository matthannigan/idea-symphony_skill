# The Audience Advocate on Notifications, Attention, and Trust Infrastructure

## Question 1: Is "opinionated minimal notifications" respect or projection — and what does non-interaction tell us?

* **The two-notification stance is a developer's preference, not a user consensus.** From the community's perspective, "minimal" assumes everyone benefits from silence — but people who've failed at habits before often need *exactly the wrong number* (their thing). The person whose mornings are chaotic doesn't want a nudge at 7 AM when they're wrangling kids; the person rebuilding after burnout might silence *both* notifications because previous apps trained them to see all notifications as manipulation. What they actually do (disable nudges, then quietly quit the app) tells us they're voting with inaction — not because two is the "right" number, but because fixed timing and one-size-fits approach ignores their reality. Real respect would be: "We start with two, but users control timing, frequency, and whether to use them at all — and we listen to who disappears."

## Question 2: The nudge as a slow-burn replacement for intrinsic motivation

* **External cues can become a habit crutch without users realizing it.** People we serve might experience this as: "I set up the app to build a habit, three months in my motivation feels hollow, and I don't know if I'm actually committed or just responding to my phone." The psychological drift from "I want to run" to "my phone told me to run, so I did" is invisible until someone stops receiving the nudge and realizes they don't run at all. What they say they want (motivation support) versus what might actually happen (dependency on external prompting) suggests a need for honest framing: "This app might become your only reason to do this — is that okay?" And measurement: track the gap between nudge days and nudge-free days to catch dependency before it hardens.

## Question 3: Notification fatigue and the pull-based pivot as trust infrastructure

* **The two-notification hard limit is only trustworthy if it's *felt* inside the app, not just stated.** Community members burned by spam need to experience the covenant, not read it. That means: the app itself never nags, never adds sneaky reminders, never pivots to widgets or watch notifications without explicit user opt-in at each step. Pull-based engagement (widgets, glanceables) only works if the emotional contract is enforced in design — if users see the app as saying "check me if you need me" rather than "I will find you." Real trust infrastructure shows up as: no dark patterns, no "helpful" notification features that sneak in later, no pressure to enable anything beyond those two. The policy text is just insurance; the felt experience is what actually rebuilds trust.

## Question 4: Weather alert tiers versus motivational push notifications

* **Signal integrity matters more than engagement metrics.** From the user's lived experience: the first few nudges feel encouraging; by month four, they've learned to ignore them like city sirens. A two-tier ecology (sacred morning nudge, ambient weekly review) only works if the app *enforces* the contract — never escalates the sacred channel, never creeps toward urgency language, never makes the nudge more intrusive when engagement drops. The temptation will be real: if users start ignoring the nudge, product pressure will mount to make it louder. Real respect is pre-committing to degradation: "If this channel stops working, we delete this notification and try something else entirely, not make it more aggressive." Communities have seen this movie before — good intentions that erode when metrics pressure hits.

## Question 5: Two notifications as a covenant

* **A public covenant reframes the relationship from "feature" to "promise."** What they actually experience: if the app openly commits to two and only two in the app store, onboarding, and settings, then every user who sees another app with five notifications knows this one is keeping its word. But that covenant only holds if the team has locked it in psychologically — if there's institutional resistance to "just one more helpful notification" when a milestone is hit or a streak breaks. People we serve who've been betrayed by apps will test this subtly: they'll notice if that covenant cracks, and they'll leave quietly. The power of this is less about the number and more about: stating it publicly, keeping it visible, and treating violations as product failures rather than "growth opportunities."
