# The Provocateur on Topic Cluster 01

## Q1: The irreducible job, rebuilt from the literature

* **Summary:** What if the real job isn't habit formation — it's *identity transformation*, but the app sabotages it by making the user track the *outcome* instead of the *story they tell themselves*? The real insight: behavioral science says identity precedes behavior, but every habit tracker reinforces outcome-tracking ("Did I do it?") instead of identity-claiming ("Who am I becoming?"). Commit devices work. Memory prosthetics work. But a *narrative prosthetic* — a tool that helps users craft and refine the story of who they're becoming — that's different. Start there, not at logging.

## Q2: Tracking vs. deciding as the framing verb

* **Summary:** What if the product made check-in *harder* than logging, not easier — if checking in required you to articulate *why* you did (or didn't do) the habit today, not just whether you did? The absurd version: require a 200-word essay before you can mark a habit done. The real insight: the moment of friction is when you're most likely to introspect. Most habit trackers remove friction from logging. What if friction at check-in forced a micro-decision: "Do I actually want this, or did I just log it reflexively?"

## Q3: Is the PRD's diagnosis of churn correct at root?

* **Summary:** What if the real churn isn't from feature bloat or streak anxiety — it's that the product is *too forgiving of bad goal selection*? What if instead of designing UX to remove guilt, you design *friction at goal creation* so people feel the weight of commitment *before* they're stuck with it? The absurd version: make users argue against their own habit in writing before they can start tracking. The real insight: habitica works partly because it lets you *fail* — streaks break, and you survive. What if the product leaned into that instead of hiding failure?

## Q4: The minimum viable behavioral intervention

* **Summary:** What if the MVP isn't a check-in + weekly review, but a single daily friction point: *one question you have to answer before the day ends, about one habit, with no way to skip it*? No streak counter. No data export. Just: "Did you do X? Why or why not?" The absurd version: the app doesn't exist except for that one question. The real insight: retention isn't about features — it's about making the user *think about their choice daily*, not about logging outcomes. If the app forces one honest micro-decision per day, that's the intervention that moves D30 retention.
