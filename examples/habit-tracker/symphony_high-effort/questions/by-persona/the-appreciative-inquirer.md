# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Modern Habit Tracker Web App

## Discovery: Peak Moments in Habit Formation

1. **The habit that stuck**: Think about a habit you've successfully maintained for six months or more — what made it stick, and what did the tool or system you used get right that others got wrong?

2. **The app that earned loyalty**: When has a productivity or wellness app genuinely delighted you — not just on day one, but after weeks of use — and what specific design decision do you credit for that staying power?

3. **The grace-under-pressure moment**: Tell me about a time when a streak break or setback actually strengthened your commitment to a habit rather than ending it — what happened, and what did the app or system do (or not do) that made that recovery possible?

## Dream: The Habit Tracker Users Are Grateful For

4. **The "I tell everyone about this" vision**: If this app became something users felt genuinely grateful for — not just useful, but worthy of recommendation to a close friend going through a hard stretch — what would they say it gave them that other apps couldn't?

5. **Day 47 as a design north star**: The PRD articulates a compelling vision: easier on day 47 than day 2. If you imagined a user's experience arc from first launch to six months in, what would the emotional high points look like, and what would the app have learned about them by then?

6. **The Sunday evening ritual**: Imagine the weekly review has become the feature users most look forward to — a small, grounding ritual rather than a chore. What would that experience feel and sound like? What would users say about it to a friend?

## Design: Structures That Amplify What Already Works

7. **Building on the "grace" instinct**: The streak-recovery mechanic — letting a one-day miss be "recovered" with a brief note — is already a strength-based design choice. What other moments in the habit lifecycle could be reframed the same way, where the app treats imperfection as information rather than failure?

8. **Simplicity as a compounding asset**: The PRD's commitment to radical simplicity (no badges, no AI coach, no leaderboards) is itself a differentiated strength. What design structures would help the team hold that line as the product grows, and as users inevitably request features that would erode it?

9. **The minimal notification as a trust signal**: Two notifications — morning nudge and weekly review reminder — is a deliberate constraint that communicates respect for the user's attention. How could that constraint be made visible to users in a way that becomes a source of pride and trust in the product?

## Destiny: First Steps and Sustaining the Vision

10. **The test that reveals what matters**: If you could run one small, low-cost experiment in the first month post-launch to discover what's working better than expected — and use that finding to double down — what would you test, and what signal would tell you you'd found something worth amplifying?

11. **The user story worth celebrating**: Imagine it's six months after launch and a user writes in to describe how the app quietly helped them through a difficult period — not by pushing them, but by being there when they were ready. What would that message say, and what design decisions made it possible?
