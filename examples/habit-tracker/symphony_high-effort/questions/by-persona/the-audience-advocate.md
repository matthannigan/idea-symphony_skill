# Brainstorming Questions from The Audience Advocate: Modern Habit Tracker Web App

## The Returning Quitter's Emotional Landscape

1. **The shame spiral before the download**: Before a burned-out user even opens the app store again, they've already rehearsed their own failure — what does the emotional journey look like in the first 90 seconds after install, and how does every interaction during that window either reinforce or interrupt the "I'll probably quit this one too" story they're telling themselves?

2. **What "streak anxiety" actually feels like at 11pm**: The PRD frames streak-breaking as an addressable feature, but from the community's perspective, the anxiety often peaks not at the moment of failure but hours earlier — how do users who've experienced streak-panic actually behave (checking the app repeatedly, bargaining with themselves, doing a rushed version of the habit) and does the grace-recovery mechanism address the behavioral loop or only the data artifact?

3. **The silent workaround gap**: People won't tell us the app's partial-logging flow is confusing because they've never seen a better one — but if you mapped what users actually do when they only complete part of a habit (skip the log, lie and tap "done," quietly abandon the habit over several weeks), where is the gap between what the design assumes they'll do and what they actually do under real-world time pressure?

## Access, Barriers, and Who Gets Left Out

4. **The 30-habit free tier as a poverty trap**: From the perspective of users with fewer resources to experiment with paid apps, the 3-habit free limit may land not as a gentle nudge toward paid but as a ceiling that confirms this tool "isn't for people like me" — what do users who live paycheck-to-paycheck experience when they hit that wall mid-session, and does the framing of the limit communicate scarcity or invitation?

5. **Cognitive load for users managing mental health**: The target user profile centers adults 25–45 who've bounced from other apps, but this population includes a significant proportion managing anxiety, ADHD, or depression — for whom a "minimal" UI can still feel overwhelming on a bad day — how does the core loop hold up when the user's executive function is impaired, and what would "invisible but present on a hard day" actually look like compared to "invisible but present on a typical day"?

6. **Language and literacy as invisible barriers**: The PRD assumes enough literacy and English-language fluency to parse "cue / routine / reward framing (Atomic Habits model)" — but the people who most need help building consistent habits may include those with lower literacy, non-native speakers, or users who've never heard of Atomic Habits — how should the habit creation flow work for someone who doesn't recognize the vocabulary of behavioral science?

## Notification Design and the Dignity of Attention

7. **The morning nudge as an intrusion**: From the community's perspective, a notification that arrives at the "wrong" moment — during a commute, during caregiving, during a meeting — isn't just inconvenient, it can generate the exact guilt and friction the app is trying to eliminate — how should the morning nudge behave when the user doesn't interact with it, and what does repeated non-interaction tell us that we should be designing for rather than pushing through?

8. **Who controls the notification surface, really**: The PRD says "opinionated minimal notifications — morning nudge + weekly review reminder, that's the entire notification surface" but opinion is the developer's opinion — what do users who've been burned by notification overload in other apps actually want the negotiation over notifications to feel like, and does a pre-set two-notification model feel like relief or like a different kind of paternalism?

## The Long Game: Day 47 and Beyond

9. **The invisible user who stopped without quitting**: Many users will simply drift — opening the app less, skipping the weekly review, not formally canceling — and the "10+ days inactive" question in the PRD treats this as an edge case, but from the community's perspective it may be the modal experience — what does the emotional state of a drifting user look like, and what would a response that preserves their dignity (rather than guilting them back) actually feel like to receive?

10. **What "success" means to someone who isn't tracking streaks**: The success metrics measure 30-day retention and paid conversion, but users experience success as something more personal and harder to quantify — what do users who consider themselves "successful" with a habit actually say and feel about their tracking tool's role, and does the product's definition of success align with or diverge from theirs?

11. **The weekly review as a mirror, not a report**: The PRD positions the weekly review as a gentle prompt, but from the community's perspective, regularly confronting your own behavioral data can be emotionally loaded — how do users who've struggled with perfectionism or self-criticism experience a structured weekly reflection, and what's the difference between a review that feels like self-compassion and one that feels like an accountability spreadsheet?

## Inclusion, Representation, and Who the App Is Designed With

12. **Secondary users who never became primary**: The PRD flags parents tracking kid-facing habits as a v2 consideration, but from the community's perspective these users have meaningfully different emotional stakes (responsibility for someone else's formation, not just their own) — what would it mean to design with parents rather than deferring their needs, and what assumptions in the core loop would need to change?

13. **The user who doesn't identify with "habit optimization" culture**: The target user is someone who's tried habit apps before, but a significant population of potential users finds the language of "habit stacks" and "behavioral science" alienating or associated with hustle culture — what does the people we're trying to serve experience when they encounter this framing, and how might the product reach people who want to build a healthier routine without identifying as someone who "optimizes" themselves?
