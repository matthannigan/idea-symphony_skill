# Topic Cluster 10: Equity, Access, and Who Gets Left Behind

## Questions

1. **The 30-second morning and users whose mornings are not reliably theirs**: The core loop assumes a user has 30 uninterrupted seconds in the morning to review habits — but the people who most struggle with habit formation often have the most disrupted mornings (caregiving responsibilities, shift work, irregular sleep, housing instability). What does the product look like for someone whose mornings are not reliably theirs, and what does a solo-developer constraint with a three-month MVP window actually enable (focus, coherence, opinionated design) that a larger team might struggle to achieve in serving them?
2. **Offline and intermittent connectivity as an equity commitment**: The PRD frames offline support as a "how common is the subway scenario" question — implicitly a convenience feature for urban commuters — but offline support is also an access question for users in areas with poor connectivity, users on limited data plans, and users who use shared or public devices. What would it mean to treat offline-first not as a technical preference but as a commitment to serving users across economic circumstances?
3. **The secondary user who never gets to speak**: The PRD names parents helping kids build habits as a v2 secondary audience, but the children who would actually use or be subject to those habits have no voice in the spec at all — whose agency does the feature serve (the parent's desire to instill habits, or the child's development of intrinsic motivation), what are the implications of building habit surveillance infrastructure for minors even with the best intentions, and has the primary target (adults 25–45 who bounced from a prior app) been interviewed, prototyped with, or observed in their actual morning routines rather than constructed from inference, given that what users say they want (less gamification) and what actually sustains engagement may not be the same thing?
