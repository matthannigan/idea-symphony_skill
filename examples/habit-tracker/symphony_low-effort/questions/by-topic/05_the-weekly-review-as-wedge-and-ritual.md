# Topic Cluster 05: The Weekly Review as Wedge and Ritual

## Questions

1. **Weekly review as signature ritual, not a buried form**: The PRD hypothesizes that weekly review is underused in competitors because it's buried, not because it's inherently weak, and targets 40% completion among paid users — but what would it take for Sunday review to become a three-minute ritual users look forward to (the way someone says "Sunday is my long run day"), surfacing existing strengths in the core loop (cue/routine/reward framing, partial logging, grace periods) and earning the kind of ownership where a user anticipates it rather than dismisses it, and what signal (completion rate, session length, next-week habit adjustment rate) would confirm within 60 days that this theory of behavior change is working?
2. **Evidence that a prompted review beats continued daily check-ins**: Even a well-surfaced weekly review prompt will fail if the cognitive cost of structured reflection feels higher than users expect on a Sunday — what evidence do we have that a prompted weekly review produces better habit outcomes than simply continuing daily check-ins, and what's the minimum weekly review completion rate needed to justify keeping it in the paid tier rather than folding its function into the daily loop?
