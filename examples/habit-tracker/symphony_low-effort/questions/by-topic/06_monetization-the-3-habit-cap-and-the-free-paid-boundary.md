# Topic Cluster 06: Monetization, the 3-Habit Cap, and the Free/Paid Boundary

## Questions

1. **The 3-habit ceiling as conversion gate or capability wall**: The free tier caps at 3 active habits, but the PRD's own core loop assumes 3–5 committed habits, and from the perspective of a user with a chronic health condition managing medications, hydration, sleep, exercise, and stress journaling, 3 is not a soft nudge toward paid — it's a wall that excludes the users who may need the tool most. What's the analytical basis for 3, how do we model the trade-off between a limit tight enough to drive upgrades and loose enough to experience genuine value before the paywall, and is the natural moment a user wants to add a fourth habit (when engagement is peaking) the worst possible moment to introduce a forced decision that interrupts the very habit-formation loop the app is trying to support?
2. **Pricing structure, LTV trade-offs, and the signal $3/month sends**: $3/mo is positioned as the paid tier, with subscription-vs-lifetime listed as an open question — what's the expected LTV delta between a retained 18-month subscriber and a $45 lifetime buyer, how does a lifetime option affect Month 1 cash position for a solo developer, is there a structure (e.g., lifetime available only in year 1) that optimizes both, and what does $3/mo actually communicate to a user who has already been burned by apps they paid for and abandoned — is there a price point that signals "low stakes" rather than "another app I might regret"?
3. **Monetization that earns its keep and the trigger moments for upgrade**: The "hard no" list (no ads, no data selling, no upsells in notifications) is itself a product decision that communicates values — what does a monetization model look like when it is built entirely on the foundation of user trust and demonstrated value, which specific in-app moments (hitting the 3-habit cap, completing a weekly review, viewing more than 30 days of history) are most likely to be the actual conversion trigger, and how should the upgrade prompt be positioned at each moment so that upgrading feels like a graduation rather than a paywall, without violating the "no upsells in the core loop" constraint?
