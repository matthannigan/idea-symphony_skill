# Topic Cluster 02: Onboarding, Trust Recovery, and the First Encounter

## Questions

1. **The burned user's scar tissue on first open**: The target persona is someone who bounced from at least one prior habit app — but what does that prior failure actually feel like when they open a new one, what specific interface moments (a badge unlocking, a streak counter appearing, a push notification at 8pm) are likely to trigger the "here we go again" response, and how would you know if your design has accidentally reactivated it rather than soothed it? If most prior bounces happened because the habit itself wasn't meaningful yet rather than because of app design, would any interface change have retained those users — and how does that reshape who the MVP is actually for?
2. **Literacy, language, and the accessibility of the core framing**: The Atomic Habits cue/routine/reward scaffolding is legible to users fluent in the self-help genre, but what happens to the person who has never read that book, who processes information differently, or whose primary language is not English — is the product being designed with its most fluent users in mind, or with its least fluent?
3. **The reluctant returner's first tap**: Imagine Marcus — 34, tried Habitica two years ago, quit after his streak broke during a work crunch. He downloads this app on a Tuesday night, skeptical. He types in one habit: "meditate 10 minutes." What does he see, feel, and decide in the next 60 seconds that determines whether he opens the app again on Wednesday morning — and how does the app's onboarding design that critical first minute without feeling like an infomercial?
4. **The desire line problem in urban planning**: Urban planners coined "desire lines" for the paths pedestrians actually walk versus the paths designers intended — often revealed as worn tracks across lawns. Most habit apps design for the intended path (daily check-in at 8am via notification). What are the desire lines in habit-tracking behavior — the workarounds users already invent (texting themselves, paper tallies, phone alarms with custom labels) — and could the app be designed around those actual paths rather than the intended ones?
