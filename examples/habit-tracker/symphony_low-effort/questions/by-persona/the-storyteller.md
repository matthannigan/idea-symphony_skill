# Brainstorming Questions from The Storyteller: Modern Habit Tracker Web App

---

## Arrival: The First Encounter

1. **The reluctant returner's first tap**: Imagine Marcus — 34, tried Habitica two years ago, quit after his streak broke during a work crunch. He downloads this app on a Tuesday night, skeptical. He types in one habit: "meditate 10 minutes." What does he see, feel, and decide in the next 60 seconds that determines whether he opens the app again on Wednesday morning — and how does the app's onboarding design that critical first minute without feeling like an infomercial?

2. **The invisible welcome**: The PRD describes a "minimal on day one, invisible-but-present by week six" experience. What does "invisible-but-present" actually feel like from the inside — what sensory or emotional cues does a user notice (or not notice) that signal the app has quietly learned their rhythm without demanding attention, and how do you design for a presence that's only felt when it's missing?

3. **The partial log moment**: Elena is trying to run 30 minutes every morning. On Thursday she only manages 12 minutes before her kid wakes up early. She opens the app — finger hovering over the checkmark. What story does she tell herself in that moment, and what interface choices either honor that story ("I showed up") or undercut it ("you failed again") — and how does the partial-log feature become a narrative device for self-compassion rather than a consolation prize?

---

## The Hero's Journey: Day 47

4. **The habit that almost died**: Picture Priya, six weeks in, three habits consistent. Then she gets the flu and misses five days. She reopens the app for the first time after recovering — phone in hand, slightly dreading what she'll find. What does she encounter, and what's the difference between an experience that makes her feel like she's picking up a dropped thread versus one that makes her feel like she's standing in the ruins of something she broke?

5. **The Sunday review as ritual**: The weekly review is positioned as a three-minute Sunday experience. Imagine what it would feel like if it worked exactly as intended — not as a form to fill out, but as a brief, honest conversation with yourself. What questions does it ask, what does it let you skip, and what does the emotional aftertaste feel like when you close it and set your phone down — and how does that ritual quality get designed rather than just described?

6. **The habit that graduates**: Over time, some habits stop needing tracking — they become automatic. Imagine a user who has logged "floss teeth" every day for four months and realizes they've been doing it without opening the app. How does the product handle this moment of graduation — does it recognize that the tracker's job is sometimes to make itself unnecessary, and what would a graceful offboarding of a completed habit look and feel like?

---

## Return: What Remains

7. **The data export as memoir**: The PRD includes CSV export as a feature. Imagine a user who, after eight months, downloads their habit log and opens it in a spreadsheet. What do they see, and what story does that data tell about who they were and who they became — and how might the product design the export experience (or the data itself) so that it reads less like a database dump and more like a quiet record of effort?
