# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Modern Habit Tracker Web App

---

## Discovery: When Habit-Building Has Actually Worked

1. **Peak check-in moment**: Think of a time when completing a daily habit felt effortless and even satisfying — what did the app or system you were using do (or not do) that made that moment work? What conditions surrounded it?

2. **The "just right" streak**: When has tracking a streak motivated rather than stressed you? What was different about that experience — the habit itself, the visual design, the pacing — and what can we learn from it to design the grace-recovery feature with that feeling in mind?

3. **Onboarding that didn't lose you**: When has a productivity or wellness app earned your trust in the first week? What did it do — or conspicuously *not* do — that made you feel respected as an adult who already knows what they want to build?

---

## Dream: The App Users Feel Grateful For

4. **Genuine love, not just use**: If users three years from now described this app to a friend not as "a habit tracker I use" but as "something I'm genuinely grateful for," what would they say it gave them that other apps didn't? What would that gratitude be about — calm, clarity, a sense of agency?

5. **The invisible-but-present experience**: The PRD describes the goal as "invisible-but-present by week six." If that aspiration were fully realized, what would a user's relationship with the app look and feel like on day 180 — and what design choices, compounding quietly since day one, would have made it possible?

6. **A model others want to learn from**: Imagine it's three years from now and this has become a benchmark that other habit-app teams study. What would they observe about how it handles the moment a user misses a day — and what philosophy behind that feature would they write about?

---

## Design: Structures That Reliably Create That Future

7. **Amplifying what already works in behavioral science**: The PRD cites the cue/routine/reward framing from Atomic Habits. When has that model genuinely helped a user cement a habit outside of apps — in journaling, coaching, or peer accountability? What structures from those non-digital contexts could be translated into the app's weekly review prompt design?

8. **The Monday-morning test**: Given that the PRD's core loop must complete in 30 seconds on a typical morning, what interaction principles from other fast, low-friction tools (physical or digital) could be borrowed? What makes a 10-second action feel like a moment of agency rather than a chore?

9. **Partial logging without complexity**: The PRD names partial logging ("I did 15 min instead of 30") as an open question. When has acknowledging partial progress — in any context — felt affirming rather than shame-inducing? What language, framing, or gesture made the difference, and how might that translate into a UI element that adds zero cognitive load?

---

## Destiny: First Steps and Sustaining the Vision

10. **The small experiment worth running this month**: What is one low-cost prototype or live test — with even a handful of real users — that would reveal whether the app's approach to missed days and streak recovery feels relieving rather than indifferent? What would you be looking for in their response?

11. **Who already has what this needs**: The solo-developer constraint is real. What strengths — in the team's existing knowledge of behavioral science, UX craft, or the target user's psychology — are already present and could be amplified to make the "minimal on day one" vision convincing to the first 100 users?
