# Brainstorming Questions from The Audience Advocate: Modern Habit Tracker Web App

---

## The Emotional Texture of Failure

1. **Shame vs. grace when streaks break**: When a user misses three days in a row, what do they actually feel in that moment — and how does the app's response either deepen that shame or interrupt it? What emotional tone does the interface need to hold at exactly this point?

2. **The silent workaround gap**: People who've bounced from previous habit apps didn't just leave — they found workarounds (paper journals, phone reminders, sheer willpower). What does mapping those workarounds reveal about what they needed that no app gave them, and how should that shape the "grace" mechanism the PRD describes?

3. **Partial completion as everyday reality**: The PRD flags partial logging ("I did 15 min instead of 30") as an open question. From the user's lived experience, how often is partial completion the norm rather than the exception — and if it's common, what does designing primarily around full completion signal to the user about their own adequacy?

4. **Reactivation after long absence**: When someone returns after 10+ days away, what do they actually need to feel in that first moment back — and what might "silent wait," "gentle nudge," or "want to pause?" each communicate emotionally to a user who's already carrying guilt about lapsing?

---

## Accessibility and Barrier Mapping

5. **Cognitive load on hard days**: The PRD targets users who've bounced from apps with "gamification overload," but cognitive load isn't just about badges. On a genuinely difficult day — grief, illness, overwhelm — what is the minimum viable interaction a user needs the app to support, and is the current "30s morning review" assumption realistic for that day?

6. **Device and connectivity realities**: The PRD notes "opened the app in the subway" as a use-case consideration for offline support, but frames it as a technical question. From the perspective of users whose primary device is a mid-range phone with inconsistent data, what features become inaccessible or friction-heavy, and how does that affect whether the app serves them or only appears to?

7. **The 3-habit free tier ceiling**: The free tier caps at 3 active habits. For a user who genuinely wants to build 5 habits but can't or won't pay, does hitting that wall feel like a product decision or like a judgment that their goals aren't worth supporting? What does that experience do to their relationship with the app at the precise moment they're most engaged?

---

## Who Gets Centered and Who Gets Assumed

8. **The primary persona's privilege assumptions**: The PRD's primary user — "adults 25–45 who have tried at least one habit app before and bounced" — is positioned as the default. What characteristics does this person implicitly have (smartphone literacy, time autonomy, previous app access) that aren't universal, and what user populations does this default invisibly exclude from the design?

9. **Designing for the secondary user through the primary**: The PRD places parents using the app for kid-facing habits in v2. From the community's perspective, what does it mean to delay a feature that extends the app's utility to family contexts — and are there design choices made for the primary user that will actively resist adaptation for that use case later?

10. **Language and framing assumptions**: The PRD uses terms like "committed habits," "intentions," "weekly review," and "cue/routine/reward." These carry implicit cultural and educational assumptions. What would a user who has never encountered Atomic Habits or productivity self-help culture make of this language — and does the framing inadvertently signal that the app is for a certain kind of self-improvement-literate person?

---

## Agency, Data, and User Trust

11. **Data export as dignity, not just feature**: The PRD lists CSV export as a paid feature. From the user's perspective, their habit log is a record of their own life and effort — not content the app created. What does it communicate about the power relationship between user and product to gate access to one's own data behind a paywall?

12. **Notification as relationship**: The PRD commits to exactly two notification types (morning nudge + weekly review reminder). People who've been burned by notification-heavy apps have often been conditioned to distrust app notifications entirely. What would it take for a user to genuinely welcome these two nudges rather than dismiss them — and what does the app need to have already demonstrated before it earns the right to interrupt someone's morning?

---

## The Journey from Skeptic to Sustained User

13. **The "day 47" promise and the path to get there**: The PRD's vision is an app that's easier on day 47 than day 2. From the user's emotional journey, what has to happen between days 1 and 47 for a skeptical returning user to still be there? Where do they feel doubt, small relief, growing trust — and which of those moments does the current feature set actually address?
