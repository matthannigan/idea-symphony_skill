# Topic Cluster 01: Regulatory Navigation and Compliance

## Questions

1. **Permit portfolio management**: Given the city's neighborhood-zone permit caps and the possibility of state preemption, what systems and contractual safeguards would protect the company and its property owners if a significant portion of managed properties lose their permit eligibility overnight?
2. **HOA compliance strategy**: With multiple target neighborhoods carrying their own STR restrictions that may differ from or be stricter than city ordinances, how should the company structure its intake process to assess and document HOA compliance—and what liability does the company bear if an owner misrepresents HOA status?
3. **Regulatory monitoring**: What ongoing process would let the company stay ahead of changes to local STR ordinances, state preemption legislation, and HOA rule amendments, and how would those changes propagate into client contracts and operational procedures before violations occur?
4. **Good-neighbor positioning under enforcement pressure**: If the city increases STR enforcement in response to political pressure, how could the company differentiate itself as a compliant operator in ways that reduce regulatory risk—noise monitoring compliance, proactive permitting assistance, local contact availability—and turn that compliance posture into a competitive sales argument with new property owners?
5. **Tax compliance architecture**: Across municipal occupancy tax, state sales tax, and federal income reporting, what combination of software, legal structure, and accounting relationships would ensure the company handles remittance correctly across all three layers—and how should this be disclosed and allocated in client agreements?
