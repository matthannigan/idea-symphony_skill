# Topic Cluster 05: Unit Economics and Capital Runway

## Questions

1. **The $80K runway versus realistic ramp**: With $80K covering legal entity formation, GL/E&O/property-damage insurance premiums, software subscriptions, cleaning vendor onboarding, marketing, the 30-minute contact obligation, and working capital — and with the first property unlikely to generate management fee revenue for 60-90 days after signing (listing setup, photography, permit processing) — how should these dollars be sequenced and sized, what is the explicit break-even property count, and at what monthly burn rate does the $80K run out before reaching it?
2. **Percentage-of-revenue model versus seasonal cash flow**: Full-service management at a percentage of booking revenue earns nothing when properties sit empty, but fixed costs (insurance, software, local contact obligation) don't pause for shoulder season — what does a 30-50% seasonal occupancy drop do to monthly cash flow in months 4-8, what management fee rate at what property count reaches break-even, and would a hybrid fee structure (base retainer plus smaller performance percentage) better stabilize finances without making the value proposition uncompetitive?
3. **Revenue concentration and client churn at early scale**: With 5-10 properties in year one, losing a single dissatisfied owner could represent 10-20% of revenue, and reaching 30-50 properties in three years requires both acquiring and retaining owners — what owner churn rate assumptions are embedded in that growth target, what contract provisions (notice periods, cure windows, non-disparagement) protect against an owner departure becoming a reputational event, and what service-quality metrics most directly predict whether an owner renews or exits?
