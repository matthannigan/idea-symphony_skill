# Topic Cluster 01: Vision and Founding Identity

## Questions

1. **Peak-moments inquiry**: Think about short-term rental experiences — in this city or elsewhere — where guests left genuinely grateful, owners felt proud, and neighbors barely noticed. What was happening in those operations that made all three feel that way simultaneously, what hidden assets were the operators drawing on, and what does that suggest about the operating model this company should aspire to?
2. **The model-citizen scenario**: Imagine it is three years from now and city council members, housing advocates, and property owners all point to this company as proof that short-term rentals and community wellbeing are compatible — what would visitors notice about how the company operates, what would its reputation be built on, and what would each stakeholder (guest, owner, neighbor) say to others about their experience?
3. **What would it feel like across three vantage points**: Describe at the company's best the experience of being a guest, an owner, and a neighbor — not just what works logistically, but the emotions and stories each person carries away, including what would make a neighbor say unprompted, "Actually, the rental next door is fine."
4. **From extraction to ecosystem**: What if the property owner-manager relationship was not a fee-for-service contract at all, but something more like a living trust or a cooperative organism — where the management company holds a genuine stake in the long-term prosperity of each property, the neighborhood, and the city simultaneously? What new legal, financial, and governance structures would need to be invented if the company's interests were constitutively aligned with community health rather than maximizing nightly rate?
5. **The reluctant landlord's leap of faith**: Imagine Margaret, a retired teacher who inherited her coastal cottage and nervously hands over the keys for the first time — what is the exact sequence of moments, communications, and small reassurances that transforms her from anxious first-timer into a confident, referring client, and how does the company design that onboarding arc deliberately rather than leaving it to chance?
