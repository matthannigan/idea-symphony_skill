# Topic Cluster 08: Guest Experience and Accessibility

## Questions

1. **Designing across guest profiles**: STR guests are not a monolithic audience — they include families with young children, travelers with disabilities, non-English speakers, digital nomads, and last-minute bookers; what does a genuinely accessible, welcoming experience look like across all of these profiles, and how does the company design communication, listing content, and in-stay support to meet people where they are?
2. **Accessibility audits and listing accuracy**: Properties under management may never have been audited for accessibility; if a guest who uses a wheelchair or has a visual impairment books based on listing descriptions that don't reflect physical realities, the experience gap can be harmful — what process accurately represents accessibility features and limitations in every listing, and how does the company handle inquiries from guests with specific access needs?
3. **The emotional arc of a guest crisis**: A guest who arrives to find a broken A/C in August or a dirty bathroom has already made decisions about transportation, childcare, and work leave — the people being served might experience this as a crisis, not an inconvenience; how does the company map the emotional arc from discovery to resolution, and what concrete commitments (response time, remedies, communication) reflect genuine accountability rather than damage control?
4. **Hospitality as civic participation**: What if short-term rental guests were not transient consumers passing through a neighborhood but temporary citizens with real stakes in its character — and what if the management company's role was to design the guest experience as a form of civic onboarding, where every stay included genuine participation in the neighborhood's life? What would it mean to build a management company whose core product is not a clean, optimized rental but a form of belonging that guests carry home and that neighbors actually welcome?
