# Topic Cluster 04: Tax Compliance and Insurance Coverage

## Questions

1. **Three-regime tax burden and statutory remittance liability**: The business must collect and remit municipal occupancy tax, state sales tax, and report federal income across all managed properties — who bears statutory liability for occupancy tax remittance when the company collects booking revenue (owner as permit holder, company as collection agent, or both jointly), what does the management agreement need to say to clearly assign this obligation, and what is the realistic administrative cost (staff time, software, accountant hours) of maintaining compliance at 10 versus 40 properties — at what scale does it require a dedicated role?
2. **Federal tax character and 1099 obligations**: The IRS treats short-term rental income differently depending on average guest stay length and owner participation — rental income below 14 days is excluded from Schedule E under IRC § 280A(g), while active STR management may trigger self-employment tax for owners; does the company's full-service model affect how owner income is characterized federally, and does the company carry disclosure or 1099 obligations that vary based on the rental regime?
3. **Coverage gap between platform protection and company E&O**: Airbnb's AirCover and VRBO's host protection are platform-administered programs with discretionary claims handling, not insurance policies the company controls — when platform coverage denies a claim or caps out, what is the residual exposure for the company (named as co-defendant in serious guest injury suits), does a standard GL policy exclude STR management as a "professional services" exclusion, and what coverage stack (platform + GL + property management E&O + umbrella + owner homeowner-with-STR-endorsement) closes the gap?
4. **Insurance requirements as contract conditions**: Rather than relying solely on company coverage, the management agreement should require owners to maintain insurance (homeowner's or landlord with STR endorsement) — what minimum coverage requirements are defensible (policy limits, named-insured status for the company, waiver of subrogation), and what is the company's contractual right to suspend or terminate management if owner coverage lapses?
