# Brainstorming Questions from The Analyst: Short-Term Rental Property Management Company

## Cluster 1: Unit Economics and Revenue Modeling

**Management fee floor vs. ceiling**: Given a percentage-of-revenue model and a starting portfolio of 5–10 properties in a seasonal coastal market, what management fee rate — and at what property count — does this business reach break-even, and how does that calculation shift when occupancy drops by 30% in off-peak months?

**Capital allocation across $80K**: The startup has $80K for initial costs that must cover legal entity formation, insurance premiums, technology platforms, cleaning vendor ramp, and working capital before revenue stabilizes — how should these dollars be sequenced and sized, and which categories carry the highest risk of being underestimated in year one?

**Revenue concentration risk**: If 5–10 properties generate all year-one revenue and even two or three underperform or churn, what does the cash flow picture look like, and what financial levers (fee adjustments, service tier changes, owner incentives) could buffer against that concentration risk?

## Cluster 2: Regulatory and Compliance Cost Structure

**Permit economics per neighborhood zone**: City STR ordinances cap permits by neighborhood zone, which constrains where growth can occur — how should the company model the cost and timeline of permit acquisition per zone, and what does a permit ceiling in a high-demand zone do to the growth trajectory toward 30–50 properties by year three?

**Multi-layer tax compliance burden**: The business must collect and remit municipal occupancy tax, state sales tax, and report federal income across all managed properties — what is the realistic administrative cost (staff time, software, accountant hours) of maintaining this compliance at 10 properties versus 40, and at what scale does it require a dedicated role?

**State preemption scenario analysis**: The state legislature is considering preempting local STR bans — what are the materially different operating scenarios (preemption passes, fails, or is delayed past year two), and how does each affect the addressable permit pool, competitive intensity, and property owner demand in this market?

## Cluster 3: Operational Scalability

**Cleaning and maintenance vendor capacity**: Full-service management requires reliable cleaning turnover between guest stays — what is the critical vendor capacity threshold (cleaners, maintenance contractors) needed to support same-day turnovers reliably, and how does the company build that supply chain before it has enough volume to offer vendors predictable work?

**30-minute local contact requirement**: The city ordinance mandates a local contact person available within 30 minutes of a guest complaint — how does this constraint translate into staffing models (employee vs. on-call contractor vs. founder on-call), what is the cost at different portfolio sizes, and where does the model become unsustainable without a dedicated operations hire?

**Technology platform selection and lock-in**: Property management software (channel management, pricing tools, guest communication platforms) involves meaningful switching costs once owner data and integrations are embedded — what criteria should drive the initial platform selection, and what does the cost and disruption of migration look like if a better tool emerges in year two?

## Cluster 4: Owner Acquisition and Retention

**Owner contract risk allocation**: The management agreement must allocate liability for guest injuries, property damage, and contractor errors between the company and the property owner — what are the realistic risk exposure ranges for each category, and how should contract terms, indemnification clauses, and insurance requirements be calibrated to protect the company without making the agreement unattractive to sign?

**Churn rate and its compounding effect on growth**: Reaching 30–50 properties in three years requires not just acquiring new owners but retaining existing ones — what owner churn rate assumptions are embedded in that growth target, and what service quality metrics or contract structures most directly predict whether an owner renews or exits?

## Cluster 5: Community and Political Risk Quantification

**City council election scenario mapping**: A city council election in six months could shift STR regulation significantly in either direction — how should the company map the realistic policy outcomes (tighter restrictions, status quo, relaxed enforcement) against its existing permit portfolio and growth pipeline, and what contingency triggers should prompt a strategic pivot?
