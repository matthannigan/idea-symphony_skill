# Brainstorming Questions from The Questioner: Short-Term Rental Property Management Company

## Cluster 1: Assumptions About What Property Owners Actually Want

**Trust signals vs. track record**: What do property owners in this market genuinely use as proxies for trustworthiness when handing over a high-value asset to a new management company — and have the founders assumed those signals align with what they can actually demonstrate in year one?

**Full-service vs. selective service**: Why do we assume property owners want full-service management rather than a modular offering where they retain control over some functions, such as guest communication or pricing, while outsourcing others? What would a company look like if it were designed around what owners actually want to delegate versus what a management company finds operationally convenient to bundle?

**Revenue percentage vs. flat fee**: What mental model are property owners using when they evaluate a percentage-of-revenue management fee — and how does that calculus shift during off-season months when bookings are sparse and the management burden may actually be higher than peak season?

## Cluster 2: Regulatory Landscape as Strategy, Not Just Compliance

**Regulation as competitive moat**: If new STR regulations create a compliance burden that smaller or informal operators can't easily absorb, how might the founders position compliance capability as a competitive differentiator that justifies a higher management fee rather than treating it purely as an operational cost?

**State preemption timing**: What if the state legislature passes preemption of local STR bans within the next 12-18 months — how does that change the company's market size projections, its neighborhood-zone compliance infrastructure, and the value of relationships it's building now with city permitting officials?

**HOA restrictions as opportunity**: Why do we assume HOA-restricted neighborhoods are simply off-limits, rather than exploring whether the company could position itself as a compliance partner that helps HOAs craft workable STR policies — and thereby gain preferential access to those neighborhoods before competitors?

**Political cycle vulnerability**: What happens to properties the company manages if the upcoming city council election shifts the regulatory environment significantly in either direction — toward stricter limits or toward deregulation — and how should the company's contract structure account for that political uncertainty without transferring all the risk to property owners?

## Cluster 3: Operational Leverage and the Scaling Bottleneck

**The 30-minute response constraint**: The local-contact requirement mandates a 30-minute response time — what does it actually mean to reliably fulfill that obligation across 30-50 properties in different neighborhood zones, and what operational infrastructure (people, on-call rotations, contractor networks) would need to be in place before the company could safely take on property number 31?

**Cleaning as a liability surface**: Cleaning and turnover are often the highest-variance operational function in STR management — what if the company treated its cleaning and inspection protocol as a core proprietary system rather than a commodity vendor relationship, and how would that change hiring, training, and quality control decisions from the outset?

**Founder role divergence at scale**: At 5-10 properties, both founders can likely stay hands-on across all functions — but what specific decisions about role division, delegation, and hiring need to be made before the portfolio reaches 20 properties to avoid the company's growth stalling on founder bandwidth rather than market opportunity?

## Cluster 4: Community Relations as a Business Function

**"Good neighbor" as measurable outcome**: What concrete, verifiable behaviors would distinguish this company's properties from absentee-managed STRs in the eyes of neighbors and city officials — and how would the company collect and communicate evidence of those behaviors systematically rather than relying on reputation alone?

**Relationship with STR opponents**: Have the founders considered engaging directly with the organized opposition to STR expansion — long-term renter advocates, neighborhood associations — not to change their minds, but to understand what specific harms they're reacting to and whether any of those harms could be addressed through the company's operating standards?

**Community benefit framing**: What if the company designed a visible community contribution program — local contractor hiring preferences, noise violation response commitments, off-season community use of managed properties — and used that program as the basis for its political engagement strategy rather than waiting to react to regulatory changes?

## Cluster 5: Business Model Stress Tests

**The $80K burn rate**: What is the realistic monthly cash burn during the first six months before management fees from 5-10 properties generate meaningful revenue — and what assumptions about ramp time, property onboarding lead time, and seasonal booking patterns underlie that projection?

**Single-city concentration risk**: Why do we assume the company should optimize for depth in one coastal city rather than exploring what a lean operational model would look like that could be replicated across two or three similar markets — and what would need to be true about the unit economics for geographic expansion to become attractive before year three?
