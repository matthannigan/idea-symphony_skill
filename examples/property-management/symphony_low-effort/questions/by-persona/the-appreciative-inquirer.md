# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Short-Term Rental Property Management Company

## Cluster 1: Discovery — Peak Moments in Property Management and Hospitality

**When has a property manager or host created something guests truly celebrate?**: Think about short-term rental experiences — in this city or elsewhere — where guests left genuinely grateful, where owners felt proud, and where neighbors barely noticed. What was happening in those operations that made all three feel that way simultaneously? What did the manager do that made those outcomes possible?

**What does it look like when a regulated industry works well for everyone?**: In other heavily regulated hospitality contexts — boutique hotels navigating zoning, bed-and-breakfasts building neighborhood goodwill — when have the rules actually elevated the experience rather than just constrained it? What can we learn from those moments about how regulation and quality reinforce each other?

**Tell me about property owners who genuinely trust their managers as partners, not vendors**: When a property owner feels their investment is truly cared for — not just maintained, but stewarded — what created that relationship? What did the manager say, do, or demonstrate in the first weeks and months that built that level of trust?

## Cluster 2: Dream — Envisioning the Company at Its Best

**If this company became the model that regulators point to when they want to show STRs can work**: Imagine it is three years from now and city council members, housing advocates, and property owners all say this company demonstrated that short-term rentals and community wellbeing are compatible. What would visitors to the city notice about how this company operates? What would the company's reputation be built on?

**What would it feel like to be a guest, an owner, and a neighbor of a property managed by this company at its best?**: Describe the experience from each vantage point — not just what works logistically, but what each person says to others afterward. What emotions and stories do they carry away? What would make a neighbor say, unprompted, "Actually, the rental next door is fine"?

**How could this company's local-contact requirement become its greatest competitive advantage rather than a compliance burden?**: The city mandates a 30-minute-response local contact. What if this constraint were reframed as an opportunity — to build genuine neighborhood presence, to be first-call for guests and owners alike? What would that version of the company look like, and what would it make possible that remote-management competitors could never offer?

## Cluster 3: Design — Structures That Reliably Create the Desired Future

**What structures would allow regulatory compliance to feel like a shared mission rather than a cost center?**: Given that the founders already bring hospitality and real estate expertise, how might they design internal processes — permit tracking, noise-monitoring review, tax remittance — so that compliance becomes a source of pride and differentiation, not just overhead? What would a "compliance-as-quality" operating model look like?

**How might the founders' complementary backgrounds become a structural asset in every owner relationship?**: One partner brings hospitality instinct; the other brings real estate acumen. Rather than dividing these domains, what if every owner interaction drew on both — so owners experienced a team that understood both investment returns and guest delight? What client-facing practices would make that dual expertise visible and valuable?

**What community-engagement practices, borrowed from other service businesses, could help this company earn standing as a genuine neighborhood stakeholder?**: Some businesses in contested regulatory environments — farmers markets, food trucks, community solar projects — have turned skeptical neighbors into advocates by showing up consistently and demonstrating tangible local benefit. What equivalent practices could a property management company adopt from the start, so that when the city council election happens, the company already has credibility on both sides?

## Cluster 4: Destiny — First Steps and Sustaining Momentum

**What could the founders do in their first 30 days — before they have a single client — to begin building the reputation they want to hold in year three?**: The desired future is a company trusted by owners, guests, and the community. What actions are available right now, with the resources and relationships already in hand, that would be the first visible expressions of that identity? Who do the founders already know who could help them take those steps?

**How might starting small — five properties instead of fifty — become the company's most powerful early story?**: Rapid scale is tempting, but intentional smallness in year one could allow the founders to learn deeply, build genuine owner relationships, and demonstrate responsible management to regulators before the political landscape shifts. What would a "we chose to grow slowly and it made us better" narrative look like, and how might it attract the right clients and the right community relationships from the start?
