# Brainstorming Questions from The Devil's Advocate: Short-Term Rental Property Management Company

## Cluster 1: Regulatory Fragility and Political Risk

**The rug-pull scenario**: The city just passed new STR ordinances, and state preemption legislation is in play — have we considered what happens to the entire business model if the political winds shift 180 degrees in six months after the city council election? If STR permits are dramatically curtailed or frozen, a company that took 6–12 months to recruit its first 10 properties could find its addressable market cut in half before it breaks even. How would the founders stress-test the business against a worst-case regulatory outcome, and what structural pivots (e.g., pivoting to long-term furnished rentals) would remain viable?

**Permit ceiling as a hard growth cap**: The new ordinance limits permits per neighborhood zone — has anyone modeled how many zones have headroom for new permits versus zones that are already saturated? If the 800 active listings are concentrated in two or three zones already near cap, the target of 30–50 properties by year three may be mathematically impossible without expanding into zones that are less desirable or less tourist-friendly. How does the founders' growth projection account for zone-by-zone permit availability?

**HOA jurisdiction layering**: Several target neighborhoods have HOA restrictions that sit on top of city STR regulations — have we accounted for the risk that a property owner signs a management contract, passes city permitting, and then is blocked by HOA enforcement the founders didn't catch during intake? What due diligence protocol would catch HOA prohibitions before a contract is executed, and who bears liability if a prohibited rental generates a guest booking?

## Cluster 2: Unit Economics and Capital Runway

**Break-even timeline versus cash burn**: With $80K in combined savings, have we considered how long that runway actually is against realistic ramp costs — entity formation, insurance (GL + E&O + property damage), software subscriptions, initial marketing, cleaning contractor onboarding, and the 30-minute local contact coverage requirement? If the first property doesn't generate management fee revenue for 60–90 days after signing (listing setup, photography, permit processing), the founders could be burning cash with no revenue for two or three months. What is the explicit break-even property count, and at what monthly burn rate does the $80K run out before reaching it?

**Percentage-of-revenue model in a bad season**: Full-service management at a percentage of booking revenue means the company earns nothing when properties sit empty — but fixed costs (insurance premiums, software, the local contact obligation) don't pause for shoulder season. Have we modeled what a 40–50% seasonal occupancy drop does to monthly cash flow in months 4–8, and whether a hybrid fee structure (base retainer plus a smaller performance percentage) would better stabilize the company's finances without making the value proposition uncompetitive?

**Client concentration risk at early scale**: With 5–10 properties in year one, losing a single dissatisfied owner could represent 10–20% of revenue. Have we considered how heavily a small portfolio concentrates business risk in individual owner relationships, and what contract provisions — notice periods, cure windows, non-disparagement — would protect the company from an owner departure turning into a reputational event?

## Cluster 3: Operational Liability and Insurance Gaps

**The cleaning contractor chain of liability**: The business model relies on third-party cleaning contractors — have we considered what happens when a contractor causes property damage, injures a guest (e.g., leaves a chemical spill), or misses a turnover and a guest checks in to a dirty unit? The company sits in the middle: the property owner will look to the management company, and the management company may struggle to collect from an underinsured independent contractor. What insurance and indemnification requirements would the company impose on contractors, and how would those requirements be verified at the time of hire and periodically audited?

**Guest injury and the management company as defendant**: STR platforms like Airbnb offer host protection insurance, but coverage limits and exclusions are murky and the management company — not the platform — is likely to be named as a co-defendant in a serious guest injury suit. Have we considered whether the company's general liability policy explicitly covers STR management as an insured activity, or whether a standard GL policy might exclude it as a "professional services" exclusion? What is the actual coverage stack (platform + company GL + owner homeowner's policy) and where are the gaps?

**The 30-minute local contact rule as a single point of failure**: City ordinance requires a local contact person reachable within 30 minutes — have we considered what happens when that person is unavailable (sick, traveling, multiple simultaneous incidents)? If the company is the designated contact across 10–20 properties and a noise complaint triggers at two properties at 2 a.m. on a Saturday, a single on-call person cannot fulfill the obligation. How does the operational model ensure redundant coverage, and what is the penalty structure if the city finds the contact obligation unmet?

## Cluster 4: Community Trust and Reputation Dynamics

**"Good neighbor" positioning versus short-term incentives**: The founders want to position the company as a responsible operator — but their fee is tied to booking revenue, which creates a structural incentive to maximize occupancy rather than minimize neighborhood impact. Have we considered how a community watchdog or city regulator would react if a company that publicly claims "good neighbor" practices is simultaneously pushing dynamic pricing to fill every available night, including weekend parties in a residential zone? What operational guardrails (guest screening, minimum stay requirements, noise monitoring response protocols) would make the positioning credible rather than cosmetic?

**The political election as a six-month exposure window**: A city council election in six months with STR regulation as a key issue means the company could be launching its brand right as anti-STR candidates are running attack ads against exactly the type of operation being built. Have we considered how the company's public profile — social media, local press coverage, Nextdoor complaints — could become campaign fodder, and whether staying low-profile during the election cycle is smarter than aggressive early marketing?

**Owner expectations versus market reality**: Property owners who hire a full-service management company often arrive with occupancy and revenue expectations built on optimistic Airbnb calculators, not the actual performance of comparable listings in the market. Have we considered how a systematic owner expectation-setting process (with documented projections at contract signing) would protect the company when a property underperforms, and what the contract says when an owner who expected $4,000/month in net revenue is receiving $2,200?
