# Brainstorming Questions from The Lawyer: Short-Term Rental Property Management Company

## Cluster 1: Multi-Jurisdictional Regulatory Architecture

**STR permit stratification by zone**: The city's new STR ordinance limits permits per neighborhood zone, but zoning maps change and permits can be revoked. What compliance infrastructure should the company build to track permit status, zone classification, and renewal deadlines across its entire portfolio — and how does the company allocate responsibility when a property owner's permit lapses or their zone designation changes mid-contract?

**State preemption as a legal event**: If the state legislature passes a preemption statute invalidating local STR bans, that would create a legal discontinuity — existing city ordinance requirements could become unenforceable overnight. What contract mechanisms (force majeure clauses, regulatory change provisions, termination triggers) should be built into owner agreements to handle the legal ambiguity during the period between a preemption bill's passage and any subsequent local re-regulation?

**HOA restriction enforceability and disclosure**: HOA CC&Rs that restrict STR use are private contractual obligations running with the land — violation can result in fines, injunctions, or liens, not just permit denial. How should the company conduct HOA covenant due diligence before onboarding a property, and what disclosure obligations does the company carry to prospective owners regarding HOA restrictions that may make their property ineligible for STR regardless of city permit status?

## Cluster 2: Tax Compliance Across Three Regimes

**Occupancy tax collection and remittance liability**: The city's new STR ordinance includes occupancy tax requirements, which layer on top of state sales tax and federal income reporting. When the company collects booking revenue as the manager, who bears statutory liability for occupancy tax remittance — the property owner as the permit holder, the company as the collection agent, or both jointly? What does the management agreement need to say to clearly assign this obligation, and what happens if Airbnb/VRBO's platform-level tax collection covers some but not all of the applicable municipal taxes?

**Federal tax character of management revenue and owner income**: The IRS treats short-term rental income differently depending on average guest stay length and owner participation levels — rental income below 14 days is excluded from Schedule E under IRC § 280A(g), while active STR management may trigger self-employment tax for owners. Does the company's "full-service management" model affect how owner income is characterized for federal tax purposes, and does the company have any disclosure or 1099 obligations to owners that vary based on the rental regime their property falls under?

## Cluster 3: Liability Allocation and Contract Architecture

**Entity structure and liability compartmentalization**: The company faces concurrent liability exposure from guest injuries (premises liability), property damage (contractor negligence), and management errors (E&O). Does a single LLC provide adequate compartmentalization, or should the founders structure separate entities — one holding contracts with property owners, one employing cleaning/maintenance contractors — to prevent a single lawsuit from reaching company-wide assets? What indemnification flows between entities would be required, and how does general liability insurance interact with entity structure?

**Contract design for the local contact mandate**: The city ordinance mandates a local contact person with 30-minute response capability. If the company serves as that local contact, it is explicitly accepting a regulatory compliance obligation on behalf of the property owner. What should the management agreement say about the company's liability if a response fails to occur within 30 minutes — is that a breach of contract, a regulatory violation attributable to the company, or both? What insurance category (E&O vs. general liability) covers a noise complaint or guest incident that escalates because the response threshold was missed?

**Contractor negligence and vicarious liability**: If the company uses independent contractors for cleaning and maintenance rather than employees, the general assumption is reduced vicarious liability — but courts in many jurisdictions impose liability when the contractor is performing work integral to the principal's business. For a property management company whose core service includes physical property condition, cleaning and maintenance contractors are likely integral to the business model. How should contractor agreements be structured — scope of work limitations, certificate of insurance requirements, indemnification clauses — to maximize liability protection while maintaining operational control over service quality?

## Cluster 4: Insurance Coverage Architecture

**Coverage gap between platform insurance and company E&O**: Airbnb's AirCover and VRBO's comparable host protection programs provide some property damage and liability coverage, but these are platform-administered programs with discretionary claims handling, not insurance policies the company or owner controls. When platform coverage denies a claim or caps out, what is the residual liability exposure for the company, and what layers of coverage — commercial general liability, property management E&O, umbrella — does the company need to carry to close that gap for both its own account and contractually for property owners?

**Insurance requirements as a contract condition**: Rather than relying solely on the company's own coverage, the management agreement should require property owners to maintain certain insurance (homeowner's or landlord policy with STR endorsement) as a condition of management. What minimum coverage requirements are defensible — policy limits, named insured status for the company, waiver of subrogation — and what is the company's contractual right to suspend or terminate management if an owner's coverage lapses?
