## Statewide Preemption as an Asymmetric Bet

* **Limited-zone dominance strategy.** Don't spread across the city chasing scale. Instead, dominate 2-3 restricted zones by locking in long-term contracts at favorable rates now—"we know you can't operate, but when preemption passes, you're already our partner." This constraint forces focus, builds deep operational expertise in specific neighborhoods, and creates switching costs. If preemption fails, you've built the compliance infrastructure those zones demand. If it passes, you own the first-mover advantage in those specific high-value zones with zero transition risk.

* **Regulatory arbitrage through expertise, not speculation.** Rather than betting on preemption, position as the STR company that actually *understands* local rules so well it can operate anywhere. Build a consulting arm that helps owners navigate zone-specific restrictions and HOA policies. This turns regulatory complexity—your biggest constraint—into your primary service. When preemption hits, you're the trusted expert. If it doesn't, you're still generating revenue from compliance guidance.

* **Structured optionality in contracts.** Write property owner agreements with activation clauses: "Management fee is X% now; if preemption passes and we expand into zone Y, it drops to X-0.5% for you as a founding-zone partner." You're not betting on preemption; you're baking the possibility into contracts in a way that reduces risk while keeping optionality alive. Restricted-zone properties become your option portfolio at near-zero cost.

* **Community goodwill as insurance against both outcomes.* The regulatory threat exists because of community tension. Use this constraint to build a differentiation: be aggressively local—hire neighborhood residents for cleaning/maintenance, donate a percentage to affordable housing initiatives, hold transparent town halls about your operations. If preemption passes, you've built trust that competitors can't replicate quickly. If local rules tighten further, that goodwill becomes your operating license when others get excluded.

* **Operational compliance infrastructure as a separating advantage.* Local rules mandate noise monitoring and 30-minute response times. Build these as core operational systems, not grudging compliance overhead. License your monitoring system to other local operators (non-competing geographies). The constraint of tight local regulation becomes a product. You're not just managing properties; you're managing regulatory risk at scale for a fragmented market.
