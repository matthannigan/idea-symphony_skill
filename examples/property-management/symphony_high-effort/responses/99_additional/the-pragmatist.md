## Statewide Preemption As An Asymmetric Bet

* **Start with compliance infrastructure first, not portfolio positioning.** Before locking in properties in restricted zones, build your operational systems to handle *both* regulatory futures simultaneously. Invest 6-9 months in dual-track infrastructure: local compliance software (noise monitoring, tax reporting, 30-minute contact-person scheduling) and a preemption-ready playbook (streamlined onboarding, rapid property upload to statewide registries). This minimizes regret—you're not betting the company on a legislative outcome, just building tools that work either way. Cost: realistic to absorb within year-one operational budget.

* **Structure contracts with unilateral termination rights, not lock-in.** Rather than binding long-term agreements with restricted-zone properties, offer 12-month renewable contracts with 60-day exit clauses for owners *and* the company. This preserves optionality: if preemption passes, you can absorb properties quickly without sunk-cost commitments that limit flexibility. If local rules harden, you can pivot to fully-compliant zones without carrying underwater contracts. Yes, retention risk rises, but you gain portfolio agility at the exact moment when agility matters most.

* **Layer in price discrimination tied to regulatory risk, not speculation.** Don't gamble on preemption outcomes—price them in. Offer owners in currently-restricted zones a discount (maybe 18% fee vs. 20% for compliant zones), explicitly framing it as you absorbing *regulatory uncertainty fee*. If preemption passes, you've already captured those relationships at fair value. If it doesn't, you've built a sustainable, compliant portfolio elsewhere and the restricted-zone properties remain low-cost optionality. This approach avoids the trap of being left holding "cheap" properties that turned out to be expensive mistakes.

* **Run a legislative monitoring pilot with 2-3 strategic partners.** Rather than betting blind on preemption, form an informal steering group with 2-3 property owners and a local real estate attorney. Meet quarterly to track bill progression, understand the actual negotiation landscape, and stress-test your dual-track playbook against emerging legislative language. Cost: negligible (coffee and coordination). Payoff: you'll have weeks of warning before preemption becomes material, and owners will feel invested in your regulatory intelligence. This transforms speculation into informed decision-making.

* **De-risk the asymmetric bet by starting lean in restricted zones anyway.** Take 1-2 properties in currently-restricted zones (at discounted fee), operate them flawlessly under current rules, and measure the operational burden honestly. If compliance overhead is manageable, you've built proof of concept for rapid scaling if preemption passes. If it's punishing, you've learned that the "heads-I-win" scenario actually carries hidden costs. Either way, you've converted an abstract strategic question into operational evidence. This is how you move from betting on legislation to building business resilience.
