# Topic Cluster 03: Permit Scarcity, Zone Concentration, and Portfolio Concentration Risk

## Questions

1. **Permit attrition and zone concentration**: What proportion of the 800 active listings sit in zones where permit caps could be hit first (the zones most attractive to tourists are likely the ones that cap out first), and how does the business model change if 20%, 40%, or 60% of the target portfolio eventually faces permit denial or non-renewal — and what is the fallback strategy if neighborhood-zoned permit caps fill before reaching the 30-50 property target?
2. **The ordinance tightening spiral**: Permit limits per neighborhood zone create scarcity; scarcity increases the value of existing permits; increased value increases the political pressure to protect them — how should the company position itself so that this regulatory tightening strengthens rather than threatens its competitive position, and at the level of fundamentals, is this a hospitality business, a compliance infrastructure business, or a permit portfolio business — does the answer change which capabilities matter most to build first?
3. **The Post-Permit Paradigm**: Imagine a world where the STR permit system becomes so entangled and fragile that it collapses — what kind of property management company would you invent from scratch if neighborhood trust, not municipal licensing, were the only legitimate currency for operating a short-term rental?
4. **Permit scarcity as political risk**: Neighborhood zone permit limits mean that permit slots are a finite, politically allocated resource. When the city issues new permits, who has informal influence over how slots are distributed — staff planners, council members, neighborhood liaisons — and how does early relationship-building with those decision-makers change your odds of securing permits for the properties you want to add?
