# Topic Cluster 10: Guest Experience, Pricing Authority, and Frontline Workforce

## Questions

1. **Guest emotional journey and accessibility barriers**: What does the emotional journey look like for a first-time STR guest from booking confirmation through check-in, what does walking into the property in the first fifteen minutes telegraph (cared-for or neglected), at which moments does friction most likely cause guests to feel unsupported, confused, or unsafe, and which guests are effectively excluded from STR use in this coastal city by barriers the company could realistically address — guests with mobility limitations, non-English speakers, travelers without smartphones, those unfamiliar with self-check-in?
2. **Frontline workforce as operational backbone**: Cleaners, maintenance contractors, and local-contact persons are the lowest-paid participants in the revenue chain and the ones who interact most directly with physical conditions and guest problems — what working conditions, payment terms, scheduling reliability, and communication practices make this a company they trust and return to versus one they leave or avoid?
3. **Dynamic pricing override authority**: Tools like PriceLabs or Wheelhouse generate revenue lift on average but performance varies significantly by market segment and property type — what decision framework determines when algorithmic pricing should be overridden, who has the authority to make that call, and how does the company measure whether manual interventions improve or erode total revenue per property?
4. **When something goes wrong from the guest's view**: From the guest's perspective, what does it feel like to have a maintenance problem, noise complaint from neighbors, or booking dispute handled by a management company rather than the property owner, and what communication practices would make that experience feel fair and resolving rather than bureaucratic and dismissive?
5. **What if guests were your actual clients and property owners were the product?** Reverse every assumption about who serves whom. If your loyalty ran to guests — guest satisfaction scores, guest veto power on property standards, guests who could "rate the owner" — how would that flip the power dynamic in ways that actually reduced your liability exposure and produced better properties? The absurd version is a guest-governed platform; the real insight is that centering guest experience as the primary fiduciary duty might be a structural differentiator no competitor has tried.
6. **The five-star narrative**: Walk through the experience of a family arriving at a managed property on a Friday evening — they've driven six hours, the kids are restless, and they discover the Wi-Fi password on the welcome card doesn't work. How does this company's guest-communication infrastructure turn that frustrating first hour into a story the family tells as "actually, they handled it perfectly," and what does that require operationally at 8pm on a Friday?
7. **The quiet guest versus the party guest**: Let me walk you through two very different guest profiles checking into the same beachfront property a week apart — a retired couple celebrating an anniversary and a group of college friends celebrating a graduation. What narrative does the company tell itself about which guests it serves, and how does that story shape its screening criteria, noise-monitoring protocols, and the owner contracts it's willing to sign?
