# Brainstorming Questions from The Questioner: Short-Term Rental Property Management Company

---

## Assumptions About the Business Model

1. **Revenue percentage assumption**: Why do we assume that a percentage-of-revenue management fee is the right structure — what would change if the company charged flat monthly fees instead, and which model would more strongly align company incentives with owner outcomes during slow seasons?

2. **Full-service as default**: What if "full service" is actually a competitive disadvantage for some property owners who want partial management — have we considered how a modular service menu might open up a larger segment of the market while preserving the premium positioning?

3. **Owner trust as a given**: We assume property owners will trust a new company with their assets from day one — what specific milestones or proof points would actually shift a skeptical owner from "interested" to "signed," and how long does that journey realistically take in a market with 800 existing listings?

4. **Growth trajectory assumptions**: The plan targets 5–10 properties in year one and 30–50 by year three — what assumptions are baked into that trajectory, and what would have to be true about the regulatory environment, competitive landscape, and capital position for that path to hold?

---

## Regulatory Risk and Jurisdictional Complexity

5. **Regulatory fragility**: What happens to the business model if the city council election six months from now results in stricter STR caps — how many properties in the current target portfolio would become unviable, and does the company have a contingency plan that doesn't require starting over?

6. **State preemption as upside or downside**: If the state legislature passes STR preemption of local bans, does that help or hurt this company — and have we thought through what a suddenly expanded market would demand from the company's operations and compliance systems?

7. **HOA restriction navigation**: The REQUEST identifies HOAs with their own STR restrictions as a distinct layer — what process would the company use to evaluate whether a given property is eligible before onboarding, and who bears the liability if that evaluation is wrong?

8. **Multi-jurisdiction compliance overhead**: The company must track permits across multiple neighborhood zones, occupancy taxes at the municipal level, state sales tax, and federal income reporting — what tools or systems would make this tractable at 30–50 properties without a dedicated compliance staff member?

9. **30-minute response mandate**: The city ordinance requires a local contact within 30 minutes — what if we questioned the assumption that this needs to be a single person, and instead designed a distributed on-call network that also functions as a growth mechanism for the company?

---

## Community Relations and Political Positioning

10. **"Good neighbor" authenticity**: The REQUEST mentions positioning as a "good neighbor" operation — what behaviors, policies, or structural commitments would make that positioning credible to long-term residents rather than just a marketing claim, and who in the community would need to vouch for it?

11. **Opponents as potential allies**: What if some of the most vocal STR opponents — particularly those concerned about housing costs — could be engaged rather than managed around, and what would this company need to offer or commit to in order to open that conversation?

12. **City council election as an opportunity**: Six months before a pivotal city council election is a narrow window — what specific actions could the company take now to shape the regulatory outcome rather than simply waiting to adapt, and what would make those actions legitimate rather than self-serving?

13. **Community tension as a design constraint**: Have we considered whether the level of community tension in this city is a temporary phase that will stabilize, or a structural feature of coastal tourist markets that the business model needs to accommodate permanently?

---

## Competitive Differentiation and Market Position

14. **Differentiation from large platforms**: National STR management platforms already operate in many coastal markets — what does this two-person local company offer that a national operator cannot replicate, and is that advantage durable as the company scales from 10 to 50 properties?

15. **Property owner switching costs**: What would it take for a property owner currently self-managing to switch to this company, and what would it take for them to switch away — and have we designed the contract and service structure with both questions in mind?

16. **Pricing power and fee competition**: If a competing management company enters the same market at a lower fee percentage, what levers does this company have to defend its position beyond price — and which of those levers requires being built in now rather than after competition intensifies?

---

## Operational Resilience and Scale

17. **Single points of failure**: What happens to existing property owners' bookings and guest experiences if one of the two founders is unavailable for a week — and what redundancy is built into the operating model to prevent a personal crisis from becoming a business crisis?

18. **Contractor quality as brand risk**: Cleaning and maintenance contractors will be the most frequent human touchpoints guests experience — what mechanisms does the company have to ensure consistent quality across all contractors, and what does "contractor negligence" liability exposure look like if those mechanisms fail?
