# Brainstorming Questions from The First Principles Thinker: Short-Term Rental Property Management Company

---

## The Core Exchange: What Are Owners Actually Buying?

1. **What is the fundamental value proposition?** Setting aside industry convention, what is the irreducible thing a property owner is paying a management company for — peace of mind, maximized revenue, compliance protection, or something else — and does the current business model actually deliver that thing directly?

2. **Why does the percentage-of-revenue fee structure exist?** If we were designing the incentive alignment between manager and owner from scratch, what fee structure would most directly tie compensation to the outcomes owners care about, and is a revenue percentage actually that structure or just an inherited industry norm?

---

## Regulatory Complexity: Constraint or Competitive Advantage?

3. **What is the fundamental nature of the regulatory problem?** The STR ordinance, HOA restrictions, and multi-jurisdiction tax obligations are framed as obstacles to navigate — but at their core, are these barriers protecting a moat for compliant operators? What would a first-principles analysis of "regulatory complexity as competitive advantage" reveal about who actually benefits from a dense compliance landscape?

4. **If permits are limited by neighborhood zone, what is the company actually managing?** At its core, is this a hospitality business, a compliance infrastructure business, or a permit portfolio business — and does the answer change which capabilities matter most to build first?

---

## Trust Architecture: Owners, Guests, and Community

5. **What is the fundamental source of distrust in this market?** Before designing community-relations tactics or "good neighbor" positioning, what is the root cause of tension between STR operators and long-term residents — is it noise, housing cost displacement, loss of neighborhood character, or something more structural — and does any management company model address the root cause or only the symptoms?

6. **What does "trust" require structurally from each stakeholder?** For property owners, guests, neighbors, and city regulators — what is the minimum structural commitment each party needs to extend trust, and which of those commitments conflict with each other in ways that cannot be papered over with messaging?

---

## Scaling Logic: What Actually Scales?

7. **What is the irreducible bottleneck in going from 10 to 50 properties?** If we strip away every assumption about how property management companies typically scale, what is the single constraint that governs whether this company can grow — is it permitting slots, management labor, owner trust, or capital — and does the current plan address that constraint directly?

8. **Why hire a cleaning crew rather than build a cleaning marketplace?** The fundamental function of cleaning coordination is matching supply (cleaners) to demand (turnovers) at the right time and price. At its core, is this a logistics problem or a quality-control problem, and does the answer determine whether the company should own the cleaning function or orchestrate it?

---

## Political Risk: Treating Regulation as a Volatile Input

9. **What is the minimum viable political position that survives any election outcome?** Given that city council composition and STR policy could shift significantly in six months, what is the simplest structural posture — not marketing position, but legal and operational structure — that makes the company viable under both a more permissive and a more restrictive regulatory regime?
