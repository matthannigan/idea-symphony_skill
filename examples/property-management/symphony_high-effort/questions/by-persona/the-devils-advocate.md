# Brainstorming Questions from The Devil's Advocate: Short-Term Rental Property Management Company

---

## Regulatory Quicksand: Are You Building on Solid Ground?

1. **Permit-zone saturation**: If the city's neighborhood-zoned permit caps fill up before you reach your 30-50 property target, what is your fallback strategy — and have we considered whether the zones most attractive to tourists are also the ones most likely to cap out first?

2. **State preemption as double-edged sword**: The state legislature is considering preempting local STR bans, which sounds favorable — but what happens if preemption instead standardizes a stricter statewide floor, superseding the city's current framework in ways that invalidate your existing permits or force costly re-registration across your portfolio?

3. **HOA veto risk concentration**: If a significant share of your target properties sit in HOA-governed neighborhoods with their own STR restrictions, what prevents a single HOA board vote from wiping out a meaningful chunk of your managed inventory overnight — and how would that concentrated exposure affect your viability?

---

## Financial Model Stress Tests: Where Does the Math Break Down?

4. **Seasonal cash-flow cliff**: Full-service management in a coastal tourist market means revenue is heavily seasonal — have we modeled what happens to your cash position during a two-month off-season trough when cleaning, maintenance, and staff costs continue but booking revenue drops sharply, and is $80K in combined savings sufficient to bridge that gap?

5. **Percentage-of-revenue fee sensitivity**: A revenue-share model means your income collapses in tandem with your owners' during a bad season, a major storm, or a regulatory freeze on new bookings — what fixed cost floor exists in your model that you cannot reduce quickly, and how long can the company sustain that mismatch?

6. **Owner churn economics**: If an owner terminates their management contract after you've invested in onboarding, photography, listing optimization, and vendor relationships, how does that sunk cost affect your break-even per property — and does your current contract structure actually recoup those costs or absorb them as losses?

---

## Liability Architecture: Who Holds the Bag When Things Go Wrong?

7. **Contractor negligence gap**: When a cleaning contractor or maintenance vendor causes damage or injury to a guest, the liability chain — owner, management company, contractor — is rarely clean. Have we examined whether your current insurance and contract structure leaves you exposed as the "deep pocket" intermediary, even for harms you didn't directly cause?

8. **Guest injury and duty-of-care ambiguity**: As a full-service manager making operational decisions (pricing, guest vetting, property readiness), courts may treat you more like an innkeeper than a passive agent — have we stress-tested whether your liability limitation clauses would actually hold up, or whether they create a false sense of security?

9. **Noise-monitoring compliance liability**: The city mandates noise monitoring devices — if a monitor fails, is misconfigured, or captures data that a neighbor or city inspector later uses against an owner, who bears that liability, and does your management agreement clearly allocate it?

---

## Community and Political Exposure: Are You Building a Target on Your Back?

10. **City council election timing**: With STR regulation as a key campaign issue and an election six months away, the regulatory environment could shift materially before you have a stable portfolio. Have we considered what it means to be scaling aggressively — signing owner contracts, building staff — during a period when the rules governing your entire business model may be rewritten?

11. **"Good neighbor" positioning fragility**: One high-profile guest incident — a party, a noise complaint that makes the local news, a guest injury — can shatter a carefully built community-relations narrative. Have we considered how a single bad actor among your guests could be weaponized by STR opponents at a politically charged moment, and what crisis response infrastructure you'd need to contain that?

12. **Regulatory capture risk from proximity**: Engaging with city council and HOA boards to shape STR policy is smart, but it also creates visibility and political exposure. Have we considered whether active advocacy makes you a named target in community opposition campaigns, and whether there's a point at which political engagement does more reputational harm than good?

---

## Scaling Assumptions: Is the Path from 10 to 50 Properties as Linear as It Looks?

13. **Operational capacity cliff**: Going from 5-10 properties to 30-50 isn't a linear expansion — it likely requires a qualitative jump in systems, staffing, and vendor capacity. Have we identified the specific inflection point where your current informal processes break down, and what the capital and time cost of building that infrastructure looks like before revenue catches up?
