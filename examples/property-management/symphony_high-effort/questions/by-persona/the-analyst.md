# Brainstorming Questions from The Analyst: Short-Term Rental Property Management Company

---

## Unit Economics and Financial Architecture

1. **Break-even per property**: Given a management fee structured as a percentage of booking revenue, what average nightly rate, occupancy rate, and seasonal distribution does each property need to achieve before it contributes positive margin — and how does that threshold shift when you factor in cleaning coordination, maintenance dispatching, and the compliance overhead specific to this city's registration and noise-monitoring requirements?

2. **Capital burn rate vs. growth curve**: With $80K in combined startup capital and a target of 5-10 properties in year one scaling to 30-50 by year three, what does the month-by-month cash flow model look like — at what property count does the operation become self-sustaining, and what are the critical cost inflection points (staffing, software, insurance thresholds) that could collapse that timeline?

3. **Fee structure sensitivity**: How does the percentage-of-revenue model perform across the full occupancy distribution — a beachfront property at 85% seasonal occupancy versus an inland unit at 40% — and what fee floor or hybrid structure (flat retainer plus percentage) would keep low-performing properties from being margin-negative at current fixed cost levels?

---

## Regulatory Compliance Cost and Risk Quantification

4. **Permit attrition risk**: Given that the city ordinance limits permits per neighborhood zone, what proportion of the 800 active listings are in zones where permit caps could be hit — and how does the business model change if 20%, 40%, or 60% of the target portfolio eventually faces permit denial or non-renewal?

5. **Multi-jurisdictional tax compliance burden**: The company sits at the intersection of municipal occupancy tax, state sales tax, and federal income reporting across multiple property owners — what is the fully loaded cost of maintaining compliant tax collection and remittance across these layers, and at what portfolio scale does it make economic sense to build this in-house versus outsourcing to a specialized STR tax service?

6. **30-minute local contact requirement as an operational constraint**: The ordinance mandates a local contact person within a 30-minute response radius — how does this single requirement shape the company's staffing model (on-call coverage, geographic limits on portfolio expansion, after-hours labor costs), and what is the marginal cost per property of maintaining that coverage as the portfolio grows beyond a single founder's capacity to personally respond?

7. **State preemption scenario analysis**: The state legislature is considering preemption of local STR bans — what are the two or three concrete regulatory scenarios that could plausibly emerge in the next 18-36 months, and how does each scenario shift the company's compliance cost structure, portfolio growth ceiling, and the strategic value of early investment in city-specific permit relationships?

---

## Operational Scalability and Quality Control

8. **Cleaning and maintenance as the scaling bottleneck**: Cleaning and maintenance are the highest-frequency, highest-variance operational touchpoints — what are the key metrics (turnaround time, defect rate, cost per clean) that distinguish scalable vendor relationships from ones that break under volume, and what operational systems or contractual structures keep quality consistent across 30-50 properties without a proportional increase in management headcount?

9. **Pricing algorithm versus human judgment**: Dynamic pricing tools like PriceLabs or Wheelhouse generate revenue lift on average, but their performance varies significantly by market segment and property type — what decision framework determines when algorithmic pricing should be overridden, who has authority to make that call, and how does the company measure whether manual interventions improve or erode total revenue per property?

10. **Portfolio onboarding throughput**: Growing from 10 to 50 properties requires a repeatable onboarding process for property setup, listing creation, owner contract execution, permit verification, and insurance confirmation — what is the realistic throughput capacity of a two-person founding team, at what point does onboarding become the binding constraint on growth, and what is the minimum viable hire (full-time, fractional, or contract) that removes that constraint?

---

## Owner Trust and Churn Risk

11. **Churn drivers and retention levers**: Property owner churn is the existential risk in a management company's early years — what are the measurable early indicators that a property owner relationship is deteriorating (response time complaints, revenue underperformance versus stated expectations, maintenance disputes), and what intervention framework converts at-risk relationships before they terminate rather than after?

12. **Performance transparency architecture**: Owners who entrust a company with their asset need visibility into revenue performance, occupancy trends, expense allocation, and regulatory compliance status — what reporting cadence and data granularity satisfies owner expectations without creating an administrative overhead that scales poorly, and how does the reporting design differ for a financially sophisticated owner versus one with a single property and no prior STR experience?

---

## Community and Political Risk as Business Variables

13. **Reputation as a measurable asset**: The company's "good neighbor" positioning is a strategic differentiator in a politically contested market — what specific operational practices (noise monitoring response times, guest screening thresholds, community complaint resolution rates) can be tracked as leading indicators of whether the company is actually building community goodwill versus simply claiming it, and how does that track record translate into a defensible position with city council or HOA boards when regulatory pressure increases?
