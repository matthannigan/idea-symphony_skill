# Brainstorming Questions from The Audience Advocate: Short-Term Rental Property Management Company

---

## Guest Experience and Accessibility

1. **First impression anxiety**: What does the emotional journey look like for a first-time short-term rental guest — from booking confirmation through check-in — and at which moments does uncertainty or friction most likely cause them to feel unsupported, confused, or unsafe?

2. **Barrier mapping**: Which guests are effectively excluded from using short-term rentals in a coastal tourist city by barriers this management company could realistically address — including guests with mobility limitations, non-English speakers, travelers without smartphones, or those unfamiliar with self-check-in systems?

3. **When things go wrong**: From the guest's perspective, what does it feel like to have a maintenance problem, a noise complaint from neighbors, or a booking dispute handled by a management company rather than a property owner — and what communication practices would make that experience feel fair and resolving rather than bureaucratic and dismissive?

---

## Property Owner Trust and Agency

4. **The trust gap**: Property owners are entrusting this company with their most significant financial asset — what are the unspoken fears and unarticulated anxieties they carry that they may not voice during the sales process, and how does the management company earn their confidence beyond the contract?

5. **Power and transparency**: From the property owner's perspective, what information asymmetries exist when a management company controls pricing, guest selection, and maintenance decisions on their behalf — and what visibility and override mechanisms would make owners feel they retain meaningful agency over their own property?

6. **Owners facing regulatory exposure**: When a compliance violation occurs (an unpermitted night, a missed noise complaint response, an occupancy tax underpayment), which party — company or owner — actually bears the reputational, financial, and legal consequence from the owner's lived experience, and does the contract match that reality?

---

## Long-Term Renter and Neighborhood Community Impact

7. **Housing cost displacement**: The people most harmed by the expansion of short-term rentals in a coastal city — long-term renters priced out or displaced — have no formal voice in how this company operates. What obligations, if any, does this company have to acknowledge and respond to that harm, and what would genuine "good neighbor" positioning require beyond noise monitoring and responsive maintenance?

8. **Neighbor relationships, not just compliance**: From the perspective of a year-round resident living next door to a short-term rental property managed by this company, what would make that adjacency feel acceptable or even positive — and what does the gap between that and "technically compliant" management look like in daily life?

9. **Community benefit vs. community cost**: If the founders were required to articulate, to a skeptical long-term renter at a city council meeting, what direct benefit their management company provides to people who don't own property and don't travel — what could they honestly say, and how might that answer shape how they design their operation?

---

## Regulatory Experience for the Non-Expert

10. **The property owner navigating permits**: Many property owners who contract with this company will not fully understand the new STR ordinance, neighborhood zone restrictions, or HOA bylaws that govern their property. From their perspective, what is the experience of discovering — after signing — that their property cannot legally operate as a short-term rental, and what does the management company owe them in terms of pre-contract due diligence and honest assessment?

11. **Guests and regulatory opacity**: Short-term rental guests typically have no visibility into whether a property is legally permitted, tax-compliant, or HOA-approved. If a guest is disrupted mid-stay because of a compliance action or HOA complaint, what does that experience look and feel like, and what disclosure obligations does the management company bear toward them?

---

## Worker and Contractor Experience

12. **The cleaning and maintenance workforce**: The operational backbone of this business — cleaners, maintenance contractors, local contact persons — are likely the lowest-paid participants in the revenue chain and the ones who interact most directly with physical conditions and guest problems. From their perspective, what working conditions, payment terms, scheduling reliability, and communication practices would make this a company they trust and return to versus one they leave or avoid?

13. **The 30-minute local contact obligation**: The city ordinance mandates a local contact person reachable within 30 minutes. From the perspective of the person actually filling that role — who may be on call nights, weekends, and holidays — what does "being available" realistically cost in terms of quality of life, and how does the company design that role in a way that is sustainable and fairly compensated rather than extractive?
