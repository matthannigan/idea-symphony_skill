# Brainstorming Questions from The Storyteller: Short-Term Rental Property Management Company

---

## The Property Owner's Journey: From Skeptic to Partner

1. **The trust-building arc**: Imagine a coastal homeowner — call her Maria — who has watched her neighbor's STR property become a revolving door of strangers and noise complaints; she's inherited a condo and feels the pull of rental income but fears becoming the neighbor everyone resents. What specific sequence of experiences, milestones, and visible results would this company need to deliver in the first 90 days to transform Maria from a cautious skeptic into an enthusiastic advocate who refers her friends?

2. **The moment of reckoning**: Picture the first time a property owner opens their monthly statement and compares what they earned against what they expected — the numbers might disappoint, a surprise deduction appears, or a maintenance charge feels unexplained. How does the company design its reporting, communication cadence, and fee transparency so that this moment of financial reckoning strengthens rather than fractures the owner relationship?

---

## The Guest's Arc: Arrival to Review

3. **The five-star narrative**: Walk through the experience of a family arriving at a managed property on a Friday evening — they've driven six hours, the kids are restless, and they discover the Wi-Fi password on the welcome card doesn't work. How does this company's guest-communication infrastructure turn that frustrating first hour into a story the family tells as "actually, they handled it perfectly," and what does that require operationally at 8pm on a Friday?

4. **The quiet guest versus the party guest**: Let me walk you through two very different guest profiles checking into the same beachfront property a week apart — a retired couple celebrating an anniversary and a group of college friends celebrating a graduation. What narrative does the company tell itself about which guests it serves, and how does that story shape its screening criteria, noise-monitoring protocols, and the owner contracts it's willing to sign?

---

## The Regulatory Landscape as a Character in the Story

5. **The city council vote as a plot point**: Imagine it's seven months from now: the city council election results come in, and the new council majority is hostile to short-term rentals — new permit freezes are on the table and the local paper is running stories about housing displacement. How does this company's origin story and the relationships it has built in those first seven months determine whether it is cast as a villain or a community partner in the news coverage that follows?

6. **The HOA confrontation**: Picture a property owner who brings in this company to manage a condo in a neighborhood where the HOA board has just passed a resolution that may — or may not, depending on legal interpretation — restrict short-term rentals. Walk through the conversation that needs to happen between the company, the owner, and the HOA board: who speaks first, what story does the company tell about itself, and what does a good outcome look like for all three parties?

---

## Building the Team: The People Behind the Brand

7. **The 2am emergency and the person who answers**: Imagine the company's first major crisis — a pipe bursts in a managed property at 2am while six guests are trying to sleep, and the local contact requirement means someone on the team must be at the door within 30 minutes. Who is that person, what did the company promise them when it hired them, and what does this single moment reveal about the kind of employer and operator this company actually is — not the kind it said it would be in its pitch deck?
