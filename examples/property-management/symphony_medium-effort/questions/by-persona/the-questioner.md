# Brainstorming Questions from The Questioner: Short-Term Rental Property Management Company

## Cluster 1: Assumptions About the Business Model

**Why percentage-of-revenue?**: Why do we assume that a percentage-of-booking-revenue model is the right fee structure — and what happens to the alignment between company incentives and owner outcomes when vacancy rates spike, peak-season pricing becomes contested, or the platform (Airbnb/VRBO) changes its commission structure?

**What "full-service" actually costs**: Have we examined what each component of "full-service" (listing optimization, guest communication, cleaning, maintenance, pricing) actually costs to deliver per property at scale versus at launch — and at what point does the bundle become a financial liability rather than a selling point?

**Who really owns the guest relationship?**: If Airbnb and VRBO own the booking transaction and guest data, what does the management company actually control — and what happens to the business if those platforms significantly restrict third-party manager access or introduce their own management tiers?

## Cluster 2: Regulatory Risk and Hidden Constraints

**What if the political wind shifts?**: The city council election in six months could tighten STR regulations significantly — what does the business look like under a 30% permit reduction scenario, and have we mapped which of the 5–10 Year One properties would survive in each zone?

**Why do we assume statewide preemption helps?**: If the state legislature passes preemption of local STR bans, we assume that expands opportunity — but what if preemption also standardizes disclosure, tax remittance, or liability rules in ways that raise compliance costs uniformly and eliminate the competitive advantage of local expertise?

**HOA restrictions as a filter or a signal**: Have we considered whether HOA-restricted neighborhoods are a constraint to route around, or whether they are telling us something about the kind of community relationships that determine whether any STR operation is sustainable in the long run?

## Cluster 3: Trust Architecture

**How do property owners define "trust"?**: When a property owner evaluates whether to hand keys and revenue to a management company, what specific failure modes are they most afraid of — and does the current service design directly address those fears, or does it assume trust will come from contract language and insurance certificates?

**What does "local contact within 30 minutes" actually require organizationally?**: The ordinance mandates a local contact person with 30-minute response capability — but have we thought through what staffing model, on-call structure, and geographic coverage that actually requires at 10 properties versus 50, and what the cost trajectory looks like?

**What proof of performance can we offer owners?**: What if we assumed property owners will demand real-time visibility into occupancy, pricing decisions, and maintenance actions — how would that transparency requirement change our tooling choices and reporting cadence from day one?

## Cluster 4: Community and Legitimacy

**What does "good neighbor" mean in practice?**: We use the phrase "good neighbor operation" as a positioning goal — but what specific, observable behaviors would skeptical long-term renters, neighborhood associations, or city council members point to as evidence that a management company is actually behaving as a good neighbor rather than performing the label?

**Why do we assume the company should be neutral in the political fight?**: The REQUEST suggests "engaging with the political process" — but have we interrogated whether neutrality between STR advocates and opponents is even coherent, or whether the company's long-term legitimacy requires choosing a visible position?

**What happens if a guest incident becomes public?**: A noise complaint, a guest injury, or a property damage event at one managed property could become a news story in a city where STR regulation is a live political issue — what crisis-response capacity does the company need on day one, and how does that shape the launch portfolio decisions?

## Cluster 5: Scale and Transition Points

**What assumptions break between 10 and 50 properties?**: The path from 5–10 to 30–50 properties is treated as growth — but what processes, relationships, and quality controls that work at launch will structurally fail at scale, and have we identified those transition points before they become crises?
