# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Short-Term Rental Property Management Company

## Cluster 1: Discovery — Peak Moments in Property Management Excellence

**When trust was earned in a single interaction**: Think about the short-term rental managers, property caretakers, or hospitality professionals you most admire — what specific moment or gesture turned a skeptical property owner into a fierce advocate, and what conditions made that trust possible?

**When regulation became an asset**: There are cases where businesses have turned a complex regulatory environment into a competitive differentiator rather than a burden — what does it look like when a property management company genuinely embraces compliance as a strength, and what does that company do differently from day one?

**When neighbors became allies**: Recall a moment when a short-term rental operation transformed a worried neighbor into a vocal supporter — what did the management company do, say, or build into its operations to make that shift happen, and how did it know it had succeeded?

## Cluster 2: Discovery — Existing Strengths Worth Amplifying

**What the hospitality background already knows**: The founding team brings a hospitality background into a field that often treats property management as purely transactional — what specific hospitality instincts, habits, or frameworks already exist in that experience that most property managers would have to learn from scratch?

**What the coastal market already rewards**: In a tourist-heavy coastal city with 800 active STR listings, there are already property owners and guests who have had exceptional experiences — what patterns, practices, or values show up consistently in those peak-experience properties, and how might a new management company systematically build on what already works?

## Cluster 3: Dream — Vivid Futures Built on Existing Strengths

**If this became the company others benchmarked against**: Imagine it is three years from now and a delegation from another coastal city visits to understand how this company built a reputation as the gold standard for responsible short-term rental management — what would they observe in daily operations, in the relationship with city hall, and in what neighbors say unprompted?

**If regulatory complexity became a community service**: Picture a future where this company's deep knowledge of permit zones, noise monitoring requirements, and occupancy tax compliance is so trusted that city officials proactively consult it when drafting new STR rules — what would have had to be true about how the company engaged with regulators from the beginning for that relationship to develop?

## Cluster 4: Design — Structures That Reliably Create the Desired Future

**What owner-partner agreements look like when they reflect shared values**: Given that property owners are trusting this company with their most significant asset, what would a management contract look like if it were designed not just to allocate liability but to express a genuine partnership — what terms, transparency commitments, or communication rhythms would signal that this company sees itself as a steward rather than a vendor?

**What a "good neighbor" operation looks like in practice, not just positioning**: The goal of being seen as a "good neighbor" operation rather than absentee management is stated — but what are the concrete, observable, repeatable practices that would make that reputation real over time, so that it is not marketing language but an accurate description of how the company operates every week?

## Cluster 5: Destiny — First Steps and Sustained Commitments

**What could be done in the first 90 days to lay the foundation**: Given the strengths the founding team already brings — hospitality instincts, real estate knowledge, $80K in capital, and a clear-eyed view of the regulatory landscape — what are the two or three actions in the first 90 days that would make everything else easier, and which of those actions is already within reach right now?
