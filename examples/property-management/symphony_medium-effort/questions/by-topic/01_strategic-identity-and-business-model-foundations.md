# Topic Cluster 01: Strategic Identity and Business Model Foundations

## Questions

1. **Choosing which problem the company actually solves**: Does the company position as a yield-optimizing pricing engine (maximum yield for owners), a community-relations manager that holds conduct standards, or a full-service hospitality operator (maximum guest experience) — these three identities are not fully compatible (aggressive pricing creates noise complaints; conduct standards reduce short-term yield; hospitality investment eats margin) — and which positioning creates the most durable competitive moat in a market likely to consolidate, especially given that established platforms (Vacasa, Evolve) already have compliance teams and capital the founders cannot match, and especially if local regulation tightens significantly in year two?
2. **Fee structure, full-service cost-to-deliver, and platform dependency**: Why do we assume a percentage-of-booking-revenue fee is the right structure — what are the quantified trade-offs against a tiered/performance-bonus alternative in terms of predictable revenue versus owner-incentive alignment, what does each component of "full-service" (listing optimization, guest communication, cleaning, maintenance, pricing) actually cost to deliver per property at scale versus at launch (at what point does the bundle become a financial liability rather than a selling point), what happens when vacancy spikes, peak-season pricing is contested, or the platform changes its commission, and given that Airbnb/VRBO own the booking transaction and guest data, what does the company actually control if those platforms restrict third-party manager access or introduce their own management tiers?
3. **What if you're actually the problem you think you're solving?**: The founders frame this as a "good neighbor" management company that brings professionalism to an otherwise chaotic STR market. But imagine the opposite were true: what if a well-run, scalable STR management company is precisely what accelerates neighborhood displacement — not the rogue operators, but the competent, compliant, growing ones? The insight: does professionalizing STR management make it easier for housing to convert at scale? If the answer is yes, what does "good neighbor" actually mean, and who gets to decide?
4. **What if the real product isn't property management — it's regulatory legitimacy for property owners?**: Here's something absurd, but stay with me: what if the management fee is irrelevant, and what owners are actually paying for is political cover — the ability to say "we use a professional company" when the city council comes calling? The insight: if regulatory legitimacy is the core product, the company's strategy, pricing, and positioning should be built around that — not around operational efficiency.
5. **What if management weren't the product at all**: Imagine a world where this company didn't exist yet and we were inventing it from scratch — not as a management firm that takes a percentage of bookings, but as something categorically different: a living trust model in which property owners, guests, and neighbors all hold co-stewardship stakes in each listing's long-term health. What new structures of accountability, shared benefit, and community belonging would have to be invented, and how would that change every decision from pricing to noise response?
