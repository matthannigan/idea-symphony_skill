# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Space-Themed 5th Birthday Party Planning

---

## Discovery: Peak Moments of Childhood Wonder

1. **The transported child**: Think back to a birthday party, holiday, or special event where a 5-year-old was completely swept up in awe and delight — what specific detail or moment created that sense of wonder, and how could that same quality be engineered into a space-themed experience?

2. **What space already sparks**: For children this age, space naturally evokes rockets, stars, and astronauts — what stories, images, or games have already lit up this particular child's imagination around the theme, and how can the party deepen and celebrate what she already loves?

3. **The host's hidden strengths**: What skills, resources, or relationships does the family already have that could become party assets — a neighbor with a projector, a parent who bakes beautifully, a backyard with dark corners perfect for a "launch pad" — and how might those existing strengths shape the party's character?

---

## Dream: The Vivid Future Memory

4. **The story she tells at 10**: Imagine the birthday girl at age 10, describing this party to a new friend with shining eyes — what three or four specific sensory details or moments would she mention, and how do those details translate into design choices for the party?

5. **The parents' celebration too**: If this party felt genuinely joyful and manageable for the adults hosting it — not just survived but celebrated — what would that look like? What would the parents say to friends afterward about what made it work, and how can those outcomes be built in from the start?

6. **Beyond "good for a kids' party"**: What would it look like if this party exceeded every guest family's expectations — not just "the kids had fun" but something that left parents and children genuinely inspired or touched? What single element would be most responsible for that reaction?

---

## Design: Structures That Reliably Create Magic

7. **Scaling wonder to 5-year-old attention**: Given that young children are naturally curious and imaginative but easily overwhelmed, what party structures — a loose narrative arc, a sequence of short activities, a clear "mission" for the kids to complete — would channel that energy toward peak moments rather than chaos?

8. **The theme as a throughline**: Space is a rich theme that could run shallow (star-shaped cookies) or deep (a story about a crew of astronaut explorers with a mission). What specific structural choices — a simple unifying story, a costume element, a ceremony — would make the theme feel genuinely immersive rather than decorative?

9. **Building in the guardrails for success**: Parties for 11 five-year-olds involve real logistical complexity. What structures — a clear schedule, designated helper roles for other parents, activity stations that work even if a child steps away — would make the event feel smooth and celebratory rather than stressful?

---

## Destiny: First Steps and Commitments

10. **The one decision that unlocks the rest**: If the family could make a single early commitment — choosing a venue, securing a central activity, deciding on a narrative arc — that would make every other planning decision easier and more coherent, what would that commitment be and why does it have such leverage?
