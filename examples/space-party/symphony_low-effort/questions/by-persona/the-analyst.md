# Brainstorming Questions from The Analyst: Space-Themed 5th Birthday Party Planning

---

## Activity Structure and Attention Span Constraints

1. **Activity sequencing**: Breaking this down by cognitive load — which activities require the most sustained attention from 5-year-olds, and how should those be sequenced relative to lower-demand moments (free play, snack time) to prevent meltdowns or disengagement across the full party arc?

2. **Duration calibration**: The key factors seem to be energy level and novelty decay — how long does a structured space activity (a craft, a "mission," a game) hold a 5-year-old's attention before it needs to transition, and what does that imply for the total number of distinct activity slots needed across a 2-hour versus 3-hour party window?

3. **Parallel vs. sequential design**: For a group of 11 children, what are the trade-offs between running all kids through a single activity at once versus setting up parallel stations — and how does each model affect adult supervision load and the risk of crowd-induced chaos?

## Budget Allocation and Cost Trade-offs

4. **Spending priority matrix**: If the total party budget has a hard ceiling, what framework would help allocate dollars across the main cost categories — venue, food/cake, decorations, favors, activities — in a way that maximizes perceived memorability for the birthday girl rather than spreading spend evenly?

5. **DIY vs. purchased thresholds**: For each decoration and activity category, what is the crossover point where DIY saves meaningful money versus consuming enough parent time and stress that purchasing becomes the higher-value choice — and which space-theme elements are most versus least favorable to DIY?

6. **Favor cost-per-child economics**: Party favors for 10 guests carry a per-unit cost that compounds quickly — what is a realistic per-child spend range that feels substantive without crossing into waste, and which favor formats (single curated item vs. goodie bag mix) deliver better perceived value at each price point?

## Safety and Risk Variables

7. **Hazard surface inventory**: This challenge has distinct safety components — choking risks (small prop pieces), allergy exposure (food/craft materials), physical hazard (active games, balloons), and supervision gaps (11 children, limited adult-to-child ratio) — which of these represents the highest probability risk for this age group, and how does that prioritization change the activity selection?

8. **Supervision ratio implications**: Given that 5-year-olds require close adult oversight during structured activities, what adult-to-child ratio is needed to run the planned activity count safely, and how should the party host recruit or assign that support in advance?

## Thematic Coherence and Developmental Fit

9. **Concept accessibility mapping**: Space/astronomy contains concepts that range from highly accessible to 5-year-olds (rockets, stars, aliens, the moon) to abstract (gravity, light-years, orbital mechanics) — what criteria determine which concepts to center the party around, and how does centering on the wrong abstraction level undermine engagement?

10. **Theme-to-activity translation**: For each planned activity (craft, game, food item), how tightly does it need to map to a recognizable space concept to register as "space-themed" to a 5-year-old versus a parent — and where do those two audiences diverge in their thematic expectations?

11. **Narrative coherence vs. modular theming**: Breaking this down further — is a party structured around a single narrative arc ("astronaut training mission") more engaging for this age group than a set of loosely space-branded individual activities, and what are the logistical costs of the narrative approach (scripting, costumes, sequencing rigidity)?

## Logistics and Execution Reliability

12. **Failure mode analysis**: The key decision points for party logistics each carry a failure mode — what happens if a planned activity runs long, a child refuses to participate, materials run out, or the cake arrives late — and which contingency gaps are most likely to cascade into a poor experience if unaddressed in advance planning?
