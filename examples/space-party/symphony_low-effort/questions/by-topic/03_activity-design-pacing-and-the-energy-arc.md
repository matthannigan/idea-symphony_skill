# Topic Cluster 03: Activity Design, Pacing, and the Energy Arc

## Questions

1. **Activity sequencing, duration calibration, and transition design**: Given that 5-year-olds typically sustain focused engagement for 10–15 minutes before novelty decay forces a transition, how should activities be sequenced by cognitive load to build intentional energy peaks and valleys toward a climactic moment (rather than spiking early and leaving children bored at the end), what does that imply for the number of activity slots needed across a 2-hour vs. 3-hour window, and what happens in the gaps between activities — the moments when one game ends and the next hasn't started — that shape the children's collective mood?
2. **Parallel vs. sequential design**: For 11 children, what are the trade-offs between running everyone through a single activity at once versus parallel stations — and how does each model affect adult supervision load and the risk of crowd-induced chaos?
3. **Ant-colony activity design**: Ant colonies thrive because each individual follows simple rules that produce complex collective behavior without top-down coordination — what activity structures would let 11 five-year-olds self-organize and stay engaged with minimal adult herding, rather than requiring constant directed instruction?
4. **The arrival story**: Imagine a child stepping through the front door and immediately knowing they've entered a different world — what sights, sounds, or sensory cues would signal to a 5-year-old within the first thirty seconds that they've just boarded a spaceship, and how does that opening moment set an emotional tone that carries through the whole party?
