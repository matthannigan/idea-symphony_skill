# Topic Cluster 07: Safety as Lived Experience

## Questions

1. **Hazard surface inventory at child's-eye view**: This challenge has distinct safety components — choking risks (small space-prop pieces), allergy exposure (food/craft materials), physical hazard (active games, balloons), and supervision gaps (11 children, limited ratio) — which represents the highest-probability risk for this age group, and have we walked the space at a 5-year-old's height to find hazards (sharp corners, trailing cords, narrow passages) that only become visible at that level?
2. **Dietary and allergy realities**: Among 11 five-year-olds the realistic probability of allergies, dietary restrictions, or sensory food aversions is high, and parents rarely volunteer this information unless explicitly asked — what proactive step in the invitation process surfaces it early enough to shape food choices, and how do you handle a last-minute disclosure on party day?
3. **Supervision blind spots and self-limiting activities**: Where are the moments in the party flow — transitions, outdoor movement, bathroom breaks — where children could be unsupervised without anyone noticing, and for activities where children test limits (jumping, climbing, throwing), how is each designed to be self-limiting or quickly correctable when a child pushes past the intended use?
