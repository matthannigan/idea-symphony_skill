# Brainstorming Questions from The Questioner: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## What "Core Value" Actually Means

1. **Assumed value vs. revealed value**: Why do we assume the app's true core value is already present among the 15 existing features — what if none of them individually captures the reason users originally downloaded it, and the real core is an experience that doesn't yet exist?

2. **The 8% retention signal**: What is the 8% of users who stayed actually doing inside the app, and have we asked them directly what would happen to their routine if the app disappeared tomorrow?

3. **Feature vs. outcome framing**: If we stopped asking "which features do users want to keep" and instead asked "what outcome do users want to achieve in their lives," how would that reframe which 3-4 capabilities belong in the MVP?

4. **The 47-second session**: What does a 47-second average session tell us about the nature of the interaction users actually want — is the goal to make sessions longer, or to make 47-second sessions so satisfying that users return 10 times a day?

5. **Whose definition of core**: Why do we assume the team, the data, and the users would all point to the same 3-4 features — and what happens when they disagree?

---

## Assumptions About Simplification

6. **The "stripping down" metaphor**: Why do we frame this as removing features from a bloated product rather than building a new, focused product that happens to share some components with the old one — and does that framing change what the team is willing to let go of?

7. **Incremental vs. clean break tradeoffs**: What would users who currently rely on the features being cut actually do — move to competitors, abandon the behavior, or advocate loudly — and have we mapped that exodus before deciding how dramatic the reset should be?

8. **The passionate minority problem**: What is the right framework for deciding that a small but passionate user base for a particular feature does not represent the product's future — is passion a signal about value, or about switching cost?

9. **Success metric redefinition**: Why do we assume the simplified product should be measured by the same DAU/MAU and retention metrics as the bloated one — what new metrics would prove the reset worked on its own terms?

10. **The team's blind spots**: Which of the 15 features does each team member privately believe is the real core, and how much of the "data-driven" decision process is actually a rationalization of those pre-existing beliefs?

---

## Hidden Constraints and Unstated Risks

11. **The 8-month runway as a constraint shaper**: How does the 8-month runway change which version of simplification is even possible — and are we designing the reset around what's actually right for the product, or around what can ship before the money runs out?

12. **Technical entanglement**: What if removing a feature that "nobody uses" turns out to break infrastructure that several other features depend on — how would that discovery change the simplification sequence?

13. **The churn math**: What percentage of the 180K downloads would need to churn post-relaunch before the reset would be considered a failure, and has that threshold been made explicit and agreed upon before any cuts are made?

14. **Burnout and judgment**: How much should we trust the current team's feature prioritization judgment when they are described as burned out — and what would it take to get them to a state where they could make decisions they'd be proud of in two years?

---

## The Relaunch as a Strategic Moment

15. **What the relaunch communicates**: What story does a dramatic simplification tell to users, to potential investors, and to competitors — and is that story intentional or just a side effect of the technical decision?

16. **The competitor opportunity**: What if the users who churn during simplification don't go to existing competitors but reveal an underserved niche that no current product owns — how would that change whether the reset is framed as retreat or repositioning?

17. **The trust repair question**: What would current users need to experience in the first week after relaunch to feel that the team listened to them rather than abandoned them — and have we designed that experience, or just the product changes?
