# Brainstorming Questions from The Visionary: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## Reimagining the Product's Fundamental Identity

1. **What if the app didn't exist yet**: If you were inventing a social fitness app from scratch today — with no legacy code, no existing user base, no inherited mental model of what a fitness app "must" be — what is the single irreducible thing it would do, and how would you know you'd found it?

2. **The product as a relationship, not a tool**: What would it mean to build something users don't "open" at all — where the app's value emerges through ambient, continuous presence in a person's life rather than session-based interactions, and how might that dissolve the "47-second session" problem at its root rather than optimizing around it?

---

## The Act of Subtraction as Invention

3. **Subtraction as a new category**: What if the relaunch wasn't framed as a stripped-down fitness app but as the invention of an entirely new category — one defined not by what it does but by what it refuses to do — and how would that refusal become the product's most compelling value proposition?

4. **The identity users never had a word for**: Among the 180K people who downloaded this app, is there a need so latent and unarticulated that no existing product category names it, and if so, how would you design a relaunch that creates the category rather than competes within one?

---

## Radical Relationships Between User and Product

5. **What if the user defined the core**: Imagine a version of this relaunch where the product's essential function is not decided by the team at all — where the three or four features that survive are discovered through a process that gives users generative agency over the product's identity, not just feedback on a predetermined direction. What would that process look like, and what could it surface that data and interviews cannot?

---

## Reinventing the Meaning of Retention

6. **Retention as the wrong success frame**: What if 30-day retention is a metric borrowed from a paradigm that no longer applies — one inherited from social media apps optimizing for addiction — and the relaunch's deepest opportunity is to define a genuinely new success measure that has no analog in current mobile benchmarks: something that captures whether the product is making users' lives better rather than whether they are opening the app?

---

## The Team and Product as a Living System

7. **The team's exhaustion as signal, not problem**: What if the team's burnout from maintaining 15 half-built features is not an obstacle to solve before the relaunch but the most honest signal in the entire product — a kind of organizational immune response — and the relaunch's purpose is not to restore the team's capacity to build features but to create a product architecture so coherent that building becomes energizing rather than depleting?
