# Brainstorming Questions from The Audience Advocate: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## The Experience of Overwhelm: What Users Actually Feel

1. **Mapping the first-open moment**: When a new user opens this app for the first time, what is their emotional state — hopeful, skeptical, overwhelmed? At exactly which screen, tap, or notification does that initial hope collapse into "I don't know where to start," and what would it take to redesign that moment so it lands differently?

2. **Silent workarounds vs. stated complaints**: User feedback says "overwhelming" and "jack of all trades" — but what are users *doing* rather than saying? Are they opening the app and immediately closing it, using only one tab repeatedly, or ignoring notifications entirely? What behaviors reveal the gap between what users were promised and what they actually experience?

3. **The 47-second session**: An average session of 47 seconds suggests users are either completing something very small or abandoning almost immediately. What does a successful 47-second session look like from a user's perspective — did they get what they came for, or did they leave frustrated? How would redesigning around the user's actual time and intent shape what the core features should be?

4. **Burden of navigation**: Six navigation tabs and a hamburger menu places a significant cognitive load on the user before they can do anything. From the perspective of someone who just wants to log a workout after a long day, what does it feel like to face this navigation structure — and what does that experience suggest about which features belong at the surface versus the background?

---

## Whose Needs Are Being Centered in the Simplification Decision

5. **The 180K who downloaded**: Of 180K people who downloaded this app, a decision to simplify will affect them differently depending on why they downloaded it. How are the team gathering real signal about the full range of user motivations — not just from the 8% who stayed, but from the 92% who didn't? What assumptions about "typical users" might be quietly baked into the feature-selection process?

6. **Users who built habits around niche features**: Some features may have small but passionate user bases who have integrated them into genuine daily routines. What is owed to users who adapted their behavior around a feature that is now being cut — and how would the team know whether discontinuing a feature would remove a meaningful support structure from someone's life (a person using the meditation guide for anxiety management, for example) versus merely inconveniencing a power user?

7. **Accessibility across user circumstances**: The proposed core features — likely workout tracking or social fitness functions — carry assumptions about users' physical abilities, time availability, gym access, and internet reliability. Which features in the current set serve users facing the most constraints (limited mobility, irregular schedules, no gym access), and would stripping down to a "core" inadvertently narrow the app's reach to users with more resources and flexibility?

8. **Churn vs. harm**: Communication about the simplification is framed as a risk of triggering churn. From the perspective of users who rely on specific features, how is "churn" different from "being abandoned by a product you trusted"? What communication approach would allow users to feel respected and informed rather than discarded — and how would the team know the difference before rollout?

---

## Whose Voice Shapes the Decision

9. **Design with vs. design for**: The current plan appears to use data analysis, user interviews, and strategic vision as inputs. What would it look like to involve actual users — particularly those who represent the app's most underserved segments — as co-decision-makers rather than informants? What would change about the outcome if users had meaningful voice in which 3-4 features survive?

10. **Who is not in the data**: Retention metrics and session analytics reflect users who created accounts and returned. They do not reflect people who downloaded and deleted, people who never downloaded because the app looked inaccessible, or people who might have been served by the app's potential but were never reached. How does the team ensure that the simplified product is shaped by the full addressable community rather than by the habits of users who persisted despite friction?

11. **Power dynamics in feedback**: User interviews and feedback channels systematically over-represent users who are articulate, motivated to engage, and comfortable with the feedback format. What mechanisms would give voice to users who are less likely to respond to surveys — younger users, non-English speakers, users with lower tech literacy — and how would their perspectives change the feature prioritization conversation?

---

## Emotional Journey Through the Relaunch

12. **The returning user experience**: Existing users who open the app post-relaunch will encounter a product that is materially different from what they downloaded. What is the emotional arc of that experience — the confusion when familiar features are gone, the possible relief if the remaining features work better, the potential grief if something meaningful was removed? How does the team design the relaunch experience to acknowledge that arc rather than ignore it?

13. **Redefining success from the user's perspective**: The team is considering redefining success metrics for the simplified product. What does success look like from a user's point of view — not DAU/MAU or session length, but something like "I opened the app and felt good about it" or "this actually helped me do the thing I came to do"? How would centering user-experienced success change which metrics the team tracks and which features they conclude are working?
