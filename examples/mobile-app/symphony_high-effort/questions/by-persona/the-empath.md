# Brainstorming Questions from The Empath: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## The Grief of Letting Go

1. **Killing your darlings, emotionally**: When the team looks at the list of features to cut, which one will feel like a genuine loss — not just strategically wrong to remove, but emotionally painful — and what does that visceral reluctance reveal about what the team actually believes the product is for?

2. **The weight of sunk cost**: The team has poured months of effort into 15 half-built features; how will it feel to publicly declare that work "done" by removing it, and how might unresolved feelings about wasted effort quietly contaminate the simplification process itself?

3. **Trust and the feature backlog**: When engineers and the designer are told that half of what they built is being cut, what emotional signal does that send about how their past contributions are valued — and how does that feeling affect whether they'll commit to building the new focused core with genuine care?

## What Users Actually Feel vs. What They Say

4. **The shame underneath "overwhelming"**: User feedback says "overwhelming" and "don't know where to start" — but what emotional state is really underneath that? Is it frustration, embarrassment that they can't figure it out, or a quiet sense of betrayal that the app promised simplicity and delivered chaos?

5. **The 47-second session and what it means emotionally**: A 47-second average session isn't just a metric — it's a feeling. What does someone actually experience in those 47 seconds that makes them put the phone down? Is it boredom, anxiety, defeat, or something else — and does the team feel that when they use their own product?

6. **The difference between leaving and grieving**: For the small but passionate user bases attached to features being cut, what is the emotional texture of losing something they rely on — and is there a way to honor that feeling in how the relaunch is communicated, rather than just managing the churn risk?

## Authenticity vs. Performance in the Relaunch

7. **The relaunch announcement and emotional credibility**: When the team communicates the simplification to existing users, what tone will feel genuine versus what will feel like corporate spin — and how will users who've already been burned by the overbuilt product decide whether to trust this new commitment to simplicity?

8. **The emotional promise of "focused"**: "Focused product" is a rational framing, but what is the emotional promise being made to users — relief, clarity, confidence in their fitness journey? If the team can't feel that promise themselves, will users sense the hollowness in the product?

## Team Energy and Morale as a Signal

9. **Burned-out builders and the creative cost**: The team is described as burned out from maintaining 15 half-built features. My gut says that burnout isn't neutral — it seeps into the work. How does the emotional state of the people building the simplified product affect the quality and feel of what gets made, and what does the team need emotionally before they can build something genuinely good?

10. **The fear behind "incremental vs. clean relaunch"**: The question of whether to simplify incrementally or do a clean relaunch sounds strategic, but it also has an emotional dimension — incremental feels safer, while a clean break feels both exhilarating and terrifying. Which direction does the team's gut pull toward, and what does that pull reveal about what they actually believe is possible?
