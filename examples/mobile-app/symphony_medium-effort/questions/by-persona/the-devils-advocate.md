# Brainstorming Questions from The Devil's Advocate: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## The Feature Selection Trap

1. **Retention signal vs. love signal**: Have we considered that the features with the highest engagement data may simply be habit loops rather than genuine value — and that users who stick around for the workout tracker do so out of inertia, not because it's the one thing this app does better than competitors? How would you distinguish a "core feature" from a "least-annoying feature" in your analysis?

2. **The passionate minority problem**: One potential challenge is that cutting a feature used by only 2% of users sounds easy until those 2% are your most vocal community members, your app store reviewers, and your press contacts. How do you account for the outsized influence of small but passionate user groups when making a ruthless cut decision — and what specifically would you offer them to retain their goodwill?

3. **Data validity under feature chaos**: Have we considered that the engagement metrics for each feature are confounded by the app's overwhelming UI — users may have abandoned potentially valuable features simply because they were buried three taps deep behind a hamburger menu, not because they didn't want them? How would you isolate genuine feature demand from discoverability failure before deciding what to cut?

---

## The Relaunch Illusion

4. **The "clean slate" assumption**: One potential challenge might be that a dramatic relaunch positions the team as having failed publicly, which can spook remaining investors, accelerate talent departures, and signal instability to users who haven't yet churned. Have we stress-tested whether the rebranding benefit actually outweighs the churn spike and PR cost of announcing "we built the wrong thing for two years"?

5. **Incremental simplification vs. big-bang reset**: This approach assumes a clean relaunch produces a measurable inflection point, but what if users simply don't notice — or worse, interpret the stripped-down version as the app degrading rather than improving? How would you design the relaunch narrative and rollout sequence to make simplification feel like a bold upgrade rather than a retreat?

6. **Eight-month countdown clock**: Have we considered that an 8-month runway may be insufficient to absorb the relaunch dip — the period between cutting features and regaining retention momentum could itself consume 3–5 months? What is the specific retention recovery timeline assumption baked into this plan, and what is the contingency if the focused product takes 10 months to prove itself?

---

## Team Dynamics and Execution Risk

7. **Sunk cost psychology at the team level**: One vulnerability in asking a burned-out team to "kill their darlings" is that the engineers who built the features being cut may interpret the exercise as an implicit performance review rather than a strategic pivot — triggering quiet disengagement or departures precisely when you need focused execution. How would you structure the decision process to make cuts feel like shared strategy rather than a verdict on past work?

8. **The 6-engineer bottleneck**: Have we considered that removing deeply integrated features from a codebase built by six engineers under time pressure is not a subtractive operation but a full rearchitecting exercise — and that technical debt from feature entanglement may consume the majority of your runway before a single user sees a simpler app? What is the concrete technical audit plan, and at what debt level does "simplify" become "rebuild from scratch"?

---

## Success Metric Redefinition Risk

9. **Moving the goalposts without a map**: One potential challenge is that redefining success metrics mid-product lifecycle is a double-edged move — it can signal strategic maturity to sophisticated investors, or it can read as post-hoc rationalization to anyone who remembers the original pitch metrics. How would you frame the new KPIs (presumably 30-day retention and DAU/MAU) so that they represent genuine product health rather than a more favorable measurement of the same underlying problem?

10. **The 47-second session ceiling**: Have we considered that a 47-second average session might reflect not just feature overwhelm but a fundamental mismatch between the app's social-and-tracking format and the actual moments in users' lives when they want fitness support? After you simplify to 3–4 features, what is the theory of behavior change that makes you confident session length will grow — and what would falsify that theory within the first 60 days post-relaunch?

---

## Strategic Identity After the Cut

11. **Competitive exposure from simplification**: One potential challenge is that a focused social fitness app occupying a single niche is now directly comparable to best-in-class incumbents (Strava, Apple Fitness+, Whoop) on the one dimension you've chosen to compete on, eliminating the "broad platform" positioning that previously obscured the comparison. How would you articulate a specific, defensible edge in the chosen niche — not just "we do fewer things better," but why this team with this codebase in this market window can be the category winner?
