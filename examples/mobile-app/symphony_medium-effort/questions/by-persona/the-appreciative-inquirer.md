# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## Discovery: Peak Moments and Hidden Strengths

1. **The feature that lit people up**: Among the 15 features, which one — even briefly, even with just a small segment of users — generated genuine enthusiasm or word-of-mouth? What was it about that experience that worked, and what does that tell you about what users actually came here hoping to find?

2. **The team's proudest build**: Looking back at what was shipped over the past year, which feature or piece of work did the engineering and design team feel most proud of — not in terms of user metrics, but in terms of craft, clarity, or execution? What conditions enabled that quality, and how might those conditions be recreated?

3. **When retention defied the trend**: The 8% 30-day retention is low overall, but within it there are surely users who stayed. Who are these users? What are they doing in the app that others aren't, and what can their behavior reveal about the app's latent core value?

---

## Dream: Imagining the App People Love

4. **The app people brag about**: Imagine it's two years from now, and people who use this app tell their friends about it unprompted — not because they were asked, but because it genuinely changed something for them. What would they say? What would the app have helped them do, feel, or become?

5. **The 47-second session transformed**: Right now, the average session is 47 seconds — likely a sign of confusion and quick exits. What would a session look like if it were 47 seconds and *satisfying* — so tightly purposeful that users left feeling accomplished rather than lost? What single interaction would make that possible?

6. **The team energized again**: Picture the moment, six months from now, when a burned-out engineer or designer looks at the simplified product and feels genuine pride — maybe even excitement — about what they're building. What does the product look like at that point? What did the team have to let go of to get there, and what did they discover they'd been protecting all along?

---

## Design: Structures That Sustain the Core

7. **The decision filter that honors strengths**: Given that some features have passionate (if small) user bases, what decision-making framework would let you honor those loyalties while still making bold simplification choices — one that builds on what the team already knows about user motivation rather than discarding it?

8. **The communication approach that turns loss into invitation**: When apps simplify, users who relied on removed features often feel abandoned. What communication approach — drawing on whatever trust and goodwill the app has already built with its 180K downloaders — could reframe this not as a loss but as an invitation into something better? What does the team already know about its users that could make this message land?

9. **The metric architecture for a focused product**: If the current metrics (DAU/MAU, 30-day retention, session length) were designed for a sprawling app trying to be everything, what success metrics would be native to a focused product with a single strong purpose? What does the team already know about user behavior that could anchor a new measurement framework?

---

## Destiny: First Steps and Sustained Momentum

10. **The smallest proof of concept**: What is the smallest, lowest-risk experiment the team could run in the next two weeks — using existing data, existing code, or a single user conversation — that would give them genuine evidence about which feature is the app's true heartbeat?

11. **The asset hiding in plain sight**: What does this team already have — in terms of user relationships, technical infrastructure, institutional knowledge, or community trust — that most teams rebuilding from scratch would envy? How could that asset become the foundation of the simplified product rather than something that gets discarded in the reset?
