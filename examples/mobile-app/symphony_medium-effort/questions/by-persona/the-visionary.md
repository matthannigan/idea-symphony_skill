# Brainstorming Questions from The Visionary: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## The Blank-Canvas Problem

1. **Reinventing fitness from scratch**: If this app didn't exist yet and you were inventing a social fitness product today with no legacy constraints — no codebase, no existing users, no feature history — what single human desire would you build the entire experience around, and how would that shift what the product even is?

2. **Beyond the feature metaphor**: What if we completely reimagined the unit of product value away from "features" altogether — what would a fitness app look like if it were structured around emotional states, life chapters, or relationships rather than capabilities?

---

## The Relationship Between Tool and User

3. **The app that disappears**: Imagine a world where the most successful outcome for this product is one where users barely notice it exists — where fitness and social connection happen in their lives and the app is nearly invisible. What would that version of the product do, and what would it never do?

4. **From transaction to covenant**: What if the relationship between this app and its users were redesigned from a transactional service model into something closer to a long-term mutual commitment — where the product actively changes shape based on the life stage of each user rather than presenting the same 15 features to everyone?

---

## Radical Subtraction as Creation

5. **The one thing that changes everything**: If you were forced to reduce this product to a single interaction — one moment per day between the user and the app — what interaction would have the most transformative effect on someone's actual fitness behavior, and what new category of product does that single interaction define?

6. **Killing as an act of invention**: What if the removal of 11 of the 15 features is not a retreat but the founding act of an entirely new product category — something that doesn't currently have a name because nothing like it exists yet? What is the name and shape of the category this simplified product could pioneer?

---

## The Users Who Stay

7. **The passionate minority as a signal, not a problem**: The small user groups deeply attached to specific features are usually treated as a churn risk or a political problem — but what if they are actually pointing toward a fundamentally different product that no one has built yet? What would it look like to design entirely around one of those passionate minorities rather than the average user?
