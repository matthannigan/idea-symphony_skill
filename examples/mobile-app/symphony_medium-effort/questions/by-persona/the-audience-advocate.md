# Brainstorming Questions from The Audience Advocate: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## The Overwhelm Experience

1. **The moment of abandonment**: When users open the app and feel "overwhelming," what is the precise emotional sequence — confusion, then shame at not understanding it, then retreat — and how does that sequence differ across the 180K users who downloaded but didn't stay?

2. **The silent workaround**: What workarounds have users developed that they never reported — unofficial "single-feature" use patterns, notification muting strategies, or app-hiding behaviors — that reveal what the app could be if the noise were stripped away?

3. **The 47-second story**: A 47-second average session tells us users open the app and leave almost immediately, but what are they actually doing in those 47 seconds — what do they try, what stops them, and what does that failed moment feel like from their side?

---

## Whose Voice Shapes the Cut

4. **The passionate minority's stake**: The features with small but passionate user bases represent people who found genuine value where others didn't — what is the ethical obligation to those users when their feature gets cut, and how do you honor their investment without holding the product hostage to it?

5. **The absent voice problem**: Data shows who stayed, but who never downloaded in the first place because the app's complexity signaled "not for me" — and what would a focused product unlock for those people who self-selected out?

6. **Designing with vs. designing for**: When the team decides which 3-4 features represent "core value," are they making that determination based on their own product intuitions, usage analytics, or genuine co-design with the people who use the app — and why does the distinction matter for whether the simplified product will actually serve users better?

7. **The power asymmetry in feedback**: User feedback ("overwhelming," "jack of all trades") is being filtered through whoever wrote the reviews and responded to surveys — who among the 180K users is least likely to have submitted that feedback, and what might their experience of the app look like?

---

## The Transition as a User Experience

8. **The disruption of routine**: For the 8% who stuck around at 30 days, the app is part of some habit or routine, however fragile — when features disappear, what is the emotional experience of discovering your familiar path is gone, and how does that loss interact with the trust users already have in the product?

9. **The communication gap**: "Communicating the simplification without triggering churn" frames the announcement as a messaging problem, but from the community's perspective it may be experienced as betrayal, relief, or confusion depending on which features they used — how do you design a communication that meets users where they actually are rather than where you hope they are?

10. **The relaunch's first impression**: A new user encountering the simplified app for the first time has no memory of what was removed — what is the emotional arc you want them to experience in their first three sessions, and how do you design for that arc rather than just for the feature set?

---

## Accessibility and the Full Spectrum of Users

11. **The fitness app's implicit audience**: Fitness and wellness apps often implicitly center users who are already motivated, physically able, and tech-comfortable — when stripping to core features, what assumptions about the "ideal user" get baked in, and who gets further left behind by a product designed for someone who doesn't need much support?

12. **Notification overload as a barrier**: "Too many notifications" isn't just an annoyance — for users managing anxiety, ADHD, or information overload, it can be a genuine accessibility barrier that caused abandonment — and when you simplify, how do you ensure the notification architecture serves diverse cognitive needs rather than just defaulting to fewer-is-better?

---

## What Success Feels Like to Users

13. **Redefining success from the user side**: The team is redefining success metrics for the simplified product, but what does success feel like from the user's perspective — not DAU/MAU ratios, but the moment someone opens the app and feels capable, motivated, and understood rather than overwhelmed and inadequate?
