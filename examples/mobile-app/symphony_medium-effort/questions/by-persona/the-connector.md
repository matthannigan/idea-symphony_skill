# Brainstorming Questions from The Connector: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## Cross-Domain Parallels: What Other Fields Have Solved This Before

1. **The restaurant tasting-menu trap**: There's a structural parallel here with high-end restaurants that tried to offer 40-item menus and collapsed under operational complexity — many recovered by cutting to 8 dishes executed perfectly, which paradoxically increased both quality and revenue. What if we borrowed the principle of "constraint as craft signal" from culinary design: which 3-4 features, if executed with the same obsession a Michelin kitchen brings to a single dish, would make users feel the app was *made for them* rather than assembled for everyone?

2. **The urban planning "desire paths" principle**: City planners discovered that the most-used sidewalks aren't the ones architects designed — they're the worn dirt paths users created themselves. There's a parallel between those desire paths and the 47-second average session: users are telling you exactly where they're going by how little time they spend going everywhere else. What unexpected usage patterns emerge when you map session flows as desire paths — and which single corridor are users clearly trying to reach before giving up?

---

## Structural Similarities: The Feature-Team-Runway System

3. **The constraint trinity as one connected problem**: These two seemingly separate challenges — "which features to cut" and "how to prevent team burnout" — are actually the same problem viewed from different angles. In lean manufacturing, the Toyota Production System discovered that worker exhaustion and product defects share a root cause: overproduction. What if the feature bloat *is* the burnout, not a cause of it, and eliminating features isn't just a product decision but a team health intervention — and how does reframing the cut as "restoring craft" rather than "admitting failure" change what the team is willing to let go?

4. **The invasive species removal model**: Ecologists managing invasive species know that removing a dominant invasive doesn't simply reveal the ecosystem underneath — it triggers cascades, sometimes allowing a secondary invasive to fill the vacuum. There's a parallel here with deeply integrated technical debt: removing one tangled feature may not simplify the codebase but instead expose the next most-entangled dependency. What if you mapped the feature removal sequence the way ecologists plan invasive removal — which features, cut in which order, create conditions for the remaining core to flourish rather than triggering a new round of technical debt cascades?

---

## Bridging User Retention and Product Identity

5. **The "passionate minority" as canary, not constituency**: There's a structural parallel between the app's niche power-users and the concept of "indicator species" in ecology — organisms whose presence or absence signals the health of the broader ecosystem. A small passionate user base for a given feature isn't necessarily evidence the feature should stay; it may be evidence that the feature attracts a different species of user entirely. What if the passionate minorities around specific features (say, the meditation guide or the marketplace) are actually signals that you're serving *multiple incompatible user archetypes* — and cutting features is really about choosing which species this ecosystem is for?

6. **The platform vs. point-solution tension from developer ecosystems**: In the developer tools world, products that tried to be platforms (IDEs, full-stack frameworks) routinely lost to point solutions (linters, formatters, test runners) that did one thing exceptionally well — until a standard emerged and platforms re-emerged on top. There's a parallel here with the fitness app market: the app may be failing not because it lacks features but because it arrived at "platform" before any point solution in the space achieved dominance. Which single capability, if executed as a best-in-class point solution for the next 6 months, would give the relaunch both a defensible identity *and* a credible foundation to selectively re-expand later?

---

## Relaunch as Ritual: Borrowing from Brand and Culture

7. **The public "burning of the ships" as commitment device**: Conquistador Hernan Cortes famously burned his ships so his crew couldn't retreat — turning an irreversible act into a motivational force. There's a structural parallel here with the communication challenge around simplification: incremental feature removal feels like decline, but a public, ceremonial relaunch with explicit "what we are no longer" messaging borrows the psychological power of an irreversible commitment. What if the relaunch is designed not just as a product announcement but as a ritual that invites existing users to *witness and co-author* the team's recommitment — transforming the narrative from "we failed at 15 things" into "we chose mastery over breadth," and what form would that ritual take across the app, the App Store listing, and the onboarding flow?
