# Brainstorming Questions from The Questioner: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## Interrogating the Assumption That Features Are the Problem

1. **What if it's not the features?**: Why do we assume the 8% retention and 47-second sessions are caused by feature overload rather than by the absence of a compelling core loop — and how would we tell the difference from the data available?

2. **Who decided "overwhelming" means "too many features"?**: The feedback says users feel overwhelmed, but have we considered whether the problem is information architecture and onboarding rather than the number of capabilities — and what would it take to test that hypothesis cheaply before committing to cuts?

3. **What does 2% DAU/MAU actually tell us?**: If 180K people downloaded the app but only 2% open it on any given day, what specific behavior does that ratio reveal — and why do we assume returning users would behave differently with fewer features rather than simply finding none of the remaining features worth returning for?

4. **Are we solving retention or acquisition?**: The stated goal is to identify features users "actually want to open every day" — but have we asked whether daily engagement is the right metric for a fitness app, or whether we're chasing a habit loop that doesn't match how people actually use fitness tools in their lives?

---

## Probing the Feature Selection Process

5. **What does "execute well" mean, and who decides?**: The current state is described as "15 features, none executed well" — but executed well by whose standard, and what would it take to distinguish a feature that's poorly built from a feature that's well-built but simply not wanted?

6. **What if the passionate minority is the signal, not the noise?**: For features with "small but passionate user bases," why do we assume these are edge cases to be accommodated rather than the most reliable indicator of where genuine product-market fit lives — and what would it mean to build around them instead?

7. **How would we know if we cut the wrong thing?**: If the team removes 11-12 features and retention does not improve, what would that tell us — and have we designed the relaunch in a way that lets us diagnose the cause of continued failure rather than simply attributing it to execution?

8. **What if data and user interviews point in opposite directions?**: The REQUEST names three methods for identifying core features — data analysis, user interviews, and strategic vision — but what do we do when they disagree, and whose judgment breaks the tie?

---

## Questioning the Relaunch Frame

9. **What does "relaunch" actually mean to an existing user?**: For the 180K people who already downloaded the app, what would they experience as a relaunch versus an update — and have we mapped how that distinction affects whether they give the simplified product a second chance or dismiss it as the same app with less stuff?

10. **Why do we assume the simplified product competes in the same market?**: If the app removes its meal planner, coaching service, and marketplace and keeps only workout tracking and social features, is it still the same category of product — and what competitors does it now face that it didn't before?

11. **What if incremental and clean relaunch aren't the only options?**: The REQUEST frames the choice as incremental simplification versus a clean relaunch, but have we considered a middle path — maintaining a "legacy" version for existing users while building the stripped MVP in parallel — and what would that cost relative to the risk of triggering churn?

---

## Examining Unstated Constraints and Assumptions

12. **What is the team actually being asked to give up?**: Beyond the product decision to cut features, what does "killing your darlings" mean for the engineers who built those features — and how does that psychological cost factor into which simplification path is actually feasible given the team is already burned out?

13. **What happens at month nine?**: The runway is 8 months, but the REQUEST doesn't specify what success looks like financially at the end of that window — so what retention or engagement milestone would make the simplified app fundable, and are we building toward that specific bar or toward a more general notion of "focus"?
