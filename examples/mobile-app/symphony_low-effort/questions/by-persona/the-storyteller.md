# Brainstorming Questions from The Storyteller: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## The User Who Gave Up

1. **The abandonment moment**: Imagine Marcus, a casual runner who downloaded the app six months ago — he opened it three times, then stopped. Walk through his experience from the moment he first launched it to the moment he decided never to return. What was the exact scene where the app lost him, and what would have had to be different for him to still be opening it today?

2. **The one-feature loyalist**: Somewhere in those 180,000 downloads is a small group of users who genuinely love exactly one thing the app does — they're frustrated that everything else gets in the way. What does their daily ritual with that single feature look like, and what does their frustration story tell you about which feature deserves to survive the cut?

---

## The Moment of Clarity

3. **The "aha" session**: Picture the moment a user opens the stripped-down, relaunched app for the first time. They land on a clean screen with one clear purpose. Describe what they feel in the first 30 seconds — and contrast that with the story of what they felt the first time they opened the old version. What is the emotional arc from overwhelm to clarity, and what must the redesigned experience do to make that arc happen reliably?

4. **The daily habit forming**: Let me walk you through what success looks like 60 days after relaunch: a user named Priya has opened the app 47 out of the last 60 days. What does her morning or evening ritual with the app actually look like? What story is she telling herself — and her friends — about why she keeps coming back?

---

## The Team's Own Story

5. **The engineer's grief**: Imagine a senior engineer who spent four months building the meal-planning feature — it's elegant code, users occasionally praise it, and now the PM is proposing it gets cut. What is the internal story that engineer tells themselves about this decision, and how does the way the team frames "cutting" versus "focusing" change whether that engineer becomes an advocate or a quiet saboteur of the simplified product?

6. **The relaunch announcement**: Picture the email or in-app notification that goes to all 180,000 existing users explaining that a third of the features they once had access to are going away. Write the emotional journey a user goes through reading that message — from confusion, to possible anger, to (ideally) curiosity or relief. What story does that message need to tell for users to feel like something was done *for* them rather than *to* them?

---

## The Long Arc

7. **The six-month retrospective**: Fast-forward to six months after the relaunch. The team is gathered for a retrospective. Some numbers are better; some are worse than hoped. An engineer stands up and says, "The thing I didn't expect was..." What is the surprising human story — from users, from the team, from the market — that nobody fully anticipated when the decision to simplify was made?
