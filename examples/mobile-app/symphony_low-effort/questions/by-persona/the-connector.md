# Brainstorming Questions from The Connector: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## Feature Selection as Ecosystem Mapping

1. **The keystone species problem**: Ecologists distinguish between features present in an ecosystem and keystone species — the few whose removal causes cascading collapse. Which of your 15 features, if removed, would make users' remaining experience meaningfully less coherent, and does that structural dependency reveal what the app's true core actually is?

2. **Signal vs. noise in behavioral data**: There's a parallel here with how epidemiologists identify disease vectors in a noisy population signal — they don't look for the most common symptom, they look for the most predictive one. Which user behavior patterns (even rare ones) most reliably predict whether someone becomes a retained user, and what does that tell you about which features are doing real work?

---

## Stakeholder Constraints as a Unified System

3. **The three-body problem**: The constraints of user retention, team capacity, and runway aren't independent variables — they're coupled, like gravitational bodies whose trajectories only make sense relative to each other. If you optimized for each constraint in isolation, you'd get three different "right" answers; what configuration of feature cuts actually satisfies all three simultaneously rather than trading one off against the others?

4. **The burned-out team as diagnostic instrument**: Organizational behavior research shows that burnout tends to cluster around work perceived as low-impact or incoherent — people don't burn out equally across all tasks. Which of your 15 features are engineers most depleted by maintaining, and does that exhaustion map onto the features users find least valuable? If those two signals align, you may have found your cut list already.

---

## Relaunch Architecture and Change Management

5. **The urban renewal trap**: City planners learned the hard way that demolishing a "blighted" neighborhood and replacing it with something clean and rational often destroys the informal social infrastructure that made the place functional — the corner stores, the walking patterns, the informal gathering spots. What informal user workflows have formed around your app's navigation complexity that a clean relaunch might accidentally destroy, and how would you preserve the underlying need while rebuilding the structure?

6. **Version migration as geological strata**: Software archaeologists studying long-lived codebases find that each major refactor leaves strata — code that was deeply integrated once often has tendrils that outlast the feature itself. Before committing to feature removal, is there a way to map the dependency graph not just technically but behaviorally — which user journeys cross feature boundaries in ways that aren't visible from the navigation tabs?

---

## Identity and Focused Product Positioning

7. **The niche media lesson**: Magazines that tried to cover everything lost to the internet; the ones that survived became very specific about the one thing they did better than anyone. There's a structural parallel here with your relaunch decision: a focused fitness app isn't just "fewer features" — it's a fundamentally different value proposition that attracts a different self-concept in users. Which specific user identity (not user behavior, but user identity — "I am a person who...") does your true core serve, and does the team actually believe in that person enough to build only for them?
