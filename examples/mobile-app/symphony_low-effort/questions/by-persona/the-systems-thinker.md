# Brainstorming Questions from The Systems Thinker: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## Feature Interdependency and Removal Cascades

1. **Hidden coupling**: Which of the 15 features are quietly load-bearing for others — where removing one triggers a cascade of broken dependencies that turns a surgical cut into a full reconstruction?

2. **Technical debt feedback loop**: The team is burned out maintaining 15 half-built features, which reduces the quality of each feature, which reduces user engagement, which creates pressure to add more features to compensate — how do you break this compounding dynamic before the runway runs out?

3. **Retention signal contamination**: With 8% 30-day retention spread across 15 features, how do you distinguish the signal of a genuinely engaging feature from the noise of users who stayed because of a different feature they happened to also use?

---

## User Behavior and the Engagement System

4. **47-second session trap**: A 47-second average session is more than a UX problem — it suggests users open the app, feel the cognitive weight of 15 entry points, and leave. What would need to change in the first 10 seconds of a session to reverse this pattern, and which features enable that change?

5. **Notification dependency risk**: If the team uses push notifications to re-engage users during the relaunch period, there is a real danger that engagement becomes externally triggered rather than intrinsically motivated — making retention numbers look better while the underlying habit-formation capacity stays weak. How do you distinguish these two dynamics in your metrics?

6. **Power user concentration**: Small but passionate user bases for niche features represent a "Success to the Successful" dynamic — those users have adapted their workflow around a feature that the broader population never learned to use. Removing it breaks their system without surfacing what made it work for them. What would it look like to study those power users before deciding?

---

## Decision Criteria and the Feature Selection Process

7. **Three decision axes**: Feature selection is being framed as data analysis vs. user interviews vs. strategic vision — but these are not alternatives, they are different lenses that answer different questions. Data tells you what users do, interviews tell you why, and strategic vision tells you what you want to be true. What happens if all three disagree, and which lens should have the final vote?

8. **Retention vs. activation distinction**: A feature could have high engagement among users who discover it and low discovery rates overall, making it look low-usage in aggregate while actually being high-value. How are you disaggregating activation rate from post-activation engagement before drawing conclusions about which features matter?

9. **The identity question**: At 180K downloads and 2% DAU/MAU, there is already a small cohort who use this app daily. What are they actually doing in those sessions? The answer may reveal the product's true core more reliably than any feature matrix.

---

## Relaunch Dynamics and User Communication

10. **Churn as information**: Users who churn when features are removed are telling you something specific — which features they valued, even if they never articulated it. Is there a way to design the removal process so that churn patterns become signal rather than just loss?

11. **The simplification announcement paradox**: Telling existing users "we removed 11 features to make the app better" risks triggering the loss aversion of users who weren't using those features but feel the product is shrinking. What framing shifts the story from subtraction to focus — and does that framing hold up when a user discovers their specific feature is gone?

12. **Clean relaunch vs. incremental pruning**: A clean relaunch creates a before/after moment that gives the team permission to define a new success baseline. Incremental pruning avoids a churn spike but keeps the team in maintenance mode for months. Given 8 months of runway, which timeline do the economics actually support — and what is the decision clock on this?

---

## Team Dynamics and Execution Capacity

13. **Morale and the ownership loop**: Engineers who built features they believe in will resist removing them — not out of stubbornness but because their sense of craftsmanship is attached to the work. If that resistance isn't named and addressed explicitly, it surfaces as slow execution, scope creep back into the simplified version, or quiet rebuilding of features through the back door. How do you make "killing your darlings" a shared act of craft rather than a management directive?
