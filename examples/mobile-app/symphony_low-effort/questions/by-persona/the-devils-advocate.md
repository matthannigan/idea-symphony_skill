# Brainstorming Questions from The Devil's Advocate: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## The Diagnostic Problem: Are You Solving the Right Crisis?

1. **Misattributed failure**: Have we considered that the 8% retention and 47-second sessions might not be caused by feature bloat at all — but by a flawed core value proposition that simplification alone cannot fix? What evidence would confirm that users are leaving because of complexity rather than because the underlying fitness motivation mechanism simply doesn't work?

2. **Data gaps masking real problems**: The team has download counts and retention metrics, but do they have per-feature engagement data that would actually reveal which features users attempt versus abandon? Without that, isn't "strip to essentials" just a gut call dressed up as a data-driven decision?

3. **Selection bias in feedback**: User feedback says "overwhelming" — but those are the users who stuck around long enough to complain. What are the 92% who churned within 30 days actually responding to, and how would we know their answer isn't "I just don't want a fitness app at all"?

---

## The Cutting Calculus: What Gets Sacrificed and at What Cost?

4. **Passionate minority problem**: The goal mentions handling features with "small but passionate user bases." One potential challenge here is that these users are often the most vocal advocates and most likely to drive word-of-mouth. If the relaunch cuts their features, have we modeled what churn among this segment costs versus what retention gains simplification actually delivers?

5. **Technical debt as Trojan horse**: Removing deeply integrated features sounds like a net positive, but have we accounted for the risk that cutting interconnected modules introduces new instability into the features that remain? What's the engineering estimate for regression risk, and does the 8-month runway actually absorb a destabilized core after aggressive removal?

6. **The 3-4 feature assumption**: The goal presupposes that the app has a recoverable core of 3-4 features. But what if the actual answer from user data is 1 feature, or 6? Have we considered that anchoring on "3-4" before the analysis is complete may cause the team to rationalize keeping features that should be cut — or cutting features that constitute a genuine, if small, differentiator?

---

## The Relaunch Gamble: Is This the Right Intervention?

7. **Incremental vs. clean-break false dichotomy**: The key considerations frame incremental simplification and clean relaunch as two options, but have we pressure-tested whether a third path — a separate, new app targeting a narrower audience — might preserve the existing user base while allowing an unconstrained fresh start? What assumption makes relaunch of the same product the default frame?

8. **Communication as fig leaf**: There's a plan to communicate the simplification to existing users, but one potential challenge is that users who experienced the bloated version carry a mental model of the app as "chaotic." Even a perfect relaunch may not overcome that first impression. What would it actually take to rebuild trust with lapsed users versus treating them as a lost cause and focusing acquisition on new users entirely?

---

## Team Psychology: The Hidden Constraint

9. **Burnout as decision bias**: The team is described as burned out from maintaining 15 half-built features. Have we considered that burned-out teams tend to favor cuts that reduce their own maintenance load over cuts that optimize for user value — and that these two selection criteria may produce different lists? What safeguard exists to ensure the feature-cutting process is user-driven, not relief-driven?

10. **Morale in reverse**: The framing of "killing your darlings" assumes the hard part is letting go of features the team loves. But what if the harder psychological problem is the opposite — that the team no longer believes in any of the features and is at risk of under-committing to even the retained core? How would we know if we're executing a focused relaunch versus going through the motions of one?
