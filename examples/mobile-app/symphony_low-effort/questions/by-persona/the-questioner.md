# Brainstorming Questions from The Questioner: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## Uncovering the Real Core Value

1. **Why users actually opened it**: Among the 180K users who downloaded this app, what behavior did they exhibit in those 47-second sessions — and what does that tell us about the one problem they were hoping to solve before the complexity overwhelmed them?

2. **What "doing nothing well" looks like**: If 8% 30-day retention is the result of 15 half-built features, what would retention look like if the product had launched with only the single feature users most frequently navigated to — and how would we find out which one that was?

3. **The assumption behind "social fitness"**: Why do we assume users want a social layer on top of fitness tracking at all? What evidence exists that the social feed was a user-requested desire rather than a team-initiated assumption about what would drive engagement?

---

## The Geometry of the Cut

4. **Where the line actually falls**: When the team says "identify the 3-4 core features," what criteria are they implicitly using to draw that line — and are those criteria based on what users value, what the team can build well, or what differentiates the app in the market?

5. **Features as proxies for something else**: What if individual features aren't the right unit of analysis? Could the 15 features actually represent 2-3 underlying user jobs-to-be-done, and would framing the cut around those jobs lead to different decisions than framing it around feature names?

6. **The passionate minority problem reframed**: When a small user segment is deeply attached to a feature being cut, what does their attachment actually tell us — is it evidence of a feature worth keeping, or evidence of a missing alternative in the market that this app accidentally filled?

---

## Assumptions Embedded in "Simplification"

7. **Whether subtraction is the intervention**: Why do we assume removing features will improve retention? What evidence is there that the 8% retention rate is driven by cognitive overload rather than by the app simply not solving the user's core problem well enough, regardless of how many features exist?

8. **The relaunch framing question**: What are we assuming when we call this a "relaunch" — that existing users can be retained through communication alone, or that the simplified product will need to be treated as a new acquisition funnel and the 180K existing users are largely unrecoverable?

9. **Incremental vs. clean break — what's really being decided**: When the team weighs "simplify incrementally" versus "clean relaunch," are they actually making a product decision, or are they making a team psychology decision dressed up as a product decision?

---

## Team and Execution Capacity

10. **What burnout is signaling**: If the 6-engineer team is burned out maintaining 15 half-built features, what does that tell us about the decision-making culture that let the product reach this state — and how would the same culture handle the discipline required to hold the line on a stripped-down MVP?

11. **The designer bottleneck**: With 1 designer supporting a 6-engineer team across 15 features, what design debt has accumulated that isn't visible in the feature count — and how much of the "overwhelming" user experience is a design execution problem rather than a feature scope problem?

---

## Measuring the Right Thing After the Cut

12. **Redefining success before cutting**: What if the team defined the target success metrics for the simplified product before deciding which 3-4 features to keep — how would working backward from "daily active users opening the app with intent" change which features survive the cut?

13. **The 47-second session as a signal**: A 47-second average session length could mean users are confused and leaving, or it could mean the app is efficiently delivering what they need. What would the team need to learn to know which interpretation is correct — and does the answer change which features get kept?
