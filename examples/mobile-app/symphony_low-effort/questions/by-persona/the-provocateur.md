# Brainstorming Questions from The Provocateur: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## The Feature Graveyard as an Asset

1. **Deliberate amnesia**: What if, instead of stripping features out quietly, you announced a public "feature funeral" — a ceremony where users could eulogize the features being cut, vote on epitaphs, and bury them with fanfare? *The real insight: what if the act of removal is itself a product moment that turns loss into loyalty?*

2. **Invert the product entirely**: What if you reframed this not as "we're cutting features" but as "we're launching an entirely new app that happens to share your data"? If you had to name it something completely different and hide the lineage, what would you keep? *The insight: the features you'd carry into the new identity reveal what the product actually is vs. what it tried to become.*

---

## The Retention Paradox

3. **Reward abandonment**: What if 8% 30-day retention is not a problem to solve but a product characteristic to exploit — an app designed to be used intensely for a month and then consciously set down? What would you build differently if the goal was to be the most useful app someone ever deleted? *The insight: designing for intentional lifecycle rather than perpetual engagement might produce a far more honest and beloved product.*

4. **Make it deliberately worse for most users**: Imagine you redesigned the app to be confusing and off-putting to 95% of people who download it — but irresistibly perfect for the 5% who are the real core. What signals would you optimize for to find and serve only those people? *The insight: the app may be failing because it's trying to be legible to everyone when it should be magnetic to someone.*

---

## The Team and the Product Are the Same Problem

5. **Burn the roadmap, keep the engineers**: What if the real resource being wasted isn't features or runway — it's the cognitive overhead of a team maintaining 15 half-built things in their heads simultaneously? What if you measured morale not in features shipped but in the number of abstractions an engineer has to hold in working memory? *The insight: feature bloat may be a technical debt problem that masquerades as a product strategy problem.*

6. **Let the product vote**: Here's something absurd, but stay with me — what if you gave each of the 15 features a two-week "survival trial" where it had to generate one specific, measurable user outcome or be cut automatically, with no PM override? What would the product look like if features earned survival rather than defaulting to continued existence? *The insight: most pruning decisions are delayed by ambiguity about ownership and accountability — hard criteria cut through politics.*

---

## The Relaunch as Time Travel

7. **Ship the version from three years ago**: Imagine going back to the earliest working version of the app — before the feature sprawl started — and relaunching that exact product today with no changes except a new UI coat. What would users love about it? What would be obviously missing? *The insight: the original product vision usually contains the core value proposition before it was buried under accumulated compromise. The delta between "what was there" and "what's missing" often maps exactly to the 3-4 features worth keeping.*
