# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## Discovery: Moments When the App Sparked Genuine Connection

1. **Peak-experience session**: Think back to the users who gave the most energized feedback — what were they doing in the app at that moment, and what made that interaction feel alive rather than frustrating?

2. **Retention bright spot**: Among the 8% of users who stuck around for 30 days, what pattern of behavior or feature combination do they share — and what does that tell us about what this product, at its best, already does remarkably well?

3. **Team pride moment**: When has a member of this six-person team pointed to something in the app and said "that part — that's genuinely good"? What made it stand out amid everything else that felt half-built?

---

## Dream: The Future Users Are Grateful For

4. **Beloved daily ritual**: If a year from now users opened this app the way they reach for a morning coffee — automatically, eagerly, because it genuinely improves their day — what would they be doing in it, and what would they tell a friend it's for?

5. **Proud relaunch story**: Imagine the team ships a radically focused version and it lands beautifully — retention climbs, sessions lengthen, the burn rate drops. What does the app do that no competing product does quite as well? What story does the team tell at a conference about how they found that clarity?

6. **Aspirational user quote**: If three years from now a loyal user wrote an App Store review that captured everything this product became — not what it had, but how it made them feel about their fitness life — what might that review say?

---

## Design: Structures That Would Reliably Create That Future

7. **Feature selection anchored in strength**: Given that some feature or interaction already resonates with your most engaged users, what decision-making structure would let that signal — rather than sunk costs or team attachment — guide which three or four capabilities make it into the focused product?

8. **Communication built on honesty**: The users who feel overwhelmed now trusted this product enough to download it and give it a real try; what would an honest, human message to them look like that honors that trust, explains the simplification, and invites them into what's coming — turning potential churn into anticipation?

9. **Team energy released by subtraction**: Maintaining 15 half-built features has drained this team; what would it look like to frame the cutting process as a gift to the team — freeing people to do one thing excellently — and what rituals or practices could make that transition feel like momentum rather than loss?

---

## Destiny: First Steps Grounded in What Already Exists

10. **Data asset already in hand**: With 180,000 downloads and months of behavioral data, what is the single most useful question the team could answer this week using data they already have — one that would give them genuine conviction about which core feature deserves to survive?
