# Brainstorming Questions from The Audience Advocate: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## Who Is Actually in the Room?

1. **The silent majority**: The app has 180K downloads but only 2% daily active users — who are the 98% who downloaded and disappeared, and what were they hoping for in those first few sessions before they stopped coming back?
2. **The 8% who stayed**: What distinguishes the users who do return within 30 days — are they a coherent group with a shared need, or a fragmented collection of people each using a different one of the 15 features, and what does that tell us about which core to build toward?
3. **Barriers to entry**: For users who said the app was "overwhelming," at what moment in their first session did they encounter that feeling — was it the navigation, the onboarding, the first push notification, or the realization that they didn't know which feature to open first?

---

## What Users Say vs. What Users Do

4. **The gap between words and behavior**: Users say the app is "jack of all trades, master of none," but what do the usage logs actually show — which features, even the half-built ones, do people return to repeatedly, and is there a discrepancy between what users complain about and what they quietly keep using?
5. **Unarticulated loyalty**: Are there features users have never explicitly praised or requested — things they would only notice if they disappeared — because they've built workarounds so seamlessly that the feature has become invisible infrastructure in their routine?
6. **The 47-second session**: A session lasting 47 seconds on average could mean the app is failing users (they can't find what they want) or succeeding for a specific use case (they complete a quick check-in and leave satisfied) — how do we distinguish abandonment from fulfillment in our session data?

---

## The Emotional Journey of Simplification

7. **The existing user's grief**: For users who have built habits around features that will be cut — even small, niche habits — what does the experience of losing those features feel like from their perspective, and how do we design the communication of that loss with as much care as we're designing the product reset?
8. **Trust repair**: The user feedback signals that people already feel let down ("don't know where to start") — if we strip down and relaunch, what would it take for a former user to give the app a second chance, and are we designing the relaunch for new users or for the people we've already disappointed?
9. **The dignity of niche users**: The key considerations mention "small but passionate user bases" for certain features — from the community's perspective, what does it feel like to have a product that was built for you suddenly decide you don't matter enough to retain, and how do we honor those users even when we can't serve them?

---

## Inclusion and Access in the Simplified Product

10. **Fitness for whom**: Social fitness apps often implicitly design for a narrow archetype — what assumptions about users' bodies, schedules, income (gym membership, equipment, food budget), and physical ability are baked into the "core" features being considered, and would the simplified product serve a narrower or broader population than the current one?
11. **Notification overload as a justice issue**: Users complained about "too many notifications" — but the people most harmed by aggressive notification design are often those with the least agency to manage it (users in demanding jobs, caregivers, people without notification literacy) — how does the simplified product redesign its relationship with users' attention, not just reduce feature count?
12. **Who gets to define "core value"**: The goal is to find the app's "true core value," but whose definition of value is driving that determination — the team's vision, the most vocal users, the data from the highest-engagement cohort, or something that accounts for users who haven't yet been able to engage deeply because the current app was too confusing to reach them?

---

## Participation and Voice in the Reset Process

13. **Co-design vs. announcement**: The relaunch plan involves communicating simplification to existing users — but is there an opportunity to involve users in deciding what to cut, and if so, which users should have voice in that process given that the loudest users are rarely the most representative?
