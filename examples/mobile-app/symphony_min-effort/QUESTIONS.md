# Brainstorming Questions: Feature-Bloated Mobile App MVP Reset

---

## Topic Cluster 01: Feature Triage and Core Value Identification

**Cluster focus**: How to rigorously identify which of the 15 features represent genuine core value versus features that were built out of assumption, ambition, or sunk-cost reasoning.

1. **Usage vs. value signal**: Among all 15 features, which data signals — engagement frequency, session-entry points, voluntary revisits, or user-initiated shares — most reliably distinguish features users depend on from features they merely tolerate?

2. **Power-user lens**: If you isolated the top 5% of users by retention and engagement, what feature combinations characterize their behavior, and does that cohort's usage pattern differ meaningfully from the median user's?

3. **Passion vs. breadth trade-off**: For features with small but highly engaged user bases, how do you weigh the risk of triggering vocal churn against the strategic cost of maintaining a feature that the majority ignores?

4. **Gut-check vs. data**: When quantitative data (usage analytics) and qualitative data (user interviews) point in different directions about which features matter most, what decision-making framework should break the tie?

5. **Ruthless subtraction test**: If you could only keep one feature and had to delete everything else tonight, which feature would it be — and does the answer change depending on whether you optimize for retention, acquisition, or revenue potential?

---

## Topic Cluster 02: Simplification Strategy and Relaunch Design

**Cluster focus**: Whether and how to execute the simplification — as a gradual wind-down, a hard cut, or a clean-break relaunch — and what that choice means for product positioning and user trust.

6. **Incremental vs. clean break**: What are the realistic risks and benefits of a phased feature deprecation (one feature removed per sprint) versus a single bold relaunch moment, given 8 months of runway and a burned-out team?

7. **Relaunch positioning**: If the simplified app is essentially a different product, should it carry the same brand name and inherit the existing 180K user base, or does a clean brand slate reduce anchoring bias and give the team a cleaner narrative?

8. **Minimum viable core**: What does a day-one session look like in the simplified app — what can a first-time user accomplish in under two minutes — and does that experience justify re-downloading for lapsed users?

9. **Navigation architecture**: With 15 features collapsed to 3-4, how radically should the information architecture change, and is there a navigation pattern that makes the focus feel like a deliberate choice rather than a budget cut?

10. **Success metric reset**: What does a healthy 30-day retention rate realistically look like for a focused fitness app, and which leading indicators (D1, D3, D7 retention; average session length; weekly active ratio) should replace the current lagging metrics as the team's north star?

---

## Topic Cluster 03: User Communication and Churn Management

**Cluster focus**: How to tell existing users that features they may have relied on are disappearing, without triggering a wave of negative reviews or abandoned accounts.

11. **Deprecation messaging**: What communication sequence — in-app notice, email, help center article, social post — minimizes churn during a feature removal, and how much lead time do users typically need before a sunset feels fair rather than abrupt?

12. **Data portability and exit ramps**: For features being cut (e.g., recipe library, progress journal), what data export or migration path would make users feel respected enough to stay even if they lose functionality they used occasionally?

13. **Re-engagement framing**: How do you frame radical simplification to existing users as "we got better" rather than "we gave up," especially when those users have already expressed frustration with the current experience?

14. **Community and power-user co-design**: Would inviting a small cohort of highly engaged users into a beta of the simplified product create ambassadors who validate the decision publicly, or does it risk amplifying dissent if those users dislike the cuts?

---

## Topic Cluster 04: Technical Debt and Team Execution

**Cluster focus**: The engineering and organizational realities of safely removing deeply integrated features while keeping a small, fatigued team productive and motivated.

15. **Interdependency mapping**: Which of the 15 features are architecturally entangled with the features being kept, and what is the realistic engineering cost of cleanly excising them without introducing regressions in the core product?

16. **Technical debt compounding**: Does removing a feature always reduce technical debt, or can poorly isolated features leave behind orphaned dependencies, unused database tables, and dead code paths that cost more to clean up than to leave dormant?

17. **Team morale and ownership**: How do you help engineers who built the features being cut experience the simplification as a win — reclaimed focus — rather than as having their work devalued?

18. **Runway allocation**: With 8 months of funding, what is a credible timeline from decision to simplified public relaunch, and how much runway should be held in reserve to respond to user feedback after launch?

---

## Topic Cluster 05: Long-Term Strategy and Competitive Positioning

**Cluster focus**: What the simplified app is ultimately competing for and whether radical focus creates a sustainable business or just a shorter path to a different set of problems.

19. **Defensible differentiation**: In a market crowded with fitness apps, what does a hyper-focused app offer that neither a broad-feature competitor nor a single-purpose tool (e.g., a standalone workout logger) can match?

20. **Re-expansion discipline**: Once the simplified product achieves healthy retention, what criteria — user-requested features, revenue thresholds, engagement signals — should gate any future feature additions, to avoid recreating the same bloat in 18 months?
