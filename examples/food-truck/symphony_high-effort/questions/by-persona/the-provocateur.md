# Brainstorming Questions from The Provocateur: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

## Cluster 1: What If the Truck Is the Wrong Unit of Analysis?

1. **What if you burned the truck**: Imagine the food truck itself is the worst possible delivery mechanism for Filipino-Mexican fusion — it's too small, too visible, too dependent on weather and parking. What if that constraint forced you to ask: what is the minimum viable physical form for this food to reach people? *The real insight: strip away "food truck" as identity and ask what the cuisine actually needs — scale, intimacy, theater, or something else entirely.*

2. **What if your customers were the distribution network**: Instead of driving to where people are, what if the people drove the food to each other? Imagine a model where regulars could pick up bulk portions and sell them at their own gatherings. What would break? What would be liberated? *The insight: which parts of the current model exist only because the founder assumed they had to own the last mile?*

## Cluster 2: What If Regulation Is a Product Feature, Not an Obstacle?

3. **What if the ordinance passes and it's the best thing that ever happened to you**: The city restricts downtown vending hours and adds licensing fees — and somehow that makes the business radically stronger. Work backward. What would have to be true? *The insight: constraints that hurt you equally hurt the 30 competitors; the question is who is best positioned to survive a thinning field.*

4. **What if you deliberately made the business impossible to permit**: Here's something absurd — what if the truck operated in a legally grey zone on purpose, as a way to become too beloved to shut down? Not as a legal strategy, but as a thought experiment: what does it reveal about how much legitimacy in this city is built on community love versus bureaucratic compliance? *The insight: the regulatory battle and the audience-building battle may be the same battle in disguise.*

## Cluster 3: What If Success Is the Catastrophe?

5. **What if the fusion concept succeeds so completely that it gets hollowed out**: Imagine the lumpia taco becomes the defining food of this city — copied by every competitor, sold at the airport, served at chain restaurants. You won. Now you've lost. What would you have needed to build in year one to own something that couldn't be replicated? *The insight: what parts of the business are actually defensible — the recipe, the story, the relationship with the community, or the founder themselves?*

6. **What if break-even is the trap**: Everyone is working toward the moment the truck pays for itself. But what if reaching break-even is the point where all the interesting options close — you're locked into the routine that produced it, afraid to disrupt the formula. What if you designed the business to stay strategically unprofitable in one corner on purpose? *The insight: which decisions get made differently when you're not optimizing purely for the number to go positive?*

## Cluster 4: What If the Brick-and-Mortar Is Already Here?

7. **What if the three-year goal is already available and you can't see it**: The plan targets brick-and-mortar in three years. But what if a version of that destination already exists — an underused commissary, a struggling restaurant with a lease, a ghost kitchen slot — and the three-year plan is just a story told to delay a decision that's actually available now? *The insight: what assumptions about sequencing and readiness are keeping options invisible that a less cautious founder would already be testing?*
