# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

## Discovery: Strengths Already at Work

1. **The fusion concept as a cultural bridge**: Think about the Filipino-Mexican fusion menu — lumpia tacos, adobo burritos, ube horchata. When has a food concept like this sparked an unexpected moment of genuine delight or recognition for a customer — what was happening in that exchange, and what does it tell us about the real value this truck could offer beyond the transaction?

2. **Culinary training as a business asset**: The founder brings a culinary background into this venture. What specific capabilities, habits, or instincts does that training cultivate — not just in the kitchen but in running an operation — that a purely business-trained founder would have to learn the hard way?

3. **The food truck scene as community infrastructure**: This city already has ~30 active food trucks and a growing scene. When has a food truck community like this one worked at its best — creating mutual support, sharing knowledge, or collectively shaping the regulatory landscape — and what made that possible?

## Dream: Vivid Futures Built on Existing Strengths

4. **The three-year picture, told as a story**: Imagine it's three years from now and this venture has become something the founder is genuinely proud of — not just profitable, but meaningful. What would a typical Tuesday look like? What would regulars say about it? What would the founder say to someone just starting out?

5. **The moment the fleet felt inevitable**: If the path from one truck to a small fleet went better than anyone expected — faster, more joyful, less stressful — what would have made that true? What conditions, relationships, or decisions would have created that outcome?

6. **Revenue diversity as resilience, not complexity**: Catering, events, daily vending — when these work together beautifully, what does that rhythm look like across a month? What does the founder get to spend their time on, and what does financial stability feel like in that picture?

## Design: Structures That Reliably Create the Future

7. **Pricing as a story the customer believes**: The best food truck pricing doesn't just cover costs — it communicates value so clearly that customers feel good about what they're paying. Given that Filipino-Mexican fusion is genuinely distinctive, what pricing structure would reflect that distinctiveness while building trust and loyalty with the core customer base?

8. **The commissary kitchen as more than compliance**: City regulations require commissary kitchen use, which most operators experience as a cost and a hassle. What would it look like to treat that relationship as a genuine asset — a home base, a community, a source of referrals or collaboration? What structures would make that reframe real rather than just rhetorical?

9. **Engaging the regulatory process from a position of strength**: The city council debate over food truck ordinances is usually framed as a threat. But food truck operators who have shaped favorable outcomes elsewhere tended to show up not as complainants but as contributors — bringing data, stories, and solutions. Given this founder's fusion concept and community ties, what role could they play in that process that would serve both their interests and the broader food truck community?

## Destiny: First Steps and Sustaining Momentum

10. **The relationships that unlock everything else**: Some of the most important assets in launching a food truck aren't financial — they're relational. Who are the two or three people (a commissary kitchen manager, a city official, a food truck association veteran, a loyal early customer) whose active support would make the first year meaningfully easier, and what's the earliest genuine step toward building those relationships?

11. **The small experiment that builds confidence**: Before committing to the full launch, what is the smallest version of this business — a pop-up, a catering gig, a farmers market slot — that would let the founder test the menu, the pricing, and their own operational stamina while generating real signal about what works? What would they be watching for, and what would a "yes, keep going" result look like?
