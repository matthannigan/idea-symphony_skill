# Brainstorming Questions from The Storyteller: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

## Cluster 1: The Founder's Journey — From Culinary Passion to Business Identity

1. **The moment the mission becomes visible**: Imagine a lunch rush on a warm Tuesday — a line forms, strangers try lumpia tacos for the first time, and one customer says "I've never had anything like this." What story does the founder want that customer to carry with them, and how does the truck's name, branding, and menu language tell that story before a single bite is taken?

2. **Navigating the identity gap**: The founder has deep culinary expertise but no prior business ownership experience. What does the arc of their first year look like — the early lessons, the unexpected competencies they discover, and the moments they realize they are becoming someone different than they were when they started?

## Cluster 2: The Customer's Experience — From Discovery to Loyalty

3. **The first-time visitor's journey**: Walk through the experience of someone who has never heard of Filipino-Mexican fusion — they stumble onto the truck at a farmers market, feel slight uncertainty about what to order, take a first bite of an adobo burrito, and become a regular. What has to be true at each moment of that journey — signage, interaction, portion, price — for that transformation to happen reliably?

4. **The regulars who become advocates**: Picture the small group of customers who show up weekly and bring coworkers, family members, and out-of-town guests. What shared story do they tell about why this truck is worth going out of your way for, and what operational or experiential choices create the conditions for that kind of word-of-mouth loyalty to take root?

## Cluster 3: The Regulatory and Community Arc — Navigating the City as a Character

5. **The city council hearing as a turning point**: Imagine the founder attending a city council session where the new food truck ordinance is being debated — surrounded by other operators, neighborhood advocates, and skeptical officials. What is the story the founder tells in that room, and how does showing up or staying silent shape not just the regulatory outcome but the founder's long-term standing in the local food ecosystem?

6. **The food truck association as a support network**: Picture the first time the founder walks into a food truck association meeting — uncertain, newer than most, but carrying a distinct culinary identity. Over the following year, how does that network become part of the business's survival strategy, and what moments of reciprocity — sharing a commissary tip, covering a spot, co-promoting an event — define the relationships that matter most?

## Cluster 4: The Expansion Decision — The Crossroads Moment

7. **The day the second truck decision arrives**: Three years in, revenue is stable, a second truck feels possible, and a brick-and-mortar landlord has reached out. Walk through the founder's internal experience at that crossroads — the pride, the fear of overextension, the weight of hiring someone else to run a truck that carries their name. What would have to be true about the first truck's story for the founder to feel genuinely ready to write the next chapter?
