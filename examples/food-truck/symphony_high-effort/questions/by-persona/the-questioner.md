# Brainstorming Questions from The Questioner: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Hidden Assumptions in the Business Model

1. **Why fusion, and for whom**: The Filipino-Mexican fusion concept is compelling on paper, but what underlying assumption about the local market is this cuisine choice based on — and what would it mean for the business if that assumption turns out to be wrong?

2. **The solo-founder ceiling**: The plan assumes one person can simultaneously cook, serve, drive, manage finances, book events, and build community relationships. At what point does the solo-founder model become the single biggest constraint on growth, and have we actually planned for that transition?

3. **Three-year timeline as anchor**: Why three years? Is that deadline derived from a real constraint (loan terms, personal finances, competitive window), or is it an aspirational guess — and how would the strategy shift if the honest answer were two years or five?

4. **What "success" means before brick-and-mortar**: The stated goal is multi-revenue-stream operation leading to brick-and-mortar, but what if the food truck itself becomes highly profitable and satisfying without that expansion — have we considered whether the brick-and-mortar goal is genuinely desired or just a socially legible marker of "real" business?

---

## The Regulatory and Political Landscape

5. **Engaging the ordinance debate proactively**: The pending city ordinance is framed as a threat — but what would it look like to treat it as an opportunity? If this founder showed up as a voice in the public process, what influence might a single engaged operator actually have on how the rules get written?

6. **What we don't know about the food truck association**: The plan mentions building relationships with the local food truck association, but what does that association actually do, what are its interests, and could joining it create obligations or dynamics that conflict with competitive positioning?

7. **The commissary kitchen as more than compliance**: City rules require a commissary kitchen, but have we considered all the ways that requirement could be turned into an asset — shared costs, community, prep capacity, catering infrastructure — rather than just a regulatory checkbox?

8. **If the restrictive ordinance passes**: Assuming the worst — downtown vending hours get cut and annual fees increase significantly — what would the actual financial impact be on the unit economics, and does the business model survive that scenario without major restructuring?

---

## Unit Economics and Capital Strategy

9. **The SBA loan decision as a values question**: The question of whether to take the $40K SBA microloan is framed as financial — but it is also a strategic and psychological one. What assumptions about risk, speed, and control are embedded in the choice to borrow, and what would it mean for the founder's decision-making if they were operating under debt pressure from day one?

10. **Food cost ratios for a fusion menu**: Filipino-Mexican fusion may combine ingredients with very different cost profiles and supplier chains. What are the actual food cost dynamics for lumpia tacos versus a simpler single-cuisine menu, and have we stress-tested the unit economics against ingredient price volatility or supply disruptions?

11. **Equipment leasing as a hidden lever**: The tradeoff between financing and leasing a truck is often framed as a monthly-cost question, but what does each option mean for flexibility — specifically, if the business needs to pivot the concept, change locations, or scale to a second truck faster than expected?

12. **The catering revenue assumption**: Catering and events are listed as revenue diversification strategies, but catering requires very different operational capacity than street vending. What would the founder need to build — in staffing, equipment, booking systems, and menu — before catering becomes a reliable revenue stream rather than a one-off opportunity?

---

## Competitive Positioning and Market Dynamics

13. **What the four similar trucks reveal**: There are already four food trucks with similar fusion concepts in this market. Rather than asking how to differentiate from them, what if we asked what their existence tells us — about whether the market is proven, saturated, or still forming — and what the founder could learn from their successes and failures before opening?

14. **The mid-size city food truck ecosystem**: A market of ~30 food trucks in a 250K-population city is a specific density. What do we know about how that ecosystem actually works — whether it's collaborative, fiercely competitive, or segmented — and how does that shape where and how a new entrant finds their footing?

15. **Pricing as cultural communication**: For a Filipino-Mexican fusion concept, pricing is not just a unit-economics decision — it's a signal about what kind of experience this is and who it's for. What message does a $14 lumpia taco send versus a $9 one, and how does that interact with the founder's identity and the audience they want to build?

---

## Growth, Identity, and Long-Term Vision

16. **Fleet expansion versus depth**: The expansion path named is a fleet plus potentially brick-and-mortar, but why is that the right direction? What if instead the founder went deep — one exceptional truck, a strong catering book, and a product line — rather than wide? What assumptions about what "growth" means are we not questioning?

17. **What happens to the food if the business succeeds too fast**: Early food truck culture is often built on the founder's personal presence and craft. If the operation grows to a small fleet, the founder can no longer be at every truck. How do we plan for the quality, identity, and culture of the food surviving that transition — or do we assume it won't matter?

18. **The brick-and-mortar as a different business**: Moving from a food truck to a brick-and-mortar location is often described as a natural progression, but the two are fundamentally different businesses with different cost structures, customer relationships, and operational demands. At what point in this plan should the founder treat the brick-and-mortar option as a genuinely separate decision — with its own feasibility analysis — rather than an assumed endpoint?
