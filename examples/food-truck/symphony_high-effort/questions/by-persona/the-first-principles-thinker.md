# Brainstorming Questions from The First Principles Thinker: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

## Cluster 1: The Core Exchange — What Are You Actually Selling?

1. **Strip away the menu**: At its core, what transaction is a food truck conducting — is the founder selling food, or selling a repeatable experience of discovery, and does the answer change what "success" looks like day to day?

2. **Define the minimum viable unit**: If you removed every assumption about how food trucks "should" operate and asked only "what is the smallest unit of value I can deliver profitably?", what would that unit be — and is a full truck the right delivery mechanism for it?

3. **Rethink the fusion premise**: The fundamental issue with any fusion concept is whether the combination creates genuine new value or just novelty that fades; what is the core flavor truth that makes Filipino-Mexican fusion irreplaceable rather than merely interesting?

## Cluster 2: Revenue Logic — What Does the Business Actually Run On?

4. **Deconstruct the revenue mix**: Setting aside industry norms about daily vending vs. catering vs. events, what is the actual cost structure of each revenue channel reduced to its simplest terms — and which channel, from first principles, requires the least capital to generate the most reliable margin?

5. **Question the growth ladder**: The conventional path is truck → fleet → brick-and-mortar, but each step compounds fixed costs; from the ground up, what is the simplest structure that generates the target income without adding operational complexity — and is multi-unit actually the right goal?

## Cluster 3: Capital and Risk — What Is the True Cost of Each Choice?

6. **Reframe the loan decision**: The fundamental question about the SBA microloan is not "can I qualify?" but "what is the real cost of capital relative to the optionality it buys?" — if you strip out conventional advice about leverage, what is the break-even scenario where borrowing $40K is strictly better than bootstrapping more slowly?

7. **Expose hidden capital assumptions**: Most equipment financing decisions are made by analogy to other operators; if you built the buy-vs.-lease calculus from scratch using only this business's actual projected utilization and cash flow volatility, what decision would the math produce?

## Cluster 4: Regulation and Competitive Position — What Constraints Are Real?

8. **Separate real constraints from assumed ones**: The pending ordinance changes feel threatening, but the fundamental issue is: which proposed restrictions actually prevent the business from operating profitably, and which are merely inconvenient — and does the answer change what political engagement is worth?

9. **Question the competitive frame**: With ~30 active food trucks and 4 similar fusion concepts in the market, the instinct is to differentiate; but from first principles, is the correct response differentiation, or is it finding the subset of the market where the product is already the obvious best choice without needing to compete at all?
