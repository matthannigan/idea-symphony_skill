# Brainstorming Questions from The Empath: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

## Cluster 1: The Emotional Weight of Going First

1. **What does it feel like to carry the whole business alone**: For a solo founder with no prior business ownership, how does the psychological weight of being the sole decision-maker shape daily choices — and what does that loneliness do to judgment under pressure?

2. **Fear vs. excitement in the first year**: When this founder wakes up before a service day, what emotional mix are they likely carrying — and which of those feelings is most likely to make them pull back from a risk that could be worth taking?

3. **What "success" feels like before it looks like anything**: Before revenue hits break-even, what small emotional wins would signal to this founder that the business is actually alive and worth continuing — and are those signals being built into the plan?

## Cluster 2: The Emotional Life of the Customer

4. **What does Filipino-Mexican fusion make people feel, not think**: Lumpia tacos and adobo burritos are not just flavor combinations — they carry cultural memory, surprise, and identity. What emotional reaction is this food trying to provoke in someone who has never tasted it, and is the current framing likely to get them there?

5. **The trust gap with unfamiliar food**: Some people will feel curious about this cuisine; others will feel quietly uncertain. How does the way this truck presents itself — name, visuals, how staff talk about the food — either close that hesitation or accidentally widen it?

6. **Loyalty is an emotional bond, not a transaction**: What would make a customer feel genuinely attached to this truck — not just satisfied, but protective of it, telling friends, showing up even when it's inconvenient? What emotional need would that attachment actually be meeting?

## Cluster 3: Authenticity and Cultural Identity Under Commercial Pressure

7. **What happens to the food when the money gets tight**: When food cost ratios start squeezing margins, the instinct is to simplify or substitute. What does it feel like to make those compromises on a cuisine that carries personal and cultural meaning — and how do customers sense inauthenticity even when they can't name it?

8. **The emotional tax of being "exotic"**: Filipino-Mexican fusion will be positioned as distinctive partly through its cultural novelty. What does it feel like — for the founder — to market their heritage as a differentiator, and is there a version of that framing that feels empowering rather than performative?

## Cluster 4: Relationships, Power, and the Regulatory Environment

9. **What does it feel like to need permission**: The founder must navigate commissary kitchens, permit offices, designated vending zones, and a city council that may restrict downtown hours. What emotional posture does that power asymmetry create — compliance, resentment, strategic friendliness — and how does that posture shape outcomes?

10. **The food truck community as emotional infrastructure**: The local food truck association is listed as a relationship to build. What would it feel like to be genuinely welcomed into that community versus tolerated as a newcomer competitor — and what signals, early on, indicate which trajectory is unfolding?

## Cluster 5: Scaling and the Fear of Losing What Works

11. **What gets lost when you grow**: The jump from one truck to a fleet — or to brick-and-mortar — carries a kind of grief that founders rarely name. What parts of the single-truck operation feel most alive and most worth protecting, and what would it emotionally cost to give those up for scale?
