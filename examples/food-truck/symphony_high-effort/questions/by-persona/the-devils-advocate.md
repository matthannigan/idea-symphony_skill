# Brainstorming Questions from The Devil's Advocate: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

## Cluster 1: The Fusion Concept as a Liability

1. **Market differentiation or market confusion**: Have we considered whether Filipino-Mexican fusion is genuinely compelling to a broad audience in a mid-size city, or whether it might be too unfamiliar to convert first-time customers efficiently — and if so, what menu design or sampling strategy could lower the barrier to trial?

2. **The 4-competitor ceiling**: One potential challenge is that four existing trucks with "similar fusion concepts" already occupy the conceptual space in a market of only ~30 trucks total — what concrete evidence suggests there is unsatisfied demand for a fifth entrant rather than a saturated niche, and how would this founder differentiate beyond cuisine type alone?

3. **Culinary identity vs. operational simplicity**: Have we considered that signature items like ube horchata and lumpia tacos may require specialty ingredients with limited local suppliers, volatile pricing, and long lead times — and what contingency menu or ingredient substitution strategy would protect margins when those supply chains break?

## Cluster 2: Financial Structure and the Loan Decision

4. **Debt service as a trap**: One potential challenge is that a $40K SBA microloan layered onto a food truck operation with inherently thin margins (~20-30% net in favorable conditions) could mean the first 12-18 months of profit go entirely to debt service — have we stress-tested the break-even timeline under conservative revenue assumptions before committing to that capital structure?

5. **The $100K is not what it seems**: Have we considered that $100K in total capital sounds substantial but may be consumed faster than projected — truck purchase or lease deposit, commissary kitchen fees, permits, POS systems, initial inventory, and working capital reserves can easily absorb $60-80K before a single sale — leaving the founder dangerously under-capitalized for the first slow season?

6. **Equipment financing vs. leasing and the sunk cost risk**: The framing of "equipment financing vs. leasing tradeoffs" assumes the truck reaches profitability — but what is the exit path if the concept underperforms in year one, and does a financed truck (vs. leased) create a debt overhang that traps the founder in a failing venture longer than is rational?

## Cluster 3: Regulatory and Political Risk

7. **Regulatory timing as an existential threat**: Have we considered that launching during an active city council debate over vending hour restrictions and new licensing fees is not just an inconvenience but a potential invalidation of the business model — specifically, if downtown vending hours are cut in half, does the unit economics case still hold, and has the founder modeled a scenario where that restriction passes before month six?

8. **Regulatory advocacy as a solo founder burden**: One potential challenge is that "engaging with the regulatory process" and "building relationships with city officials" requires time, political savvy, and sustained presence that a solo founder running daily service operations simply may not have — what is the minimum viable political engagement strategy that protects this business without consuming founder bandwidth?

9. **The food truck association as a double-edged network**: Have we considered that the local food truck association may represent incumbent operators who benefit from restricted new entrants, and that joining it exposes the founder's location strategy and pricing to direct competitors before the business is established — is there a way to engage selectively without revealing strategic information prematurely?

## Cluster 4: The Three-Year Expansion Narrative

10. **Premature scaling as a distraction**: Have we considered that the three-year plan to add a fleet and potentially open brick-and-mortar may be the kind of aspirational framing that causes founders to underinvest in making the first truck ruthlessly profitable — and what would it look like to commit to mastering unit one before treating expansion as a goal rather than a horizon?

11. **The brick-and-mortar leap is a different business**: One potential challenge is that the skills, capital structure, and operational model of a single food truck have almost no overlap with running a restaurant — lease negotiation, FOH staffing, liquor licensing, and fixed overhead are categorically different problems — so what is the decision framework for knowing when (or whether) to cross that threshold, rather than treating it as a default endpoint?

12. **Fleet expansion math under current capital**: Have we considered that adding a second truck typically requires $50-80K in additional capital, a proven commissary workflow, and a manager the founder trusts enough to leave unsupervised — and that none of those conditions are likely to exist within 36 months on a $100K starting base without a reinvestment strategy that is currently absent from the plan?

## Cluster 5: Operational Resilience and the Solo Founder Risk

13. **Single point of failure**: One vulnerability in this business model is that it is entirely dependent on one person who is simultaneously the chef, operator, bookkeeper, marketing lead, and regulatory liaison — what is the minimum staffing threshold or cross-training plan that prevents a single illness, injury, or personal emergency from shutting down revenue entirely?
