# Brainstorming Questions from The Accountant: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

## Cluster 1: Unit Economics and Pricing Architecture

1. **Food cost ratio as a structural constraint**: At a 28-32% food cost target (typical for food service), what does a lumpia taco or adobo burrito need to retail for given ingredient costs — and does Filipino-Mexican fusion cuisine, with its specialty inputs like ube extract and calamansi, structurally push food costs above that band, requiring either premium pricing or a simplified menu to stay viable?

2. **Contribution margin per item and per service mode**: If daily vending, catering, and events each have different overhead loads (fuel, labor, commissary time, permit fees), how does the contribution margin per dollar of revenue differ across these three channels — and which channel is actually generating the most economic value per hour of the founder's time?

3. **Menu engineering and margin mix**: A food truck menu is a portfolio, not a collection of equal items. Which items in the Filipino-Mexican fusion lineup are likely high-margin anchors (simple, fast, low-ingredient-cost) versus low-margin signature items that drive traffic but don't pay the bills — and what does the revenue mix need to look like to hit a target blended margin?

## Cluster 2: Break-Even and Capital Structure

4. **Break-even at launch vs. steady-state**: With ~$100K in starting capital ($60K savings + $40K SBA microloan), what are the realistic all-in startup costs (truck purchase or lease, outfitting, commissary deposits, permits, initial inventory, working capital buffer) — and at what daily revenue does the business cover its fixed costs, including debt service on the loan?

5. **SBA loan vs. bootstrap tradeoff, quantified**: The SBA microloan adds ~$450-600/month in debt service (assuming $40K at 6-8% over 7 years). How does taking that loan change the break-even revenue threshold, the monthly cash burn during a slow month, and the timeline to profitability — versus bootstrapping from $60K alone with a lower-cost truck entry strategy?

6. **Truck acquisition economics — buy vs. lease vs. used**: A new outfitted food truck runs $75-150K; a used truck can be $20-50K with unknown maintenance risk; leasing runs $1,500-3,000/month with no equity. How do these three paths compare on total 3-year cost of ownership, monthly cash impact, and flexibility to exit or upgrade — and which structure preserves the most working capital for the first year?

## Cluster 3: Cash Flow and Seasonal Risk

7. **Seasonality-adjusted cash flow modeling**: A mid-size city food truck operation in a northern or variable climate can see 30-50% revenue drops in winter months. If strong months generate $15-20K in revenue and slow months generate $8-10K, what does monthly operating cash flow look like across a full year — and is there enough margin in the strong months to fund the slow ones without drawing down savings or deferring loan payments?

8. **Revenue diversification as cash flow hedge**: Catering and private events can be booked months in advance, providing predictable revenue that offsets daily vending volatility. At what point in terms of catering revenue as a percentage of total revenue does the business materially reduce its cash flow risk — and what's the economic cost of pivoting founder time toward booking and executing catering versus maximizing daily vending volume?

9. **Working capital requirements and cash timing**: Food truck revenue is largely cash/card-same-day, but costs (commissary fees, ingredient orders, permit renewals, propane, truck maintenance) arrive on irregular cycles. What does a realistic 13-week cash flow projection look like in the first year — and how large a working capital reserve is needed to avoid a cash crunch during a slow stretch or an unexpected repair?

## Cluster 4: Growth Economics and Expansion Paths

10. **Unit economics of a second truck vs. brick-and-mortar**: Adding a second truck roughly doubles variable costs and requires a second operator/driver, while a brick-and-mortar adds high fixed costs (rent $3-8K/month, staff, build-out) but potentially higher ticket counts and catering capacity. What are the revenue thresholds at which each path becomes self-funding — and which expansion option has a shorter payback period given the capital required?

11. **Regulatory cost as a variable in the expansion model**: If the pending city ordinance passes — restricting downtown vending hours and adding annual licensing fees — what is the estimated annual revenue impact, and how does that change the timeline to profitability and the relative attractiveness of the brick-and-mortar pivot? At what fee level or restriction severity does the ordinance materially change the business case?

## Cluster 5: Hidden Costs and Economic Sustainability

12. **The true cost of the founder's labor**: A solo founder with no business ownership experience is simultaneously the cook, driver, bookkeeper, marketer, and permit liaison. At what monthly revenue does the business justify paying the founder a market-rate salary (say, $45-55K/year equivalent) — and until then, what is the effective hourly rate the founder is earning, and is that rate above or below their culinary employment alternatives?
