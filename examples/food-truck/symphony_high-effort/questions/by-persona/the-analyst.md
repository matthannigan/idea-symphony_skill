# Brainstorming Questions from The Analyst: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

## Cluster 1: Unit Economics and Break-Even Structure

**Cost floor per service day**: Breaking this down into fixed vs. variable components — what does a fully-loaded daily cost floor look like (commissary rental, fuel, truck depreciation or lease payment, permits, insurance, labor), and at what average ticket price and transaction volume does the truck cover that floor before generating any profit?

**Food cost ratio targets by menu item**: The key factors for Filipino-Mexican fusion profitability seem to be ingredient complexity and spoilage risk — what are realistic food cost percentages for each revenue stream (lumpia tacos vs. adobo burritos vs. ube horchata), and which items are likely to drag the blended cost ratio above or below the 28–32% target typical in fast-casual operations?

**Revenue ceiling per service day**: Given a realistic throughput of a single-window food truck (service speed, queue capacity, operating hours), what is the maximum gross revenue achievable on a high-volume vending day, and how does that ceiling compare to estimated daily operating costs at full build-out?

**Break-even velocity across scenarios**: How many covers per day, at what average order value, are required to break even under three scenarios — bootstrapped at $60K, fully capitalized at $100K with SBA loan, and a conservative ramp assuming 50% capacity utilization for the first six months?

## Cluster 2: Capital Structure and Financing Trade-offs

**SBA loan payback load on early cash flow**: The key tension here is between having more operating capital upfront versus carrying fixed debt service during a demand-uncertain ramp — how does a $40K SBA microloan at current rates translate into monthly debt service, and at what monthly revenue level does debt service represent an acceptable share (say, under 10%) of gross revenue?

**Equipment acquisition cost-benefit decomposition**: Breaking this down into three decision axes — financing vs. leasing vs. used-truck purchase — what does the total 36-month cost look like under each path when you account for maintenance reserves, depreciation, opportunity cost of tied-up capital, and the flexibility value of not owning an asset during a period of uncertain demand?

**Capital allocation sequencing**: Given $60–100K of available capital, what is the optimal sequencing of expenditure categories (truck acquisition, initial inventory, permitting, commissary deposit, working capital reserve, marketing) to minimize the risk of running short before the business reaches cash-flow breakeven?

## Cluster 3: Revenue Diversification and Channel Mix

**Channel margin comparison**: How do the three revenue streams — daily street vending, catering contracts, and ticketed event bookings — compare on contribution margin per labor-hour, once you account for the different labor, logistics, and food preparation requirements of each?

**Seasonal cash flow trough quantification**: In a mid-size city with meaningful winter weather, what does the likely revenue dip look like during low-season months, and what minimum cash reserve — expressed in weeks of fixed costs — should the business maintain to bridge the trough without taking on emergency debt?

**Catering as a stabilizing anchor**: What minimum catering booking volume (number of events per month, average contract value) would be needed to cover fixed monthly costs entirely, effectively making daily street vending revenue purely incremental — and is that booking volume realistic in a market of ~250K with 30 active food trucks?

## Cluster 4: Competitive Positioning and Market Sizing

**Addressable demand for Filipino-Mexican fusion**: The critical variables seem to be daily foot traffic at viable vending locations, per-capita willingness to pay for specialty fusion, and repeat purchase frequency — what data points or proxy signals (comparable truck revenue, foot traffic counts, event attendance) could be used to bound a realistic demand estimate rather than relying on gut feel?

**Differentiation durability against the four comparable competitors**: Breaking this analysis down — how sustainable is the fusion concept as a differentiator once the four existing fusion trucks are accounted for, and what secondary factors (consistency, brand story, community ties, digital presence) determine which trucks build loyal repeat customers versus competing purely on novelty?

## Cluster 5: Regulatory Risk and Expansion Decision Sequencing

**Ordinance risk quantification**: The pending city council debate introduces a concrete financial risk — if downtown vending hours are restricted or annual fees increased, what is the estimated revenue impact per scenario (mild restriction, severe restriction, fee increase only), and how should that risk factor into the choice between conservative bootstrapping and aggressive SBA-funded expansion?

**Three-year expansion trigger criteria**: Rather than targeting fleet or brick-and-mortar expansion on a calendar timeline, what specific quantitative thresholds — sustained monthly revenue, average occupancy rate, working capital cushion, repeat customer ratio — should serve as decision triggers for each expansion step, and in what sequence do those steps most de-risk the overall trajectory?
