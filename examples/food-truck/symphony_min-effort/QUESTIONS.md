# Brainstorming Questions: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Topic Cluster 01: Unit Economics and Financial Sustainability

**Cluster focus**: How to structure pricing, cost controls, and capital decisions to achieve break-even quickly and build a financially resilient operation from day one.

1. **Food cost targeting**: Given the complexity of Filipino-Mexican fusion ingredients (specialty items like ube, fresh lumpia wrappers, achiote), what food cost percentage is realistic for this menu, and how should menu engineering balance signature items with higher-margin offerings to hit overall unit economics targets?

2. **Financing decision trade-offs**: Weighing the $40K SBA microloan against more conservative bootstrapping — what factors beyond interest rate (personal risk tolerance, cash flow timing, credit implications, operational constraints) should drive this decision, and what does each path imply for the growth timeline?

3. **Break-even modeling under seasonality**: In a mid-size city with pronounced weather-driven slow seasons, what revenue floor is needed to sustain operations through low months, and how should the capital reserve strategy account for the gap between seasonal peaks and troughs?

4. **Equipment financing vs. leasing**: For the primary truck and commissary kitchen equipment, what criteria (expected lifespan, maintenance risk, cash flow impact, tax treatment) should determine whether to finance, lease, or buy used outright — and how does this choice affect flexibility if the concept needs to pivot?

---

## Topic Cluster 02: Regulatory Environment and Political Strategy

**Cluster focus**: How to navigate the current permitting landscape and actively shape the pending ordinance changes rather than simply absorb their impact.

5. **Engaging the ordinance process**: With the city council actively debating new vending restrictions and annual licensing fees, what concrete actions — testimony, coalition-building with other food truck operators, data presentations — could most effectively influence the outcome, and what is the realistic window to intervene before the ordinance is finalized?

6. **Commissary kitchen leverage**: Since the city already requires commissary kitchen use, what strategic advantages (shared prep labor, cold storage, licensing alignment, catering capacity) could be extracted from this mandatory relationship to offset its cost burden?

7. **Zone diversification as regulatory hedge**: If downtown vending hours are restricted by the new ordinance, what alternative location strategy — corporate campuses, hospital districts, event venues, neighborhood commercial corridors — would maintain revenue continuity while reducing dependence on any single permitted zone?

8. **Food truck association membership ROI**: Beyond general networking, in what specific ways could active participation in the local food truck association translate into tangible business outcomes — regulatory intelligence, shared equipment, cross-promotion, collective bargaining with event organizers — and what is the realistic time cost of that involvement for a solo founder?

---

## Topic Cluster 03: Brand Differentiation and Market Positioning

**Cluster focus**: How to carve out a defensible identity in a crowded local market where four competitors already occupy adjacent fusion territory.

9. **Competing against established fusion concepts**: With four similar fusion trucks already operating, what dimensions of differentiation — ingredient sourcing story, authentic cultural narrative, service experience, visual identity, price positioning — are most likely to build loyal repeat customers rather than one-time curiosity visits?

10. **Filipino-Mexican fusion storytelling**: The cuisine carries a genuine cross-cultural story (Philippine-Spanish colonial history, California migration corridors, shared flavor profiles). How could that narrative be embedded into the brand, menu language, and customer experience in a way that feels authentic rather than marketable gimmick?

11. **Signature item as entry point**: Menu anchors like lumpia tacos and ube horchata can function as both discovery hooks and shareability drivers. What criteria should guide which one item is elevated to flagship status — operational simplicity, margin, photo-worthiness, repeat purchase frequency — and how does that choice constrain or enable the rest of the menu?

---

## Topic Cluster 04: Revenue Diversification and Growth Sequencing

**Cluster focus**: How to layer catering, events, and fleet expansion onto the single-truck base without overextending a solo founder before the core operation is stable.

12. **Daily vending vs. catering vs. events prioritization**: These three revenue streams have very different operational profiles, margin structures, and lead-time requirements. In the first 12 months, what sequencing logic — fixed schedule vending first to build brand recognition, then corporate catering, then events — is most likely to build sustainable cash flow without fragmenting attention?

13. **Catering market entry timing**: Catering offers higher per-event revenue but demands advance planning, staffing flexibility, and equipment redundancy a solo founder may lack early on. At what operational milestone (daily revenue consistency, staff headcount, equipment backup) does it make sense to actively pursue catering contracts rather than treat them as opportunistic add-ons?

14. **Brick-and-mortar transition criteria**: The 3-year horizon for a potential fixed location is ambitious for a solo founder with no prior business ownership. What leading indicators — sustained monthly revenue, brand recognition, customer request patterns, neighborhood interest from landlords — should signal genuine readiness versus premature expansion pressure?

15. **Fleet expansion vs. deepening single-truck operations**: A second truck doubles operational complexity and capital requirements without doubling revenue certainty. What model — owner-operator truck #2, employee-operated truck, licensed ghost kitchen partner — best preserves quality control and personal bandwidth while testing whether the concept scales?

---

## Topic Cluster 05: Operations, Staffing, and Founder Capacity

**Cluster focus**: How a solo founder with culinary but not business ownership experience builds operational systems that don't depend entirely on their personal presence.

16. **First hire decision**: The first employee fundamentally changes the business — payroll taxes, training burden, scheduling complexity, HR exposure. What role (prep cook, service window, part-time driver) provides the highest operational leverage for a solo founder, and what minimum revenue threshold justifies that hire?

17. **Operational systems for quality consistency**: Filipino-Mexican fusion involves significant prep complexity. What systems — standardized recipes, pre-portioning protocols, commissary prep schedules — are essential before the second revenue stream (catering or a second truck) is added, to ensure the product doesn't degrade as volume increases?

18. **Founder skill gap prioritization**: Moving from culinary professional to business owner surfaces gaps in financial management, marketing, hiring, and regulatory compliance simultaneously. Given limited bandwidth, which of these gaps poses the greatest near-term operational risk, and what is the most efficient way to address it — self-learning, hiring, or outsourcing?

19. **Customer feedback loops**: Early-stage food trucks often rely on informal social media signals to understand what is working. What more structured feedback mechanisms — loyalty program data, event debrief surveys, repeat customer interviews — would generate actionable insight about menu performance and pricing tolerance without requiring significant time investment?
