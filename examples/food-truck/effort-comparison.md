## 1. Headline Finding

The core thesis survives intact across all four levels, and it is a single claim: **the truck-to-fleet-to-restaurant ladder in the brief is an unexamined default, and the highest-value move is to treat the single truck as the unit to validate and possibly to keep, not as step one toward something bigger.** Every level reaches this — but the *strength of the challenge to the brief escalates monotonically*. `min` accepts the truck and reframes the risk as sequencing discipline. `low` reframes the roadmap as "a narrative, not a plan." `medium` calls the ladder "an unexamined cultural script" and asks whether to expand at all. `high` reframes the truck itself as "a research instrument rather than a destination." The same insight, interrogated progressively closer to its foundations.

A second through-line is equally stable: **the fusion menu is the weakest part of the moat; defensibility lives in story, relationships, and presence.** All four levels say this.

No `USER-QUESTIONS.md` is present in any run, and all four PLAN.md files confirm "No user-defined questions." The comparison is therefore clean: every insight below is something each level discovered on its own, with no user-supplied questions seeding the angles.

## 5. Productive Drift Between Levels

Four zones where the recommendation sharpens (rather than contradicts) as effort rises.

**The SBA loan.** `min`: a one-variable rule — take it if post-launch reserve < $8K, bootstrap if $15K+ remains. `low`: reframes it as a filter via the declined-loan two-column exercise; resize or decline if nothing existential is loan-dependent. `medium`: explicit sequence — bootstrap $60K, validate 6–8 weeks across more than one location, *then* draw $40K as growth capital. `high`: hardens to "do not borrow to launch" on an asymmetry-of-failure argument, with a pre-approved undrawn line of credit as the hedge. *Direction: from a threshold rule → to a sequenced, conviction-backed refusal to borrow before proof.*

**MVP scope / how to validate.** `min`: validate by calculating plate cost and tracking repeat-vs-first-time orders once running. `low`: a 2–3 week soft launch at a low-traffic spot with failure criteria set in advance. `medium`: a single-location vending-only test for 12–18 months before adding a channel. `high`: validate *before the truck exists* — a ~$2–5K, 4–6 week pop-up, instrumented for COGS, repeat rate, and revenue per event hour against a 60–65% margin line. *Direction: validation moves earlier and cheaper, ultimately preceding the capital purchase entirely.*

**Pricing posture.** `min`: price into the story; premium margin beats volume. `low`: flags the tension that a solvent premium price may exclude the community the brand serves. `medium`: makes one-price-vs-tiered an explicitly unresolved choice the founder must make consciously. `high`: a live three-price test ($9/$12/$14) during the pilot to capture real elasticity, with pricing framed as identity ("a too-low price reads as an apology"). *Direction: from an assertion → to a named tension → to an empirical test.*

**Expansion trigger.** `min`: a 70%-of-theoretical-capacity gate before any new stream. `low`: a formal 18-month performance checkpoint plus a "systems test." `medium`: numeric pause-expansion triggers and a 15–22% net margin sustained six months; realistic fleet timeline 4–5 years, not 3. `high`: replace the calendar entirely with a multi-condition trigger set, gate a second truck on a manager apprenticeship started by month six, and "graduate to brick-and-mortar when supply is the binding constraint, not when demand looks hot." *Direction: from a single capacity ratio → to a multi-condition, relationship-gated trigger system.*

## 6. Fidelity Check — What Gets Lost at Lower Levels

The counterpoint to §3. Concrete insights that only higher levels carry:

- **Validate before buying the truck.** Only `high` proposes the ~$2–5K pop-up *before* the $60K purchase. `low` and `medium` have soft-launch / single-location tests, but those assume the truck already exists. The cheapest, highest-leverage de-risking move is a `high`-only insight.
- **The genuinely unknown daily cost floor.** Only `high` flags that the daily-cost estimate spans an unusable $90–$760 and names validating it as the single most important pre-launch task. Lower levels treat food cost carefully but assume the fixed-cost floor is roughly knowable.
- **Cultural-integrity stakes as a binding constraint.** `low` first surfaces this (as a "conspicuous absence" outside both lenses). `medium` operationalizes it (convene 3–4 Filipino + 3–4 Mexican tasters pre-launch). `high` deepens it structurally — the excluded constituency (unbanked, non-English-speaking, elderly) *overlaps* the honored one. Absent entirely at `min`.
- **The risk/feasibility dialectic and livelihood-vs-business.** Both first appear at `low` and are absent at `min`. The single-voice `min` run cannot stage the disagreement and never forces the prior question of what kind of business this is.
- **Quantified founder economics and burnout cost.** `medium` quantifies burnout (8–12% margin penalty); `high` quantifies the founder's effective year-one wage ($8–20/hr vs. a $45–55K culinary alternative). `min`/`low` note founder limits qualitatively only.
- **The catering-margin nuance.** All levels flag catering as a hedge, but only `medium` (recurring-contract structure) and `high` (track in margin dollars, not revenue share) explain *how* to make it accretive rather than dilutive. `min` recommends catering yet never builds the pipeline — an acknowledged gap.

## 7. When Each Level Earns Its Cost

**`min`** is best for a fast, decisive first pass when the founder mainly needs concrete thresholds to convert judgment calls into pre-committed decisions — its output is unusually rich in specific numbers ($8K reserve floor, 70% capacity gate, $2,500/week hire trigger). Characteristic weakness: a single confident voice that accepts the brief's framing, stages no real disagreement, and leaves whole domains (labor cost, cultural stakes) as unaddressed absences.

**`low`** earns its cost when the founder needs the central *prior* questions named — livelihood-vs-business, the "deliberate small" alternative — and a first taste of structured disagreement via the risk/feasibility pairing. Characteristic weakness: only two lenses, so dissent is binary; nuances that need a third or fourth perspective (contract-structured catering, equity overlap) stay out of reach.

**`medium`** is the level where genuine debate appears: four perspectives per cluster, a named cross-cluster meta-tension, and three honestly *unresolved* choices handed back to the founder. Best for decision-shaping where the founder wants the trade-offs surfaced rather than papered over. Characteristic weakness: it identifies the open choices (lease-vs-buy, pricing) but does not yet supply an empirical way to resolve them.

**`high`** earns its substantial cost (84 responses) when the founder wants the brief's premises stress-tested to their foundations and turned into testable experiments — the pre-truck pop-up, the three-price elasticity test, quantified convergence ("N of M perspectives"), and explicit minority reports. Characteristic weakness: its sheer optimism-by-register and breadth mean the most consequential choice (which end-state) is still, correctly, deferred to a scheduled reassessment rather than answered — the depth buys better questions, not a final decision.

## 8. Final Summary

- **Preservation:** The core thesis (question the expansion ladder; the menu is the weakest moat; validate before committing) and roughly ten through-lines — including above-benchmark food cost, loan-as-filter, founder-as-constraint, designed cash flow, and pre-vote regulatory action — survive intact from `min` to `high`. A founder reading only `min` gets the right spine.
- **Scaling:** Effort scales fastest in Phase 3 (5 → 18 → 36 → 84 responses, ~17×) via the brainstormers-per-cluster multiplier (1 → 2 → 4 → 7). Questions (~4×) and clusters (~2.4×) grow more gently; next-steps stay fixed at 15. Depth, not breadth-of-recommendation-count, is what money buys.
- **Divergences:** Disagreement becomes progressively visible — one voice (`min`) → two opposed lenses (`low`) → four perspectives with open tensions (`medium`) → seven with quantified convergence and named dissents (`high`). The critique of the brief deepens monotonically along the same axis.
- **Irreplaceable high-effort insights:** Validating with a ~$2–5K pop-up *before* buying the truck; flagging the genuinely unknown daily cost floor ($90–$760); the asymmetry-of-failure case against launch debt; the accessibility-equals-authenticity constituency overlap; and the three-price elasticity test. These appear only at `high` and would be lost at any lower setting.
