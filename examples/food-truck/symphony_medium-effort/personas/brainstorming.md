## Per-Topic Rationale

### Cluster 01: Business Model Foundations & Identity

**Selected:** Devil's Advocate, Pragmatist, Visionary, Analogist

**Topic classification:** Strategic/Vision with novel/ambiguous elements. Questions probe identity, sustainability definitions, category-invention framing ("invent this from scratch"), and the one-truck-forever provocation — foundational framing questions rather than execution specifics.

- **Devil's Advocate (core):** Questions like Q1 (fusion as differentiator vs. crowded-market risk) and Q8 (growth narrative as trap) are already structured as critique; DA extends that critical stance to identity assumptions across the cluster.
- **Pragmatist (core):** Grounds abstract identity framing ("category invention," "what sustainable means") in minimum-viable action — what's the smallest identity commitment the founder can make in year one?
- **Visionary (strong fit, strategic/vision):** Q6 (category invention over category entry) and Q8 (one-truck-forever) invite paradigm-shift thinking. Visionary pushes past "food truck competing in 30-truck market" to what this *could* be — a cultural form, a roving proof-of-concept.
- **Analogist (strong fit, novel/ambiguous):** Filipino-Mexican fusion as a *founder-identity* question doesn't map to an established playbook; Analogist imports identity-formation patterns from other domains (craft operators, chef-driven concepts, artisanal single-unit businesses that chose depth over scale). Complements Visionary: Visionary invents; Analogist imports.

### Cluster 02: Unit Economics & Break-Even

**Selected:** Devil's Advocate, Pragmatist, Accountant, First Principles Thinker

**Topic classification:** Financial/Business. Three questions explicitly about food cost percentages, break-even architecture under capitalization scenarios, and margin buffers against cost spikes.

- **Devil's Advocate (core):** Q11 already asks what breaks the break-even model — DA presses which assumptions are most likely wrong and which fusion-competitor price ceilings have already been tested.
- **Pragmatist (core):** Translates ratio analysis into "what do you actually need to know before launch day?" — minimum viable unit economics model.
- **Accountant (strong fit, financial/business):** Directly matches. Food cost ratios, fixed vs. variable cost decomposition, break-even under three capitalization scenarios, and 20%-cost-spike sensitivity analysis are core Accountant territory. No anti-pattern risk — this is a real business with real margins.
- **First Principles Thinker (strong fit, deconstruction):** Q9 asks what the *realistic* food cost percentage is for a fusion menu when ube and adobo components have different sourcing profiles than standard Mexican fare. FPT strips benchmarks back to the ingredient level rather than importing industry averages uncritically. Complements Accountant (who works within conventional frameworks) by questioning the frameworks themselves.

### Cluster 03: Capital Structure & Financing Decisions

**Selected:** Devil's Advocate, Pragmatist, Accountant, Systems Thinker

**Topic classification:** Financial/Business with systems-dynamics framing (leverage amplifier, risk distribution, working-capital contingency cascades).

- **Devil's Advocate (core):** Q12 and Q15 already frame the SBA loan as a both-ways leverage bet and an unvalidated-model risk; DA extends the "what if the loan is delayed or denied?" pressure across every financing choice.
- **Pragmatist (core):** Grounds three-year TCO comparisons in "what can actually be decided at the point of truck purchase?" — minimum viable financing decision.
- **Accountant (strong fit, financial/business):** Loan amortization, debt-service ratios, TCO comparisons across lease/own/used-truck, working-capital reserve sizing in months-of-fixed-costs — all squarely financial planning.
- **Systems Thinker (good fit, financial with feedback loops):** Q12 explicitly frames the loan as a *leverage amplifier in both directions* — a feedback structure where higher fixed-cost floor interacts with revenue volatility. Q14 describes the cascade when the first mechanical failure hits. Systems Thinker maps these reinforcing/balancing loops (debt service ↔ revenue volatility ↔ cash reserve) rather than treating financing choices as static scalar comparisons. Not redundant with Accountant: Accountant sizes the reserves; Systems Thinker maps when they get drained.

### Cluster 04: Revenue Diversification & Cash Flow

**Selected:** Devil's Advocate, Pragmatist, Accountant, Futurist

**Topic classification:** Financial/Business with strategic seasonality framing. Questions focus on channel margin comparison, cannibalization, seasonal reserve calculations, and concentration risk.

- **Devil's Advocate (core):** Q21 (single-revenue-stream provocation) already inverts the diversification premise; DA asks which parts of the diversification plan are genuine opportunity vs. "anxiety dressed up as planning."
- **Pragmatist (core):** Minimum viable revenue mix for year one — which channel do you have to get right *first* before adding complexity?
- **Accountant (strong fit, financial/business):** Revenue-per-hour by channel, margin-accretive vs. margin-dilutive catering math, minimum cash reserve to bridge a 10-week slow season — direct financial-planning work.
- **Futurist (good fit, seasonality and trend-grounded positioning):** Q18 and Q22 (the winery's dual-revenue calendar) explicitly invite trend-grounded thinking about seasonal demand curves, pop-up residencies, pre-sold meal kits, wine-club analogs. Futurist extrapolates from observable patterns in adjacent food/beverage industries (DTC meal kits, brewery collaborations, farm-share subscriptions). Not redundant with Accountant: Accountant models the existing mix; Futurist proposes channels that aren't yet in the mix.

### Cluster 05: Regulatory Strategy & Engagement

**Selected:** Devil's Advocate, Pragmatist, Lawyer, Politician

**Topic classification:** Regulated/Compliance AND Governance/Political. This is the clearest case in the whole cluster set for both specialized lenses — pending city ordinance + active council debate + food-truck-association coordination dynamics.

- **Devil's Advocate (core):** Q26 (ordinance-gift provocation) already inverts the threat framing; DA extends the "what if we're wrong about which posture is right?" to every regulatory-engagement assumption.
- **Pragmatist (core):** Grounds regulatory advocacy in "what can one solo founder actually do with finite hours this month?" — minimum viable engagement.
- **Lawyer (strong fit, regulated/compliance):** Genuine regulatory stakes — ordinance language, licensing fee structures, permit enforcement, commissary compliance interacting with ordinance timelines. This is not the "HIPAA for a birthday party" anti-pattern; this is a pending ordinance that could cut 30–50% of downtown revenue. Lawyer lens is earned.
- **Politician (strong fit, governance/political):** Q25 and Q27 explicitly frame the food-truck association as a coordination vehicle and Portland's food-cart pods as a coalition-building analog. Q28 asks which relationships will matter most after the vote. Politician handles coalition math, association leverage, and the cost/benefit of advocacy hours vs. revenue-generating hours. This is not the "factional caucus for decorations" anti-pattern; this is real municipal politics.

*Note:* This is the only topic with two outer-ring personas. Justified because (a) Tier 3 selection memo in PLAN.md explicitly identified Politician as "strong" (deferred only due to medium ceiling of 1 at Phase 2A), and (b) the cluster itself is the reason the ordinance shows up in the REQUEST. Both lenses are needed and neither is over-applying.

### Cluster 06: Commissary Kitchen Infrastructure

**Selected:** Devil's Advocate, Pragmatist, Analogist, Constraint Flipper

**Topic classification:** Implementation/Ops with a novel reframing angle (compliance-burden-as-strategic-asset, hub-and-spoke analog).

- **Devil's Advocate (core):** Q29 already frames commissary as single-point-of-failure dependency; DA extends to "what breaks if the backup commissary is also fragile?"
- **Pragmatist (core):** Minimum viable commissary relationship — what's the smallest contract that protects against the failure modes Q29 names?
- **Analogist (strong fit, novel/ambiguous; Q31 literally invokes airline hub-and-spoke):** Q31 asks how mandated commissary can be designed as operational spine of a future fleet, drawing explicitly on airline networks and ghost-kitchen operators. Analogist's job. Also imports from co-working spaces, shared-use creameries, incubator kitchens.
- **Constraint Flipper (strong fit, implementation/ops):** Q30 asks what it would look like to treat commissary as strategic asset rather than compliance burden — the constraint-as-opportunity reframing is Constraint Flipper's exact move. Q30's second half (geographic equity of access in commissary-adjacent zones) also invites reframing the required vending-zone geography as a deliberate routing choice toward underserved neighborhoods.

*Complementarity check:* Analogist imports concrete solutions from other hub-based industries; Constraint Flipper reframes the local constraint. Distinct moves.

### Cluster 07: Customer Experience, Access & Equity

**Selected:** Devil's Advocate, Pragmatist, Empath, Audience Advocate

**Topic classification:** User-Facing/Community. Every question in the cluster is about a customer's lived experience — unfamiliar cuisine, mobility limits, dietary restrictions, cultural legibility, discovery without social media, first-transaction arc, invisible-customer provocation, loyal-regular relationship.

- **Devil's Advocate (core):** Q37 (invisible-customer provocation) already inverts the "deepen loyalty among enthusiasts" premise; DA extends by pressing whether the current concept is a niche that converts or a niche that consolidates.
- **Pragmatist (core):** Minimum viable accessibility — which one or two friction points, fixed at launch, unlock the biggest repeat-visit gain?
- **Empath (strong fit, user-facing):** Q32's "what emotional state do you want that first-timer to leave in" and Q36's first-person arc from "walking past, curious about the smell" to "sharing a photo with a friend" are pure Empath questions. Emotional resonance is the unit of analysis.
- **Audience Advocate (strong fit, user-facing):** Q33 explicitly enumerates stakeholder segments — income band, mobility limits, elderly customers, parents with young children, dietary-restricted diners, customers from Filipino and Mexican communities. This is stakeholder-needs mapping, which is Audience Advocate's core move.

*Complementarity check:* Empath operates on emotional resonance (how does this *feel* to that person?); Audience Advocate operates on needs mapping (which needs does this person have that we're missing?). Validated as low-overlap within the human-centered triad.

### Cluster 08: Brand, Community & Cultural Identity

**Selected:** Devil's Advocate, Pragmatist, Storyteller, Audience Advocate

**Topic classification:** User-Facing/Community with strategic positioning elements. Questions range over reputation compounding, differentiation among four fusion trucks, cultural partnership ethics, first-fifty loyal customers, observing thriving operators.

- **Devil's Advocate (core):** Q44 and Q45 (business beyond the truck, owned by people who love it) already invert conventional brand-building assumptions; DA extends to "which of these reframings are actually revenue-generating vs. marketing mythology?"
- **Pragmatist (core):** Minimum viable brand — what's the single relationship or story that could be built this month that materially compounds?
- **Storyteller (strong fit, user-facing, cultural legibility):** Q41 (welcomed collaborator vs. outsider capitalizing on community identity), Q42 (belonging brand, stories customers tell about discovering you), Q38 (the regular's story) — all named-character narrative questions. Storyteller specifically excels at communities and cultural-identity topics, and the Filipino/Latinx community events in Q41 make this a clear fit.
- **Audience Advocate (strong fit, user-facing):** Q42's "first fifty loyal customers, catering clients, informal advisors" is literal stakeholder mapping. Q43 (respectful conversation with a thriving operator) also centers who the relevant stakeholders *are* among ~30 existing trucks.

*Complementarity check:* Storyteller produces lived-experience narratives (what a Filipina grandmother feels seeing adobo burritos on the menu); Audience Advocate maps which stakeholder groups and relationships most compound brand equity. Validated low-overlap pairing within human-centered triad.

### Cluster 09: Expansion Path — Fleet & Brick-and-Mortar

**Selected:** Devil's Advocate, Pragmatist, Futurist, Visionary

**Topic classification:** Strategic/Vision with strong financial trigger-metric component. Questions explicitly probe fleet-trigger thresholds, brick-and-mortar-as-trap, system-change-vs-extension, three-year dream picture, anti-restaurant provocation, proof-of-concept-before-scale, brick-and-mortar-that-isn't.

- **Devil's Advocate (core):** Q48 and Q51 already frame brick-and-mortar as trap and "worst possible outcome is that you succeed so well you become a restaurant"; DA extends to pressing every expansion-readiness assumption.
- **Pragmatist (core):** Minimum viable proof-of-concept — what's the single operational metric that must be demonstrably stable before truck #2?
- **Futurist (strong fit, strategic/vision with trend-grounded positioning):** Q47 (fleet trigger metrics, run rates, waitlist demand) and Q52 (McDonald's proof-point playbook, milestone discipline) invite trend-grounded extrapolation from today's unit economics to tomorrow's expansion triggers. Q49 (second-order effects on operations) asks what happens as the business evolves — Futurist's temporal lens.
- **Visionary (strong fit, strategic/vision):** Q51 (anti-restaurant provocation) and Q53 (the brick-and-mortar that isn't) explicitly ask for paradigm-shift thinking — a form of permanence that "doesn't yet exist." Visionary ignores current reality; Futurist extrapolates from it. Both questions are in the cluster, so both lenses are earned.

*Complementarity check:* ~19–20% Visionary-Futurist convergence documented in guidance. Both are included here because the cluster contains *explicit* questions for each frame (Q52 for trend-grounded proof-of-concept; Q51/Q53 for paradigm shift). Not a stacking risk — a topic match.
