## Food Cost Ratio For A Fusion Menu

* **Budget sourcing strategy with premium positioning.** Target 32% food cost on blended menu by sourcing Filipino staples in bulk (ube, adobo paste, calamansi concentrate) from ethnic suppliers at 15-20% discount vs. specialty food importers. Price signature items (lumpia tacos, adobo burritos) at $9-11 (40% higher margin) while offering lower-cost sides (horchata, rice bowls) at $6-7 (28-30% cost). This mixed-margin approach matches the 28-35% industry range while capturing customer preference for specialty items.

* **Cost-plus pricing with competitive ceiling awareness.** Research the four fusion competitors' menu pricing (mystery shop or survey their customers). If their $10-12 price points represent 30% food cost, your ingredient sourcing determines your advantage: lower cost means higher margin at same price, or competitive price at higher margin. Filipino ingredient suppliers in a 250K city typically run 10-15% more than Mexican suppliers; budget $0.35-0.50/unit premium on Filipino items, then price $0.50-0.75 higher than Mexican equivalents to cover the cost and signal quality.

* **Variable-cost stress test under supply disruption.** Assume a 15-20% ingredient cost spike during your first 6 months (seasonal shortage, supplier change, or supply chain volatility). At 32% blended cost, a 15% spike = 36.8% cost. Can you absorb that without a price increase? A $10 burrito at 36.8% cost drops margin from 32% to 26.8% (4% absolute margin loss). Pre-calculate which items you'll raise (signature items, low price elasticity) and by how much ($0.50-1.00 raises average ticket by $0.15-0.30, recovering 1-2% of margin). This becomes your first-crisis playbook.

## Break-Even Architecture At The Truck Level

* **Bootstrap scenario ($60K capital, equipment + working capital).** Fixed monthly costs: truck payment/lease ($800-1,200), commissary ($400-600), permits & insurance ($200-300), fuel ($300-500), loan interest (none), loan servicing (none) = **$1,700-2,600/month, call it $2,100**. Break-even: divide by average contribution margin (if 32% cost, that's 68% revenue). At 68% contribution margin, need **$3,090/month in sales = $103/day over 30 days**. At $9.50 avg ticket = **11 transactions/day**. Across 20 vending days/month (accounting for supply days, weather, seasonal slowdown), you need 220 transactions = 11 transactions/day.

* **SBA-loan scenario ($100K capital with debt service).** Add $400-500/month debt service (5-year SBA microloan at ~7%) = **$2,600-3,100/month fixed costs**. Break-even = **$3,820-4,560/month in sales**, or **127-152 transactions/20 vending days = 6-8 additional transactions/day** above bootstrap scenario. This is material: your foot-traffic target shifts from 11 to 18-19 transactions/day. Success probability drops by 15-25% (site selection becomes less forgiving, seasonal variation becomes riskier).

* **Sensitivity analysis: transaction volume is your primary risk.** A single truck in a new location typically hits 8-12 transactions/day in month 1-2, peaks at 15-25 by month 4-6 (word-of-mouth, location lock-in). If you're targeting 11-19, you're betting on mid-to-upper range. Scenario: you hit 12/day instead of 18. In the SBA loan case, monthly shortfall = (18-12) × $9.50 × 20 = **$1,140/month under break-even**. That's a cumulative $11,400 burn per year. With $100K capital and $2,100-2,600 monthly fixed costs, you have ~8-10 months of runway. Hitting 12/day instead of 18 compresses runway to 6-7 months — that's your margin of error.

## Break-Even As A Moving Target

* **Margin-buffer strategy: lock in 12-15% before-tax operating margin.** At current 32% food cost + estimated 40-45% labor (including payroll taxes and owner draw) + 8-10% fixed costs (rent, permits, insurance, fuel), operating margin is ~10-15%. A 20% food cost spike (32% → 38.4%) eats 6.4 percentage points of margin, leaving 3.5-8.5%. That's survival-level, not sustainable. Before opening, build a 15% minimum margin buffer: either (a) start with food cost target of 27-28% instead of 32% through ruthless supplier sourcing, or (b) plan for price increases immediately — a 6-8% menu price increase recovers the 6.4 point margin loss, but likely costs 8-15% of volume in a price-sensitive market. Better to start lean.

* **Three-trigger price adjustment plan.** Establish clear decision rules: (1) If food cost hits 35% (20% spike), raise menu prices 5% on low-elasticity items (signature items that drew customers) and absorb the spike. (2) If food cost hits 37%+, raise all prices 6-8% and plan for 5-10% volume loss. (3) If permits/commissary fees rise by >10%, raise prices 3% and simultaneously explore catering or event bookings to diversify revenue. Pre-calculate the price increases now while you have data; don't make decisions under stress.

* **Worst-case scenario: simultaneous cost pressures.** Model the case where food cost rises 20%, permit fees increase 25% (new annual licensing fee of $500 mentioned in REQUEST), and commissary cost rises 15%, *simultaneously* in month 8-10. Food cost rises from $3.04 to $3.65 per $9.50 transaction. Commissary cost rises from $500 to $575/month. Permit fees add ~$42/month (assuming $500/year). Total impact: -4.1% transaction margin + $117/month fixed cost increase = **$329/month impact on a 20-transaction/day operation**. That's 3.5% of gross revenue gone. The only response is a combination of 7-10% price increase and 5-10% volume loss acceptance — which means your breakeven ticket count rises from 11-19 to 12-21 transactions/day, further compressing margin. This is the case for a 12-month contingency fund of $5-8K.
