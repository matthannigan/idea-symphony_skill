# Brainstorming Questions from The Devil's Advocate: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Assumptions Baked into the Capital Plan

1. **Loan dependency risk**: The 3-year expansion plan assumes $100K in available capital, but what happens if the SBA microloan is denied or delayed by 6-12 months — which path to profitability remains viable on $60K alone, and does that change whether launching is advisable at all?

2. **Break-even timing mismatch**: Food truck income is heavily front-loaded by location and season, yet loan repayments are fixed monthly — if the truck underperforms in its first two quarters, what specific financial triggers should prompt the founder to pause expansion rather than accelerate it, and has that threshold been defined before committing capital?

3. **Equipment cost creep**: Used food trucks often require unexpected repairs in year one that can consume 15-20% of initial capital — what contingency reserve is built into the $60K/$100K budget, and if none, what gets cut first when the first major mechanical failure hits?

## Regulatory Fragility

4. **Ordinance timing exposure**: The pending city ordinance changes could restrict downtown vending hours before the truck even reaches its first full season — have we considered what happens to the unit economics if the highest-revenue downtown slots are cut by 30-50%, and is there a fallback location strategy that doesn't depend on downtown access?

5. **Commissary dependency as a single point of failure**: Requiring a commissary kitchen creates a critical dependency on a third-party relationship — what happens to daily operations if the commissary raises rates, loses its health permit, or terminates the agreement on 30 days' notice, and is there a backup commissary already identified?

## Market Viability and Competitive Position

6. **Fusion concept saturation**: With 4 existing trucks already running similar fusion concepts in a 30-truck market, what specific evidence supports the assumption that Filipino-Mexican fusion represents an underserved niche rather than a crowded one — and if the differentiation is primarily culinary rather than positional, how durable is that advantage if a competitor copies the menu?

7. **Solo-founder operational ceiling**: A single founder with no prior business ownership must simultaneously cook, manage inventory, handle bookings, navigate permits, and build a customer base — at what weekly revenue or order volume does this model break down operationally, and what's the plan for crossing that threshold before burning out?

## Expansion Path Stress Tests

8. **Fleet scaling assumptions**: The 3-year plan anticipates expanding to a small fleet, but each additional truck requires capital, a licensed driver, and its own permitting — if the first truck never reaches consistent profitability (say, net margin below 12%), what criteria prevent the founder from throwing good money after bad by adding a second truck anyway?

9. **Brick-and-mortar as a trap**: Brick-and-mortar is framed as a destination in the 3-year plan, but restaurant failure rates remain high and the capital requirements dwarf food truck costs — have we considered whether a permanent location is actually the right end state, or whether it's a default assumption that deserves to be questioned before it becomes a sunk-cost commitment?

## Revenue Diversification Risks

10. **Catering as margin dilution**: Catering and event bookings are cited as revenue diversification levers, but they require advance inventory commitment, staff coordination, and often delayed payment — what's the minimum margin threshold that makes a catering booking worth taking versus vending that same day, and without that calculus, how does the founder avoid underpricing catering contracts that look like revenue but destroy cash flow?
