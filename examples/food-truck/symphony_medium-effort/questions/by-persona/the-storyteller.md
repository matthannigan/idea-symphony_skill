# Brainstorming Questions from The Storyteller: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## The First Customer's Experience

1. **The opening-day arc**: Imagine the first person who discovers your lumpia taco truck — they're walking past at lunch, curious about the smell, and they've never heard of Filipino-Mexican fusion. Walk through their entire experience from that first glance to sharing a photo with a friend: what moments along that journey are you designing intentionally, and which ones are you leaving to chance?

2. **The regular's story**: Picture a customer who becomes a loyal weekly visitor six months in — what does that relationship look like, what keeps drawing them back beyond the food itself, and how does your operation need to be structured to make that kind of loyalty feel mutual rather than transactional?

## The Solo Founder's Day

3. **A Tuesday in year one**: Describe what a typical Tuesday looks like for you — from commissary kitchen prep before dawn through breakdown and close-out at night — and at which points in that day does the business feel most fragile, most alive, and most in need of a second set of hands you don't yet have?

4. **The moment it almost breaks**: Every solo founder hits a day when everything goes wrong at once — a permit delay, an equipment failure, a slow week after a great one. What does your version of that day look like, and what financial, operational, or personal resources would need to be in place for you to absorb it and keep going rather than fold?

## The City as a Character

5. **The regulatory turning point**: Imagine it's six months from now and the city council has passed the new food truck ordinances — whether they're favorable or restrictive. Walk through how a typical vending week would feel differently under each scenario: what would you have wished you had done in the months before the vote, and what relationships would prove to matter most?

6. **The food truck scene's shared story**: Picture the local food truck community — the 30 trucks, the association, the event organizers — as a neighborhood you're moving into. What's the story of how you go from newcomer to trusted member of that community, and what does that standing unlock for you operationally (prime spots, catering leads, emergency commissary access) that you couldn't get on your own?

## The Expansion Moment

7. **The inflection scene**: Fast-forward to the moment you decide to add a second truck or sign a lease on a brick-and-mortar space — what has to be true about the business for that decision to feel like a natural next step rather than a leap of faith, and what does the founder's life look like the week before and the week after that decision is made?
