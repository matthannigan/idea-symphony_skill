# Brainstorming Questions from The Analogist: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Franchising as a Stage Map: Sequential Expansion Patterns

1. **Proof-of-concept before scale**: The early franchise playbook — Ray Kroc didn't build 100 McDonald's locations until the San Bernardino original had proven its unit economics, kitchen choreography, and repeat-customer pull. Given that this business starts with one truck and $100K in capital, what are the minimum proof points (daily revenue threshold, food cost ratio, average transaction size, customer return rate) that should be demonstrably stable before a second truck or catering vertical is layered on — and how does that milestone discipline change the 3-year timeline?

2. **The hub-and-spoke commissary model**: Airline networks solved the problem of serving many destinations from limited hubs by routing everything through high-throughput central nodes. The commissary kitchen requirement is typically seen as overhead, but hub-and-spoke operators in food manufacturing and ghost kitchen networks have turned their central production facility into a strategic asset — a place where sauces are batched, proteins are prepped, and quality is standardized across multiple service points. How could the mandated commissary relationship be designed from the outset as the operational spine of a future fleet rather than a cost of compliance?

---

## Seasonal Resilience: Lessons from Tourism and Agriculture

3. **The winery's dual-revenue calendar**: Wineries in cold climates survive by stacking tasting room revenue (spring/summer/fall) against case sales, wine club subscriptions, and private events (winter), so no single channel carries the whole year. A food truck in a mid-size city faces analogous seasonality — outdoor foot traffic contracts in winter, festivals end, office lunch crowds thin during holidays. What revenue mix (catering contracts, pop-up residencies inside bars or breweries, pre-sold meal kits using the commissary) could buffer seasonal downturns, and what minimum monthly floor does each channel need to cover to keep the business cash-flow positive through its lowest quarter?

4. **Crop rotation as menu engineering**: Farmers rotate crops not only to restore soil nutrients but to manage demand cycles, input costs, and price variation by season. A Filipino-Mexican fusion menu built around seasonal ingredients (mangoes in summer, root vegetables in winter, chiles varying by harvest) could simultaneously reduce food cost volatility, sharpen the brand story around freshness, and create natural limited-time offers that drive urgency. How intentionally is the menu being engineered around ingredient seasonality and cost cycles, and what would a 12-month crop-rotation-style menu calendar reveal about food cost ratios across the year?

---

## Regulatory Navigation: Analogies from Other Contested Public Spaces

5. **How food carts shaped Portland's street grid**: Portland's food cart pods emerged partly because operators organized collectively, proposed designated pod locations to the city, and framed the ask around economic development and neighborhood activation rather than asking for regulatory exemptions. The pending city ordinance debate is a live process — not a fixed constraint. What would it look like to participate in that process not just defensively (protecting existing vending hours) but proactively (proposing a food truck district framework, co-authoring standards for licensed zones, or presenting unit-economics data that reframes trucks as economic anchors rather than nuisances)?

---

## Capital Structure: The Bootstrapped vs. Leveraged Growth Dichotomy

6. **The lean startup's minimum viable product lens**: Eric Ries's core insight was that the cost of learning should be minimized before capital is deployed at scale — validated learning is the unit of progress, not features shipped. The SBA microloan question maps directly onto this: borrowing $40K before the first truck has demonstrated its unit economics is a bet on an unvalidated model; borrowing $40K after six months of proof-of-concept data is a bet on a validated one. What specific operational and financial milestones (break-even month, consistent weekly revenue band, catering booking rate) would constitute sufficient validation before drawing on debt, and how does that sequencing affect the loan terms available?

7. **The media startup's dual flywheel**: Successful independent media companies (newsletters, podcasts, local news outlets) have survived by building two interlocking flywheels — audience growth feeds advertising revenue, which funds content quality, which grows the audience. A food truck can build an analogous dual flywheel: daily vending builds local brand visibility and a loyal follower base, which makes the truck attractive to event organizers and corporate catering clients, which generates higher-margin revenue that funds equipment upgrades and menu R&D, which sharpens the brand that drives daily vending. What would it take to deliberately design and measure this flywheel from month one — and which metric (followers, email list size, catering inquiries per month, average event contract value) serves as the best leading indicator that the flywheel is spinning?
