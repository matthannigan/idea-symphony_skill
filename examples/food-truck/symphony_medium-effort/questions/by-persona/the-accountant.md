# Brainstorming Questions from The Accountant: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Unit Economics and Break-Even Reality

1. **Food cost target vs. actual menu math**: Filipino-Mexican fusion items like lumpia tacos and adobo burritos likely carry different food cost ratios — what is the realistic cost-of-goods percentage for each major menu item, and what blended food cost ratio does the menu produce at the intended price points, and does that ratio leave enough margin to cover labor, commissary fees, fuel, and overhead while still turning a profit?

2. **Break-even at the truck level**: Given fixed monthly costs (commissary kitchen rental, insurance, permit fees, loan servicing if applicable, vehicle maintenance reserve) plus variable costs per service day, roughly how many service days per month — and at what average revenue per day — does the truck need to reach break-even, and how does that compare to realistic daily revenue in a mid-size city food truck market?

3. **Pricing headroom vs. competitive ceiling**: If the four existing fusion-concept competitors have established a de facto price ceiling in the local market, what happens to the unit economics when food cost inputs fluctuate (ingredient price swings, seasonal availability of Filipino staples), and at what food cost increase does the current pricing model become unprofitable without a menu price adjustment?

---

## Capital Structure and Financing Trade-offs

4. **SBA microloan total cost of capital**: Beyond the principal, what does the $40K SBA microloan actually cost — interest rate over the loan term, origination fees, required collateral or personal guarantee exposure — and at what monthly revenue level does debt servicing become a material drag versus the working capital benefit the loan provides?

5. **Equipment financing vs. leasing vs. used truck purchase**: Each path to a road-ready truck implies a different cost structure — a new truck financed over five years, a leased truck, or a used truck purchased outright from the $60K savings — what are the monthly cash outflows, maintenance risk profiles, and residual value implications of each option, and which structure preserves the most operating flexibility in year one when revenue is unpredictable?

6. **Bootstrap threshold before external capital**: If the founder chooses to delay the SBA loan and bootstrap from $60K savings, what is the minimum viable launch budget, how many months of operating runway does the remainder provide at a realistic burn rate before the business must reach cash-flow positive, and what happens to the expansion timeline (fleet and brick-and-mortar) if the loan is deferred by one or two years?

---

## Revenue Mix and Cash Flow Management

7. **Revenue stream economics by channel**: Daily vending, catering contracts, and event bookings each carry different revenue-per-hour, food cost, and labor cost profiles — what does the gross margin look like for each channel at realistic volume, and which mix of channels produces the most financially stable monthly cash flow given that daily vending is weather- and foot-traffic-dependent?

8. **Seasonal cash flow gap and reserve requirement**: A mid-size city food truck likely faces a revenue trough in winter months — what is a realistic revenue differential between peak and trough months, what cash reserve is needed to bridge the gap without drawing on credit, and how does locking in catering or event contracts in advance change the reserve calculation?

---

## Expansion Economics: Fleet and Brick-and-Mortar

9. **Second truck financial entry point**: Expanding to a second truck before the first is fully profitable creates compounded operating risk — at what minimum monthly net income from truck one, sustained over how many months, does the economics of a second truck become defensible, and what incremental fixed costs (second permit, commissary slot, hired operator labor) does the model need to absorb?

10. **Brick-and-mortar transition cost and revenue bridge**: A brick-and-mortar location carries capital costs (build-out, equipment, lease deposit) and a higher fixed cost base than a truck — what order-of-magnitude investment is required to open a small counter-service or fast-casual space in a mid-size city, what monthly revenue is needed to cover that fixed cost base, and does the truck's brand equity and catering revenue realistically support that transition within a three-year window, or does the timeline need to be extended?
