# Brainstorming Questions from The Analyst: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Unit Economics and Break-Even Architecture

1. **Food cost ratio decomposition**: For the Filipino-Mexican fusion menu — lumpia tacos, adobo burritos, ube horchata — what is the realistic food cost percentage for each item category, and how does the blended menu food cost ratio compare to the industry benchmark range of 28–35%, given that Filipino ingredients (ube, adobo components) may have different sourcing profiles than standard Mexican fare?

2. **Break-even transaction volume**: Given estimated fixed costs (truck payment or lease, commissary rental, permits, insurance, fuel) and variable costs per transaction, at what average ticket size and daily transaction count does the truck break even — and how does that threshold shift under the low ($60K bootstrap), middle ($100K with SBA loan), and high-capitalization scenarios?

3. **Revenue per service hour**: Breaking total revenue into daily vending, catering bookings, and event revenue streams — what is the expected revenue per operational hour for each channel, and which channel produces the best margin once labor, travel time, and setup costs are fully loaded?

4. **SBA loan leverage math**: If the $40K SBA microloan is used to finance equipment rather than working capital, what is the fully-loaded cost of that capital (interest, fees, origination costs over the loan term) relative to the equipment's productive life, and at what monthly net revenue does the debt service become a manageable fraction (under 15%) of gross revenue?

---

## Capital Allocation and Equipment Decisions

5. **Lease-versus-own decision variables**: The key factors in the lease-versus-purchase decision for the primary truck include monthly cash outflow, maintenance liability, depreciation, and flexibility for upgrade — what is the 3-year total cost of ownership for a purchased used truck versus a lease arrangement, assuming 250 operating days per year, and which scenario preserves more working capital in year one when cash flow risk is highest?

6. **Commissary cost as a strategic variable**: Commissary kitchen fees are a fixed operating cost but also a ceiling on production capacity — what are the cost-per-hour ranges for commissary access in mid-size cities, and at what daily prep volume does it become economically rational to explore shared kitchen co-ops, co-lease arrangements, or early brick-and-mortar transition as an alternative commissary solution?

7. **Working capital reserve sizing**: Beyond the initial truck and equipment outlay, what is the appropriate working capital reserve (expressed as months of fixed costs) for a solo-founder food truck in its first 12 months, and how does that reserve calculation change if commissary fees, permits, and insurance are paid annually versus monthly?

---

## Revenue Diversification and Channel Mix

8. **Catering margin differential**: Catering events typically carry different economics than daily street vending — higher ticket, lower transaction volume, different food cost profiles. What variables determine whether catering bookings are margin-accretive or margin-dilutive compared to the base vending channel, and what minimum per-person catering price is needed to justify a full-day booking over a standard vending day?

9. **Seasonal cash flow modeling**: A mid-size city food truck likely experiences demand fluctuations across weather seasons, local event calendars, and school year cycles — what is the expected revenue variance between peak and trough months, and what financial instruments or revenue channels (indoor event bookings, catering contracts, pre-sold loyalty programs) could smooth that variance to prevent working capital gaps?

10. **Event booking concentration risk**: If event revenue becomes a meaningful share of total revenue, what level of concentration in a single event type or single event organizer creates fragility — and what diversification across event categories (corporate catering, weddings, festivals, farmers markets) reduces single-point-of-failure risk to an acceptable level?

---

## Regulatory and Ordinance Risk Quantification

11. **Ordinance impact scenario analysis**: The pending city ordinances restricting downtown vending hours and adding annual licensing fees represent a quantifiable risk — breaking this down: what is the estimated revenue impact of a downtown hours restriction (as a percentage of total location revenue), what is the cost range of new annual licensing fees relative to current permit costs, and what is the expected timeline for ordinance passage that should inform the startup's go-live planning?

12. **Regulatory engagement cost-benefit**: Active engagement with the city council process (attending hearings, joining the food truck association, coordinating with other operators) requires founder time that has opportunity cost — how should a solo founder allocate engagement hours between regulatory advocacy and revenue-generating operations, and at what point does organizing with other food truck operators create sufficient collective leverage to shift the ordinance outcome?

---

## Expansion Path Decision Criteria

13. **Fleet expansion trigger metrics**: The plan targets growth from one truck to a small fleet within three years — what specific financial and operational thresholds (net margin percentage, monthly revenue run rate, operational efficiency metrics, waitlist demand) should trigger the decision to acquire a second truck, and how does the sequencing of catering/event revenue development affect the readiness conditions for fleet expansion?
