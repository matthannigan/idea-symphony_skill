# Brainstorming Questions from The Visionary: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Reimagining What a Food Truck Business Actually Is

1. **The business beyond the truck**: What if the food truck weren't the business at all, but rather a roving proof-of-concept for a cultural brand — where the truck's purpose is to generate belonging and story rather than to serve meals, and revenue flows from that cultural gravity rather than from transactions at a window?

2. **Category invention over category entry**: Imagine this Filipino-Mexican fusion concept didn't exist yet and you were inventing it from scratch today — not as a food truck competing in a 30-truck market, but as an entirely new kind of experience that happens to involve food. What would that look like, and what would it make the truck permit, the commissary kitchen, and the SBA loan mean in that world?

---

## Inverting the Relationship Between Founder and Community

3. **Owned by the people who love it**: What if the path from one truck to a fleet weren't a solo founder scaling up, but instead a community of loyal regulars who co-own the expansion — not through a traditional co-op model, but through some genuinely new form of distributed stake that has no current analog in the restaurant industry?

4. **The regulatory process as creative medium**: Rather than treating the pending city ordinance debate as a constraint to navigate or a threat to mitigate, what if the founder treated it as a design space — an opportunity to invent a new category of relationship between mobile food vendors and municipal government that the city itself doesn't yet have language for?

---

## Reinventing the Arc from Truck to Permanence

5. **The brick-and-mortar that isn't**: Imagine the three-year goal of a brick-and-mortar location was replaced by something that provides permanence and scale without the fixed overhead — not a ghost kitchen, not a franchise, not a pop-up series, but something categorically different that doesn't yet exist. What would it need to provide emotionally and economically that a physical storefront currently provides, and what would it free the founder from?

6. **Dissolving the seasonal fluctuation problem entirely**: What if instead of managing cash flow through seasonal swings, the business was structured so that seasonal variation became its primary generator of meaning — where winter scarcity and summer abundance were features of the brand story rather than financial problems to smooth out? What new revenue architecture would make that possible?

---

## The Cuisine as Paradigm Shift

7. **Filipino-Mexican fusion as a new cultural form**: What if lumpia tacos and adobo burritos weren't fusion food — a genre with decades of precedent — but the seed of an entirely new culinary culture that doesn't yet have a name, a community, or an institutional home? What would the founder need to build (beyond a menu and a truck) to make that culture real, and what would "scaling" even mean in that frame?
