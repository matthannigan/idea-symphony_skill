# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Discovery: Peak Moments and Existing Strengths

1. **Culinary identity as a competitive advantage**: When you have prepared Filipino-Mexican fusion food for others — at family gatherings, pop-ups, or informal settings — what moments made it clear that this cuisine could create something special? What was it about those moments that made the food resonate so deeply with people?

2. **The founder's hidden assets**: Beyond culinary skill, what knowledge, relationships, or experiences from your life and career are already in place that most food truck founders would have to spend years building? What do you already know how to do that you might be taking for granted?

3. **The city's food truck scene as resource, not obstacle**: Among the ~30 food trucks already operating in your city, which operators seem to have built something thriving and sustainable — and what do you observe them doing that distinguishes them from trucks that struggle? What could a respectful conversation with one of them reveal?

---

## Dream: Imagining the Thriving Future

4. **The three-year picture**: Imagine it is three years from now and this venture has become a model that other Filipino-Mexican fusion entrepreneurs are studying. You have a small fleet, a loyal following, and a clear path toward brick-and-mortar. What would a visitor to your city notice first about what you built — and what would regular customers say to their friends about why they keep coming back?

5. **A brand people feel belonging to**: If this food truck became something customers genuinely felt proud to be associated with — not just food they like, but a community touchstone — what would that look like? What stories would customers tell about discovering you, and how would those stories spread?

6. **The ordinance as an opening**: The city council is debating new ordinances that could restrict downtown vending. In the version of this story where that process goes well, what role does this founder play — and what does the outcome unlock for the entire food truck community, not just this one truck?

---

## Design: Structures That Reliably Create the Future

7. **Revenue rhythms that sustain rather than drain**: Given that catering, events, and daily vending each have different energy demands and cash-flow profiles, what combination of revenue streams would let the founder show up creatively every day without burning out — and what structures (booking systems, boundaries, relationships) would make that mix feel effortless rather than exhausting?

8. **Building the commissary kitchen relationship as a strength**: Many founders treat commissary kitchen requirements as a compliance burden. What would it look like to treat the commissary kitchen as a strategic asset — a place to build relationships with other food operators, share knowledge about suppliers and regulations, or even pilot catering partnerships? What structures would turn a regulatory requirement into a community advantage?

9. **Financial scaffolding that grows with the business**: The $60K in savings and potential SBA loan represent a real foundation. What financial practices — tracking methods, decision rules about reinvestment, criteria for when to hire — would ensure that the first year's hard-won revenue gets channeled into the growth infrastructure that makes year two and three genuinely easier rather than just bigger?

---

## Destiny: First Steps and Sustaining Momentum

10. **The relationship map already in reach**: Who in this founder's existing network — culinary instructors, neighbors, community members, other small business owners — could become the first fifty loyal customers, catering clients, or informal advisors? What is one conversation that could happen this week that would turn a connection into a genuine collaborator?
