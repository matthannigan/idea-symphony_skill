# Brainstorming Questions from The Questioner: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Hidden Assumptions About the Business Model

1. **Why fusion as the anchor**: Why do we assume a Filipino-Mexican fusion concept is a strategic differentiator rather than a positioning risk — and what evidence from the existing 4 similar fusion trucks in this market would change that assumption one way or the other?

2. **What "sustainable" actually means**: When the goal says "sustainable food truck business," what is the minimum viable definition of sustainability — covering costs month-to-month, paying a livable owner wage, building equity, or something else — and how does the answer change which decisions to make in year one?

3. **The solo founder constraint**: What if the real bottleneck to reaching a small fleet isn't capital or permits, but the founder's personal bandwidth, and what does the path to expansion look like if we design around that constraint from day one rather than treating it as something to hire away later?

4. **Why brick-and-mortar at all**: Have we interrogated why a brick-and-mortar location is in the three-year plan — is it a genuine business goal, a signal of success for funders or family, or an assumption that "real" restaurants are the endpoint of every food business?

---

## Regulatory Uncertainty as a Strategic Variable

5. **The ordinance as opportunity**: What if the pending city council ordinance that restricts downtown vending hours and adds licensing fees turns out to be a competitive filter that actually benefits an operator who can navigate it — and how would the business strategy differ if we treated the ordinance as an asset rather than a threat?

6. **Engaging versus waiting**: Why do we assume the right posture toward the city council debate is to monitor and adapt after the fact, rather than actively shape the outcome — and what would it cost in time, relationships, and credibility to participate in the process now?

7. **Commissary kitchen as leverage**: What if the required commissary kitchen relationship is not just a compliance cost but a platform for catering capacity, co-branding, or shared equipment access — what would it take to make that relationship strategically generative rather than merely obligatory?

---

## Capital Structure and Risk Tolerance

8. **What the $40K SBA loan is really financing**: If the SBA microloan were only approved for equipment (not working capital or marketing), how does the business model change, and does bootstrapping more conservatively produce a fundamentally different kind of business or just a slower version of the same one?

9. **The break-even question behind the break-even question**: What assumptions about daily cover count, average ticket size, and operating days per year is the break-even analysis built on — and which of those assumptions are most likely to be wrong in the first six months of actual operation?

10. **Seasonal cash flow as a design problem**: Rather than asking how to manage cash flow through seasonal fluctuations, what if we asked whether the revenue mix (daily vending, catering, events) could be designed from the start so that the truck's slow seasons are deliberately filled by higher-margin catering work — and what would have to be true for that to work?

---

## Revenue Diversification and Growth Path

11. **What catering and events actually require**: What capabilities, equipment, staffing, and lead time does catering work genuinely require that daily vending does not — and have we mapped whether those requirements conflict with or complement the single-truck operation before assuming catering is an easy revenue add?

12. **The fleet expansion logic**: Why do we assume that a second truck is the right next step rather than a ghost kitchen, a licensed product line, or a franchise-style agreement with another operator — and what is the theory of value that makes truck number two better than those alternatives?

---

## Identity, Market, and Customer Relationships

13. **Who the regulars will actually be**: What if the customer base for Filipino-Mexican fusion in this mid-size city is shallower than projected — not because the food is bad but because the concept requires customer education that a food truck format (fast, transactional, high-turnover) doesn't support well — and what would we do differently if we believed that?
