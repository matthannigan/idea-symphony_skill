# Topic Cluster 07: Customer Experience, Access & Equity

## Questions

32. **Unfamiliar cuisine and the first-transaction arc**: Filipino-Mexican fusion may be entirely unfamiliar to a large portion of the mid-size city population — what does the experience feel like for someone who has never eaten lumpia or adobo before, what information, framing, or staff behavior helps them feel welcome rather than out of their depth, and since the first visit (order, wait, handoff, first bite) sets the entire relationship, what emotional state do you want that person to leave in and what specific moments most risk producing the opposite (confusion, embarrassment, disappointment) — given that a fast, transactional, high-turnover food truck format may not support the customer education the concept requires?
33. **Economic, dietary, and mobility accessibility**: If the target customer is someone who regularly eats out, what income band does that assume and how does pricing account for neighbors or workers in lower-wage industries who might be nearby but priced out, what does the experience of waiting in a food truck line feel like for someone who is elderly, has a mobility limitation, is managing young children, or is on a strict lunch break — and which friction points could be mitigated through ordering design, shade, seating nearby, or signage without major capital — and what portion of the likely audience has dietary restrictions (vegetarian, halal, gluten-free) such that the absence of alternatives becomes a real barrier to repeat visits or catering bookings from organizations with diverse membership?
34. **Cultural legibility and honoring origins**: For Filipino and Mexican community members who recognize these dishes, how does the menu and brand honor the cultural origins rather than aestheticize them for a non-immigrant audience — what would it feel like to a Filipina grandmother or a Mexican family to see adobo burritos on the menu board, and what would make that feeling pride rather than erasure?
35. **Discovery channels beyond social media**: Someone who wants to try the truck for the first time has to know where it will be, when it will be there, and that it will actually be open — what are the realistic discovery channels for people who are not already active on social media, and how does the announcement strategy reach people who would love this food but would never check Instagram?
36. **The opening-day arc**: Imagine the first person who discovers your lumpia taco truck — they're walking past at lunch, curious about the smell, and they've never heard of Filipino-Mexican fusion. Walk through their entire experience from that first glance to sharing a photo with a friend: what moments along that journey are you designing intentionally, and which ones are you leaving to chance?
37. **The invisible-customer provocation**: What if the food truck deliberately targeted people who would never consciously seek it out — the person who has never heard of Filipino food, who finds "fusion" slightly suspicious, who eats at the same three places every week? What would the menu, the location strategy, and the brand identity look like if conversion of the indifferent was the primary design constraint rather than deepening loyalty among the already-enthusiastic? *The real insight: is the current concept a niche that converts or a niche that consolidates — and which of those is actually the right bet for a mid-size city with a growing but not yet saturated food truck scene?*
38. **The regular's story**: Picture a customer who becomes a loyal weekly visitor six months in — what does that relationship look like, what keeps drawing them back beyond the food itself, and how does your operation need to be structured to make that kind of loyalty feel mutual rather than transactional?

---

**Question count**: 7
**Cluster focus**: Structuring the vending experience, discovery channels, and menu so that first-time, recurring, economically diverse, dietary-constrained, culturally connected, and mobility-limited customers all find a welcoming path to the truck.
