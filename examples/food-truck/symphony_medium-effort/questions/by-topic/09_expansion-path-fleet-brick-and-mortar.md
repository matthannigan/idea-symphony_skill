# Topic Cluster 09: Expansion Path — Fleet & Brick-and-Mortar

## Questions

47. **Fleet expansion trigger metrics and the fixed-cost trap**: The plan targets growth to a small fleet within three years — what specific financial and operational thresholds (net margin percentage, monthly revenue run rate, operational efficiency metrics, waitlist demand, catering booking rate) should trigger the decision to acquire a second truck, at what minimum monthly net income from truck one sustained over how many months does the economics become defensible, what incremental fixed costs (second permit, commissary slot, hired operator labor) must the model absorb — and because fixed costs don't double linearly at expansion (some scale, some spike, creating a fixed-cost trap), at what revenue threshold does adding a second truck actually improve margins versus just adding complexity, and what criteria prevent the founder from throwing good money after bad by adding a second truck if the first never reaches consistent profitability (say, net margin below 12%) — especially given that a second truck is only one option alongside a ghost kitchen, a licensed product line, or a franchise-style agreement?
48. **Brick-and-mortar as trap versus destination**: Have we interrogated why a brick-and-mortar location is in the three-year plan — is it a genuine business goal, a signal of success for funders or family, or an assumption that "real" restaurants are the endpoint of every food business — and given that restaurant failure rates remain high, capital requirements dwarf food truck costs, and the transition carries build-out, equipment, and lease-deposit outlay on a higher fixed-cost base, what order-of-magnitude investment is required to open a small counter-service or fast-casual space in a mid-size city, what monthly revenue is needed to cover that base, does the truck's brand equity and catering revenue realistically support the transition within three years or does the timeline need to be extended, and is permanence actually the right end state before it becomes a sunk-cost commitment?
49. **Brick-and-mortar as a system change, not a system extension**: Opening a fixed location doesn't just add a revenue stream — it changes the nature of the operation entirely (fixed staff, rent obligation, no mobility to chase demand, year-round cost base regardless of season) — what are the second-order effects on the food truck operation itself when the founder's attention, capital, and energy are split between two operating models, and how would you know you're ready to absorb that complexity?
50. **The three-year dream picture**: Imagine it is three years from now and this venture has become a model that other Filipino-Mexican fusion entrepreneurs are studying. You have a small fleet, a loyal following, and a clear path toward brick-and-mortar. What would a visitor to your city notice first about what you built — and what would regular customers say to their friends about why they keep coming back?
51. **The anti-restaurant provocation**: What if the worst possible outcome for this business is that it succeeds so well it becomes a brick-and-mortar restaurant — and in doing so, destroys the exact thing that made it worth eating at? If permanence is the enemy of the food truck's soul, what would it mean to design a growth strategy that deliberately refuses to settle down? *The real insight: what virtues of impermanence — novelty, mobility, low overhead, street-level intimacy — get quietly sacrificed as the business "matures," and which of those are actually the competitive moat?*
52. **Proof-of-concept before scale**: The early franchise playbook — Ray Kroc didn't build 100 McDonald's locations until the San Bernardino original had proven its unit economics, kitchen choreography, and repeat-customer pull. Given that this business starts with one truck and $100K in capital, what are the minimum proof points (daily revenue threshold, food cost ratio, average transaction size, customer return rate) that should be demonstrably stable before a second truck or catering vertical is layered on — and how does that milestone discipline change the 3-year timeline?
53. **The brick-and-mortar that isn't**: Imagine the three-year goal of a brick-and-mortar location was replaced by something that provides permanence and scale without the fixed overhead — not a ghost kitchen, not a franchise, not a pop-up series, but something categorically different that doesn't yet exist. What would it need to provide emotionally and economically that a physical storefront currently provides, and what would it free the founder from?
54. **The inflection scene**: Fast-forward to the moment you decide to add a second truck or sign a lease on a brick-and-mortar space — what has to be true about the business for that decision to feel like a natural next step rather than a leap of faith, and what does the founder's life look like the week before and the week after that decision is made?
