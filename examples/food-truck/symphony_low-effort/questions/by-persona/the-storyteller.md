# Brainstorming Questions from The Storyteller: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## The First-Year Founder Experience

1. **The morning ritual**: Imagine it's six months in — what does a typical Tuesday look like for this solo founder, from commissary kitchen prep at 5 a.m. through the final cash count at night, and which moments in that day feel exhilarating versus quietly draining?

2. **The breakthrough customer**: Picture the first regular customer who becomes a vocal evangelist — someone who tells their office, posts on social media, and brings their family on weekends. What did they experience in that very first interaction that turned a transaction into a story they wanted to share?

---

## Navigating the Regulatory Landscape as a Human Story

3. **The city hall moment**: Walk through the scene where the founder attends a city council meeting about the proposed ordinance restricting downtown vending hours — who else is in that room, what does the founder say or choose not to say, and how does that single evening shape the business's relationship with local government for years to come?

4. **The association invitation**: Imagine the founder gets a call from the local food truck association inviting them to join. What would it feel and look like six months later to have built genuine relationships with the other 30 operators — including the four fusion competitors — in ways that made the whole ecosystem stronger rather than more cutthroat?

---

## The Filipino-Mexican Identity as a Competitive Story

5. **The dish that defines them**: Of all the menu items — lumpia tacos, adobo burritos, ube horchata — which one is most likely to become the thing customers describe to friends in a single excited sentence, and what is it about that dish's story (its origin, its surprise, its sensory experience) that makes it stick?

6. **The fusion skeptic turned convert**: Picture a customer who arrives doubtful ("Filipino-Mexican, really?") and leaves planning their return. What happened in the 90 seconds between ordering and first bite that changed their mind, and how can the founder design that experience intentionally rather than leaving it to chance?

---

## The Three-Year Arc

7. **The brick-and-mortar threshold**: Imagine it's three years from now and the founder is standing inside a small restaurant space they've just signed a lease on. What had to be true — financially, operationally, emotionally — for that moment to feel like a confident next chapter rather than an overextended risk, and what's the version of the story where the truck itself is still central to the brand even after the doors open?
