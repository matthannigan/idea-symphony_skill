# Brainstorming Questions from The Accountant: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Capital Structure and Launch Economics

1. **SBA loan vs. bootstrap tradeoff**: Given $60K in savings and a potential $40K SBA microloan, what does the debt service on a $40K loan at typical SBA microloan rates (~8–13%) cost per month, and at what average daily revenue does the truck break even before loan repayment versus after — and does that difference change the case for taking the loan at all?

2. **Truck acquisition cost structure**: When comparing buying a used food truck ($30–60K) versus leasing one ($1,500–3,000/month), what is the break-even holding period at which ownership becomes cheaper, and how does that timeline interact with the 3-year horizon to brick-and-mortar, where the truck may be an asset to sell or a liability to offload?

3. **Working capital reserve**: Beyond the truck and equipment, how much of the $60K–$100K starting capital needs to remain liquid as a cash operating buffer — covering commissary fees, food inventory, fuel, permits, and payroll during the weeks before revenue stabilizes — and what is the realistic minimum runway before the business must be self-funding?

---

## Unit Economics and Pricing Architecture

4. **Menu item contribution margins**: If lumpia tacos and adobo burritos are the anchor items, what food cost percentage is achievable for Filipino-Mexican fusion (ingredients like ube, adobo proteins, and lumpia wrappers may have higher unit costs than standard taco components), and what price point per item sustains a 28–32% food cost ratio while remaining competitive in a mid-size city market?

5. **Daily break-even transaction volume**: At an average ticket of $12–15 and a food cost ratio of 30%, what daily gross margin is generated per operating hour, and how many covers per service period does the truck need to clear fixed daily costs (commissary kitchen rental, truck depreciation or lease, fuel, permit amortization, and labor) before generating owner income?

6. **Catering vs. daily vending margin comparison**: Catering events typically command higher per-head pricing but carry setup costs, staffing overhead, and minimum quantities — what is the effective margin per dollar of revenue for a catering booking compared to a peak street-vending shift, and at what catering-to-vending revenue mix does the business's blended margin improve meaningfully?

---

## Seasonal Cash Flow and Revenue Diversification

7. **Seasonal revenue modeling**: In a mid-size city with cold winters, outdoor food truck revenue likely compresses significantly in Q1 and Q4 — what does a realistic 12-month revenue curve look like if peak summer months generate 2–3x low-season volumes, and what fixed cost obligations (loan payments, commissary minimums, insurance) persist through the trough months regardless of revenue?

8. **Event and festival economics as a bridge**: Food truck festivals and private events can anchor revenue during seasonal dips, but booth fees, event insurance riders, and staffing for long operating hours add costs — what is the net margin on a well-attended festival booking after all-in event costs, and how many such bookings per quarter are needed to stabilize cash flow through slow street-vending months?

---

## Regulatory Cost and Ordinance Risk

9. **Ordinance change cost sensitivity**: If the pending city ordinance restricts downtown vending hours and adds annual licensing fees, what is the estimated annual revenue impact of losing, say, two peak lunch-hour slots per week in the downtown zone, and how does that revenue loss compare to the cost of building catering and event bookings as an alternative channel to offset it?

---

## Expansion Path and Capital Allocation

10. **Staged expansion economics**: The 3-year path from one truck to fleet-plus-brick-and-mortar implies multiple capital deployment decisions — at what point does adding a second truck (est. $50–80K additional capital or financing) generate returns that outpace investing that same capital in catering infrastructure, a commissary kitchen ownership stake, or a brick-and-mortar down payment, and what financial milestones on Truck 1 should gate the decision to expand?
