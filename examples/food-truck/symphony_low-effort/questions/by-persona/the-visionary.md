# Brainstorming Questions from The Visionary: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Reimagining the Food Truck as a Living Entity

1. **The truck as organism, not vehicle**: What if the food truck weren't a fixed culinary offering that moves through space, but a living concept that continuously reshapes its identity based on where it lands — not just adapting the menu, but becoming a different cultural institution at each stop, something that has no current name?

2. **Disappearing as a growth strategy**: Imagine a world where expansion doesn't mean more trucks, more locations, more visibility — what if this business grew by becoming deliberately scarce, rare, and sought-after in a way that makes the brick-and-mortar concept irrelevant rather than a milestone?

---

## Reinventing the Relationship Between Food and Community

3. **The customer as co-creator of the business**: What if the path from single truck to multi-revenue operation wasn't designed by the founder at all, but emerged entirely from what customers collectively decided to fund, participate in, or request — and what kind of relationship structure would that require that doesn't currently exist in food service?

4. **Filipino-Mexican fusion as a portal, not a product**: What if ube horchata and adobo burritos weren't items on a menu but the opening of a genuinely new culinary category — not "fusion" (which is a known thing) but something with no existing label — and what would it take to name and own that category before anyone else does?

---

## Escaping the Regulatory Frame Entirely

5. **What if the ordinance debate is the wrong fight?**: Rather than engaging with city council on food truck licensing terms, what would it look like to make the regulatory category itself obsolete — to invent a model of mobile food that city governments have no existing framework to restrict because it doesn't map onto any prior concept?

---

## The Three-Year Vision as a Constraint to Dissolve

6. **Replacing the roadmap with emergence**: The plan envisions a linear path — one truck, then fleet, then brick-and-mortar. What if we completely scrapped the concept of a predetermined expansion path and instead asked: what does this business want to become based on what proves irresistible, and what structures need to exist to let that emergence happen without forcing a pre-written ending?

7. **What if "scaling" meant going deeper, not wider?**: Instead of adding trucks or locations, what would it mean to go radically deeper into one relationship — one neighborhood, one community institution, one cultural partnership — until the food truck becomes something that community couldn't imagine losing, regardless of its legal category or physical form?
