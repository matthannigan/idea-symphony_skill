# Brainstorming Questions from The Analyst: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Unit Economics and Break-Even Structure

1. **Food cost ratios by menu item**: Breaking this down by SKU — what is the actual food cost percentage for each item (lumpia tacos, adobo burritos, ube horchata), and which items carry the margin weight that would allow the business to survive a slow week?

2. **Daily break-even volume**: Given estimated ingredient costs, labor, commissary kitchen fees, fuel, and permit overhead, what is the minimum number of transactions per service day needed to cover variable costs — and at what point does a day become net-positive versus break-even?

3. **Revenue model decomposition**: The three revenue channels — daily vending, catering, and event bookings — have fundamentally different margin profiles and demand patterns. What does the contribution margin look like across each channel at realistic volume assumptions, and which channel should be prioritized first?

4. **Capital structure decision**: With $60K in savings and a potential $40K SBA microloan on the table, what are the quantified tradeoffs — monthly debt service impact on cash flow, interest cost over the loan term, and how much operating runway each scenario buys before the first break-even threshold?

---

## Cost Architecture and Equipment Decisions

5. **Lease vs. purchase cost-of-ownership**: For the food truck itself, what does the full cost-of-ownership calculation look like over a 3-year horizon — purchase price plus maintenance and depreciation versus lease payments plus exit flexibility — and at what revenue threshold does ownership become the more rational choice?

6. **Commissary kitchen as a fixed cost variable**: Commissary kitchen fees are a recurring cost tied to regulatory compliance rather than production volume. How does this fee scale (or not) with catering and event work, and does it represent a meaningful portion of fixed overhead that would change the break-even calculation?

7. **Seasonal cash flow modeling**: Food truck revenue in a mid-size city likely follows weather and event-calendar patterns. What does a realistic 12-month cash flow forecast look like with conservative, base, and optimistic assumptions — and how many months of operating reserves are needed to survive the two or three lowest-revenue months without drawing on the SBA loan principal?

---

## Competitive Positioning and Market Variables

8. **Competitive differentiation metrics**: There are 4 trucks with similar fusion concepts operating in a city of ~250K. Breaking this down analytically — what data points would best reveal whether the market is oversaturated in the fusion segment, and what variables (price point, location patterns, social following, event presence) most predict which competitors are winning?

9. **Pricing strategy and price elasticity**: How sensitive is the target customer to a $1–2 price increase per item, and what is the evidence from comparable food truck markets for the price range at which Filipino-Mexican fusion items optimize for both volume and margin?

---

## Regulatory Risk Quantification

10. **Ordinance change impact modeling**: The pending city ordinance restricting downtown vending hours and adding annual licensing fees is a named risk. What is the quantified revenue impact if downtown vending is restricted to non-lunch hours — expressed as percentage of projected weekly revenue — and how does that compare to the cost of engaging in the regulatory process (time, association dues, legal consultation)?

11. **Permit and zone dependency mapping**: The business currently depends on designated vending zones. What is the geographic and temporal concentration of vending locations relative to projected revenue, and how exposed is the model to disruption if one or two high-volume zones become unavailable?

---

## Expansion Pathway and Decision Triggers

12. **Fleet expansion decision criteria**: The goal is growth from one truck to a small fleet within 3 years. What are the specific financial thresholds — monthly net revenue, operating margin, cash reserves — that should trigger the decision to acquire a second truck, and how does adding a truck change the fixed-to-variable cost ratio?

13. **Brick-and-mortar transition analysis**: A brick-and-mortar location introduces a fundamentally different cost structure: rent, full-time staff, longer operating hours. At what point does the food truck operation generate enough proof-of-concept data (customer volume, repeat rate, catering revenue) to make the transition economically defensible rather than premature?
