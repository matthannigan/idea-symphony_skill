# Brainstorming Questions from The Devil's Advocate: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Fusion Concept Viability and Market Saturation

1. **Differentiation depth**: Filipino-Mexican fusion is a compelling pitch, but with four similar fusion trucks already operating in a city of 250K, what is the actual mechanism by which this concept earns a durable customer preference — and if the answer is "better execution," how does a solo founder with no prior business ownership sustain that edge through the operational chaos of year one?

2. **Cuisine legibility**: Lumpia tacos and adobo burritos sound inventive, but how confident are we that the target market — not just adventurous early adopters — can understand and trust this combination quickly enough to drive repeat visits at the volume needed to break even, and what happens to the concept if the "novelty" wears off before a loyal base is established?

3. **Competitor response**: Have we considered what the four existing fusion trucks will do once a fifth enters the market? If they respond with price cuts, menu mimicry, or aggressive positioning at the same vending zones, what is the plan for maintaining volume without eroding margins that are already thin?

---

## Capital Structure and Runway Risk

4. **SBA loan downside**: The $40K SBA microloan looks like a clean funding boost, but if revenue underperforms in months three through six — a realistic scenario for any new food truck — what does debt service look like against actual projected cash flow, and is there a threshold where the loan accelerates failure rather than enabling growth?

5. **Bootstrap optionality**: One potential challenge with pursuing the SBA loan is that it commits the founder to a repayment schedule before the business model is validated. How would the expansion timeline and decision-making calculus change if the founder chose to launch on $60K alone and treated profitability on the first truck as the only gate to the next phase?

6. **Equipment financing trap**: Leasing a truck preserves capital but creates a fixed monthly obligation that doesn't flex with revenue; buying ties up capital but eliminates that obligation. Have we mapped out what happens to unit economics under a 30% revenue shortfall scenario for each option — because the right answer may depend entirely on which failure mode is more survivable?

---

## Regulatory Exposure and Dependency

7. **Ordinance timing risk**: The plan assumes the founder can build a viable business while the city council debates new vending restrictions — but if restrictive ordinances pass in month four, forcing shorter downtown operating windows and higher annual fees, how much of the projected revenue model depends on access to exactly the locations that may be curtailed, and what is the contingency?

8. **Regulatory engagement as a strategy**: Building relationships with city officials and the food truck association sounds prudent, but a solo founder with no prior business experience is unlikely to have meaningful political capital in year one. What is the realistic leverage available to a single new operator, and might investing that time and energy in customer acquisition actually produce more durable protection than advocacy?

---

## Solo Founder Operational Limits

9. **Single-point-of-failure risk**: A food truck run by a solo founder is, structurally, a business that stops generating revenue whenever the founder is sick, injured, or burned out. Have we considered how long this operation can sustain a one-person model before the physical and cognitive load of daily service, sourcing, bookkeeping, and catering coordination begins compressing both quality and the owner's ability to make good strategic decisions?

10. **Expansion sequencing assumptions**: The three-year path from single truck to fleet to brick-and-mortar assumes each phase funds the next — but what is the honest failure condition at each stage? If the first truck achieves only modest profitability, does the founder have the capital, credit, and operational bandwidth to add a second truck without the first truck's quality degrading in the process?
