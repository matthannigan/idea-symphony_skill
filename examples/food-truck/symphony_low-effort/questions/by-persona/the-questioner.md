# Brainstorming Questions from The Questioner: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## The Assumption Beneath the Plan

1. **Why 3 years?**: The goal names a 3-year horizon for fleet expansion or brick-and-mortar — what assumption about cash generation rate, personal risk tolerance, or market timing is baked into that number, and what would change if the honest answer were 5 years or 18 months?

2. **What's the real goal here?**: Is the aim to build a business that eventually runs without the founder, or to build a livelihood that keeps a culinary artist doing the work they love — and how differently would the expansion path look if those two answers were made explicit from the start?

3. **Why assume expansion at all?**: What if the single-truck operation, optimized and stabilized, produced better risk-adjusted returns than a fleet or brick-and-mortar ever would — what evidence would need to be true for that to be the right answer?

---

## Capital Structure and the SBA Decision

4. **What does the loan actually buy?**: If the $40K SBA microloan were declined tomorrow, which specific startup activities would be deferred, scaled back, or eliminated — and does that list reveal what the loan is really for, as opposed to what the application says it's for?

5. **What if the loan comes with strings that matter?**: Beyond the interest rate, SBA microloans often carry reporting requirements, use restrictions, and lender relationships — have we considered how those obligations interact with the operational flexibility a solo founder in a volatile, regulation-shifting environment will actually need?

6. **What does "conservative bootstrap" really mean in practice?**: If $60K is the floor and the loan is declined, what menu item gets cut, what location gets skipped, what marketing doesn't happen — and is that a strategic choice or just a funding gap in disguise?

---

## Identity, Differentiation, and the Competitive Field

7. **Why would a customer choose this truck over the four other fusion concepts?**: Not in marketing language, but in a concrete Tuesday-at-lunch scenario — what is the one thing this truck does or is that the others cannot easily copy in six months, and how durable is that advantage really?

8. **What assumptions are hidden in the term "Filipino-Mexican fusion"?**: Have we considered whether the target customer already knows and loves both cuisines, is curious about one of them, or has no prior frame for either — and does the menu design, price point, and location strategy actually match that customer's starting knowledge?

9. **What if the fusion concept is ahead of the market?**: If local appetite for Filipino-Mexican fusion is thinner than expected in year one, what does the truck do — pivot, educate, or wait — and has that contingency been named and budgeted for?

---

## The Regulatory Environment as a Variable, Not a Constraint

10. **What if the ordinance passes in its worst form?**: The pending restrictions on downtown vending hours and increased licensing fees are treated as background context — but what is the actual financial impact if the worst-case ordinance passes on day 90 of operations, and does the business model survive that scenario?

11. **Why do we assume the regulatory process is something to react to rather than shape?**: The REQUEST notes the city council is actively debating — what would it look like for this founder to show up as a stakeholder in that process rather than a subject of it, and what does joining the local food truck association actually unlock versus just being a networking gesture?

---

## Revenue Mix and the Risk of the Calendar

12. **What does the revenue calendar actually look like?**: Catering, events, and daily vending each have different booking cycles, lead times, and cancellation risks — what is the honest month-by-month revenue picture in year one, including the months where two of those three streams are silent?

13. **What if catering and events are a distraction in year one?**: Revenue diversification sounds prudent, but each channel requires its own sales effort, logistics, and relationship-building — have we asked whether pursuing all three in the first year dilutes the core operation before it has a stable identity and customer base?
