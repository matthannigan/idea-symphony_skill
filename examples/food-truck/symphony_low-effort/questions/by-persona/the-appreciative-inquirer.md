# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Discovery: Peak Moments and Existing Strengths

1. **Culinary-to-business crossover**: Think about a time when your culinary instincts — knowing what a dish needed, reading a kitchen's rhythm, improvising under pressure — translated directly into a smart business decision. What was happening, and what does that tell you about the strengths you're already bringing to this venture?

2. **Fusion as an asset**: Filipino-Mexican fusion is an unusually compelling pairing with real cultural and flavor logic behind it. When have you seen a dish or concept surprise people — convert a skeptic, spark a conversation, create a moment of genuine delight — and what was it about that experience that made the connection land?

3. **Community footholds already in place**: What relationships, networks, or goodwill do you already have in this city — with neighbors, food communities, potential regulars, or local institutions — that could serve as early anchors for the business even before the truck hits the street?

---

## Dream: A Future Built on Those Strengths

4. **The line that means something**: Imagine it's two years from now and there's a consistent Saturday lunch line at your truck — not just hungry customers, but people who feel like regulars, who bring friends, who tell a story about why they come back. What would those customers say when asked why this place matters to them, and what would you have built to make that true?

5. **The moment the fleet makes sense**: Picture the day you decide to add a second truck — not because the spreadsheet finally cleared a threshold, but because something felt undeniably right. What would you be seeing, feeling, and celebrating in that moment? What would have had to be true about the first truck for that confidence to be earned?

6. **Regulatory relationship as a resource**: The pending city ordinance debate is often framed as a threat, but imagine a version of this story where your early engagement with the process becomes an asset — where you're known as a constructive voice, where your relationships with city officials and the food truck association open doors. What would that version look like, and what would you have done early on to make it possible?

---

## Design: Structures That Would Reliably Create That Future

7. **Operations that scale your strengths**: Given that your culinary instinct and product quality are your clearest competitive advantages, what operational structures — staffing, prep systems, menu discipline — would protect and amplify those strengths as you grow from one truck to two or three, rather than diluting them?

8. **Revenue mix as resilience**: Catering and events can smooth the seasonal and regulatory volatility that daily vending can't control. What combination of revenue streams would let you lean into what you do best — the food, the experience, the story — while giving the business enough stability to invest in the next step?

---

## Destiny: First Steps and Sustained Momentum

9. **The smallest true test**: Before the truck launches, what is the leanest experiment you could run — a pop-up, a catered gathering, a farmers market slot — that would tell you something real about how customers respond to the food and the concept, and give you a genuine win to build from?

10. **Who already has your back**: You're a solo founder, but you don't have to navigate this alone. Who in your existing network — a mentor, a fellow food entrepreneur, a financial advisor, someone who's run a small business before — already has the knowledge or relationships that would accelerate your path, and what would it look like to bring them into this more deliberately?
