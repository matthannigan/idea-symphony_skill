# Brainstorming Questions from The Audience Advocate: Food Truck Startup and Expansion Strategy

---

## Who Is Actually Being Served — and Who Gets Left Out

1. **Visibility beyond the usual crowd**: The people most likely to discover the truck through Instagram or food-truck-finder apps are already plugged in. Who in this mid-size city *won't* find out about the truck through normal promotion channels — and what does that mean for who the customer base actually becomes?

2. **Language and menu accessibility**: Lumpia tacos and adobo burritos are exciting to people with some cultural familiarity, but for customers who encounter Filipino cuisine for the first time, what does the menu experience actually feel like — and how might the framing of dish names, descriptions, or signage either welcome or alienate them?

3. **Price point and economic access**: If break-even math pushes prices to $12–16 per item, what does that mean for the segment of the city that eats lunch on $8? How does a premium price signal interact with aspirations to serve a *community* food experience rather than a specialty-market one?

---

## The Emotional Journey of a First-Time Customer

4. **The approach experience**: Before anyone orders, they walk up to the truck, read the menu board, maybe wait in line. What are the moments of friction, confusion, or hesitation that happen in those 90 seconds — and what would make a first-time customer feel immediately welcomed rather than uncertain?

5. **The uncertainty of fusion**: People who love Mexican food may hesitate at lumpia; people who love Filipino food may feel the fusion "dilutes" it. What does it feel like to be a Filipino-American customer encountering your cultural food remixed — and how might that experience differ radically from the non-Filipino customer's delight at novelty?

6. **Repeat visit motivation**: What makes someone who enjoyed the truck once actually come back — and is that different from what makes them *tell someone else*? The word-of-mouth path is a human journey, not just a marketing funnel. What emotional moment does someone need to have in order to become an evangelist?

---

## Serving the Neighborhood, Not Just the Market

7. **Whose downtown is it**: City ordinances are debating downtown vending restrictions. The people most affected by where the truck *can't* go aren't just the owner — they're the workers in downtown buildings, the residents of nearby neighborhoods, and the communities who depend on accessible lunch options without a car. Who loses if vending hours shrink, and have their voices entered the regulatory conversation at all?

8. **Community anchor vs. passing attraction**: Many food trucks build followings by being *in* a neighborhood rather than just passing through. From the perspective of a neighborhood resident, what does it feel like when a food truck shows up consistently versus sporadically — and what would it take for this truck to feel like *theirs*?

9. **Commissary kitchen as community asset**: The required commissary kitchen use is framed as a regulatory compliance burden. But commissary spaces often sit in specific neighborhoods and employ people. Who are the people in that commissary ecosystem, and is there a relationship there worth cultivating rather than just a cost to manage?

---

## Catering and Events — Whose Celebrations Get Served

10. **Event catering and cultural fit**: Catering is listed as a revenue stream. From the perspective of a Filipino community organization planning a cultural event, or a family celebrating a quinceañera, does the fusion framing serve their needs — or does it create an awkward mismatch between what the business offers and what the community actually wants for a meaningful occasion?

11. **Whose events get access**: High-margin private events tend to skew toward clients with existing social capital — corporate lunches, wedding receptions, festival organizers. What would it look like to deliberately hold capacity for community events with smaller budgets, and what would that cost in revenue versus what it might build in loyalty and rootedness?

---

## The Founder's Story as a Community Experience

12. **The solo founder's visibility**: A solo founder with a culinary background who is building a Filipino-Mexican fusion concept carries a personal and cultural story. From the community's perspective, how much does that story matter to the experience — and what happens to the brand's sense of authenticity and connection if/when the founder is less present as the fleet expands?

13. **Who gives feedback and who doesn't**: Customers who love the food will tell you. Customers who had a confusing experience, felt the price wasn't worth it, or didn't understand the menu will mostly just not return. What intentional feedback mechanisms would surface the voices that otherwise go silent — especially from first-timers or lower-frequency visitors who represent the gap between the business's potential audience and its current regulars?
