# Brainstorming Questions from The Analyst: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## Demand and Utilization Economics

1. **Usage rate baseline**: Breaking this down by household type — what percentage of the ~2,000 nearby households are likely active borrowers in year one versus year three, and what borrowing frequency per household would be needed to justify the overhead of staffing, software, and insurance?

2. **Tool category demand distribution**: What does the likely demand curve look like across tool categories — power tools, hand tools, specialty equipment, seasonal items — and which 20% of the inventory is likely to generate 80% of the borrowing activity?

3. **Seasonal utilization patterns**: How do demand cycles for different tool types (garden tools in spring, heating/weatherization tools in fall) interact with volunteer availability, and what does that imply for inventory staging and storage allocation within a 500 sq ft space?

4. **Renter vs. homeowner borrowing profiles**: Given the neighborhood's mix of homeowners and renters, how do the tool-borrowing needs and use-case profiles of these two groups differ, and what does that imply for which tools to stock first versus defer?

---

## Cost Structure and Financial Sustainability

5. **Unit economics of tool replacement**: What is the expected useful life — in borrowing cycles — for high-demand tools like drills, circular saws, and ladders, and what annual replacement budget per tool category would keep the library solvent without relying on continuous grant funding?

6. **Break-even membership model**: If the library adopts a voluntary membership or sliding-scale fee structure, what membership price points and participation rates would cover recurring costs (insurance, software, consumables, storage), and at what point does charging risk deterring the most cost-sensitive members the program is meant to serve?

7. **Volunteer labor valuation and dependency risk**: The operation rests on 8-10 committed volunteers — what is the implicit hourly labor subsidy they provide, what happens to cost structure if volunteer availability drops by half, and which functions should be systematized or documented to reduce single-point-of-failure risk?

---

## Operational Systems and Decision Variables

8. **Inventory selection framework**: What criteria should govern the initial tool selection — replacement cost, borrowing frequency potential, safety complexity, storage footprint — and how should those criteria be weighted given the constraint of ~500 sq ft and a modest initial budget?

9. **Checkout friction and loss prevention trade-offs**: What are the critical decision points in designing the loan transaction process — ID requirement, deposit, loan duration, late fee structure — and how does each parameter affect both equity of access and rate of tool loss or damage?

10. **Condition assessment and maintenance triggers**: What systematic approach — intake checklist, periodic inspection schedule, damage threshold criteria — would allow volunteers with varying skill levels to consistently determine when a tool needs maintenance, repair, or retirement?

---

## Risk, Liability, and Governance Structure

11. **Liability exposure decomposition**: Breaking down the liability surface — tool malfunction, user injury, property damage caused by a borrowed tool — which risk categories are insurable at reasonable cost, which require operational mitigation (user instruction, safety signage, tool inspection), and which might require the nonprofit to exclude certain tool types entirely?

12. **Governance and decision-making structure**: What are the key decision categories the library will face repeatedly — new tool acquisitions, policy changes, member disputes, tool retirement — and what governance structure (volunteer committee, staff lead, advisory board) produces consistent, defensible decisions without creating bottleneck delays?

---

## Equity Access and Community Reach

13. **Barrier analysis by community segment**: Looking at potential barriers to participation — language, awareness, digital access for online reservations, ID requirements, transportation within the walking catchment — which barriers disproportionately affect which community segments, and what is the minimum design change that would meaningfully reduce each barrier?
