# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## Discovery: Moments When Community Sharing Has Worked Brilliantly

1. **Peak lending moment**: Think of a community resource — a library, a seed swap, a neighborhood mutual aid network — that exceeded everyone's expectations in its early days. What was happening in that community at the time, and what did the people running it do that made neighbors show up and keep coming back?

2. **Existing generosity in action**: This neighborhood already has ~2,000 households and a core group of eight to ten committed volunteers. When has this volunteer group — or this community more broadly — already demonstrated remarkable generosity or resourcefulness together? What strengths are already alive here that a tool library could amplify?

3. **Borrowers who become champions**: In thriving lending programs, some borrowers become the library's most passionate advocates. What does that transformation look like — what experience tips someone from casual user to committed steward — and how might a tool library be designed to create that tipping point intentionally?

---

## Dream: Imagining the Library at Its Best

4. **The three-year vision**: It's 2029, and visitors from three other neighborhoods have come to study your tool library as a model they want to replicate. What do they notice first when they walk in? What do longtime borrowers tell them made the difference? What does the volunteer team celebrate most?

5. **The renter's experience**: A renter in a third-floor apartment has never owned a drill. She borrows one for the first time, returns it, and tells her neighbor it was "the easiest thing I've ever done." Walk us through what that interaction looked like — the finding, the borrowing, the returning — and what made each step feel effortless and welcoming for someone who might have felt this wasn't for her?

6. **A tool that changed something**: Imagine a borrower who used a tool from the library to complete a project that genuinely changed their situation — a repair that made their apartment livable, a garden bed that fed their family. What is that story? What conditions did the library create to make that outcome possible?

---

## Design: Structures That Reliably Create Success

7. **The trust infrastructure**: Lending programs that last tend to have built something that goes beyond a checkout system — a culture of care and mutual accountability. Given what you know about what makes communities trustworthy with shared resources, what specific practices, rituals, or structures would you build into this library from day one to cultivate that culture?

8. **Volunteer energy that renews itself**: The founding team of eight to ten volunteers is a genuine asset. What kinds of roles, recognition, and community rituals would keep that core energized past the first year — and what structures would make it easy for new volunteers to step in naturally, so the library doesn't depend on any one person staying forever?

9. **Equitable access by design**: Some of the most celebrated community lending programs have found creative ways to reach people who might hesitate to walk in — renters who assume a tool library isn't for them, residents with language barriers, households working multiple jobs. What design choices — hours, language, outreach, intake process — would signal from the start that this library belongs to everyone within walking distance?

---

## Destiny: First Steps Grounded in What's Already Here

10. **The first 90 days as proof of concept**: The storefront, the volunteers, and the community are already in place. What could you do in the first 90 days — not to build the perfect library, but to create one genuine, memorable experience that proves the concept and gives the team something real to build on? What existing assets and relationships would you draw on to make that happen?
