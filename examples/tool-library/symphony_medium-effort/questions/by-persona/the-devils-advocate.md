# Brainstorming Questions from The Devil's Advocate: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## Sustainability After the Honeymoon Period

1. **Volunteer burnout cliff**: The founding 8-10 volunteers will carry enormous early load — have we considered what happens when even two or three burn out or move away, and what succession structures prevent the library from collapsing with them?

2. **Enthusiasm decay in lending demand**: Community enthusiasm tends to spike at launch and fade within 6-12 months — what evidence do we have that ongoing demand will justify ongoing operational costs, and how might we design the model so that low-utilization periods don't quietly drain the organization's reserves?

3. **Tool replacement funding gap**: Initial tool inventory will depreciate and break — have we mapped out what a realistic annual replacement budget looks like, and what happens if donations and grants don't materialize at the rate assumed in year one?

---

## Equity and Access Tensions

4. **Who actually uses it**: Community lending programs frequently end up serving the most organized and digitally connected residents, not the most underserved ones — how might we stress-test whether this library will genuinely reach renters, non-English speakers, and lower-income households, rather than primarily benefiting already-resourced homeowners?

5. **The liability waiver exclusion trap**: Requiring waivers, ID verification, or membership fees — even small ones — can be invisible barriers that systematically exclude the people the library is designed to serve; how do we design access policies that protect the organization without quietly becoming gatekeeping mechanisms?

6. **Hours and physical access**: A 500-square-foot storefront operated by volunteers will have limited and inconsistent hours — have we considered whether the operating schedule will actually accommodate the working hours of residents who most need affordable tool access, or whether it will default to times convenient for volunteers?

---

## Liability, Safety, and Damage Allocation

7. **The first serious incident**: One potential challenge is that a single injury from a borrowed tool — even if user error — could expose the organization to a lawsuit that exceeds its insurance coverage and effectively ends operations; how robustly have we thought through the liability architecture, and is a standard nonprofit general liability policy actually sufficient for power tools and ladders?

8. **Damage disputes and community trust**: When a borrower returns a damaged tool and disputes responsibility, the organization faces an impossible choice between absorbing the cost (unsustainable) or confronting a neighbor (relationship-destroying) — what adjudication process exists that is both fair and enforceable without eroding community goodwill?

---

## Operational Complexity and Scale

9. **Inventory management underestimated**: Have we considered that tracking 100+ items across constant check-ins and check-outs — with volunteer staff who rotate — is an inventory management challenge comparable to running a small retail operation, and whether the operational systems being planned are actually adequate for that complexity?

10. **The popular tool problem**: High-demand tools like circular saws or pressure washers will either be unavailable when needed (frustrating users) or require multiple units (expensive) — what demand-management mechanism exists that doesn't create a first-come-first-served system that, again, advantages the most organized residents?
