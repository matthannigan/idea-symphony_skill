# Brainstorming Questions from The Provocateur: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## What If Nobody Needed the Tools?

1. **The problem is tool scarcity — or is it?**: What if the real shortage in this neighborhood isn't tools at all, but the relationships that would normally produce tool-sharing on their own? What if building a library is the wrong answer to a loneliness problem wearing a hardware costume?

2. **Imagine the library succeeds so completely it destroys itself**: In ten years the tool library is beloved, oversubscribed, and culturally embedded — and because of that, the nonprofit can't shut it down even when the costs overwhelm everything else it does. What institutional trap is being set right now that will be invisible until it's too late to escape?

---

## What If You Ran It Backwards?

3. **Make it deliberately inconvenient**: What if borrowing a tool required you to first teach one skill to another member — not as a punishment, but as the actual point? The friction is the feature. What would a lending library look like if the act of getting a tool was inseparable from the act of giving something back, right then, in person? *The real insight: what if transaction costs are actually the community-building mechanism, and removing them removes the product?*

4. **What if the tools owned the library?**: Imagine every tool in the collection is tracked not by condition or location, but by the story of every person who used it. The drill has a biography. Borrowing the drill means inheriting its history and adding a chapter. What changes about how people treat tools, return them, or choose which one to borrow if the object carries social weight rather than replacement cost?

---

## What If the Constraints Were Inverted?

5. **What if you started with too many tools and had to get rid of most of them?**: Right now the library is imagining scarcity — which tools to acquire, how to fund replacements. Here's the opposite: a city surplus auction drops 800 tools in your lap tomorrow. You must choose 40 to keep. What criteria do you use? What does the forced curation reveal about what the library is actually for?

6. **What if liability was the whole point instead of the main obstacle?**: Every instinct says to minimize liability — waivers, insurance, restricted tool lists. But what if accepting liability openly, visibly, on behalf of the community was actually a trust signal? What if the library said "we will stand behind you when something goes wrong" rather than "sign here to confirm we won't"? What would that library look like, and what would it cost to run?

---

## What If the Community Was the Problem?

7. **What if the 8-10 volunteers are the ceiling, not the foundation?**: Here's something absurd but stay with me — what if the founding volunteer group's enthusiasm is the single biggest threat to the library's long-term survival? Their commitment creates invisible ownership; their norms become the library's culture; their availability becomes the assumed staffing model. What would you design differently on day one if you assumed every founding volunteer would be gone by year three and replaced by strangers who resent the inherited culture?
