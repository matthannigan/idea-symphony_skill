# Brainstorming Questions from The Storyteller: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## The First Visit: What Does Belonging Feel Like?

1. **The neighbor who almost didn't come in**: Imagine Maria, a renter who has lived in the neighborhood for three years but never quite felt like she belonged — she walks past the storefront on a Saturday morning, peers through the window, and sees a volunteer helping someone load a wheelbarrow. What would need to be true about that moment — the signage, the interaction she witnesses, the way the space is arranged — for her to decide to walk through the door and become a member?

2. **The awkward first transaction**: Walk through the full experience of someone borrowing a tool for the first time: they find the library, figure out how to become a member, locate the tool they need, sign it out, use it, and return it. At which moment in that journey is the experience most likely to feel confusing, embarrassing, or off-putting — and how would you redesign that moment so it becomes a small triumph instead?

---

## Sustaining the Story: When the Novelty Wears Off

3. **The six-month volunteer**: Picture David, one of your eight core volunteers, twelve months after opening. The initial excitement has faded, the same people keep requesting the same tools, and he's not sure the library is changing anything. What would need to happen — what visible evidence of community impact, what ritual or recognition, what evolving role — for David to feel that the work still means something and to keep showing up?

4. **The forgotten borrower**: Imagine a household that borrowed a power sander in month two, used it gratefully, returned it on time, and has never come back. What story do they tell themselves about why they haven't returned — busyness, not needing anything, not feeling fully welcome — and what would the tool library need to do differently to make a second visit feel as natural as going back to a neighborhood coffee shop?

---

## Equity in the Lived Experience

5. **The renter vs. the homeowner**: Set two scenes side by side: a longtime homeowner borrowing a post-hole digger for a weekend fence project, and a renter borrowing a drill for a single afternoon to hang shelves before a landlord inspection. How differently do these two people experience the tool library — in what they need, how long they need it, what they fear about returning something late — and what would a truly equitable design do to serve both without privileging one?

---

## The Crisis Scene: When Things Go Wrong

6. **The broken tool moment**: A borrower returns a circular saw with a cracked blade guard and says nothing. The next person to borrow it is an older neighbor who has never used a power tool before. Walk through what happens next — the discovery, the decision, the conversation — and ask: what policies, what relationships, and what culture would need to already be in place for this situation to be resolved without blame, without the library losing a member, and without anyone getting hurt?

---

## Legacy: The Story the Neighborhood Tells Later

7. **Five years forward**: Imagine that five years from now a local journalist is writing a short piece about the tool library as a quiet success story in neighborhood resilience. She interviews three people: the volunteer coordinator, a longtime member, and someone who moved into the neighborhood six months ago. What does each of them say was the most unexpected thing the library did for them — not the tools, but the human experience of it? What would the library need to have deliberately built, over those five years, to earn those three testimonials?
