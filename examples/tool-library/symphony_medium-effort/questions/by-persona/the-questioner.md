# Brainstorming Questions from The Questioner: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## What Are We Actually Solving For?

1. **The real goal**: Why do we assume the primary value of this library is tool access — could it be that the deeper need the community is expressing is trust, reciprocity, or a sense of shared ownership, and tools are merely the vehicle?

2. **Defining success**: What would it look like in three years for this library to be considered a genuine success by the households who use it least — and how is that different from success as seen by the most active borrowers?

3. **The sustainability assumption**: We call this a "sustainable" library, but sustainability for whom and on what terms — is it sustainable if 8 volunteers carry the full operational burden indefinitely, or does sustainability require the community to eventually own and run it without that founding cohort?

---

## Who Is This Really For, and Who Gets Left Out?

4. **Defining the community**: The REQUEST names ~2,000 households within walking distance — but who among those households is implicitly assumed as the "default user," and what design choices would look different if we centered the least-resourced households from the start rather than retrofitting equity later?

5. **The renter-homeowner gap**: What if the mix of homeowners and renters isn't just a demographic detail but a fundamental tension in tool selection — homeowners accumulate tools over time while renters often have neither storage nor long-term stake — and how does that tension reshape what a "useful" inventory actually means?

6. **Invisible barriers to access**: Beyond hours and location, what are the non-obvious reasons someone might never walk through the door — language, fear of judgment about skill level, distrust of institutions, not knowing what tools exist — and which of those barriers does the current plan address?

---

## What Assumptions Are Built Into "Lending"?

7. **The lending model itself**: Why do we assume a library model — where tools return to central stock — is the right structure, rather than a tool-share cooperative where ownership is distributed, or a repair cafe model that combines tools with skill transfer?

8. **Trust and accountability without bureaucracy**: What if requiring ID, deposits, or formal sign-ups to borrow tools functions as a filter that systematically excludes the people who most need access — and what alternative accountability mechanisms could work without those barriers?

9. **The condition problem**: Have we considered what happens to community relationships when a returned tool is damaged — who bears the cost, who makes the judgment call, and what process could handle that tension without corroding the trust the library is meant to build?

---

## How Does This Change the Neighborhood Over Time?

10. **Second-order effects**: What new behaviors, relationships, or community dynamics might emerge from having a tool library that we haven't anticipated — and are any of those unintended outcomes worth designing toward deliberately?

11. **The volunteer ceiling**: The library depends on 8-10 committed volunteers, but what happens when that group shrinks, burns out, or ages out — is there a design for the library that doesn't require this specific cohort to remain engaged forever?

12. **Scope creep and mission drift**: What forces might pull this initiative away from its original purpose over time — toward serving different populations, adding services, or prioritizing revenue — and what governance structure could hold the mission steady against those pressures?

---

## What Would Make This Fail, and What Would Make It Thrive?

13. **The most likely failure mode**: If this library closes within two years, what is the most probable reason — and is that failure mode visible in the current plan, or does it only become apparent after launch?
