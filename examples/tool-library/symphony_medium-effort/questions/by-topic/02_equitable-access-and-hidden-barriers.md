# Topic Cluster 02: Equitable Access and Hidden Barriers

## Questions

1. **Barriers that don't look like barriers**: What non-obvious barriers — language, literacy, digital reservation systems, ID requirements, fear of judgment, distrust of institutions, transportation within the catchment, hours that default to volunteer convenience — would systematically exclude the residents who would benefit most (renters, hourly workers, non-English speakers, newcomers without established ties), and what is the minimum design change that would meaningfully reduce each before they harden into invisible gatekeeping?
2. **The scheduling gap**: How might the library's hours, reservation system, and pickup/return process be designed around the schedules of residents with the *least* flexibility — shift workers, two-job households, caregivers — rather than around the times most convenient for volunteers, given that a 500-square-foot storefront with limited hours will otherwise default to serving the time-flexible?
3. **Multilingual and low-literacy by design**: What does a genuinely multilingual and low-literacy-friendly experience look like at every touchpoint — signage, borrowing agreements, registration, intake conversations — so non-English speakers and residents uncomfortable with paperwork can use the library without needing a translator at their side?
