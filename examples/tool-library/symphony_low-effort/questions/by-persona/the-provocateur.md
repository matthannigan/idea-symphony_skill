# Brainstorming Questions from The Provocateur: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## Inverting the Premise

1. **The Library as the Problem**: What if the tool lending library itself became the source of neighborhood conflict — the thing that exposed latent tensions over who owns shared space, who gets to set rules, and whose needs define "community" — and what does that forced reckoning reveal about what a tool library actually needs to get right before it opens its doors?

2. **Tools That Refuse to Be Borrowed**: Imagine the library was designed so that the most powerful, useful tools were the hardest to access — wrapped in ritual, requiring sponsorship from existing members, demanding a demonstration of need — not as a bug but as a deliberate feature; what would that friction-as-gatekeeping model expose about who currently benefits from "easy access," and what alternative access logics might it unlock?

---

## Radical Reframes

3. **What If the Tools Were the Members**: Suppose you had to design the library as if the tools themselves were the stakeholders — each drill, ladder, and tile saw with its own usage history, wear patterns, and preference for certain kinds of projects — and the humans were just temporary custodians rather than owners; how would that inversion change what the organization tracks, values, and optimizes for, and what blind spots in the current caretaker model does that absurdity expose?
