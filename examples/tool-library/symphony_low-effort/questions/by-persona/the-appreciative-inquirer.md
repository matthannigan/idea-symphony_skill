# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## Discovery: Community Strengths and Peak Moments

1. **Borrowing from what already works**: When you think about informal tool-sharing that already happens in this neighborhood — the neighbor who lends a ladder, the friend who passes along a drill — what makes those moments feel easy and trustworthy, and how might a formal library be designed to preserve exactly that texture?

2. **Volunteer spark**: What drew the core group of 8–10 volunteers to this project, and what is it about this particular idea that ignited their commitment in a way other community projects may not have?

3. **Community connection at its best**: Can you recall a time when a neighborhood resource — a community garden, a mutual aid group, a block association — created an unexpected sense of belonging among people who wouldn't otherwise have met? What were the conditions that made that happen, and which of those conditions already exist here?

---

## Dream: Aspirational Futures

4. **Three years from now**: Imagine it's 2029 and this tool library has become something the neighborhood genuinely treasures — not just a utility, but a source of pride and connection. What would a first-time visitor notice walking through the door? What would longtime members say to their friends about what makes it special?

5. **The moment that made it real**: Picture a specific scene — a single household, a single Saturday — where borrowing from this library made something genuinely possible that wouldn't have been otherwise. Who are the people in that scene, what are they building or fixing, and what does it mean to them?

6. **A model others want to copy**: If organizations from other neighborhoods were visiting to learn what made this library work so well — what would be the two or three things you'd most want to show them? What are you most proud of?

---

## Design: Structures That Amplify Strengths

7. **Building on neighbor trust**: Given that informal tool-sharing in this community already operates on personal trust and reciprocity, what membership or borrowing structures would honor and extend that spirit rather than replacing it with bureaucracy?

8. **Welcoming the full neighborhood**: The neighborhood includes homeowners and renters, long-timers and newcomers, people with very different relationships to home repair and making. What specific design choices — in hours, language, physical layout, or borrowing process — would make every one of those groups feel genuinely included from day one?

9. **Sustaining the volunteers**: The 8–10 volunteers who are the heart of this project bring real skills and enthusiasm. What roles, rhythms, and recognition practices would help them stay energized and feel valued over the long haul — and how might the library be structured so that volunteer effort naturally grows the community rather than burning them out?

---

## Destiny: First Steps Rooted in Existing Assets

10. **Starting with what you have**: Looking honestly at what the volunteer group already brings — skills, relationships, neighborhood knowledge, donated space — what could be built or launched in the first 30 days that would demonstrate real value and invite the broader community in?
