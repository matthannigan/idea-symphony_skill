# Brainstorming Questions from The Devil's Advocate: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## Sustainability After the Launch Glow Fades

1. **Volunteer attrition**: The core group of 8–10 volunteers feels adequate at launch, but what happens when two or three burn out or move away in year two — have we built a recruitment and onboarding pipeline robust enough to survive that loss, or are we one resignation away from collapse?

2. **Founder dependency**: If the library's operations are currently held together by one or two highly motivated individuals, what specific structural safeguards (documented procedures, shared credentials, distributed responsibilities) exist to ensure the library survives their departure?

3. **Enthusiasm-to-habit gap**: Initial community excitement for a novel program is almost always higher than sustained participation — what concrete mechanisms will convert first-time borrowers into habitual users and active advocates rather than one-time curious visitors?

---

## Financial Fragility and True Costs

4. **Hidden cost accounting**: Tool libraries routinely underestimate the true cost of operations — replacement parts, sharpening, cleaning supplies, storage hardware, software subscriptions, insurance premiums — so what does a fully-loaded annual budget look like beyond the obvious tool purchase line, and is the nonprofit actually prepared to fund it?

5. **Revenue model stress test**: If the initial donor or grant funding dries up after 18 months, what fee structures, membership tiers, or earned-income strategies could sustain the library without pricing out the lower-income residents the program is meant to serve?

6. **Liability exposure**: One serious injury from a malfunctioning tool that was returned damaged and not caught during check-in could expose the nonprofit to a lawsuit that dwarfs its entire annual budget — what inspection protocols, liability waivers, and insurance coverage are actually in place, and have they been reviewed by legal counsel?

---

## Equity Gaps Hidden in Plain Sight

7. **Access without awareness**: Offering equitable access is not the same as achieving it — if the library's outreach relies primarily on English-language flyers and social media, which segments of the 2,000 households (renters, non-English speakers, elderly residents without smartphones) will effectively be excluded from day one, and how does the design account for them?

8. **The "wrong" tools problem**: A tool inventory selected by the volunteer committee reflects the committee's own project types and class assumptions — what process will surface the specific tools that renters, recent immigrants, or residents with non-Western DIY traditions actually need but might never think to request?

---

## Operational Failure Modes

9. **Damage attribution**: When a tool is returned broken or missing a component, the library needs a fair, consistent, and enforceable policy for assigning responsibility and recovering costs — what does that policy look like, and have we thought through how it will affect community trust and willingness to borrow when it is first invoked against a neighbor?

10. **Inventory sprawl vs. depth**: There is a temptation to accept any donated tool to grow the collection quickly, but a 500-square-foot space filled with low-quality duplicates and rarely-used specialty items is harder to manage and less useful than a curated inventory — what criteria govern what gets accepted, what gets refused, and what gets retired?
