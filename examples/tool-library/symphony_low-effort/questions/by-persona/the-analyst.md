# Brainstorming Questions from The Analyst: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## Resource Economics and Cost Structure

1. **Tool acquisition cost model**: Breaking this down by category — what is the realistic cost per tool across power tools, hand tools, and specialty equipment, and how does the acquisition mix shift when you factor in donated vs. purchased inventory and the replacement frequency each category demands?

2. **Break-even utilization threshold**: What minimum checkout rate per tool per month would the library need to achieve before the cost of maintaining, repairing, and eventually replacing that tool is offset by membership fees or other revenue — and which tool categories are likely to fall below that threshold, suggesting they should be deprioritized or priced differently?

3. **Volunteer labor as a hidden cost**: The library's 8–10 core volunteers represent real capacity, but volunteer hours are finite and subject to burnout; what is the implied annual labor value those volunteers are providing, and how does that figure change the picture of what the library can sustainably operate without paid support?

---

## Operational Systems and Failure Modes

4. **Checkout logistics and friction points**: What are the critical moments in the borrower experience — discovery, reservation, pickup, return, inspection — where process breakdowns are most likely to occur, and which of those failure points carries the greatest downstream cost (damaged tools, disputes, lost inventory)?

5. **Inventory health tracking**: What metrics and inspection cadence would allow the library to distinguish between normal wear-and-tear depreciation and damage attributable to a specific borrower, and how does the answer shape both the tool replacement budget and any deposit or accountability policy?

6. **Liability exposure decomposition**: The REQUEST flags liability as a key concern; decomposing this, what are the distinct risk categories the library faces — tool defect claims, borrower injury, property damage — and how does each category map to different mitigation levers (insurance, waivers, tool inspection protocols, training requirements)?

---

## Membership and Demand Modeling

7. **Demand segmentation within 2,000 households**: The neighborhood contains both homeowners and renters with likely different tool needs and borrowing patterns; what does the demand curve look like when you separate these groups, and how should that segmentation inform which tools the library stocks in year one versus year two?

8. **Membership fee elasticity**: Given a mix of income levels in an urban neighborhood, at what annual membership fee does pricing begin to meaningfully exclude lower-income households — and what is the quantitative trade-off between maximizing fee revenue and maximizing equitable access across the ~2,000 households in range?

---

## Sustainability and Organizational Capacity

9. **Revenue diversification by source**: What are the two or three revenue streams most compatible with a small nonprofit's administrative capacity — membership fees, grants, tool donation drives, fee-for-service workshops — and what is a realistic order-of-magnitude revenue estimate for each in year one, given the 500 sq ft space constraint and volunteer bandwidth?

10. **Volunteer sustainability model**: With 8–10 core volunteers, what is the maximum sustainable weekly operational load in hours before the library risks volunteer attrition — and how should operational decisions (hours of operation, checkout window length, inspection requirements) be calibrated to stay within that constraint?
