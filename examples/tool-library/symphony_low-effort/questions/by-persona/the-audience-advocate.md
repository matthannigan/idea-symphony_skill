# Brainstorming Questions from The Audience Advocate: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## Who Actually Shows Up — and Who Doesn't

1. **The absent borrower**: Which community members are least likely to walk through the door on opening day — and what specific barriers (language, work schedule, unfamiliarity with borrowing culture, distrust of institutions) would prevent someone who genuinely needs a tool from ever becoming a member?

2. **Renter vs. homeowner experience**: Given that the neighborhood mixes homeowners and renters, how might the tool library inadvertently signal that it was designed for homeowners (who do maintenance projects) rather than renters (who may face different repair needs or landlord restrictions), and how would you detect and correct that bias?

3. **The first visit emotional arc**: When a first-time borrower walks in — possibly unsure of the process, maybe embarrassed that they don't own a basic tool — what do they think, feel, and do in those first five minutes, and what would need to be true about the space, the signage, and the volunteer interaction to make them leave feeling capable rather than patronized?

---

## Access Beneath the Surface

4. **The hidden transportation barrier**: For community members who don't have a car and are borrowing something heavy or unwieldy — a tile saw, a pipe cutter, a ladder — what does the actual journey from the library to their home look like, and how could the library design around that reality rather than assume borrowers can simply carry things away?

5. **Literacy and language in practice**: Beyond translating a flyer, how would the library ensure that a member who is not comfortable with written English (or with reading in any language) can navigate borrowing, understand tool use instructions, and feel confident returning something that may have broken during use — without shame or confusion?

6. **Informal and undocumented community members**: What would it mean for the membership and ID requirements to be designed in a way that does not exclude neighbors who lack government-issued ID, a fixed address, or documentation, and how could the library build trust with those residents before asking for any identifying information?

---

## Dignity, Power, and Who Has a Voice

7. **Designed with vs. designed for**: Beyond a launch survey, what ongoing mechanisms would allow community members — especially those who are quieter in public meetings or uncomfortable in volunteer-run spaces — to shape the tool selection, the hours, the policies, and the culture of the library over time?

8. **The moment something goes wrong**: When a borrowed tool breaks during use, or a member is accused of damaging something, what does the experience feel like from the borrower's side — and how could the library's conflict resolution process protect the dignity and continued participation of someone who is already economically vulnerable?

---

## Meeting Diverse and Shifting Needs

9. **Seasonal and project-driven demand**: How might the library serve community members whose tool needs cluster around specific moments — a renter doing a minor repair before a lease inspection, a family preparing for a religious holiday gathering, a recent arrival setting up a new home — and what would it take to be genuinely useful in those time-sensitive situations rather than just available in theory?

10. **Tools that reflect the whole community**: Whose definition of "useful tools" is currently shaping the initial inventory, and how might the tool selection look different if it were informed by the specific household repair, gardening, food preservation, craft, and cultural practice needs of the actual demographic mix of the neighborhood's 2,000 households?
