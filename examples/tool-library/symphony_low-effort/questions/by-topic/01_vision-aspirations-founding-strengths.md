# Topic Cluster 01: Vision, Aspirations & Founding Strengths

## Questions

1. **Borrowing from what already works**: When you think about informal tool-sharing that already happens in this neighborhood — the neighbor who lends a ladder, the friend who passes along a drill — what makes those moments feel easy and trustworthy, and how might a formal library be designed to preserve exactly that texture?
2. **Three years from now**: Imagine it's 2029 and this tool library has become something the neighborhood genuinely treasures — not just a utility, but a source of pride and connection. What would a first-time visitor notice walking through the door? What would longtime members say to their friends about what makes it special?
3. **Volunteer spark and the model worth copying**: What drew the core group of 8–10 volunteers to this project with a level of commitment other community efforts may not have ignited, and if organizations from other neighborhoods came to learn what made this library work, what are the two or three things you'd most want to show them — the hidden assets and peak-moment practices you'd be proudest to point to?
4. **Starting with what you have**: Looking honestly at what the volunteer group already brings — skills, relationships, neighborhood knowledge, donated space — what could be built or launched in the first 30 days that would demonstrate real value and invite the broader community in?
5. **The Reluctant Donor**: Picture a long-time homeowner who has a garage full of tools they rarely use but feels uncertain about lending out something valuable to strangers — what story would need to unfold, what assurances or moments of trust would need to happen, for them to eventually not just donate a tool but become one of the library's most vocal advocates in the neighborhood?
6. **Tools as relationship catalysts**: What if the real unit exchanged in this library weren't tools but skill-time — where borrowing a drill press also means inheriting a 30-minute tutorial from the last person who used it, transforming every transaction into a layer of accumulated community knowledge that makes the tool more valuable with each lending cycle?

---

**Question count**: 6
**Cluster focus**: Surface the latent assets, peak moments, and aspirational futures that already exist in the neighborhood so the library is built on what works rather than on novelty alone.
