# Effort-Level Comparison: Community Tool Lending Library

**Topic:** A community tool lending library for a small neighborhood nonprofit (~2,000 households, 500 sq ft donated storefront, 8–10 volunteers, modest budget).
**Effort levels covered:** `min`, `low`, `medium`, `high` (all four present).
**Scope:** This comparison reads one `REQUEST.md` (the `high` copy), and all four `PLAN.md`, `QUESTIONS.md`, and `BRAINSTORM.md` files. Counts in §2 are sourced from directory listings of `questions/`, `responses/`, and `synthesis/`, with `PLAN.md` and `QUESTIONS.md` as fallbacks. `SUMMARIES.md` and `SYNTHESIS.md` were deliberately not read.
**Generated:** 2026-06-10.

---

## 1. Headline Finding

The core thesis survives intact across all four levels and actually *strengthens* as effort rises: **this is not a project about tools — it is a project about trust, relationships, and the social permission to ask for help, and the procedures that keep a tiny volunteer operation manageable are the same ones that quietly exclude the people it exists to serve.** Every level lands on access-over-friction, the donated storefront as a single point of failure, deferred tool replacement as the slow killer, and founder dependency / the post-launch cliff as the central sustainability risk.

What changes is not *whether* these insights appear but how confidently and radically they are held. `min` accepts the brief's "library" framing and optimizes within it. `low` introduces the first real disagreement (a two-lens debate). `medium` reframes the project ("relationships are the product"). `high` interrogates the premise itself — "the riskiest assumption in the current plan is the word 'library'" — and quantifies convergence across seven perspectives.

No `USER-QUESTIONS.md` is present in any run, so there is no user-supplied-question confound: each level discovered its material through its own generators.

## 4. Where Depth Scales with Effort

### 4.1. Surfacing dissent and tension

- **`min`** reads as one confident, coherent voice. It has a "Central Tensions" section, but the tensions are framed as the *project's* tensions (access vs. risk absorption), not as disagreement *between perspectives* — a single generic responder cannot disagree with itself.
- **`low`** introduces the first genuine dissent: two named lenses, a risk-oriented Devil's Advocate and a feasibility-oriented Pragmatist, work each cluster and visibly split — most sharply on "whether sliding-scale membership is a buildable path... or a quiet exclusion mechanism."
- **`medium`** adds texture with four lenses per cluster: dissents become "productive" and specific ("turnover-proofing documentation often goes unused," "a six-month volunteer's doubt may be an accurate read of a broken program").
- **`high`** quantifies convergence and minority positions: "Six of the seven perspectives romanticize dissolution... while one consistent counter-voice argues..."; "A minority insists trust has a floor." It is the only level that reports agreement as a fraction of N perspectives.

### 4.2. Deepening of the critique

Ranked by how radically each level questions the input brief:

1. **`high`** — questions the noun. "The riskiest assumption in the current plan is the word 'library'." Willing to conclude the project "should not be a library at all" (repair café, buying co-op, skills exchange).
2. **`medium`** — questions the framing. "This is not really a project about tools"; leaves open "whether a lending library is even the right vehicle."
3. **`low`** — questions the defaults but not the premise. Founding choices "quietly favor people like the founders"; the library *is* taken as the right vehicle.
4. **`min`** — accepts the framing entirely. Optimizes governance, access, operations, and launch *within* the assumption that a tool library is the right answer.

## 5. Productive Drift Between Levels

Where recommendations *sharpen* (not contradict) as effort rises.

**Membership / money.** `min`: tiered voluntary dues (Free / $5–10 / $20+) as a real revenue pillar. `low`: surfaces the dispute — sliding-scale is buildable self-sufficiency or a quiet exclusion mechanism. `medium`: *decouples* revenue from dues ("the fee that covers costs is roughly the fee that excludes the people the library is for"; charge institutions). `high`: fee structure is "an identity decision rather than a revenue one." *Direction: from pricing the member toward de-monetizing membership.*

**MVP / launch scope.** `min`: 20–30 items, 30-household invite-only pilot, gated exit criteria. `low`: same pilot, plus founding decisions labeled "provisional" with a review date. `medium`: a "$0 micro-pilot" and 3-week diagnostic *before buying inventory*. `high`: a "shelf-and-spreadsheet pilot" to validate the premise, willing to abandon the library form. *Direction: from staged buildout toward premise-testing before any buildout.*

**Inventory composition.** `min`: 20–30 items, demand-driven expansion. `low`: community audit at real touchpoints; rebalance toward renters if under 40%. `medium`: curated 40–80 high-leverage tools; "the constraint is the answer." `high`: ~150–200 tools ranked by cost-per-use, instrumented for invisible demand. *Direction: from minimal-and-safe toward validated-and-instrumented.*

**Liability / insurance.** `min`: names insurance as a *conspicuous absence* (no organizational form established). `low`: call the carrier pre-launch; seal the informal-loan gap. `medium`: written broker quotes, tier the catalog, exclude the most dangerous items. `high`: full audit + rider + attorney duty-of-care review; safety "engineered upstream." *Direction: from acknowledged gap toward engineered design problem.*

**Volunteer sustainability.** `min`: role rotation and recognition. `low`: a month-six planning exercise; a paid coordinator "simply relocates founder dependency." `medium`: assume founders gone by year three; make the labor subsidy a budget line. `high`: design for the depleted "month-eight" core (40–60% attrition). *Direction: from retention tactics toward designing for guaranteed departure.*

## 6. Fidelity Check — What Gets Lost at Lower Levels

Insights carried only at higher levels (the counterpoint to §3):

- **Premise validation.** The willingness to ask whether a tool library is the right vehicle at all appears only at `medium` (raised, left open) and `high` (made the central next step). `min` and `low` never question it.
- **The reframe to connection-as-product.** "Relationships are the product; tools are the medium" is absent below `medium`. `min`/`low` treat relationships as a *means* to a working lending operation, not as the operation's actual output.
- **Quantified, adversarial convergence.** Only `high` reports positions as fractions ("six of seven," "a minority"). `low` and `medium` show disagreement but cannot say how widely a view is shared; `min` shows none.
- **The synchronized replacement cliff.** `min`/`low` treat replacement as a steady reserve line; only `medium`/`high` model it as a concentrated spike (year 3–5) when a launch-era cohort of tools dies together.
- **Two-lane service design.** The explicit renter-lane-first / homeowner-lane architecture is a `medium` contribution (cluster 3), sharpened at `high`; `min`/`low` treat renters as an outreach target rather than a distinct service design.
- **"Whose norms govern."** The structural point that a homogeneous volunteer core authors exclusion and that the fix is *changing who writes the rules* (not better wording) is a distinct `high` cluster (10) with no real equivalent below.
- **Premise-level metric conflict.** The tools-per-hour vs. relationships-formed metric tension is named only at `high` (and gestured at in `medium`); `min`'s metrics chapter is about adoption-vs-novelty, a narrower concern.

Conversely, the most concrete, immediately actionable operational specifics (exact dollar thresholds, zone dimensions, a fixed 9-hour schedule) are *most crisply stated at `min`*, because a single voice unconstrained by debate commits to numbers freely. Higher levels sometimes trade that crispness for "decide this on purpose."

## 7. When Each Level Earns Its Cost

**`min`.** Best for a fast, decisive first pass when the framing is already trusted and the user wants a clean, committed checklist. Its single generic voice produces the most concretely prescriptive output (specific thresholds, zones, schedules) with no hedging. Characteristic weakness: it cannot disagree with itself, so it accepts the brief's premise wholesale and its "tensions" are described rather than dramatized — blind spots (insurance, legal form, undocumented-resident outreach) surface only as a list of absences.

**`low`.** Best when the user wants a reality check — one credible advocate and one credible skeptic. The two-lens structure earns its cost by surfacing the first genuine fork (fees vs. reach) and by separating reversible from irreversible decisions. Characteristic weakness: with only two lenses, dissent is binary; nuance ("connecting" vs. "deterring" friction, two-lane service) and premise-questioning are still out of reach.

**`medium`.** Best when the user wants the project *reframed*, not just pressure-tested. Four lenses per cluster produce the pivotal "relationships are the product" reframe and resolutions that restructure the plan (decouple revenue from dues, design for departure). Characteristic weakness: it reframes the project but largely stops short of questioning whether the project should exist; convergence is visible but not quantified.

**`high`.** Best for a foundational, high-stakes decision where the premise itself deserves scrutiny and the user wants to see how widely each conclusion is shared. Seven lenses per cluster across 12 clusters let it question the noun "library," quantify convergence and minority dissent, and engineer issues (safety, replacement cliff) as design problems. Characteristic weakness: cost (84 responses) and a tendency to convert some firm recommendations into "decide this deliberately," which is more honest but less immediately executable than `min`'s crisp numbers.

## 8. Final Summary

- **Preservation:** All ten core through-lines (§3) — access-over-friction, storefront-as-single-point-of-failure, deferred replacement, founder dependency, start-small, trust-over-deposits, trusted intermediaries, success-masks-the-gap, make-the-invisible-visible, insurance-gap — survive intact from `min` to `high`. The skeleton is stable at every effort level.
- **Scaling:** Effort concentrates in Phase 3 (4 → 16 → 40 → 84 persona responses). Questions (19 → 70) and clusters (4 → 12) scale more modestly; next-step count is fixed at 15 throughout. Synthesis artefacts deepen from 1 doc/cluster (`min`/`low`) to 3 (`medium`/`high`, adding `_synthesis` and `attributed/`).
- **Divergences:** The productive drift zones are membership/money (price the member → de-monetize membership), launch scope (staged buildout → validate-before-building), inventory (minimal-safe → validated-instrumented), liability (acknowledged gap → engineered design problem), and volunteer sustainability (retention tactics → design-for-departure).
- **Irreplaceable high-effort insights:** Premise validation ("the word 'library'"), quantified adversarial convergence ("six of seven"), the synchronized year-3-to-5 replacement cliff, and the "whose norms govern" structural critique appear only at `high` and have no equivalent at lower levels. The connection-as-product reframe and two-lane service design are the irreplaceable `medium`+ contributions.
