# Brainstorming Questions from The Storyteller: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## The First Visit

1. **The reluctant newcomer's arc**: Imagine Rosa, a renter who's never owned a drill and feels vaguely embarrassed about it — she walks past the storefront three times before stepping in. What does she encounter in those first five minutes that either makes her feel like she belongs here or sends her back out the door, and what design choices create the difference between those two outcomes?

2. **The moment borrowing becomes community**: Picture the first time two neighbors — one a longtime homeowner, one a recent immigrant — find themselves reaching for the same tool at the same time. How does that moment unfold, and what about the library's physical layout, staff culture, or check-out ritual could transform an awkward collision into the beginning of a friendship?

---

## The Life of a Tool

3. **A wrench's biography**: Follow a single socket wrench through its first year in the library — from donation to intake inspection, to its tenth borrower, to the afternoon it comes back broken, to the volunteer's decision about whether it's worth repairing. What does that journey reveal about what the library actually values, and what stories does the tool carry that a hardware store's inventory never could?

4. **The tool that taught someone something**: Imagine Marcus, a first-time borrower, who checks out a tile saw he's never used before. Walk through his experience from the moment he picks it up to the moment he returns it — what instruction, peer knowledge, or embedded guidance could make that experience transformative rather than terrifying, and what does the library need to provide beyond the tool itself?

---

## Seasons and Sustainability

5. **The volunteer who almost quit**: Picture one of the eight founding volunteers — call her Diane — six months in, on a rainy Saturday when the storefront is quiet and the gratitude feels distant. What is she feeling, what almost drove her away, and what about the library's culture, recognition practices, or sense of shared mission could be the thing that keeps her showing up the following week?

6. **The two-year anniversary**: Fast-forward to the library's second birthday celebration. The founding enthusiasm has faded, two of the original volunteers have moved away, and the donated storefront lease is up for renewal. Whose story gets told at that anniversary — what kind of borrower journey, what kind of community transformation — that makes the landlord want to renew and a new wave of volunteers want to join?

---

## The Edges of Access

7. **The neighbor who never came in**: Imagine Hector, a homeowner two blocks away who knew about the library from the flyer but never used it — not because he didn't need tools, but because something about the experience felt like it wasn't for him. What was that something? What story did the library's outreach, signage, hours, or intake process tell him about who this place was really for, and what would it have taken to tell a different story?
