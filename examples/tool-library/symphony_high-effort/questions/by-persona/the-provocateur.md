# Brainstorming Questions from The Provocateur: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## What If the Whole Point Is Wrong

1. **Sharing tools is the product — but what if the tools are incidental?** What if we made the tool lending library deliberately bad at lending tools — long wait times, limited inventory, inconvenient hours — but exceptional at something else entirely, like spontaneous neighbor introductions? *The absurd version: tools are just the excuse. The insight: what if the library's real output is not tool access but the awkward, unexpected human collisions that happen when strangers need the same drill?*

2. **What if we removed all the tools and asked what was left?** Imagine the storefront emptied — no hammers, no ladders, no wheelbarrows. What remains that still has value? If the answer is "nothing," the library is just a hardware store proxy. If the answer is "community," then the tools are props. *The insight: what does the library provide that cannot be bought at Home Depot, and are you designing for that thing?*

---

## What If Failure Was the Feature

3. **What if the library was designed to fall apart in exactly five years?** Not neglected into collapse, but intentionally engineered to sunset — with the dissolution itself being the final community event, the moment when everything is given away, auctioned, or buried in the lot. *The absurd version: planned obsolescence for a neighborhood institution. The insight: what would you design differently if you knew the end date? What dependencies would you refuse to create?*

4. **What if losing a tool was celebrated, not lamented?** Imagine a culture where a borrowed drill that never comes back is framed as "adopted into a home that needed it" — a gift, not a loss. What would that do to procurement strategy, to inventory tracking, to the psychology of volunteers managing returns? *The insight: loss aversion shapes almost every policy decision in lending programs; what opens up if you design around acceptance of attrition instead?*

---

## What If the Constraints Were Inverted

5. **What if the library was staffed exclusively by people who have never used the tools?** Not as a failure state but as a deliberate design choice — tool novices running the counter, learning alongside borrowers, with no expert gatekeeping. *The absurd version: competence is disqualifying. The insight: what assumptions about authority, credibility, and trust are baked into the volunteer model, and which of them are actually necessary?*

6. **What if the 500 square feet were not in the storefront but distributed across forty front porches?** No central location — instead, each neighbor holds three to five tools and lists them in a shared ledger. The library exists as a network, not a room. *The insight: centralization solves some problems (findability, accountability) while creating others (access hours, volunteer dependency). Which of those trade-offs are load-bearing for this neighborhood, and which are just habit?*

---

## What If Success Was the Crisis

7. **Imagine the library succeeds so completely that every household within walking distance borrows something every week — and then ask what breaks.** Not "how do you scale" but "what becomes impossible at that volume that works fine at low volume?" Borrowing culture changes character as it becomes normal. What gets lost when this stops being special and starts being infrastructure? *The insight: the things that make an initiative feel meaningful at the start — scarcity, novelty, personal connection — are often destroyed by the success you are trying to achieve. Which of those things are you willing to sacrifice, and which must be protected even as the program grows?*
