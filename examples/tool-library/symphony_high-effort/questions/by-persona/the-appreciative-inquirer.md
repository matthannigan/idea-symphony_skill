# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## Discovery: Peak Moments and Existing Strengths

1. **Shared resource that sparked connection**: Think of a time you borrowed something from a neighbor — a ladder, a lawn mower, a mixing bowl — and that exchange turned into more than just getting the item. What made that interaction memorable, and what does it reveal about what a tool library could be at its best?

2. **Community already doing this well**: What informal tool-sharing or mutual aid practices already exist in this neighborhood — even small, unorganized ones — and what strengths do those practices demonstrate that a formal library could amplify rather than replace?

3. **Volunteer energy at its peak**: Recall a moment when this volunteer group worked together and something clicked — everyone was energized, the work felt meaningful, and the outcome exceeded expectations. What conditions made that possible, and how might those conditions be designed into how the tool library operates?

---

## Dream: Aspirational Futures Built on Real Strengths

4. **The scene three years from now**: Imagine it's three years from launch and the tool library has become something the whole neighborhood points to with pride. Visitors from other neighborhoods come to see how it works. What are they observing? What does the space feel like? What are people saying to each other?

5. **The member who almost didn't come**: Picture a renter who moved in six months ago, doesn't own much, and has never felt fully part of the neighborhood. She walks into the tool library for the first time. What experience would make her feel genuinely welcomed — not just accommodated — and what would she tell a friend about it afterward?

6. **Beyond tools, a place**: If the tool library became a gathering point where neighbors who would never otherwise meet found themselves side by side working on projects, what would that look like? What would need to be true about the space, the policies, and the culture for that to happen naturally?

---

## Design: Structures That Reliably Create the Future

7. **From the volunteer core outward**: Given that 8–10 committed volunteers are already here and clearly care about this community, what structures would let their energy and knowledge grow the library's capacity rather than sustain it alone — so that members gradually become contributors without being pushed?

8. **Policies that feel like welcome, not rules**: The most beloved community institutions have policies that feel like expressions of shared values rather than restrictions. Given what you know about this neighborhood's mix of homeowners and renters, what would a membership or borrowing policy look like if it were designed first to communicate trust and belonging, and only secondarily to manage logistics?

9. **The maintenance flywheel**: When a tool comes back worn or broken, that moment could feel like a problem — or it could be the seed of a new skill-sharing event, a repair café, a donor relationship. What practices would turn routine tool maintenance into ongoing community-building, so that keeping the inventory healthy also deepens participation?

---

## Destiny: First Steps and Commitments Grounded in What's Already Here

10. **The strength hiding in plain sight**: What does this neighborhood already have — relationships, spaces, skills, traditions, informal networks — that the tool library could immediately lean on, without waiting to build anything from scratch? What is the one asset most likely to be overlooked that could be the library's most powerful foundation?
