# Brainstorming Questions from The Empath: Community Tool Lending Library

---

## The Feeling of Asking vs. The Feeling of Belonging

1. **The shame of not owning**: When a renter walks in to borrow a drill, what does it feel like — relief, or a quiet reminder that they don't own things the way homeowners do? What emotional signals in the space, the sign-up process, or the language could accidentally reinforce that gap?

2. **The vulnerability of borrowing**: There's something exposed about borrowing — it announces what you can't afford or don't have. How do we design an experience where borrowing feels like savvy resourcefulness rather than quiet admission of lack?

3. **The unspoken social contract**: My gut says that when neighbors borrow from each other, there's an invisible ledger of obligation — and digital tool libraries inherit that anxiety even when they try to erase it. What does it feel like when that ledger feels balanced, and what happens emotionally when it tips?

---

## The Emotional Life of Volunteers

4. **Burnout dressed as dedication**: This feels like a project where the first six months run on pure excitement and then someone quietly starts resenting Tuesday evenings. What does the emotional arc of a volunteer look like, and what's the feeling we need to protect to keep people from drifting away without drama?

5. **The feeling of being taken for granted**: My instinct says the moment a volunteer stops feeling seen — not thanked, just genuinely *seen* — is the moment they start counting down to their exit. What rituals or rhythms give volunteers the feeling that their contribution actually matters, not just that the library functions because of it?

---

## Trust as an Emotional Texture

6. **What trust feels like in a physical space**: There's a difference between a space that feels like a library and one that feels like a room full of forms and warnings. What is the emotional texture we want someone to feel the first time they walk in — and are our liability policies subtly broadcasting distrust before the person even picks up a tool?

7. **The dread of being the one who broke it**: My gut says a significant portion of people will quietly avoid borrowing tools they're uncertain about — not because of the actual replacement cost, but because of the *feeling* of being the person who broke the community's thing. How do we design a culture where returning a damaged tool feels like honest participation rather than shameful failure?

---

## Who Feels Welcome Without Being Told They Are

8. **The belonging signal before the front door**: Something feels important about the moment before someone decides to walk in for the first time. What does that person feel — curiosity, skepticism, the sense that this is or isn't for someone like them? The storefront either radiates welcome or it doesn't, and it's doing that work before a single word is exchanged.

9. **Language as emotional gate-keeping**: My instinct says membership forms, borrowing agreements, and posted rules can quietly signal who this place was designed for. What emotional response do our policies and paperwork produce in someone who doesn't feel like a "default" community member — and what does it feel like when that paperwork is genuinely designed with them in mind?

---

## The Long Emotional Arc: Enthusiasm to Institution

10. **The feeling when the spark fades**: Every community project like this has a moment where it transitions from "exciting new thing" to "part of the neighborhood infrastructure" — and that transition can feel like loss even when it's actually success. How do we help founders and early volunteers feel proud of that maturation rather than abandoned by it?
