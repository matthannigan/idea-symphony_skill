# Topic Cluster 02: Equitable Access and Community Engagement

## Questions

6. **Reaching renters**: Since renters have fewer obvious reasons to borrow tools than homeowners, what programs, partnerships (with landlords, tenant associations, block clubs), or tool categories would make the library genuinely relevant to the renter half of the ~2,000 households?
7. **Language and cultural access**: In a diverse urban neighborhood, what would it take to make the borrowing process equally navigable for residents whose first language isn't English, including signage, intake forms, and volunteer communication norms?
8. **Reducing friction for first-time borrowers**: What are the most common barriers — paperwork, deposit requirements, hours of operation, fear of damaging something — that would prevent a low-income household from using the library even if they wanted to, and how can intake design remove those barriers before they become drop-off points?
9. **Community co-ownership**: Beyond using the library as a service, how could residents — especially those from historically underrepresented groups — be meaningfully involved in shaping which tools are stocked, how policies are set, and how conflicts are resolved?
10. **Programming beyond lending**: What community events, skill-share workshops, or repair cafés could the tool library host to build social connection and trust, and how do those programs reinforce rather than distract from the core lending mission?

---

**Question count**: 5
**Cluster focus**: How the library can genuinely serve a diverse neighborhood — including renters, non-English speakers, and lower-income households — rather than defaulting to whoever is most comfortable asking.
