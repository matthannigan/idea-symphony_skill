# Topic Cluster 06: Alternative and Hybrid Paths

## Questions

1. **The hybrid role audit**: Before concluding a full JD is necessary, have you genuinely mapped the landscape of intersection roles — medical-legal partnership coordinator, immigration paralegal with clinical oversight, patient navigator with legal case management, policy advocate in immigrant health, healthcare navigator at a refugee resettlement agency, medical advocate embedded in an immigration legal clinic, public health law positions? What specifically about those roles falls short of what you want, are they being evaluated against an honest read of what law practice actually involves, and what would a genuine "design from scratch" intersection role look like that combines what nursing gave you with new legal tools?
2. **Shorter credential paths and scope-of-practice math**: Paralegal certification ($2–10K, 6–18 months), Board of Immigration Appeals accreditation, certified DOJ accredited representative status, an MSW (~$30–60K for a two-year program), or a health law certificate can each support immigrant advocacy without a JD. How do scope, compensation, career ceiling, and impact-per-year compare across these alternatives over a 10-year horizon, and at what income level and debt load does the full JD investment actually pay off relative to the DOJ accreditation or MSW route?
3. **Travel nursing as a deliberate capital-building bridge**: Travel nursing contracts pay $80–130K+ annually with housing stipends. A strategic 12–18 months before law school could add $20–50K to savings, pay down the $22K loan balance, and improve the financial position entering school — at the cost of delaying the transition. How does the cash-flow math look, and what is the structural feasibility of maintaining per-diem or travel nursing during school given clinical scheduling, licensing, and 1L cognitive demands?
4. **A new category of advocate**: What if the very concept of a "public interest lawyer" is too narrow a frame — what if you were the first person to invent an entirely new role that doesn't yet exist, one that combines clinical triage expertise with legal standing in a way that changes how immigrants access the healthcare and legal systems simultaneously, rather than navigating them as two separate ordeals?
