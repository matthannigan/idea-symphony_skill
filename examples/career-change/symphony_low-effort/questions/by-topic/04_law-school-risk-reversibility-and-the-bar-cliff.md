# Topic Cluster 04: Law School Risk, Reversibility, and the Bar Cliff

## Questions

1. **Reversibility and option value at each stage**: How reversible is each decision at each stage — staying in nursing, taking the LSAT, enrolling, completing the JD — and at what point does the path become effectively irreversible, financially or professionally? What is the value of maintaining optionality versus committing early to reduce uncertainty costs, and how would that change whether to defer a year before applying?
2. **School rank and employment-outcome correlation**: In public interest immigration law, employer networks and clinical program quality vary enormously by school. What is your plan if your LSAT score limits you to lower-ranked regional programs whose graduates have demonstrably weaker placement rates in immigration practice — and would you still proceed under those conditions, or is there an LSAT-score floor below which the math no longer works?
3. **The bar passage cliff**: Bar passage rates hover around 50–60% on first attempt at many schools, and a failed bar adds months of limbo with no income and compounding debt. Have you stress-tested the plan against one or two failed attempts, and what is the recovery path if that happens?
4. **The law school first week**: Let me walk you through the opening week of 1L year. You're 33 years old in a classroom with 22-year-olds, $45K in savings beginning to drain, your nursing license sitting unused. Describe what you imagine that version of yourself feeling on the walk home after the first day of Contracts. What story are you telling yourself about who you are in that moment — and is that a story you can live inside for three years?
