# Brainstorming Questions from The Accountant: Career Change from ICU Nursing to Public Interest Law

---

## Total Cost of Transition

1. **All-in transition cost**: If law school costs $50–80K/year at a mid-tier public interest-friendly school (or $60–100K at a top-14), the three-year direct cost is roughly $150–240K in tuition and fees alone — before living expenses. With $45K in savings and $82K in foregone salary annually, the total economic outlay over three years (foregone income + living costs + tuition, net of savings) could reach $350–450K. What is your complete cost model for the transition, and which variables — school selectivity, in-state tuition eligibility, living arrangement, part-time work during school — have the biggest leverage on that number?

2. **Existing debt load entering law school**: You have $22K in nursing school loans today. After three years without income, that balance will grow with interest while you add law school debt on top. What is the realistic total debt load you would carry on day one of your first legal job — nursing loans, law school loans, and any accumulated interest — and does your financial model account for this compounding effect?

3. **Living cost structure during law school**: Your $45K in savings is a meaningful cushion but not a three-year runway at most cost-of-living levels. If your annual living expenses run $30–45K, savings alone cover 1–1.5 years. What is your actual monthly burn rate today, and how would it change in law school — and what's the plan for the gap between savings depletion and graduation?

---

## Lifetime Economics and Return on Investment

4. **Lifetime earnings comparison**: Public interest attorney salaries typically start at $50–70K at nonprofits and $60–80K at government agencies, versus your current $82K as an experienced ICU nurse — with nursing salaries often reaching $95–120K+ with experience, overtime, and travel nursing options. Across a 30-year remaining career, the lifetime earnings delta between these two tracks could easily exceed $500K–$1M before accounting for loan repayment. How have you modeled the total lifetime economic trade-off, including the years of foregone income during school?

5. **Break-even timeline**: At what age and salary level does the law career path break even with the nursing path in cumulative lifetime earnings — and does the math change materially if you factor in PSLF loan forgiveness, which could eliminate $150–200K or more in law school debt after 10 years of qualifying public interest work?

---

## Loan Repayment Programs and Debt Management

6. **PSLF and LRAP eligibility and value**: Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF) forgives remaining federal loan balances after 10 years of qualifying payments at a public or nonprofit employer. Law school loan repayment assistance programs (LRAPs) at many law schools offer additional grants or zero-interest loans for graduates in low-paying public interest roles. How confident are you in your ability to access both, and have you calculated what PSLF forgiveness would be worth in present-value terms given your likely debt load — because at $150K in law school loans on an income-driven repayment plan, the forgiven amount after 10 years could represent $100–180K in economic value?

7. **Income-driven repayment cash flow**: On an income-driven repayment plan (e.g., SAVE), a $60K public interest salary might generate monthly student loan payments in the $100–300 range, not the $1,500–2,000 a standard 10-year plan would require. What does your monthly cash flow actually look like at a $60K starting salary with IDR payments, after taxes, housing, and the $22K nursing loan balance — and is it livable in the cities where public interest immigration work is concentrated?

---

## Financial Stress and Cash Flow During School

8. **Part-time income during law school**: Many law schools restrict or prohibit outside employment during the 1L year, and the demands of a JD program make consistent outside income difficult. Some students earn $15–25K over their 2L and 3L summers through law firm internships or fellowships. How much of your law school funding gap are you counting on part-time or summer income to close, and how firm is that assumption?

9. **Financial stress and decision quality**: Financial pressure during a high-intensity academic program has real costs — on health, on studying, on early career decisions (accepting a higher-paying corporate job to manage debt rather than staying in public interest work). What is your honest threshold for financial stress, and how would carrying $200K+ in total debt change your career options or psychological headroom when you finish school?

---

## Alternative Path Economics

10. **Partial-transition cost structures**: The full JD path is not the only way to do immigration advocacy work. Paralegal certification costs $2–10K and 6–18 months; nonprofit program director roles, social work (MSW, ~$30–60K for a two-year program), and health policy advocacy are all adjacent paths. A certified Department of Justice (DOJ) accredited representative can practice immigration law without a JD through certain programs. What is the cost structure, salary ceiling, and scope-of-practice comparison across these alternatives, and at what income level and debt load does the full JD investment actually pay off relative to the DOJ accreditation or MSW route?

11. **Travel nursing as a financial bridge**: Travel nursing contracts typically pay $80–130K+ annually with housing stipends. A strategic 12–18 months of travel nursing before law school could add $20–50K to savings, pay down the $22K loan balance, and improve your financial position entering school — at the cost of delaying the transition. How does the cash-flow math look for using a high-earning nursing stint as a deliberate capital-building phase before the law school income gap begins?
