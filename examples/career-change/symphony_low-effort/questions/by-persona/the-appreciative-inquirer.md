# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Career Change from ICU Nursing to Public Interest Law

---

## The Strength Already in the Room

1. **Peak moment of advocacy**: Think back to a specific time in the ICU when you navigated a situation for an immigrant or refugee patient that went beyond clinical care — when did your presence as a nurse change what happened to that person in a way that a purely legal advocate could not have? What did you do, and what made it possible?

2. **What your nursing years built**: After eight years in a Level I trauma center, you have developed capabilities — reading a room, rapid triage, holding someone's fear while staying functionally calm — that most law students never acquire. Which of those capabilities have already shown up as assets in your volunteer refugee resettlement work, and what did that feel like?

3. **The volunteer evidence**: You spent two years working with a refugee resettlement organization while holding a demanding ICU position. That's a meaningful commitment under real conditions. What kept pulling you back, and what moments from that work felt most alive — when did you sense you were doing exactly what you were built to do?

---

## The Future Worth Building Toward

4. **The vivid ten-year picture**: Imagine it's ten years from now and you are exactly where you hoped to be — a public interest immigration attorney who is sought out and trusted by her clients in a way that is genuinely unusual. What does a Tuesday look like? What do your clients say about why they came to you specifically, and what does that tell you about the value you're creating?

5. **The integration prize**: What would it look like if your nursing background wasn't merely tolerated in an immigration law context but was actually decisive — the thing that made you a better attorney than someone who came straight through law school? What kinds of cases, relationships, or systemic changes would become possible because of that combination that would be harder to achieve without it?

6. **If the transition succeeded brilliantly**: If this career change went better than you dared to plan — if you found a way through the financial and educational challenge that left you stronger rather than depleted — what would you want to say was true about how you made that happen? What would you have trusted in yourself?

---

## The Conditions That Enable the Best Outcomes

7. **What sustainable advocacy requires**: You've described burnout as part of what's driving this change, but you've also stayed committed to vulnerable populations through all of it. When you've seen other people sustain long careers in demanding human-centered work — nursing, social work, advocacy — what seems to make the difference between burning out and burning brightly? Which of those conditions could you build into a legal career that you couldn't build into bedside nursing?

8. **Designing the financial bridge**: Rather than framing the financial challenge as a loss — three years of income gone — what would it look like to design those three years as an investment with a return you could calculate and feel good about? What assumptions would need to be true for that framing to hold, and which of those assumptions can you actually test before committing?

---

## The Hybrid Paths Worth Imagining

9. **The paths that combine rather than replace**: Some people who have made transitions like this discovered that the most valuable thing they could create wasn't a full career replacement but a new role at the intersection — health law, medical-legal partnerships, policy work for immigrant health equity. What would it mean to design a career that keeps the best of what nursing gave you while adding legal tools? What would you be gaining, and what would you be giving up, compared to a clean break?

10. **The first signal worth noticing**: If there's one small, concrete step you could take in the next month — not a commitment, just an exploration — that would generate real evidence about whether this path is right for you, what would it be? And if that step went well, what would it tell you that you don't currently know?
