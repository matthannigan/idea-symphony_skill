# Brainstorming Questions from The Storyteller: Career Change from ICU Nursing to Public Interest Law

---

## The Patient Who Stayed With You

1. **The face that changed everything**: Walk me through one specific patient encounter — the immigrant or refugee whose situation made you feel most powerless as a nurse. What were you watching happen that you couldn't fix with clinical care? And when you imagine yourself in that same room, three years from now, holding a law degree instead of a stethoscope — what changes, and what doesn't?

2. **The moment the calling crystallized**: There's a scene — maybe at the bedside, maybe during a volunteer shift with the refugee resettlement organization — where you first thought "I should become a lawyer." Paint that scene for me. What was happening around you, what did you feel in your body, and does that memory still carry the same force today when you revisit it, or has it faded?

---

## A Day in the Life, Five Years from Now

3. **The attorney's Tuesday**: Imagine it's a Tuesday, five years from now. You're a public interest immigration attorney. Walk me through your morning — the commute, the cases open on your desk, the client who calls before noon with a crisis. What does the emotional texture of that day feel like compared to a Tuesday in the ICU? Where do you feel relief, and where do you feel a new kind of weight?

4. **The client who reminds you why**: In this future scene, picture a client — a refugee family, a detained worker, someone at a deportation hearing — whose case you've spent weeks preparing. The moment the outcome is decided: what are you hoping to feel? Now sit with the alternative: what does it feel like in that scene if the outcome goes badly, and you've exhausted every legal option? Is that a kind of loss you can sustain differently than losing a patient?

---

## The In-Between Years

5. **The law school first week**: Let me walk you through the opening week of 1L year. You're 33 years old in a classroom with 22-year-olds, $45K in savings beginning to drain, your nursing license sitting unused. Describe what you imagine that version of yourself feeling on the walk home after the first day of Contracts. What story are you telling yourself about who you are in that moment — and is that a story you can live inside for three years?

---

## The Road Not Taken

6. **The hybrid path you haven't fully pictured**: Imagine a character — someone like you but who chose differently — who stayed in nursing and instead became a patient advocate, a health policy director, or a liaison embedded in an immigrant health clinic. Walk me through her workday five years from now. What does she have that the attorney version of you doesn't? What is she missing? When you look at her life, do you feel envy, relief, or something harder to name?

---

## The Story You'll Tell at 50

7. **The narrative that holds**: At 50, you're having dinner with someone younger who's facing the same crossroads. You've had nearly two decades of either continued nursing or public interest law behind you. What's the one-sentence version of the story you want to be able to tell — not about what you accomplished, but about who you became? And which path gives you a better chance of being able to tell it honestly?
