# Brainstorming Questions from The Storyteller: Career Change from ICU Nursing to Public Interest Law

---

## The Day You Walk In: Two Professions, Two Versions of You

1. **The first morning as a law student**: Imagine it is September of the year you start law school — you wake up, you are no longer going to the hospital, you are going to a classroom full of 22-year-olds with no clinical scars. What feelings, doubts, or unexpected discoveries would you expect to encounter in that first week, and how would those early experiences shape your conviction that you made the right call?

2. **The day that makes it worth it**: Picture yourself five years from now, sitting across from a family — perhaps a Congolese mother and her teenage daughter, navigating an asylum application — and you are the attorney in the room instead of the nurse in the hallway. What does that moment feel like, and what would have to be true about the path you took for you to feel that this version of yourself was worth becoming?

---

## The Patient Who Changed Your Mind — and What They Can't Tell You

3. **The immigrant patient you remember**: You have spent eight years watching immigrant patients in the ICU navigate impossible situations without legal support. Who is the one patient or family whose situation you carry with you most, and what does their story reveal about whether your impulse to become an immigration attorney is rooted in a specific, durable calling — or in the acute pain of witnessing helplessness you could not relieve?

4. **The gap you saw from the bedside**: When you stood at a bedside and watched a patient struggle because there was no attorney in the building, what exactly did you imagine an attorney could have done — and how confident are you that public interest immigration law, as it is actually practiced day-to-day, would let you fill that specific gap rather than a different one?

---

## The In-Between Years: Telling Your Story While Living It

5. **The story you will tell on law school applications**: Your nursing background is a story — eight years in a Level 1 trauma center, two years with a refugee resettlement organization — but law school admissions committees will read hundreds of "I want to help people" essays. How would you narrate the through-line of your life in a way that makes your application feel inevitable rather than impulsive, and what moments from your nursing years would you put at the center of that narrative?

6. **The financial story you will live through**: Imagine you are 34, eighteen months into law school, your savings have dropped from $45K to near zero, you are carrying new debt, and someone asks you at a holiday dinner why you left a stable $82K nursing career. What version of the story do you tell — and more importantly, what version do you need to genuinely believe — to keep going when the numbers feel abstract and the finish line feels very far away?

---

## After the Bar: The Career That Awaits

7. **The public interest attorney's actual Tuesday**: Forget the idealized version of the work. Imagine a completely ordinary Tuesday as a staff attorney at a nonprofit immigration legal services organization — the caseload pressure, the bureaucratic frustrations, the clients who cannot show up to their own hearings, the grant reporting due at 5pm. How does that ordinary Tuesday compare to your worst ordinary Tuesday as an ICU nurse, and what does the comparison tell you about whether you are running toward something or away from something?
