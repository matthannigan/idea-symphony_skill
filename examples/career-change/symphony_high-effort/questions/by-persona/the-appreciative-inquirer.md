# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Career Change from ICU Nursing to Public Interest Law

---

## Discovery: Existing Strengths and Peak Moments

1. **Peak moments of impact with patients**: Tell me about a specific time in the ICU when your presence — your particular skills, empathy, or knowledge — made a decisive difference for an immigrant or refugee patient navigating the healthcare system. What did you bring to that moment that a lawyer or social worker alone could not have brought, and what does that tell you about the unique assets you would carry into immigration law?

2. **Volunteering breakthroughs**: In your two years with the refugee resettlement organization, when did you feel most effective — when did you sense that you were doing exactly the work you were meant to do? What conditions made that moment possible, and which of those conditions might you be able to recreate as a public interest attorney?

3. **Transferable clinical credibility**: Nursing has given you the ability to read distress in a human being — physiological, emotional, and communicative — with precision that most lawyers never develop. When has that capacity for acute human observation served you powerfully in a non-clinical setting, and how might it become a durable competitive advantage in immigration casework?

---

## Dream: Aspiring Toward What Could Be

4. **The attorney you want to become**: Imagine it is five years from now and you are practicing immigration law and you feel, on most days, that you have found your vocation. What does a typical week look like? What kinds of cases are you handling, who are your clients, and what is the texture of your daily work? What do clients say to each other about you?

5. **A career that integrates both worlds**: If your nursing background and your legal training could combine into something that neither field currently offers its clients alone — a form of advocacy that no one else in your city does quite as well — what would that look like? What gap in the system would it fill, and who would be most grateful that it existed?

6. **The flourishing version of this transition**: Picture the version of this career change that goes exceptionally well: you finish law school energized rather than depleted, you land a position that excites you, and you look back at the transition with pride rather than regret. What would have had to be true about how you managed the years of training, the financial pressure, and the identity shift for that outcome to emerge?

---

## Design: Structures That Would Reliably Create That Future

7. **Building on the burnout insight**: You already know something many people never learn — exactly what kind of work depletes you. Given that hard-won self-knowledge about your limits from the ICU, what structures, boundaries, or working conditions would you want to deliberately build into a legal career so that the emotional sustainability problem does not simply follow you into the new field?

8. **Leveraging clinical networks as legal infrastructure**: You have eight years of relationships with trauma center colleagues, social workers, interpreters, and community health workers who all touch the same immigrant populations you want to serve legally. How could those professional relationships become a referral network, a credibility bridge, or a community of co-advocates that accelerates your effectiveness as a new attorney in ways that a lawyer without your background could not replicate?

9. **Financial design from a position of strength**: You have $45K in savings and an eight-year career with marketable skills — more runway than most career changers ever have. If you treated that financial position as a genuine asset rather than merely a buffer against risk, what strategic choices would it open up during law school — choice of school, geographic flexibility, part-time work options, clinical program selection — and which of those choices would most directly serve the future you are imagining?

---

## Destiny: First Steps and Sustaining Momentum

10. **The smallest credible experiment**: Before committing fully to law school, what is the smallest experiment you could run in the next six months that would give you real — not imagined — evidence about whether immigration law practice energizes you the way volunteering with the resettlement organization does? What would you learn from it, and what would a positive result look like?

11. **The community that already exists around this transition**: People have already made the leap from healthcare to public interest law, and some of them have written about it, organized around it, or are willing to talk. Who in that community would be most valuable to find in the next 90 days — not for permission or validation, but for concrete intelligence about what the path actually requires — and what specific questions would make that conversation worth your time and theirs?
