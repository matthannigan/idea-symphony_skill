# Brainstorming Questions from The Accountant: Career Change from ICU Nursing to Public Interest Law

---

## Law School Cost Structure and Runway Math

1. **Three-year cash flow gap**: Given $45K in savings, $22K in existing nursing school debt, and a current salary of $82K/year, what is the total capital shortfall to fund three years of law school — including tuition, living expenses, loan interest accrual, and the forgone salary — and which of those components is largest?

2. **Tuition tier trade-offs**: How does the choice of law school tier (top-14, regional, or lower-ranked with high scholarship) affect the total debt load at graduation, and what does that imply for which loan repayment assistance programs you qualify for and how long you remain dependent on them?

3. **Savings drawdown timeline**: If $45K in savings funds living expenses at a rate of roughly $2,500–3,000/month during law school (modest urban budget), how many months does that bridge before the savings run dry, and what financial mechanism — loans, part-time work, assistantships — covers the remaining runway?

4. **Opportunity cost of delayed entry**: Every year spent in law school is a year not compounding nursing salary increases and retirement contributions; what is the 10-year cumulative income gap between staying in ICU nursing (with realistic raises and overtime) versus completing law school and entering public interest law, and at what point — if ever — does the public interest law path catch up in lifetime earnings?

---

## Public Interest Salary and Debt Sustainability

5. **Entry-level public interest income reality**: Starting salaries at nonprofit immigration legal aid organizations typically run $50–70K in most metros, considerably below the current $82K nursing salary — what monthly budget would be sustainable on a $55K gross salary after taxes, mandatory bar dues, and loan payments, and does that budget cover actual cost of living in markets where immigration legal work is concentrated?

6. **PSLF and income-driven repayment math**: The Public Service Loan Forgiveness program forgives remaining federal loan balances after 10 years of qualifying payments on an income-driven repayment plan — given a projected $150–250K in total law school debt and an anticipated public interest salary, what would monthly payments look like under SAVE or IBR, and how much would actually be forgiven after 10 years versus paid down?

7. **LRAP coverage gaps**: Law school Loan Repayment Assistance Programs vary significantly — some cover only loans above a salary threshold, some cap reimbursement at $5–10K/year, and some require you to stay in qualifying employment — what are the actual net-benefit calculations for LRAPs at the specific schools you're considering, and under what salary and debt combinations does LRAP meaningfully change the financial picture versus PSLF alone?

---

## Hidden Costs and Transition Expenses

8. **LSAT preparation and application costs**: Before a single tuition dollar is spent, what is the realistic budget for LSAT preparation (courses, practice materials, retakes run $200–2,000), application fees across 8–12 schools ($75–100 each), and any pre-law coursework or credential evaluation — and is this a one-time sunk cost or a cost that scales with the number of application cycles needed?

9. **Bar exam costs and licensing timeline**: After graduation, the bar exam carries registration fees ($500–1,000+), prep course costs ($2,000–4,000), and potentially 2–6 months between graduation and results during which income is uncertain — what is the total cost and cash-flow exposure of this transitional period, and does it require a separate financial reserve beyond the law school runway fund?

10. **Existing debt interaction**: The $22K in nursing school loans is still accruing interest during law school unless deferred — under income-driven deferral, interest capitalizes and grows the principal; what does the nursing school debt look like at law school graduation under different deferral and payment scenarios, and how does it interact with the new law school debt in terms of total debt-to-income ratio on a public interest salary?

---

## Economic Alternatives and Hybrid Path Valuation

11. **Comparative economics of alternative paths**: Several hybrid paths exist — healthcare social work, health policy advocacy, paralegal work at an immigration nonprofit, or community health worker certification — that combine healthcare expertise with immigrant advocacy without the full law school cost structure; what is the income ceiling, debt requirement, and time-to-entry for the two or three most realistic alternatives, and how do those compare to the public interest law trajectory on a 5- and 10-year net present value basis?
