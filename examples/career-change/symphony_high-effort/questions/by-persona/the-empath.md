# Brainstorming Questions from The Empath: Career Change from ICU Nursing to Public Interest Law

---

## The Emotional Undercurrent of Burnout

1. **Burnout versus calling**: When you imagine yourself five years into immigration law, does the feeling in your body more closely resemble relief — or does it carry the same low-grade dread that has settled into your ICU shifts? What does your gut tell you about which emotion is driving this decision?

2. **The grief of leaving**: What feelings surface when you actually picture walking out of the ICU for the last time — not as a thought experiment, but as a real goodbye? Is there grief underneath the exhaustion, and what does that grief point to about what nursing has genuinely meant to you?

3. **Escape versus arrival**: My gut says there is a meaningful emotional difference between running away from a place that has ground you down and running toward something that lights you up. Which of those two feelings is louder right now, and does the answer change your read on what this decision is really about?

## The Felt Sense of Identity in Transition

4. **Who you are without the scrubs**: Nursing is a vocation that carries a deep identity — people feel it, not just do it. What emotions come up when you try to imagine introducing yourself as a law student, and not a nurse, for the first time? Does that feel like freedom, loss, or something harder to name?

5. **The immigrant patients you have already known**: You watched immigrant patients navigate impossible situations with no legal support at the bedside. What did it feel like to be the person present in that moment but unable to address the core problem? How much of this decision is being driven by that specific, repeated helplessness?

6. **Imposter energy in a new world**: Law school will place you alongside people who have never held a patient's hand through a terrifying procedure or communicated a death to a family in a second language. My instinct is that this could feel isolating before it feels empowering — what emotional resources would you need to trust your own competence in an environment that may not immediately recognize it?

## Emotional Sustainability and the Risk of Replicating Burnout

7. **Trading one high-stakes field for another**: Public interest immigration law is not a low-intensity career — it involves clients in genuine crisis, systemic obstruction, and chronic under-resourcing. What emotion do you feel when you honestly ask yourself whether you are drawn to the weight of this work, or hoping the new context will somehow make the weight feel different?

8. **The compassion fatigue question**: You have spent eight years absorbing the suffering of critically ill patients. Something in you is clearly built to hold that — but something in you is also depleted. What does your gut say about whether legal advocacy work will draw on the same emotional reserves, or tap something that still feels intact?

## What the People Around You Are Really Feeling

9. **Your family's fear versus your own**: Your family thinks this is risky, but my instinct is that risk is not actually what they are afraid of — they are afraid of watching you struggle, of not being able to help, of you being unhappy in a new way. How does it feel to hold their concern alongside your own desire, and what does the difference between those two feelings tell you?

10. **The immigrant community's unspoken reception**: You want to serve immigrants and refugees as a lawyer. My gut says that some of those communities will feel your nursing past as a profound form of credibility — you have been in the room at the worst moments. Does it feel important to you that that history be seen and recognized in your new work, or are you prepared for it to be invisible?
