# Topic Cluster 10: Decision Architecture — Tests, Reversibility, First Principles

## Questions

1. **The pre-commitment stress test and minimum-viable entry**: Before committing to a three-year, high-debt path, what is the minimum viable experiment that would give you real signal about fit — six months volunteering at an immigration legal clinic, shadowing a public interest attorney, a pro bono project with an immigration legal organization, an LSAT diagnostic, informational interviews with immigration attorneys, reviewing scholarship award patterns at specific schools — and in what order should those steps be sequenced to create the clearest early kill-or-proceed signal at lowest cost? If you were designing the fastest path to doing meaningful immigration legal work — not the most prestigious or credential-complete path — what would the first 12 months look like as a discovery mechanism, and what would you need to observe to feel genuinely confident this is the right move rather than a reactive decision?
2. **Reversibility, exit ramps, and the irreversibility test**: A first-principles decision analysis weights only the genuinely foreclosed options, not the ones that feel permanent but aren't — what is truly irreversible about choosing either path, and what is recoverable later? If the transition begins and proves wrong (law school isn't a fit, public interest salaries cause unsustainable financial stress), what are the meaningful exit points along the path (before applying, after 1L year, after bar passage), and what does each exit cost in time, money, and career trajectory?
3. **Hidden assumptions about law school as the entry point**: The path as stated begins with LSAT preparation and a 3-year JD, but what fundamental capability gap does law school actually address? What components of that gap could be acquired through supervised practice, certificate programs, or paralegal work at lower cost and time — and which cannot?
4. **Building structures from a position of strength to avoid second burnout**: You already know something many people never learn — exactly what kind of work depletes you. Given that hard-won self-knowledge from the ICU, what structures, boundaries, working conditions, school choice, geographic flexibility, part-time work options, and clinical-program selection would you want to deliberately build in so the emotional sustainability problem does not simply follow you, and which of those choices would most directly serve the future you are imagining?
5. **Peak moments of impact with patients and the volunteering breakthroughs**: Tell me about a specific time in the ICU when your particular skills, empathy, or knowledge made a decisive difference for an immigrant or refugee patient navigating the healthcare system — what did you bring that a lawyer or social worker alone could not, and what does that tell you about unique assets you would carry into immigration law? And in your two years of refugee resettlement volunteering, when did you feel most effective — when did you sense that you were doing exactly the work you were meant to do? What conditions made that moment possible, and which of those conditions might you be able to recreate as a public interest attorney?
6. **Make the transition deliberately harder, not easier**: What if instead of looking for pathways that smooth the transition — bridge programs, online law school, loan forgiveness — you designed the hardest possible version of this path on purpose? Full-time, top-14 law school, maximum debt, no shortcuts. What would that tell you about how committed you actually are — and is commitment even the right thing to be measuring, or is it a trap that leads people to confuse suffering with meaning? *The real insight: distinguishing genuine vocation from the cultural story that worthwhile things must be hard.*
7. **The constraint of emotional depletion as a filtering device**: Most people apply to law school chasing status or salary; you're applying after having already burned out in service of vulnerable populations — how could that scarce credibility signal (having already done the unglamorous frontline work and still choosing more of it) become the most compelling part of your application and your eventual client relationships?
