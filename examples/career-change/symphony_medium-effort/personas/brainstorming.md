## Per-Cluster Rationale

### Cluster 01 — Burnout as Diagnosis, Not Direction

**Topic type:** Strategic/Vision with strong Novel/Ambiguous notes — the questions are introspective and diagnostic ("push vs. pull," "signal vs. noise in the exit impulse," "differential diagnosis on your own exit impulse"). They don't map cleanly to a single domain.

**Selections:**
- **Devil's Advocate (core)** — Many questions explicitly invite stress-testing the "calling" hypothesis ("what if you are solving the wrong crisis entirely," "well-rationalized" calling). DA is the right voice to challenge motivated reasoning.
- **Pragmatist (core)** — Anchors the introspection in actionable evidence: what would it take to *settle* the diagnostic question before three years and six figures.
- **First Principles Thinker (Inner Ring — Strategic Good Fit, also valuable on Novel/Ambiguous):** Q1, Q5, Q6, and Q7 literally ask for first-principles deconstruction ("what does it mean to have actual leverage over outcomes," "differential diagnosis applied to your own exit impulse"). FPT's "why does this need to exist?" mode is a precise match.
- **Empath (Inner Ring — Human-Centered):** The cluster is about the user's own emotional architecture — burnout, peak-moment evidence, the "felt experience of a foreclosed future." Empath is the one persona that can sit with the gut-level texture of those questions rather than reasoning around them. (Audience Advocate would map stakeholders; here the stakeholder is the decider herself.)

**Cluster-family check:** Pragmatist + FPT are both Analytical Core (2 of 3 — within limit; not stacking the full triad on a non-technical topic per the guide's caution). Empath is Human-Centered. DA is Challenge. Diversity is healthy.

### Cluster 02 — Sustainability in the New Field

**Topic type:** Strategic/Vision (designing a sustainable 20-year career) with Implementation/Ops content (structural features, support structures, organizational norms).

**Selections:**
- **Devil's Advocate (core)** — Q4 explicitly asks "what if the burnout follows you" — the adversarial frame is built into the cluster.
- **Pragmatist (core)** — Tied directly to feasibility: what structural features actually protect rather than deplete.
- **Systems Thinker (Inner Ring — Strategic Strong Fit):** This cluster is fundamentally about feedback loops between client suffering, institutional dysfunction, and personal depletion. ST maps how the "burnout drivers" of one system (ICU) are or aren't structurally equivalent to another (immigration law) — exactly Q1 and Q4.
- **Constraint Flipper (Inner Ring — Implementation Strong Fit):** Q2 and Q4 ask what would have to be *built into* the new career to prevent burnout. CF reframes the chronic underfunding/caseload constraints as design parameters rather than fates.

**Cluster-family check:** Pragmatist (Analytical), Systems Thinker (External Perspectives), Constraint Flipper (Challenge), DA (Challenge). Two from Challenge family — both add distinct value (DA critiques the assumption of sustainability; CF reframes the constraints). Within the 3-per-family limit.

### Cluster 03 — Total Cost & Funding the JD

**Topic type:** Financial/Business — almost entirely about cash flow, debt, and runway math.

**Selections:**
- **Devil's Advocate (core)** — Q1 explicitly contrasts the "rosier version" with the realistic $150-250K debt scenario. DA challenges the optimistic plan.
- **Pragmatist (core)** — Listed as Good Fit for Financial topics; the cluster's core question is whether the financial picture survives contact with real numbers.
- **Accountant (Inner Ring — Financial Strong Fit):** This cluster is the textbook case for Accountant — actual sticker prices, COA modeling, hidden pre-law costs, runway calculations, ABA part-time work constraints. Anti-pattern risk is low because the dollar amounts ($150-250K) are genuinely material.
- **Simplifier (Inner Ring — Implementation/Ops Strong Fit, applied here as cost-stripping):** Surfaces the "what could be cut from the COA" angle (in-state vs. private, scholarships, lower COL city, summer income) that Accountant alone might leave implicit. Counterweights "model every cost" with "what's the minimum-viable financial plan."

**Cluster-family check:** Accountant (Specialized Lens — financial), Pragmatist (Analytical), Simplifier (Tension Pair), DA (Challenge). No family stacking concern.

### Cluster 04 — PSLF, Loan Repayment & Long-Term Financial Sustainability

**Topic type:** Financial/Business with strong Regulated/Compliance overlay — PSLF eligibility rules, REPAYE/SAVE/IBR statutory regimes, qualifying-employment definitions, and explicit legislative-risk questions.

**Selections:**
- **Devil's Advocate (core)** — Q1 directly asks "what is the plan if PSLF is reformed, capped, or eliminated"; DA is essential for stress-testing a load-bearing assumption.
- **Pragmatist (core)** — Listed as Good Fit for Regulated/Compliance and Financial; required to test what's actually livable on a public interest income.
- **Accountant (Inner Ring — Financial Strong Fit):** Compounding arc, IDR floor math over 20 years, retirement trajectory disruption — these are precisely the analyses Accountant exists for, and the dollar amounts are non-trivial.
- **Lawyer (Inner Ring — Regulated/Compliance Strong Fit):** PSLF eligibility, REPAYE/SAVE/IBR rule changes, qualifying-employer status, and forgiveness-as-taxable-income are *literally regulatory questions*. Anti-pattern risk (Lawyer over-citing on low-stakes topics) does not apply: the legal regime *is* the topic.

**Cluster-family check:** Accountant + Lawyer are both Outer Ring Specialized Lenses, but the guide's "no more than 3 from same family" rule treats Analytical Core + Specialized Lenses together — so we have Pragmatist + Accountant + Lawyer = 3 from that combined family. At the cap, not over. Justified because both lenses are independently load-bearing for this specific cluster (financial math + statutory risk are inseparable here). DA provides the off-family Challenge perspective.

### Cluster 05 — Nursing as Legal Asset — Skills, Credentials, Niche Risk

**Topic type:** Strategic/Vision with strong Novel/Ambiguous character — the "bilingual professional" framing, "credential as currency across systems," "boundary spanner," and "what clinical training makes possible in a courtroom" questions all explicitly invoke cross-domain inspiration. The guide's selection algorithm flags this exact case ("If the topic doesn't clearly map to a single established domain... select Analogist").

**Selections:**
- **Devil's Advocate (core)** — Q10 ("what if your nursing experience was actively disqualifying") and Q5 (gravitational anchor risk) literally invite adversarial framing.
- **Pragmatist (core)** — Anchors the speculative cross-domain mapping in "what's actually load-bearing in practice."
- **Analogist (Inner Ring — Novel/Ambiguous Strong Fit):** The cluster repeatedly reaches for analogies (bilingualism, load-bearing walls, boundary spanners, architecture). Analogist's job — importing concrete solutions/models from other fields — is exactly what Q6, Q7, and Q8 are asking for.
- **Connector (Inner Ring — Novel/Ambiguous Good Fit, also chosen as Tier-1 swap in Phase 2A for the same two-system reason):** Q3, Q4, and Q5 are about identifying structural parallels between clinical practice and legal representation. Connector maps those parallels at the systems level (where the dynamics rhyme), distinct from Analogist's "import this specific solution" mode.

**Cluster-family check:** Analogist + Connector are 2 of 3 in the External Perspectives Triad — within limits and complementary per the guide ("There's a natural flow: Systems Thinker maps the dynamics → Connector identifies parallel domains → Analogist imports solutions"). Pragmatist (Analytical) and DA (Challenge) round out family diversity.

**Topic type:** User-Facing/Community — the cluster is wholly about clients' dignity, voice, trust, and the texture of their journey through the system.

**Selections:**
- **Devil's Advocate (core)** — Critical for asking "is centering client dignity happening in practice or only in mission statement language" (Q1) and challenging design-for-the-easiest-to-help defaults.
- **Pragmatist (core)** — Tests what client-centering actually looks like operationally vs. as aspiration.
- **Audience Advocate (Inner Ring — User-Facing Strong Fit):** Q3 explicitly asks how to design practice for "people at the end of the access gap" rather than the easiest-to-help — pure stakeholder-needs mapping. Q4 ("are we designing this with the community or for the community?") is AA's central question.
- **Empath (Inner Ring — User-Facing Strong Fit):** Q1 ("what would it actually feel like to be a client encountering this new attorney for the first time"), Q2 (sitting with scope-of-practice gap as moral injury), and Q3 (the emotional journey of someone undocumented and desperate) require gut-level emotional resonance — Empath's mode.

**Cluster-family check:** Empath + Audience Advocate are 2 of 3 in the Human-Centered Triad — within limits and complementary (AA maps stakeholder segments; Empath operates on emotional resonance). Storyteller deferred to high effort. Pragmatist (Analytical) and DA (Challenge) round out.

**Selections:**

**Cluster-family check:** Analogist (External Perspectives) and Visionary (Forward-Looking) are different families. Pragmatist (Analytical) and DA (Challenge) round out. Diverse spread.

### Cluster 08 — Validation Steps Before Committing

**Topic type:** Implementation/Ops — minimum-viable tests, milestones, support-structure activation, "what specific information would have to be true before you hit go." This is operational decision-architecture.

**Selections:**
- **Devil's Advocate (core)** — Pressure-tests whether proposed validation steps would actually generate disconfirming evidence (vs. confirmation-bias signal).
- **Pragmatist (core)** — Implementation/Ops Good Fit; the cluster is essentially "what's the smallest meaningful test."
- **Momentum Builder (Inner Ring — Implementation Strong Fit):** Q1 (designing the next 6-18 months of progressively deeper legal-services exposure) and Q3 (activating the existing network) are progressive-elaboration questions — MB's "yes, and" mode for sequencing concrete next steps.
- **First Principles Thinker (Inner Ring — Novel/Ambiguous Good Fit, used here for go/no-go criteria):** Q2 ("what would the version of this plan that is definitively not crazy actually look like... what specific information, milestones, or test results would have to be true") asks for first-principles derivation of decision criteria — what must be true for "go" to be defensible. FPT excels at deriving criteria from fundamentals rather than convention.

**Cluster-family check:** Pragmatist + FPT are 2 of 3 Analytical Core (within limit; appropriate here because the cluster is genuinely analytical/criteria-defining). MB (Tension Pair) and DA (Challenge) provide off-family voices. No stacking violation.

**Topic type:** Strategic/Vision — explicitly long-horizon ("10-year trajectory rather than binary go/no-go," "what does the system look like at year 10," "decade-out scene," "20-year arc").

**Selections:**
- **Devil's Advocate (core)** — Q8 ("work backward from the version everyone hates") is structurally a pre-mortem, DA's strongest mode.
- **Pragmatist (core)** — Reversibility, age-32 timeline interactions, and felt cost of starting over are feasibility-anchored questions.
- **Futurist (Inner Ring — Strategic Strong Fit):** Q1 (lifetime-earnings projection across two scenarios), Q3 (timeline interactions with partnership/children/aging parents), Q4 (10-year system view of three futures), and Q7 (decade-out Tuesday afternoon) are trend-grounded extrapolation from current realities — exactly Futurist's mode.

**Cluster-family check:** Futurist (Forward-Looking) and Systems Thinker (External Perspectives) are different families and complementary. Pragmatist (Analytical) and DA (Challenge) round out. Visionary deferred to high effort (Futurist's grounded mode is the better single-pick at medium for a topic anchored in concrete life-stage timing).
