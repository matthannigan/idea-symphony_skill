# Total Cost & Funding the JD - The Devil's Advocate

## Question 1: All-in Cost of Attendance and Realistic Debt at Graduation

* **Summary.** Have you actually modeled the $150-250K debt scenario rather than hoping for scholarships? The math is brutal: $246K forgone salary + $210-300K tuition/living = $456-546K total outflow, against a $45K starting cushion. Many people graduate assuming they'll refinance or find 130K loan forgiveness, but Public Service Loan Forgiveness requires 10 years of on-time payments on qualifying loans — that's a decade of financial constraint before relief. What's your Plan B if PSLF rules tighten, if your salary stays $55K for five years, or if you graduate and decide immigration law isn't the fit? Build a hard budget that assumes zero forgiveness and see if the decision still holds.

## Question 2: Hidden Pre-Law Costs Already Eating the Runway

* **Summary.** Your $45K cushion evaporates faster than you think. LSAT prep ($1,500 realistically if you need a course), applications to 10 schools at $85 each ($850), and transcript fees add another $2,500 before you set foot in law school. If you reduce nursing hours to study for the LSAT, that's another $5-10K in foregone income. You're actually starting law school with $30K, not $45K — and that assumes no medical expense, car repair, or family emergency during prep. Have you built in a 15% contingency buffer, or are you planning to hit exactly zero and hope nothing breaks?

## Question 3: Part-Time Work Feasibility Under ABA Constraints

* **Summary.** Nursing's flexibility is a mirage during law school. Yes, you *could* pick up weekend shifts in years 2-3, but the ABA cap is real, and most firms and clinics track hours strictly. More critically, 1L year is a crucible — law school grading is curved, networking happens in unreported ways, and a single B+ can close BigLaw doors that fund careers. Working even 15 hours during 1L often means missing study groups, networking events, and cold-calling prep that classmates use to build advantage. The math says weekend work = $500/month extra. Is that worth potentially lower grades and fewer mentorship relationships? Explore schools that offer tuition scholarships instead — that's a cleaner win than betting on per-diem income during your most competitive year.

## Question 4: Savings Runway and Minimum Cash Floor at Graduation

* **Summary.** One potential challenge: you're conflating two different questions — can I survive law school, and will I be secure *after*? Graduating with $200K debt and zero emergency savings is survival, but it's not security. Public interest starting salaries are $55-65K; your $22K in existing loans stays; and you're 35 with no retirement savings yet. What's your actual minimum acceptable cash floor at graduation? If it's $15K, that's a different repayment strategy than $50K. Have you talked to working PD attorneys and legal aid lawyers about what cash reserves felt *necessary* by year 2-3 of practice? And is there a scenario where you'd regret this not because of money, but because you wanted options later — sabbatical, relocation, burnout recovery — and debt foreclosed them?

## Question 5: The 1L Winter

* **Summary.** This moment defines everything. January of 1L is when motivation meets reality. Your cohort includes people with trust funds, spousal income, and zero financial pressure; you're up at midnight stressed about debt accrual and whether you'll see a $82K paycheck again. Have we considered *whether* your reasons for leaving — "burned out from bedside, deeply committed to vulnerable populations" — will sustain you when the commitment becomes abstract (contracts, property law, research) and the burnout is immediate and personal (debt, precarity, Socratic method humiliation)? One mitigation: volunteer with a legal aid clinic starting in the summer before 1L, not waiting until 2L. If you find the work hollow or the people toxic, you learn that when you can still change course. Don't let a $40K sunk cost be your only permission to quit.
