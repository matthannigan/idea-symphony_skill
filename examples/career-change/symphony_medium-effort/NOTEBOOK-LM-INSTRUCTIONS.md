## Artifact 1: Presentation

**Format:** Presentation (slide deck)
**Sources to upload:** `BRAINSTORM.md` + all `synthesis/*_summary.md` files
**Target length:** 18-22 slides

**Customize box prompt:**

```
Source framing: These documents are the output of a facilitated brainstorming session on Career Change from ICU Nursing to Public Interest Law — structured ideation across multiple thinking styles, not primary research or validated findings. The presentation should frame claims as ideas the session surfaced ("the brainstorm proposed…", "one perspective in the cluster argued…"), not as facts ("research shows…", "experts agree…"). Convergence across lenses is an internal quality signal, not external validation.

Create a presentation for the person making this decision (a burned-out ICU nurse weighing a pivot to public interest immigration law) covering the findings from this brainstorming session on Career Change from ICU Nursing to Public Interest Law.

Audience expertise: Working knowledge of nursing careers, law school, public interest law, and loan repayment. Skip introductory definitions of terms like ICU, JD, LSAT, PSLF, and bar exam.

Slide structure:
1. Title slide — the project and the central question: is leaving ICU nursing for public interest immigration law a sound move?
2. Session Overview — one slide on what was explored and how the session approached it (medium effort, nine clusters, four perspectives per cluster).
3. Executive Summary — 1-2 slides on the highest-confidence findings: diagnose burnout before deciding; test the work reversibly before betting the credential.
4. Central Tensions — one slide per tension (maximum 4 slides).
5. Key Themes — 2-4 slides on cross-cluster patterns (diagnose before deciding, buy information before a credential, model the worst case, sustainability is engineered, dignity is infrastructure).
6. Per-cluster summaries — one slide per cluster, in the order from BRAINSTORM.md. Each slide names the cluster's central tension and one or two key insights.
7. What the brainstorm did not engage with — one slide on the gaps: community input never gathered, survivorship bias in the research, the family conversation treated as a vibe check.
8. Recommended Next Steps — 1-2 slides, ordered by impact.

Tone: Analytical and direct. Avoid bullet-point-as-marketing-slogan phrasing. Each bullet should be a complete, specific claim — not a 3-word teaser. No hype words ("transformative," "game-changing," "revolutionary"). No exclamation marks.

Speaker notes: Include 2-3 sentences per slide explaining the underlying analysis. A reader of the notes should understand the reasoning, not just the headline.

Visuals: Prefer concept diagrams and 2-axis trade-off charts over decorative stock imagery. If a tension or trade-off is the subject of a slide, render it as a diagram, not a bulleted list.
```

Work through the material one section at a time — Executive Summary, then Central Tensions, then Key Themes, then Topic Summaries. End with a concise synthesis tied to the Recommended Next Steps, not a hype recap.

Nuance: Give equal weight to what the brainstorm did not engage with and to the trade-offs as you do to the convergent findings. The strongest unresolved tensions are the most analytically interesting parts — spend time on them.

Focus: Concentrate on the three most load-bearing themes — diagnose the burnout before deciding, test the work reversibly before betting on the JD, and treat PSLF as upside rather than the financial foundation. Treat tactical implementation details as context rather than the main subject.
