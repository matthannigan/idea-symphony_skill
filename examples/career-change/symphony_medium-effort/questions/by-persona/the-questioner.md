# Brainstorming Questions from The Questioner: Career Change from ICU Nursing to Public Interest Law

---

## Untangling the Burnout Signal

1. **What is the burnout actually telling you**: If the burnout from ICU nursing were fully resolved — say, you shifted to a less acute nursing role with more manageable hours — would the pull toward immigration law still feel equally compelling, or does the urgency of this desire shrink without the pressure of exhaustion underneath it?

2. **Where does the mission actually live**: When you imagine doing meaningful work for immigrants and refugees ten years from now, what does that look like specifically — is it courtrooms and legal briefs, or is it the direct human contact in a clinical or advocacy setting, and have you questioned whether a JD is the only path to that image?

3. **What assumptions are embedded in "helping"**: Why do we assume that the most effective form of help for immigrant patients navigating impossible situations is legal representation specifically, rather than, for example, hospital-based patient advocacy, social work, or policy work — what did those particular gaps look like in the ICU that makes law feel like the right instrument?

---

## The Hidden Costs of Each Path

4. **What does the opportunity cost really include**: Beyond the $22K in existing loans and the 3 years of foregone $82K salary, what is the opportunity cost of leaving a career where you are already highly competent and trusted — and have you mapped out what it would feel like to start over at the bottom of a professional hierarchy at age 35?

5. **What does public interest law's burnout look like**: What if the emotional exhaustion in immigration law — chronic underfunding, impossible caseloads, clients in trauma, systemic losses you cannot fix — is structurally similar to ICU nursing burnout, just with different trigger points; have you talked to immigration attorneys who are 8 years in about what their version of this moment looks like?

6. **What are the non-financial costs of staying**: If you stay in ICU nursing for three more years rather than pursuing law school now, what specifically gets worse — is it the work itself, your sense of purpose, your physical health, or the feeling of a foreclosed future — and how much of that is reversible versus permanent?

---

## Leveraging the Asset That Is You

7. **How are you thinking about the nursing credential as a legal differentiator**: What would it mean for your practice if you were the immigration attorney who could read a client's medical record, understand what an ICU admission actually meant for their asylum claim, or explain clinical trauma responses to a judge — have you researched how rare that combination is in immigration law specifically?

8. **What bridges already exist that you haven't walked**: Have you investigated roles that already sit at the intersection of clinical expertise and immigrant legal advocacy — hospital-based legal aid partnerships, medical-legal partnerships (MLPs), or health law NGOs — and if so, what did you find lacking that still makes the full JD path feel necessary?

9. **What credibility does 8 years of ICU nursing confer in a law school application**: Why do we assume a non-traditional applicant with deep clinical experience in trauma care is at a disadvantage in law school admissions or the public interest legal job market — what would it actually mean to enter that world already knowing viscerally what your clients have been through?

---

## The Financial Architecture

10. **What does the real debt ceiling look like**: What if you modeled the scenario where law school debt reaches $150K-$200K on top of existing loans — at what monthly repayment level does your envisioned public interest salary (~$50-70K at many nonprofits) become mathematically unsustainable without PSLF, and how confident are you that PSLF remains intact and that you qualify?

11. **What financial floor do you actually need**: Beyond covering expenses, what level of financial security do you need to feel like yourself — do you have a number, and have you tested whether a public interest attorney's salary in your target market actually meets it, including if you ever want to buy a home, start a family, or absorb a financial emergency?

---

## Alternative Trajectories and Reframes

12. **What if you ran a small-scale experiment first**: What would it look like to spend 12-18 months before law school deepening your work with the refugee resettlement organization in a more significant role — perhaps moving into case management, immigration paralegal work, or advocacy — to test whether the legal dimension specifically is what you're drawn to, rather than discovering this after taking on six-figure debt?

13. **What does "not crazy" actually require**: You asked whether this is a "crazy idea" — but what would the version of this plan look like that is definitively not crazy, and what specific information, milestones, or test results would have to be true before you hit "go" with full confidence rather than as an escape from something painful?
