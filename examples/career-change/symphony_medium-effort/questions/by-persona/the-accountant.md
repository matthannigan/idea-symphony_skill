# Brainstorming Questions from The Accountant: Career Change from ICU Nursing to Public Interest Law

---

## The Full Cost of the Transition

1. **All-in transition cost**: Beyond tuition, what is the actual total cash outflow of this transition — including living expenses for three years without income, bar prep costs (~$3-5K), bar exam fees (~$1-2K across applications and retakes), professional clothing and networking expenses, and the opportunity cost of foregone nursing income (~$246K pre-tax over three years at your current salary — how does that figure land against your financial plan?

2. **Law school funding strategy**: Law school sticker price at a mid-tier institution runs $45-75K per year in tuition alone; total cost of attendance including living expenses is typically $70-100K per year. With $45K in savings and $22K in existing debt, what is the realistic debt load at graduation — and have you modeled the scenario where you graduate with $150-250K in total student debt (existing + new) rather than the rosier version?

3. **Hidden transition costs**: Which pre-law costs — LSAT prep ($500-3,000), application fees ($75-100 per school across 8-12 applications), potential gap-year income reduction if you reduce nursing hours to study — have you already factored into your starting cash position, and which are still invisible in your plan?

---

## Public Interest Salary and Debt Repayment Economics

4. **Public interest salary reality**: Entry-level public interest immigration attorneys at nonprofit legal aid organizations typically earn $50-70K in major metros, with senior positions reaching $80-95K. That's a pay cut from your current $82K nursing salary on the far end, and potentially a significant cut in year one. What does your monthly budget look like at $55K gross — after taxes, living costs, and minimum debt service — and is there a number below which the life you want becomes genuinely unworkable?

5. **PSLF mechanics and risk**: Public Service Loan Forgiveness is the financial model most people in your position rely on — 10 years of qualifying payments at a nonprofit or government employer, then forgiveness of remaining federal loan balance. But PSLF has a documented administrative failure rate, program eligibility rules have shifted, and it requires sustained employment in a qualifying role for a full decade. What is your plan if PSLF fails, you leave public interest before 10 years, or your employer loses qualifying status — and have you modeled the loan repayment cost without PSLF forgiveness?

6. **Income-driven repayment floor**: Under SAVE or IBR, your monthly payment on $200K in federal debt at a $60K salary could run $200-400/month, with the remainder forgiven after 20-25 years (and potentially taxed as income). How does that payment burden, sustained for two decades, compare to the financial trajectory of staying in nursing and aggressively paying down your existing $22K debt over the next 2-3 years?

---

## Opportunity Cost and Comparative Paths

7. **Cost of staying vs. cost of leaving**: If you remain in nursing for five more years at your current salary and save aggressively, what financial position do you reach — and does that position make a later law school transition easier, harder, or irrelevant to the question? What specific financial threshold, if any, would make the timing feel right versus premature?

8. **Partial-path economics**: Several hybrid roles — healthcare social worker, patient advocate, immigration paralegal, refugee case manager — carry salaries of $45-70K but require far shorter retraining timelines (0-2 years and $0-30K in costs). What is the economic comparison between spending $150-250K and three years on a JD versus spending $20-40K and one year on a graduate certificate in social work or a paralegal credential, given that both paths lead to direct work with immigrant populations?

---

## Financial Sustainability Over Time

9. **Savings runway and career floor**: Your $45K in savings represents roughly 6-8 months of living expenses if you're covering full law school cost of attendance from savings, or a much thinner cushion if you're also servicing the $22K in existing nursing loans during school. What is your minimum acceptable cash reserve at graduation, and what is the financial floor below which you would feel genuinely at risk — not just uncomfortable?

10. **The income-floor question for public interest work**: Public interest legal work is notoriously high-burnout and low-paid relative to law school debt. Attorneys who leave public interest typically do so because the economics are unsustainable, not because the mission changed. What salary floor would you need to earn in an immigration law role to feel financially stable rather than chronically stressed — and is that number realistic within the public interest sector, or does it require moving into private immigration practice?

---

## Long-Term Financial Architecture

11. **Retirement trajectory disruption**: Eight years of nursing has presumably allowed some retirement savings accumulation. Three years of zero contributions plus potential 401(k) liquidation risk, followed by years of income-driven repayment at reduced salary, materially alters your retirement trajectory. What is the estimated retirement savings gap created by this transition, and at what point — if ever — does the long-term earning potential of an attorney's career close that gap relative to staying in nursing?
