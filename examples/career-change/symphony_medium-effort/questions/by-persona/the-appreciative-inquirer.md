# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Career Change from ICU Nursing to Public Interest Law

---

## Discovery: Strengths Already in the Room

1. **Peak advocacy moment**: Think back to a specific immigrant or refugee patient in the ICU — a moment when you intervened, interpreted, translated, or simply stayed present in a way that changed their experience. What did you do, and what did you understand about their situation that no one else in that room understood?

2. **Transferable mastery**: ICU nursing requires triage under pressure, reading a patient's full situation at a glance, and communicating across fear and language barriers. When have you drawn on exactly those capacities in an advocacy or volunteer context — and how did they land in that new setting?

3. **What the volunteer work revealed**: During your two years with the refugee resettlement organization, when did you feel most effective — most like someone who was genuinely changing an outcome for a family? What were you doing in those moments, and what made it possible?

---

## Dream: The Future Built on What Already Works

4. **The ideal case, three years in**: Imagine it is 2030 and you are a public interest immigration attorney who colleagues and clients describe as unusually good at her work. What are they pointing to specifically? What does your clinical background make possible that a lawyer without it could not do as well?

5. **A practice that feels sustainable**: You have flagged burnout as a real concern — one high-intensity field for another. If you picture a version of this legal career that does not repeat the exhaustion, what does a typical week look like? What structural features of the work protect you rather than deplete you?

6. **Bridging the two worlds at full strength**: In the best version of this transition, your nursing experience is not baggage you carry quietly into a new profession — it is a recognized asset that opens doors and shapes how you practice law. What would it look like for a law school, a public interest organization, or a client community to actively seek out and value exactly the combination you bring?

---

## Design: Structures That Would Reliably Create That Future

7. **Building the financial runway**: You have $45K in savings and a clear-eyed view of the costs ahead. What financial structures — repayment programs, part-time clinical work during school, targeted scholarships, fellowship pipelines — would need to be in place for this transition to feel not just possible but genuinely secure? Which of those structures already exist, and which would you need to build?

8. **The credential bridge**: Law school admission, financial aid, and post-graduation public interest placement all respond to a compelling story about why this applicant is different. Given the genuine depth of experience you are bringing — clinical, advocacy, multilingual patient care — what conditions would let that story be told in its full strength, rather than filtered into a conventional pre-law narrative?

9. **Alternative architectures worth designing**: Some of the most effective advocates at the intersection of healthcare and immigration have created roles that did not exist before — health justice legal clinics, hospital-based immigration legal aid programs, policy positions that require both licenses. If you were designing a role from scratch that used all of you, what would it actually look like?

---

## Destiny: First Steps and Sustaining the Momentum

10. **The low-risk experiment**: Before committing to three years of law school, what is the smallest meaningful test you could run in the next six months that would give you real signal about whether legal advocacy work fits you — not in theory, but in practice? What would you be looking for, and how would you know what you found?

11. **The support structure that makes it real**: You have a supportive family, two years of resettlement volunteer work, and eight years of high-stakes clinical experience. Who in your existing network has already navigated a transition like this — or who is doing the kind of work you imagine — and what would it take to be in genuine conversation with them before you decide?
