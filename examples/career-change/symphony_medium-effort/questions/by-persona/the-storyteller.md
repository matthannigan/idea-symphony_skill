# Brainstorming Questions from The Storyteller: Career Change from ICU Nursing to Public Interest Law

---

## The Moment That Starts the Story

1. **The precipitating scene**: Imagine the specific shift — the patient, the situation, the helplessness you felt — that crystallized your sense that legal advocacy was the missing piece. What does that scene tell you about whether law is the tool you actually need, or whether a different kind of advocacy might have solved the same problem?

2. **The identity crossroads**: Picture yourself five years from now, sitting across from a client in a cramped legal aid office. You've just spent three years in law school and passed the bar. When you look back at the ICU, does the distance feel like relief, like loss, or like something you're still carrying — and what does that emotional picture tell you about what you're actually seeking?

---

## Two Worlds, One Person

3. **The bilingual professional**: Imagine you're in a courtroom or a client consultation, and you find yourself instinctively reading the room the way you read a patient's vitals — noticing distress cues, managing a family's panic, translating impossible complexity into something a frightened person can absorb. Walk through what that scene looks like: which of your nursing instincts become legal superpowers, and which ones might actually get in the way?

4. **The credibility gap**: Imagine explaining your background to a law school admissions committee, then to your first supervising attorney, then to a client who just arrived at the border. Each audience sees your nursing career differently. What story does each of them need to hear, and does that story feel true to you — or are you still writing it?

---

## The Long Middle

5. **The 1L winter**: Picture the moment in your first year of law school — maybe January, maybe during finals — when the novelty has worn off, you're $40K deeper in debt, your former nursing colleagues are earning $82K and going home, and you're studying contract doctrine at midnight. What does that scene look like, and what has to be true about your reasons for leaving nursing to keep you in your chair?

---

## The Paths Not Taken

6. **The hybrid protagonist**: Imagine a version of you who never left nursing, but over the next three years built a formal bridge role — maybe as a patient advocate at a hospital with a legal aid partnership, a policy advisor at a refugee resettlement nonprofit, or a community health worker embedded in an immigration clinic. Walk through a week in that person's life. Does it feel like a consolation prize, or does it feel like the story you actually want to live?

---

## The Ending You're Writing Toward

7. **The decade-out scene**: Imagine yourself at 42, a decade after this decision point. In one version of the story, you became an immigration attorney and you love it. In another, you tried and left law after five years. In a third, you stayed in nursing and redirected your advocacy energy into policy or community organizing. Describe what a Tuesday afternoon looks like in each version — not the career highlights, but the texture of the ordinary day. Which scene feels most like home?
