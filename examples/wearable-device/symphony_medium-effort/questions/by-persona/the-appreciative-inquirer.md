# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: On-Device ML Health Monitoring Wearable

---

## Discovery: Peak Moments and Existing Strengths

1. **Early validation wins**: When has the team seen early evidence — even from prototypes, demos, or user interviews — that on-device health monitoring created genuine relief or trust for users? What was happening technically and relationally in those moments that made it resonate so strongly?

2. **Privacy as a differentiator**: Think of a context where the decision to keep raw biometric data on-device became a clear competitive or relational advantage rather than just a constraint — what conditions made privacy-by-design feel like a genuine asset rather than a compliance burden?

3. **Cross-discipline collaboration**: When has the firmware, ML, hardware, or mobile app teams solved a hard problem together in a way that felt surprisingly generative — what made that collaboration work, and what structural conditions enabled it?

---

## Dream: Aspirational Futures Built on Existing Strengths

4. **A device users feel grateful for**: Imagine it is three years from now and a 52-year-old with a family history of heart disease tells a friend, "This device may have saved my life, and I trust it completely." What would the technical architecture, alert experience, and data relationship need to look like to produce that story?

5. **The gold-standard clinical partnership**: If this device became the preferred platform for a leading cardiology research center — not just approved, but actively chosen — what would the on-device ML pipeline, privacy architecture, and regulatory posture need to look like? What capabilities would the clinical partners cite as the reason they picked this over alternatives?

6. **Battery life as a wellness rhythm**: Picture a future where users never think about charging the device — it fits seamlessly into a weekly rhythm, like a Sunday evening routine. What would the power architecture, sensor scheduling, and user experience design look like to make "7-day battery" feel invisible rather than a feature to manage?

---

## Design: Structures and Practices That Create the Desired Future

7. **Sensor fusion as a strength to amplify**: Given that this device already combines PPG, accelerometer, skin temperature, and microphone data, what architecture choices would let that multi-signal richness become a durable competitive moat — not just for arrhythmia and sleep apnea detection now, but as a platform for expanding clinical claims later?

8. **Model update pipeline as trust infrastructure**: What firmware update and on-device model refresh architecture would let the ML improve continuously over time while reinforcing — rather than eroding — users' confidence that their raw data never leaves the device? What would a transparency mechanism look like that made each update feel like good news?

9. **Regulatory path as a capability-building journey**: Rather than treating the consumer wellness launch and the eventual FDA 510(k) pathway as two separate tracks with a gap between them, what design decisions made now would mean the clinical-grade evidence, audit trail, and safety architecture are already largely in place when the team chooses to pursue clearance?

---

## Destiny: First Steps and Sustaining Momentum

10. **The smallest experiment with the biggest signal**: Given the team's current capabilities — 4 firmware engineers, 3 ML engineers, a Series A budget, and 18 months of runway — what is the single most leveraged experiment they could run in the next 90 days to generate real-world evidence that the on-device ML pipeline performs reliably at the accuracy and power targets? What would success look like, and who on the team already has the skills to run it?
