# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: On-Device ML Health Monitoring Wearable

---

## Discovery: Existing Strengths and Peak Moments

1. **Where the team's cross-discipline craft has already shined**: Think about a moment during early prototyping when your firmware, ML, and hardware engineers tackled the same constraint — power budget, inference latency, or sensor noise — and arrived at a solution none of them could have reached alone. What did that collaboration look like in practice, and what conditions made it possible?

2. **When on-device privacy architecture became a genuine asset**: Imagine a conversation — with a potential clinical partner, a privacy-conscious user, or an investor — where the decision to keep raw biometrics entirely on-device landed not as a limitation but as a compelling differentiator. What was said, and what did it reveal about the strengths already built into the design?

3. **The sensor fusion moment that exceeded expectations**: When has the combination of PPG, accelerometer, skin temperature, and breathing-pattern signals produced an insight or detection that none of the individual signals would have surfaced on their own? What made that fusion work, and what does it suggest about the architecture's latent capability?

---

## Dream: Aspirational Futures Built on Demonstrated Strengths

4. **The device that earns genuine trust from a 40+ user**: Imagine it is three years from now and a 55-year-old with a family history of cardiac events tells a cardiologist that this device gave them a confidence in their own body they have never had before. What is the device doing — not just technically, but experientially — that produces that response? What strengths already visible in the current design are the seeds of that outcome?

5. **A model of privacy-first clinical collaboration**: Picture a future in which this product has become the reference architecture that other health-tech teams study when designing HIPAA-compliant, on-device ML pipelines. What design decisions made today — in the data pipeline, the sync protocol, the NPU integration — would visitors to your codebase point to and say "that's the part that made everything else possible"?

6. **A firmware update cycle that feels like continuous learning**: If the periodic model-update pathway via firmware evolved into something that clinicians and engineers both trusted as rigorous and safe, what would that process look like at its best? What would it feel like to a user who receives an update that measurably improves their arrhythmia detection accuracy overnight?

---

## Design: Structures and Practices That Reliably Create That Future

7. **The battery-budget discipline that enables everything else**: Given that early engineering decisions about power allocation across sensing, processing, display, and communication will shape every tradeoff downstream, what principles or practices — already emerging in the team's working style — could be formalized into a shared battery-budget discipline that makes future decisions faster and more confident?

8. **A false-positive governance practice rooted in user trust**: The team already holds real data about alert thresholds and their human consequences. What would a standing practice look like — a review cadence, a cross-functional forum, a design principle — that used those insights to keep false-positive rates low while honoring users' need for timely, meaningful signals rather than noise?

9. **Bridging the consumer wellness and clinical device identities**: The product today is a consumer wellness device with a credible path to 510(k) clearance. What architectural or organizational practices, built now while the team is small and coherent, would let the device grow into its clinical identity without abandoning the consumer simplicity that made it trustworthy in the first place?

---

## Destiny: First Steps and Sustaining Momentum

10. **The smallest experiment that moves toward the most ambitious vision**: Given everything the team already knows — about the NPU's capabilities, the sensor fusion signals, the users' lived experience — what is one focused experiment, achievable in the next sprint or the next two weeks, that would generate concrete evidence about whether the most ambitious version of this device is reachable?
