# Effort-Level Comparison: Rural School District Consolidation

**Topic:** Whether and how to consolidate three small rural K-12 districts (~1,800 students, ~$28M budget, one district two years from insolvency) into a single unified district.
**Effort levels covered:** `min`, `low`, `medium`, `high` (all four present).
**Scope:** Comparison reads one `REQUEST.md` (the `high` copy; copies differ only in frontmatter) plus `PLAN.md`, `QUESTIONS.md`, and `BRAINSTORM.md` from each level. Exact counts sourced from directory listings of `questions/`, `responses/`, and `synthesis/`. `SUMMARIES.md` and `SYNTHESIS.md` were deliberately not read.
**Generated:** 2026-06-10.

---

## 1. Headline Finding

The core thesis survives intact across all four levels, almost verbatim: **the financial case for consolidation is real but softer, slower, and later than the headline `$1.2M` savings figure implies, so the durable strategy is to lead with structural commitments and the cost of inaction rather than with net savings.** Every level reaches this independently. Every level also lands on the same three load-bearing moves — bound the cash-flow vulnerability window with named numbers, encode protections as binding instruments rather than promises, and run three district-specific campaigns rather than one. The fidelity from `min` to `high` is the strongest single result of this comparison.

What scales is not the thesis but its *interrogation*. `min` accepts the county's "whether and how to merge" framing and answers within it. `high` reframes the question itself — "the real decision is *how much* to consolidate, not whether" — and surfaces a shared-services hybrid that captures 60-85% of the savings without a ballot. The cheaper levels tell you the answer; the expensive levels question the question.

No `USER-QUESTIONS.md` is present in any run, and no user files were submitted. All questions were model-generated, so each level's findings reflect what it discovered on its own — there is no user-supplied confound.

## 4. Where Depth Scales with Effort

### 4.1. Surfacing dissent and tension

A clean monotonic gradient.

- **`min`** reads as one confident analytical voice. It names three "Central Tensions," but they are framed as design problems with clean resolutions ("structural mechanisms make both speed and stability achievable"), not as disagreement between perspectives.
- **`low`** introduces the first genuine *counterpoint*: each cluster is run through a feasibility lens and a risk lens, and the executive summary leans on the risk reading ("a case built on cost savings will not survive expert scrutiny").
- **`medium`** adds texture by reporting where perspectives split rather than converge — e.g. on naming closures pre- vs. post-vote, and on committing to a smaller footprint vs. preserving optionality, "with the perspectives split."
- **`high`** quantifies convergence and preserves named dissents. It reports "all seven lenses agree," flags "a productive dissent runs underneath," and in cluster 11 sets "six lenses champion a transition ritual; one adversarial lens challenges every part of that consensus." Only `high` can say *N of M agreed* — a signal the lower levels structurally cannot produce.

### 4.2. Deepening of the critique

Ranked by how radically each level questions the input REQUEST:

1. **`high` (most radical).** Rejects the REQUEST's binary framing outright: "the real choice is how much to consolidate, not whether." Argues the feasibility study should *follow* political consensus-building, not precede it, and that inability to fund student protections "is itself a feasibility signal" — i.e. the merger may not be feasible here at all.
2. **`medium`.** Stays inside "whether and how" but pushes hard on the federated/affiliate *phasing* alternative as "the most consequential strategic idea," reframing the referendum from a bet on promises to a vote on evidence.
3. **`low`.** Accepts the merger frame but attacks the *justification*, insisting the public case be rebuilt around enrollment sustainability before it reaches voters.
4. **`min` (least radical).** Accepts the REQUEST's framing wholesale and optimizes within it: the question is "whether consolidation is better than what each community actually faces otherwise."

## 5. Productive Drift Between Levels

Five zones where the recommendation *sharpens* (not contradicts) as effort rises.

**1. The savings figure.** `min`: real and recurring, payback 4-7 yrs. `low`: cannot bear the weight; harmonization may consume 40-60%. `medium`: explicitly gross-not-net, discounted to `$600-850K`, break-even yr 3-4. `high`: `$670-900K` after coordination overhead and backfill, net positive only by yr 3. *Direction: from "real, just delayed" toward "smaller, conditional, and must be modeled to break even without the bonus."*

**2. Structural alternative to full merger.** `min`/`low`: full three-district merger is the assumed object; alternatives are fallbacks (two-district, shared services as a low-resistance pre-step). `medium`: federated phasing becomes "the most consequential strategic idea." `high`: shared services is reframed as a near-substitute capturing 60-85% of savings, with full merger as an optional "finishing move." *Direction: the alternative migrates from footnote to headline.*

**3. Closure decision-making.** `min`: a five-factor rubric scored mechanically, published first. `low`: deferred maintenance (not sentiment) drives the sequence; anchor-institution collapse is non-linear. `medium`: name closure as a *possibility* pre-vote, decide specifics after, with a town-by-town loss ledger. `high`: closure is "a community economic-development decision, not a facilities-efficiency problem" — a `$5-6M` local-economy loss that can reverse the case. *Direction: from scoring rubric toward economic-development reframe.*

**4. The enrollment horizon.** `min`: essentially absent from the design. `low`: design for ~1,400 with charter triggers. `medium`: resolve a 1,000-1,200 footprint deliberately rather than defer it. `high`: design backward from a ~1,200 student 2045 end-state and make the merger conditional on state formula reform. *Direction: from unaddressed to a load-bearing, explicitly-forced design decision.*

**5. The campaign pitch.** `min`: comparative trajectory chart + asymmetric district messages. `low`: credible messengers are skeptics, not proponents. `medium`: convert the vote from a blank check into ratification of a binding agreement. `high`: "sell control, not cost" + sequence endorsements + publish a concession register. *Direction: from messaging tactics toward a sequenced, structurally-anchored persuasion architecture.*

## 6. Fidelity Check — What Gets Lost at Lower Levels

- **Design-for-decline / the long horizon.** `min` never asks what the district looks like at 1,200-1,400 students. `low` introduces it ("design for ~1,400"), `medium` forces the footprint choice, and `high` makes it the reordering insight (carrying capacity, 2045 end-state). A reader of `min` alone would over-size the institution.
- **The "how much, not whether" reframe and the shared-services hybrid.** Present only at `high`. This is the single most decision-altering finding in the whole set and is wholly absent below `high`.
- **Distributional equity for specific students.** `min` treats students as headcount. `low` flags it explicitly as a never-modeled absence; `medium` and `high` carry it into binding IEP-continuity rules, fee waivers, and disaggregated-by-origin-school reporting.
- **Quantified convergence and surviving dissent.** Only `medium` (split-on-X language) and especially `high` ("six lenses champion … one adversarial lens challenges") expose where the analysis is contested. `min`'s single voice cannot show the seams.
- **The per-pupil bonus is self-dissolving.** The insight that consolidation itself removes the political rationale for the 12% subsidy first appears at `medium` and is sharpened at `high`. `min`/`low` treat the bonus as a stable gain.
- **State as an engaged party, not a backdrop.** Only `high` flags that formula reform, a rural floor, and a bridge loan all "run through the state, yet no cluster established it will move," and proposes making the merger conditional on it.

## 7. When Each Level Earns Its Cost

**`min`.** Best for a fast, competent orientation: it correctly identifies the savings-timing trap, the structure-over-promises rule, and the asymmetric-campaign need in ~2,200 words off five generic responses. Its characteristic weakness is a single confident register — it presents resolutions where real tension exists, omits the long-horizon design problem entirely, and never questions the brief's framing. Good for "what is this problem about"; weak for "is this even the right question."

**`low`.** The dual feasibility/risk lens delivers the highest marginal return per added unit of effort here: for the cost of one extra brainstormer per cluster it surfaces the first genuine counterpoint and opens the design-for-decline and distributional-equity frontiers. Its weakness is that the two lenses still resolve into a single per-cluster summary, so disagreement is reported rather than preserved, and it stops at the 0-5 year transition.

**`medium`.** Earns its cost when the deliverable must survive scrutiny: four perspectives plus a second synthesis tier produce the tiered savings number, the self-dissolving-bonus insight, and the federated-phasing strategy, while reporting where perspectives split. Its catch-all `99_additional` cluster adds a genuine cross-cutting synthesis rather than overflow. Weakness: at ~3,480 words it is long, and several findings (carrying capacity, "how much not whether") are still latent here and only crystallize at `high`.

**`high`.** Earns its cost only when reframing the decision is on the table. Seven lenses across thirteen clusters produce the question-the-question reframe, quantified convergence, surviving adversarial dissents, and the state-as-actor and economic-development insights nothing below it reaches. Its weakness is cost and redundancy: much of its 91-response, ~3,770-word output restates the stable core of §3 at higher resolution, so for a reader who only needs the answer rather than its interrogation, `medium` or even `low` already delivers it.

## 8. Final Summary

- **Preservation:** The central thesis — savings are real but soft, lead with structure and cost-of-inaction, run three asymmetric campaigns — is fully intact from `min` to `high`. Eight of ten core through-lines are already visible at `min`.
- **Scaling:** Effort buys *deliberation*, not output volume. Persona responses scale ~18× (5 → 91) while next-step count holds flat at 15. The growth lives almost entirely in Phase 3 plus the second synthesis tier added at `medium`/`high`.
- **Divergences (sharpening, not contradiction):** The savings figure tightens (real-but-delayed → `$670-900K`-conditional), the structural alternative migrates from footnote to headline, closure shifts from scoring rubric to economic-development decision, and the enrollment horizon moves from unaddressed to a forced design choice.
- **Irreplaceable high-effort insights:** The "how much, not whether" reframe and the shared-services hybrid; carrying capacity as the true driver of decline; making the merger conditional on state formula reform; quantified convergence with preserved adversarial dissent ("six lenses agree, one challenges"). None of these survive a drop below `high` — and the `min` reader would, without them, build an over-sized district around an unquestioned premise.
