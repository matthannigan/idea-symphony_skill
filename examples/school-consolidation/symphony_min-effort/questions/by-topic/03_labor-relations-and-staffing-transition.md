# Topic Cluster 03: Labor Relations and Staffing Transition

## Questions

11. **Contract harmonization strategy**: Given three separate collective bargaining agreements with potentially different pay scales, benefits, and work rules, what negotiation approach — uniform adoption of the most favorable terms, fresh bargaining from scratch, or a step-down hybrid — is most likely to gain union support while remaining fiscally sustainable?
12. **Retention of teaching staff**: With 14 existing vacancies already signaling recruitment difficulty, what commitments or incentives in the consolidation plan would prevent additional departures during the uncertainty of the transition — and which roles are highest flight risk?
13. **Administrative reduction process**: The projected $1.2M savings assumes eliminating duplicated superintendent and department-level positions; what process for those reductions — voluntary separation, attrition, or layoff — is most defensible legally and politically, and how does that choice affect the timeline for realizing savings?
14. **Recruiting advantage of consolidation**: In what ways could a larger, unified district actually improve teacher recruitment — stronger curriculum teams, better benefits leverage, clearer career ladders — and how should the consolidation plan proactively market those advantages to prospective hires?
