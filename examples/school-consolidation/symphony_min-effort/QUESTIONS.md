# Brainstorming Questions: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## Topic Cluster 01: Financial Viability and Fiscal Modeling

**Cluster focus**: Whether consolidation's projected savings actually outweigh transition costs, and how the combined entity sustains itself under continued enrollment decline.

1. **True cost calculus**: Beyond the $1.2M administrative savings estimate, what one-time transition costs — severance, system integration, legal fees, facility remediation — would offset those savings, and over what payback period does consolidation become net-positive?
2. **Enrollment decline stress testing**: If the projected 15% further decline by 2035 materializes (or accelerates), what per-pupil funding levels and minimum enrollment thresholds does the consolidated district need to remain solvent, and how does that compare to the status quo trajectory?
3. **Debt and liability inheritance**: How should the consolidated district structure the assumption of each legacy district's bonded debt and deferred maintenance obligations — particularly the $4.2M backlog — to avoid inheriting a liability crisis on day one?
4. **Property tax equalization**: Given that the three districts likely have different tax rates and assessed values, what approach to property tax equalization minimizes shock to taxpayers in the lowest-tax district while meeting the consolidated district's revenue needs?
5. **State funding timing**: The 12% per-pupil funding increase from the state formula takes effect at what point in the consolidation process, and how does the district bridge cash flow during the transition window before that revenue arrives?

---

## Topic Cluster 02: Facilities Strategy and Transportation

**Cluster focus**: Which physical assets to keep, close, or repurpose, and how geographic scale affects cost and equity of student access.

6. **Facility decision framework**: What criteria — utilization rate, building condition, geographic centrality, community anchoring role, remediation cost — should drive decisions about which of the seven buildings to retain, close, or convert, and who has standing to weigh in on those criteria?
7. **Closure sequencing and politics**: If at least one school building must close, what sequencing or phasing approach minimizes political resistance while still capturing facility savings on a meaningful timeline?
8. **Repurposing vs. disposal**: For buildings that are closed, what alternative uses — community centers, early childhood programs, municipal services, lease to nonprofits — preserve community investment in those structures and generate offsetting revenue or goodwill?
9. **Transportation cost modeling**: Across a larger geographic district, what does the true per-student transportation cost increase look like under different facility configurations, and at what point does transportation spending erode the administrative savings consolidation was supposed to produce?
10. **Rural access and equity**: For students in the most remote communities, how does increased travel time affect participation in extracurriculars, early/late programs, and family engagement — and what mitigation strategies are financially realistic?

---

## Topic Cluster 03: Labor Relations and Staffing Transition

**Cluster focus**: How to reconcile three separate union contracts and manage staffing change in ways that retain teachers and preserve instructional quality.

11. **Contract harmonization strategy**: Given three separate collective bargaining agreements with potentially different pay scales, benefits, and work rules, what negotiation approach — uniform adoption of the most favorable terms, fresh bargaining from scratch, or a step-down hybrid — is most likely to gain union support while remaining fiscally sustainable?
12. **Retention of teaching staff**: With 14 existing vacancies already signaling recruitment difficulty, what commitments or incentives in the consolidation plan would prevent additional departures during the uncertainty of the transition — and which roles are highest flight risk?
13. **Administrative reduction process**: The projected $1.2M savings assumes eliminating duplicated superintendent and department-level positions; what process for those reductions — voluntary separation, attrition, or layoff — is most defensible legally and politically, and how does that choice affect the timeline for realizing savings?
14. **Recruiting advantage of consolidation**: In what ways could a larger, unified district actually improve teacher recruitment — stronger curriculum teams, better benefits leverage, clearer career ladders — and how should the consolidation plan proactively market those advantages to prospective hires?

---

## Topic Cluster 04: Community Identity and Political Coalition Building

**Cluster focus**: How to address the "our school is our town" emotional reality and construct a voting coalition capable of winning approval in all three districts simultaneously.

15. **Identity preservation strategies**: Beyond keeping a school building open, what structural or symbolic elements — team names, local advisory councils, feeder school identities, community use agreements — can a consolidated district offer to communities worried about losing their local character?
16. **Coalition asymmetry**: If the fiscal case for consolidation is strongest in the most financially distressed district and weakest in the district with the healthiest fund balance, what benefits or assurances would make consolidation compelling to voters in the district with the least to gain?
17. **Winning the voter approval campaign**: Given that all three districts must approve consolidation simultaneously, what sequencing of public engagement — joint forums, district-specific listening sessions, feasibility study rollout — builds the broadest possible informed consent before the vote?
18. **Governance design for trust**: What board structure, representation formula, and transition governance arrangements for the new unified district would give each legacy community confidence that its interests won't be steamrolled by the combined majority?

---

## Topic Cluster 05: Implementation Planning and Risk Management

**Cluster focus**: Designing a realistic consolidation roadmap that manages execution risk, preserves optionality, and anticipates failure modes.

19. **Phased vs. full consolidation**: Under what conditions does a phased approach — shared services first, then administrative merger, then full academic consolidation — reduce risk enough to justify the slower savings realization, versus a faster single-step consolidation?
20. **Contingency planning for a failed vote**: If one district votes against consolidation, what alternative arrangements — two-district merger, shared services agreements, state receivership triggers — remain viable, and how should the feasibility study frame these options to avoid them being perceived as threats?
