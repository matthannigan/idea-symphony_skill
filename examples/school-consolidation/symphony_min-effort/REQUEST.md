# Request: Rural School District Consolidation

**Request Date:** 2026-02-24

## Topic
A rural county is evaluating whether to consolidate three small school districts into a single unified district to address declining enrollment, budget pressures, and difficulty attracting qualified teachers.

## Context
- **Districts:** Three K-12 districts serving a combined ~1,800 students across a rural county
- **Enrollment Trends:** Combined enrollment has declined 22% over the past decade; projections show another 15% decline by 2035
- **Financials:** All three districts are running structural deficits; the smallest district has a 2-year fund balance remaining before insolvency; combined annual operating budgets total $28M with $4.2M in deferred building maintenance; state per-pupil funding formula penalizes small districts, meaning consolidation would increase per-pupil allocation by ~12%
- **Staffing:** 14 teaching vacancies across the three districts last year; consolidated district could reduce administrative duplication (3 superintendents, 3 HR departments, 3 transportation directors) saving an estimated $1.2M annually
- **Facilities:** 7 school buildings, several underutilized; consolidated district would need to decide which to keep, repurpose, or close
- **Stakeholders:** Teachers' unions (3 separate contracts to reconcile), parent groups, town identity advocates ("our school IS our town"), fiscal conservatives, and state education officials encouraging consolidation
- **Process:** County board of education has commissioned a feasibility study; any consolidation requires voter approval in all three districts

## Goals
Develop a comprehensive analysis of whether and how to consolidate the three districts, addressing the political, financial, and community identity dimensions that will determine whether a consolidation vote can succeed.

## Key Considerations
- Financial analysis: true cost savings vs. transition costs, facility decisions, debt consolidation
- Revenue impact: state funding formula changes, property tax equalization across districts with different tax bases, bond obligations
- Budget modeling for the consolidated entity (staffing, transportation, facilities maintenance)
- Long-term fiscal sustainability under continued enrollment decline scenarios
- Union contract reconciliation and staffing transition plans
- Community identity and the "our school is our town" resistance
- Building a coalition that can win voter approval in all three districts
- Governance structure for the new unified board
- Managing the politics of facility closures (which towns lose "their" school?)
- Transportation logistics and costs for a larger geographic district
- Timeline and phasing of consolidation
