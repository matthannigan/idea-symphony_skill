# Brainstorming Questions from The Questioner: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## Hidden Assumptions in the Savings Calculus

1. **The $1.2M savings assumption**: The estimated $1.2M in administrative savings assumes three superintendents, three HR departments, and three transportation directors can simply be eliminated — but what severance costs, transition contracts, and interim leadership gaps are embedded in that figure, and over what time horizon does the net savings actually materialize?

2. **Deferred maintenance and the "savings" framing**: The $4.2M in deferred building maintenance exists regardless of consolidation — so why do we assume consolidation resolves this liability rather than concentrating it into a single entity that may be less politically able to defer it further? What does the true fiscal picture look like if maintenance costs are treated as a current obligation rather than a future problem?

3. **Per-pupil funding as a permanent gain**: The 12% increase in per-pupil allocation assumes the state funding formula remains stable post-consolidation — what happens to this calculation if the state revises its formula in response to rural consolidation trends across multiple counties?

---

## What the Enrollment Decline Is Actually Telling Us

4. **The cause behind the decline**: A 22% enrollment decline over ten years reflects something happening to the population of this county — but have we interrogated whether consolidation addresses the underlying cause, or whether it is simply a fiscal adaptation to demographic forces that will continue regardless?

5. **The 2035 projection and its dependencies**: The 15% further decline projected by 2035 presumably rests on trend-line extrapolation — what assumptions about housing development, employer arrivals or departures, and family formation rates are embedded in that model, and how sensitive is the financial case for consolidation to scenarios where decline accelerates or plateaus?

6. **What "viability" means when enrollment keeps falling**: If the consolidated district still faces structural enrollment decline after merger, at what enrollment level does the unified district itself become unsustainable, and are we solving for a 10-year window or a 30-year one?

---

## Community Identity and the Limits of "Our School Is Our Town"

7. **What the identity claim actually protects**: When stakeholders say "our school is our town," what specific, concrete things are they protecting — athletic rivalries, proximity for elementary-age children, local employment for teachers and staff, a building that anchors civic life? And does each of those things actually require a separate district, or only a separate school building?

8. **The asymmetry of loss across three districts**: If facility decisions will inevitably favor some towns over others, what does the political geometry of all-three-districts voter approval look like when one or two towns are likely to "win" the facility question and one is likely to "lose" — and what concession structures might make approval achievable in the losing town?

9. **Identity preservation without district preservation**: What models exist from comparable rural consolidations where community identity — mascots, athletics, elementary school siting — was deliberately preserved within a unified governance structure, and what did those design choices cost relative to full rationalization?

---

## Governance and the Post-Consolidation Power Structure

10. **Board composition and geographic representation**: A unified board elected countywide could rapidly become dominated by the largest population center — what governance structures (geographic seat allocation, weighted voting, advisory councils by community) would ensure smaller towns retain meaningful voice after consolidation, and how do those structures affect accountability and decision speed?

11. **Union contract reconciliation as a hidden timeline driver**: Reconciling three separate collective bargaining agreements is not merely a cost question but a sequencing one — what is the realistic timeline for a unified contract, and during the interim period when three contracts coexist, what operational constraints does that create for staffing decisions and facility assignments?

---

## The Voter Approval Problem

12. **Simultaneous vs. sequential referendum strategy**: Requiring approval in all three districts simultaneously means a single "no" vote kills the consolidation — have we considered whether a sequential approval process (highest-benefit district first) could build momentum, or whether state law forecloses that option?

13. **What "no" looks like for the smallest district**: The smallest district has a two-year fund balance before insolvency — if that district votes no and consolidation fails, what actually happens next: state receivership, forced merger under different terms, or something else, and does making that scenario explicit change the political calculus for voters in that district?
