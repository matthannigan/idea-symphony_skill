# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## Discovery: When Schools and Communities Have Thrived Together

1. **Peak moments of cross-district collaboration**: Think back to a time when two or more of these three districts worked together on something that genuinely benefited students — a shared program, a joint event, a combined resource. What made that collaboration succeed, and what does it reveal about the latent capacity for a unified district to do the same at scale?

2. **The school as community anchor**: In moments when one of these schools has served as a true anchor for its town — rallying people through a crisis, celebrating a local milestone, generating shared pride — what specifically did the school do, and what does that tell us about what a consolidated district must preserve or replicate in each community?

3. **Staff who stayed and why**: Among the teachers and administrators who have remained in these rural districts despite better-paying alternatives elsewhere, what has kept them? What does their loyalty reveal about the genuine strengths of small-district culture that a consolidated district could build on rather than dissolve?

---

## Dream: Imagining the Unified District at Its Best

4. **A model others travel to study**: Imagine it is 2031 and a delegation from another rural county arrives to study this consolidated district as a success story. What would they observe in the classrooms, the governance, and the community relationships that made it work? What would former skeptics — the "our school is our town" advocates — say surprised them most about how consolidation preserved what they valued?

5. **The student experience in the best version**: In the most optimistic but grounded scenario, what does a student's K-12 journey look like inside the unified district five years after consolidation? What courses, activities, mentors, and community connections become available to that student that simply were not possible across three underfunded, understaffed districts?

6. **Financial strength redirected toward children**: If the $1.2M in administrative savings were fully redirected to student-facing programs rather than absorbed into operational gaps, what would be the most energizing investments the unified district could make — and how might those investments reshape community perceptions of what consolidation was actually for?

---

## Design: Structures That Would Reliably Create That Future

7. **Governance that builds trust across three towns**: Given that some districts have successfully unified governance without erasing local identity — through community advisory councils, neighborhood school committees, or distributed board representation — what governance structures would give each of the three communities genuine voice and accountability in the new unified board, drawing on the existing civic participation habits already present in these towns?

8. **Facility decisions that strengthen rather than wound communities**: Rather than framing facility decisions as "which towns lose their school," how might the consolidation plan design a role for each building that leverages its existing relationships and physical presence — community education centers, early childhood hubs, vocational training sites — so that no town experiences pure loss, and the facility portfolio becomes a community asset network?

9. **Union contract reconciliation as a signal of values**: The process of reconciling three separate union contracts could be experienced as a bureaucratic ordeal — or it could become an early demonstration of how the unified district treats its people. What principles and practices, drawn from the best labor-management relationships already existing within these three districts, would make the reconciliation process a foundation of trust rather than a source of grievance?

---

## Destiny: First Steps and Sustaining Momentum

10. **Coalition builders who are already present**: Who in each of the three communities — a parent who bridges town loyalties, a teacher respected across district lines, a retired administrator, a local business owner — already has the relationships and credibility to begin building the cross-community coalition needed for a successful vote? What could be asked of those people in the next 90 days that would leverage what they already know and who they already are?

11. **A small win before the vote**: What is one concrete, low-cost action the county board of education could take in the next six months — before any consolidation vote — that would give residents in all three towns a tangible preview of what a unified district could offer, and begin shifting the narrative from "what we might lose" to "what we are already building together"?
