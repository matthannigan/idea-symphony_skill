# Brainstorming Questions from The Analyst: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## Financial Modeling and Break-Even Analysis

1. **True net savings vs. transition costs**: Beyond the headline $1.2M in administrative savings, what are the full one-time transition costs — severance, legal fees for contract reconciliation, systems integration, facility assessments — and at what year does the consolidated district reach cumulative net positive cash flow?

2. **Per-pupil funding sensitivity**: The 12% increase in per-pupil state allocation assumes current enrollment; given projected 15% further enrollment decline by 2035, what does the per-pupil funding trajectory look like under consolidation vs. status quo, and at what enrollment floor does the formula benefit disappear?

3. **Facility cost modeling under different closure scenarios**: Breaking down the 7 buildings by utilization rate, deferred maintenance liability ($4.2M total), and debt obligations — what does the cost-per-student-served look like across consolidation scenarios that close one, two, or three buildings, and how does each scenario affect transportation costs?

4. **Structural deficit trajectory without consolidation**: If consolidation does not happen, what is the specific year each district reaches insolvency under current trends, and what emergency mechanisms (state takeover, emergency millage, borrowing) would activate — and what do those alternatives cost compared to consolidation?

---

## Union Contract and Staffing Transition

5. **Contract reconciliation cost structure**: The three teacher union contracts likely differ in pay scales, benefits, seniority rules, and work rules — what is the estimated cost of harmonizing upward to the most favorable terms vs. the implementation risk of harmonizing downward, and how does each scenario affect the projected $1.2M administrative savings?

6. **Vacancy fill rate and staffing leverage**: With 14 teaching vacancies across three districts last year, what is the causal breakdown — compensation uncompetitiveness, geographic isolation, housing availability, working conditions — and which factors does consolidation actually address vs. which persist regardless?

7. **Administrative reduction sequencing**: Reducing from three superintendents, HR departments, and transportation directors to one is the headline savings driver, but what is the realistic timeline for those positions to vacate (retirements, buyouts, performance transitions), and what transition leadership structure is needed in the gap years?

---

## Governance Structure and Decision Rights

8. **Board composition and representation equity**: A unified district board must represent communities that currently have separate elected boards — what governance models (at-large seats, geographic districts, weighted representation by enrollment) have analogous rural consolidations used, and what are the equity implications of each for smaller communities who would lose proportional voice?

9. **Fiscal authority and property tax equalization**: The three districts likely have different property tax bases and existing bond obligations — what is the legal and financial mechanism for equalizing tax rates across the consolidated district, who bears the adjustment costs, and what does this mean for taxpayers in higher-assessed-value areas?

---

## Facility Closure and Community Impact Quantification

10. **Measuring community economic dependence on school buildings**: School facilities often anchor community activity beyond education — what is the measurable economic and social function each of the 7 buildings currently serves (employment, community gathering, childcare, athletics), and how does that inform which closures generate manageable vs. destabilizing community impact?

11. **Repurposing vs. disposal decision criteria**: For buildings that are closed, what is the decision framework for evaluating repurposing options (community center, early childhood, remote worker hub) vs. sale or demolition — and what are the break-even conditions under which repurposing generates enough community benefit to justify carrying costs?

---

## Long-Term Sustainability and Decision Trigger Points

12. **Scenario analysis for continued enrollment decline**: Under the projection of 15% further enrollment loss by 2035, what are the key inflection points — enrollment thresholds, per-pupil cost ratios, bond rating triggers — at which the consolidated district itself would need to revisit its facility and staffing footprint, and how should consolidation planning account for those future decision points now?
