# Topic Cluster 08: Strengths, Vision, and Coalition-Building

## Questions

1. **Peak moments of cross-district collaboration and the staff who stayed**: Think back to a time when two or more of these three districts worked together on something that genuinely benefited students — a shared program, joint event, combined resource — and consider the teachers and administrators who have remained in these rural districts despite better-paying alternatives elsewhere. What made those collaborations succeed, what has kept those staff loyal, and what does that reveal about the latent capacity for a unified district and the genuine strengths of small-district culture that consolidation could build on rather than dissolve?
2. **The model others travel to study**: Imagine it is 2031 and a delegation from another rural county arrives to study this consolidated district as a success story, and a student five years post-consolidation is describing her K-12 journey — the courses, activities, mentors, and community connections newly available to her, plus the $1.2M in administrative savings fully redirected to student-facing programs rather than absorbed into operational gaps. What would observers see in classrooms, governance, and community relationships that made it work, what would former "our school is our town" skeptics say surprised them most, and what would have to be true for this consolidation to become a prototype that other rural counties actively study and replicate?
3. **Coalition builders already present and a small win before the vote**: Who in each of the three communities — a parent who bridges town loyalties, a teacher respected across district lines, a retired administrator, a local business owner — already has the relationships and credibility to begin building the cross-community coalition needed for a successful vote, and what is one concrete, low-cost action the county board of education could take in the next six months that would give residents in all three towns a tangible preview of what a unified district could offer, beginning to shift the narrative from "what we might lose" to "what we are already building together"?
