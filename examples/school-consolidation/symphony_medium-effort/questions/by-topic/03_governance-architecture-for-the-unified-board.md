# Topic Cluster 03: Governance Architecture for the Unified Board

## Questions

1. **Board composition, power geography, and the governance learning curve**: The unified board will be read by each community as a signal of whether their town still has meaningful voice or has been absorbed — what governance models (weighted representation, equal seats, geographic subdistricts, staggered terms) have been used in comparable rural consolidations, what are the measurable outcomes for decision equity and community trust, how can a board be sized to be both representative and functionally governable, what accountability mechanisms would prevent the new board from making facility and resource decisions that systematically favor the largest or most affluent former district under the cover of "neutral efficiency," and how long does the governance learning curve typically run before a newly constituted board operates as effectively as mature single-district governance?
2. **Why "unified" at all — federation as a lower-trust alternative**: Why do we assume a single governance structure is required to capture the financial benefits — and are there federated or shared-services models that would generate similar savings while preserving more local control, and if so, why have those been set aside?
3. **Board composition and district parity (political settlement framing)**: The governance structure of the new unified board will be read by each community as a signal of whether their town still has meaningful voice or has been absorbed — what board composition, seat allocation, and transition structure would give each of the three communities credible representation without creating a design so fragmented it prevents coherent governance, and who in each community needs to be consulted before a structure is publicly proposed?
