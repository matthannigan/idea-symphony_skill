# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## Discovery: When Community and Schools Have Thrived Together

1. **Cross-district collaboration peak moments**: Think back across the history of these three districts — when has there been a moment of genuine, energizing collaboration between them, even informally? What was happening, who drove it, and what did it make possible that wouldn't have happened otherwise?

2. **The school as community anchor**: In each of these three communities, what is the single most vivid example of the local school strengthening the broader community — not just educating children, but generating belonging, pride, or resilience? What conditions made that possible, and which of those conditions still exist today?

3. **Successful transitions that stick**: Rural districts across the country have navigated hard changes — budget cuts, school closures, leadership transitions — and emerged stronger. What examples from these three districts (or comparable communities nearby) show that this community knows how to move through difficulty and come out with its identity intact?

---

## Dream: Imagining a Unified District That Communities Would Celebrate

4. **The model others want to copy**: Imagine it's 2032, and education policy researchers have chosen this newly consolidated district as a national case study in how rural consolidation can be done right. What are they writing about? What did this community get right that others got wrong? What do the students, parents, and teachers say when the researchers interview them?

5. **What students gain that they couldn't before**: If consolidation were designed around student flourishing rather than budget reduction, what would become possible for a 10th grader in this county that simply isn't possible today across three small, under-resourced districts? What courses, mentors, pathways, or experiences would open up?

6. **A governance model that earns trust**: What would a unified school board and superintendent look like if it were genuinely trusted by all three communities — not just tolerated, but seen as a fair steward of each town's children and identity? What would that board do differently than a typical consolidated district?

---

## Design: Building on What Already Works

7. **Shared strengths as architectural foundations**: Each of the three districts has developed something worth preserving — a teaching culture, a community partnership, a program that serves kids well. If a consolidated district were designed to amplify and spread those existing strengths rather than flatten them, how would it be structured? What would the first design principle be?

8. **The identity infrastructure already in place**: "Our school is our town" is a genuine and legitimate attachment. Rather than asking communities to give it up, what existing structures — school names, traditions, athletic identities, community events, advisory councils — could be built into the consolidated district's design to honor and sustain that sense of local belonging?

9. **Union collaboration as a design asset**: Three teachers' union contracts must be reconciled, which sounds like a problem — but it also means three sets of educators who have organized around shared professional values. What in those existing contracts and union relationships reflects something worth protecting and amplifying in the new unified district? What would a contract reconciliation process look like if it started from "what do all three agreements get right?" rather than "what do we have to give up?"

---

## Destiny: First Steps and Who Already Has What It Takes

10. **The coalition that already exists**: Before any formal vote campaign, there are already people in each of these three communities who understand both the financial reality and the emotional stakes — parents, teachers, local business owners, former board members. Who are they, what do they already believe, and how could they become the authentic voice of a "yes" campaign that speaks from within each community rather than at it?
