# Brainstorming Questions from The Analyst: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## Financial Anatomy: True Costs and Savings

1. **Break-even timeline**: If the estimated $1.2M in annual administrative savings is accurate, and transition costs (severance, system integration, facilities rationalization, legal fees for contract reconciliation) are modeled across realistic ranges, in what year does the consolidated district reach net positive financial benefit — and how sensitive is that break-even point to the accuracy of the savings estimate?

2. **Structural deficit trajectory**: The smallest district has roughly a 2-year fund balance before insolvency; what does the per-district deficit trajectory look like under a no-consolidation scenario, and at what point does the state likely step in with emergency oversight — and how does that forced-outcome cost compare to voluntary consolidation transition costs?

3. **Deferred maintenance allocation**: The $4.2M in deferred building maintenance is distributed across 7 buildings, but facility decisions (keep, repurpose, close) will concentrate or eliminate that liability unevenly — what framework should guide deciding which buildings' deferred maintenance obligations are worth absorbing versus divesting, and how should stranded costs for closed buildings be accounted in the financial model?

4. **Property tax equalization**: The three districts likely have different tax bases and mill levy histories; what does the range of possible property tax impacts look like for residential and commercial property owners in each district under different equalization scenarios, and which scenario most fairly distributes burden without triggering political opposition that could sink the consolidation vote?

---

## Enrollment Decline: Forecasting and Long-Run Sustainability

5. **Enrollment decline drivers and variance**: The 22% enrollment decline over the past decade and the 15% projected decline by 2035 are aggregate figures — what are the underlying demographic drivers (birth rate trends, out-migration, housing stock, economic base) in each sub-district, and how much variance exists in the forecast depending on economic development scenarios for the county?

6. **Per-pupil funding unit economics at scale**: Given that consolidation increases per-pupil state allocation by approximately 12%, what is the per-pupil cost structure under consolidation at 2026 enrollment versus 2035 projected enrollment, and what enrollment floor exists below which even the consolidated district cannot operate at sustainable unit economics?

7. **Staffing-to-enrollment ratio optimization**: With 14 teaching vacancies currently unfilled across three districts, what is the optimal teacher-to-student ratio at different consolidated enrollment levels, and how does the consolidated district's staffing model compare to rural consolidated districts of similar size that have achieved fiscal stability?

---

## Governance Architecture and Decision Variables

8. **Board seat allocation and power balance**: The consolidated district will require a new unified board, but the three districts have different sizes, tax bases, and community cultures — what governance models (weighted representation, equal seats, geographic subdistricts) have been used in comparable rural consolidations, and what are the measurable outcomes in terms of decision equity and community trust for each model?

9. **Collective bargaining reconciliation variables**: Three separate union contracts with different salary scales, benefits, seniority ladders, and working conditions need to be reconciled into a single agreement — what are the specific divergence points across the three contracts, what is the estimated cost range of bringing all three to a common standard, and which contract provisions are legally required to be maintained versus negotiable in the state's public employee labor law framework?

10. **Facility decision criteria framework**: Decisions about which of the 7 buildings to keep, repurpose, or close involve at least four distinct analytical dimensions (structural condition, enrollment proximity, community use, financial carrying cost) — what does a decision matrix look like when these dimensions are scored for each building, and where do the buildings cluster when scored together?

---

## Implementation Sequencing and Risk Variables

11. **Critical path and decision dependencies**: Consolidation requires voter approval in all three districts, union contract negotiation, governance design, facility decisions, and transportation system redesign — which of these decision tracks are serial dependencies versus parallel workstreams, and what is the minimum realistic implementation timeline from vote to operational consolidation?

12. **Transportation cost modeling at geographic scale**: A unified district spanning a full rural county will have significantly longer average bus routes and higher transportation costs per pupil than three smaller districts — what does the transportation cost model look like at different facility configuration scenarios (e.g., 3 buildings retained vs. 2 vs. 1), and at what point do transportation costs offset the administrative savings the consolidation is premised on?

---

## Comparables and Evidence Base

13. **Rural consolidation outcome data**: Comparable rural school consolidations in similar demographic and fiscal contexts have documented outcomes — what patterns emerge in the evidence on academic performance, teacher retention, fiscal sustainability, and community cohesion outcomes at 5- and 10-year post-consolidation marks, and what distinguishes consolidations that achieved their projected savings from those that did not?
