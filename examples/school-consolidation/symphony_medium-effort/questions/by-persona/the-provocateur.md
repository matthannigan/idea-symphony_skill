# Brainstorming Questions from The Provocateur: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## What If the Problem Is Backwards

1. **Decline as signal, not failure**: What if declining enrollment isn't a problem to be solved but a message the county hasn't decoded yet — and what might the community be revealing about itself that no feasibility study will capture?

2. **Consolidation as symptom**: Imagine the opposite were true: what if the real cause of fiscal distress isn't having three districts, but something upstream that consolidation will merely hide for a decade? What would you have to believe about this county's future for consolidation to be the wrong move even if every dollar figure checks out?

---

## Deliberately Making It Worse

3. **Weaponized identity**: Here's something absurd, but stay with me — what if each town leaned all the way in and made its school *more* distinct, more expensive, and more locally irreplaceable before any vote? What does that extreme scenario reveal about which community attachments are genuinely non-negotiable versus which ones dissolve the moment a compelling alternative is offered?

4. **The consolidation that fails on purpose**: What if the county designed a consolidation process that was *intended* to fail the first vote — a deliberate loss that surfaced every objection publicly before the real proposal launched? What would a strategically sacrificed first attempt teach planners that no feasibility study can?

---

## Inverting Who Holds the Power

5. **Students as the deciding institution**: Imagine the consolidation vote was held not by adult taxpayers in three districts but by the ~1,800 students currently enrolled. What governance structures, facility decisions, and identity arguments would survive that electorate — and what does the gap between their verdict and the adults' tell you about whose interests the current system actually serves?

6. **The district that opts out wins**: What if whichever of the three districts *refuses* to consolidate gets to keep the $1.2M in administrative savings the other two generate by merging? What dynamics does that inversion reveal about how the current negotiation is structuring incentives — and are those incentives pointing toward the outcome everyone claims to want?

---

## Collisions Across Scale and Time

7. **Solve for 2055, not 2035**: The projections stop at 2035, but enrollment has been declining for a decade and the smallest district has two years of runway. What if you designed the consolidated district not for the enrollment you'll have in ten years but for the enrollment you'll have in thirty — and what decisions made today would look catastrophically different under that longer frame?
