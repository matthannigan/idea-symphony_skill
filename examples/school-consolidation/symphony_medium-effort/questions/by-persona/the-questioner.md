# Brainstorming Questions from The Questioner: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## Hidden Assumptions in the Financial Case

1. **The savings estimate baseline**: The $1.2M administrative savings estimate assumes three separate administrative functions can simply be merged — but what if each district's administrative structure reflects locally negotiated constraints, union protections, or statutory obligations that would survive consolidation and limit actual savings?

2. **Per-pupil formula timing**: Why do we assume the 12% per-pupil funding increase would materialize at consolidation rather than phase in over several years, and what happens to the fiscal case if state formula changes lag the transition costs by two or three budget cycles?

3. **The deferred maintenance inheritance**: How do we determine which district's deferred maintenance liability is absorbed under consolidation versus which buildings get closed — and what if closing the most deteriorated buildings is politically impossible because each one anchors a different town's identity?

4. **Enrollment decline as fixed context**: What if the enrollment decline projections are themselves shaped by the current condition of these schools — meaning that consolidation, by improving program offerings and facilities, could partially reverse the trend rather than simply managing a predetermined decline?

---

## What Voter Approval Actually Requires

5. **Defining "winning" in three simultaneous referenda**: What would it take to construct a consolidation proposal that creates genuine winners — not just "less bad outcomes" — for residents in each of the three districts, and how different would that proposal look from one designed primarily to satisfy fiscal and administrative logic?

6. **The sequence problem**: If all three districts must approve, what happens when the first two pass but the third fails — and have we thought through whether the sequencing of the vote (simultaneous versus staggered) changes the political calculus in ways that could either help or hurt passage?

7. **Opponents as informants**: What if the "our school IS our town" resistance contains genuine information about what consolidated governance would actually destroy, rather than just being sentiment to be managed — and how would the consolidation proposal change if those concerns were treated as design requirements rather than obstacles?

---

## Governance Structure and Long-Term Accountability

8. **Board composition and power geography**: How would the governance structure of the new unified board ensure that residents of the smallest, most financially distressed district retain meaningful voice — and what historical precedents from other rural consolidations show that early governance design either preserves or erases that voice over time?

9. **The incentive inversion after consolidation**: Once consolidation occurs, what accountability mechanisms would prevent the new unified board from making facility and resource decisions that systematically favor the largest or most affluent former district, particularly when those decisions are framed as neutral efficiency choices?

10. **What "unified" actually means**: Why do we assume a single governance structure is required to capture the financial benefits — and are there federated or shared-services models that would generate similar savings while preserving more local control, and if so, why have those been set aside?

---

## The Three Contracts and the Staffing Reality

11. **Reconciliation versus renegotiation**: What if reconciling three separate union contracts isn't primarily a legal or procedural challenge but a political one — and what leverage do teachers currently hold that would increase, decrease, or shift if consolidation moves forward, and how does that change the negotiating dynamic?

12. **Vacancy causes versus consolidation remedies**: The 14 teaching vacancies last year — do we actually know why those positions went unfilled, and what evidence exists that consolidation would address those specific causes rather than simply restructuring around a persistent rural teacher shortage?

---

## Facilities, Community, and the Long Arc

13. **The building as the bet**: If facility decisions are made before the consolidation vote, communities can evaluate what they're actually agreeing to — but if they're deferred until after, voters are being asked to approve an open-ended restructuring. What is the right sequence, and what does each sequence signal about who is trusted to make those decisions?
