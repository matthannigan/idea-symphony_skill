## Per-Topic Rationale

### Topic Cluster 01: Financial Case, Transition Costs, and Tax Burden

- **Topic classification:** Financial/Business. The cluster is anchored on dollar figures (the $1.2M projected savings, $4.2M deferred maintenance, ~12% per-pupil funding bonus), break-even modeling, formula dependency over a 10–20 year window, debt assumption sequencing, and tax equalization.
- **Inner Ring Pick 1 — Accountant:** "Strong Fit" for Financial/Business. The cluster requires honest accounting of one-time transition costs vs. recurring savings, stranded-cost treatment for closed buildings, bond and debt assumption modeling, and equalization scenario analysis — exactly what an Accountant lens is designed to surface. This is a topic where the over-financialization anti-pattern does not apply: the financials are genuinely load-bearing on the consolidation decision.
- **Inner Ring Pick 2 — Systems Thinker:** "Good Fit" for Financial/Business. Multiple questions in this cluster explicitly call out feedback dynamics: the deferred-maintenance compounding curve ($200K roof becoming $800K), enrollment-revenue-quality spirals, the long-fuse risk that consolidation removes the political rationale for the per-pupil subsidy. Systems Thinker maps these reinforcing/balancing loops in a way Accountant alone would not.
- **Diversity check:** Accountant (specialized lens) and Systems Thinker (External Perspectives Triad) come from different families. Pragmatist sits in Analytical Core. Devil's Advocate sits in Challenge Triad. Four families represented — well within the ≤3-from-same-family ceiling.

### Topic Cluster 02: Voter Approval and the Three-District Coalition

- **Topic classification:** Governance/Political. The cluster is structurally about coalition formation across three independent electorates, asymmetric opposition profiles, sequencing of endorsements, informal gatekeepers, and state-agency leverage.
- **Inner Ring Pick 1 — Politician:** "Strong Fit" for Governance/Political. The cluster's central question — how to assemble a minimum winning coalition across three asymmetric districts where the weakest coalition wins — is the prototypical Politician lens. Multi-stakeholder governance and coalition building are explicitly named as Politician's "Include When" criteria.
- **Inner Ring Pick 2 — Audience Advocate:** "Good Fit" for Governance/Political and "Strong Fit" for community-facing dimensions. The cluster requires segmentation of voter blocs (fiscal skeptics, identity defenders, union households, recent arrivals, renters) and tailored persuasion paths per district. Audience Advocate maps stakeholder needs without collapsing them into one undifferentiated electorate.
- **Diversity check:** Politician (specialized lens), Audience Advocate (Human-Centered Triad), Pragmatist (Analytical Core), Devil's Advocate (Challenge Triad) — four families.

### Topic Cluster 03: Governance Architecture for the Unified Board

- **Topic classification:** Governance/Political. The cluster is about board composition, weighted vs. equal representation, geographic subdistricts, federation alternatives, and accountability mechanisms preventing post-consolidation drift toward the largest community.
- **Inner Ring Pick 1 — Politician:** "Strong Fit" for Governance/Political. Designing a board structure that gives each former district credible voice without producing fragmentation is a textbook political-settlement question. Politician brings the institutional vocabulary (weighted seats, staggered terms, supermajority thresholds, federated alternatives) that this cluster needs.
- **Inner Ring Pick 2 — Audience Advocate:** "Good Fit" for Governance/Political. Each former district must be able to read the board structure as preserving its voice. Audience Advocate ensures that the three communities are treated as distinct constituencies with distinct legitimacy thresholds, rather than a single undifferentiated electorate.
- **Diversity check:** Same as Cluster 02 — four distinct families. No same-family stacking.

### Topic Cluster 04: Union Contract Reconciliation and the Staffing Reality

- **Topic classification:** Governance/Political with significant financial and human-experience dimensions. Reconciling three union contracts is a multi-year political negotiation with three bargaining units, but it also has a "smallest district's teachers" lived-experience dimension and a financial-erosion-of-savings dimension.
- **Inner Ring Pick 1 — Politician:** "Strong Fit" for Governance/Political. Three bargaining units with different leadership, different memberships, and different fears is a coalition problem with leverage points and sequencing — Politician territory. The "which union's support is the most achievable early win" question is precisely a coalition-formation question.
- **Inner Ring Pick 2 — Empath:** Selected over Audience Advocate here because the cluster contains explicit lived-experience questions about teachers in the smallest district whose experience may be "erased in a 'harmonized' contract" and about why 14 vacancies actually went unfilled (compensation, isolation, professional growth, housing). Empath's emotional-resonance lens surfaces what teachers fear, want, and would lose — not just what they need on a stakeholder map. Audience Advocate is Pick 2 on adjacent governance clusters; Empath is the more differentiated pick here.
- **Diversity check:** Politician (specialized), Empath (Human-Centered Triad), Pragmatist (Analytical Core), Devil's Advocate (Challenge Triad) — four families.

### Topic Cluster 05: Facilities Decisions, Closures, and Symbolic Loss

- **Topic classification:** User-Facing/Community with strong implementation/political-sequencing dimension. The cluster is about a decision matrix scoring 7 buildings on structural, enrollment, financial, and political dimensions — but every question is anchored on what a closure feels like to the community whose school is taken: "the empty building at the center of town," "the night the results come in."
- **Inner Ring Pick 1 — Storyteller:** "Strong Fit" for User-Facing/Community and explicitly "Include When" for community/institutional topics with diverse user journeys. Two of the questions are framed as named-character scenarios ("a parent in the smallest district sitting at a kitchen table"). Storyteller's lived-experience narratives are the natural mode for those prompts.
- **Inner Ring Pick 2 — Empath:** "Strong Fit" for User-Facing/Community. Symbolic loss, identity grief, and the "story of change rather than abandonment" framing all sit in Empath's emotional-resonance register. Empath complements Storyteller (gut feeling vs. named-character narrative) rather than duplicating it.
- **Diversity check:** Storyteller and Empath are both in the Human-Centered Triad — that's 2 from one family, well under the 3-cap. The cluster genuinely warrants this human-centered concentration because the political voltage of facility decisions is precisely the lived experience of loss. Devil's Advocate (Challenge Triad) and Pragmatist (Analytical Core) provide the counterweight.

### Topic Cluster 06: Transportation, Equity, and Daily Lived Experience

- **Topic classification:** User-Facing/Community with implementation/operational dimension. Cost modeling at different facility configurations sits next to questions about students with IEPs, English language learners, families without reliable cars, and "a day in the life" for the eighth-grader at the geographic edge of the county.
- **Inner Ring Pick 1 — Audience Advocate:** "Strong Fit" for User-Facing/Community. The cluster explicitly names special populations — IEP students, English language learners, students experiencing housing instability, students in poverty — and asks whether they are being explicitly tracked. Audience Advocate's stakeholder-segmentation discipline is what surfaces who is being modeled as "typical" and who is being averaged away.
- **Inner Ring Pick 2 — Storyteller:** "Strong Fit" for User-Facing/Community. The "day in the life, redefined" question is framed as a named-character narrative. Storyteller's lived-experience mode is a direct match.
- **Diversity check:** Audience Advocate and Storyteller are both Human-Centered Triad — 2 from one family, under the 3-cap. The cluster genuinely warrants this because equity questions are most clearly seen through stakeholder mapping plus daily-experience narrative. Pragmatist and Devil's Advocate cover the operational and critical registers.

### Topic Cluster 07: Enrollment Decline and the Long Arc

- **Topic classification:** Strategic/Vision. The cluster spans 2026–2055 timeframes, asks whether consolidation merely defers the underlying demographic trend, and contains explicit reframes ("decline as design signal," "decline as signal not failure," "solve for 2055 not 2035").
- **Inner Ring Pick 1 — Futurist:** "Strong Fit" for Strategic/Vision. Trend-grounded extrapolation is exactly what this cluster requires — birth rate trends, out-migration, housing stock, economic-base scenarios, the 30-year frame. Futurist anchors projections in observable trend data without dismissing them as mere forecasts.
- **Inner Ring Pick 2 — Visionary:** "Strong Fit" for Strategic/Vision. The reframe questions ("design an institution deliberately sized for what serves a small dispersed rural community" rather than benchmarking against growth metrics; "a message the county hasn't decoded") are paradigm-shift questions. Visionary's "ignore current reality" stance is the right register for those prompts. Forward-Looking Pair (Futurist + Visionary) is explicitly recommended for "what should this become?" questions.
- **Diversity check:** Futurist and Visionary are the Forward-Looking Pair — 2 from one family. The cluster genuinely warrants completing the pair because tactical lenses cannot answer 30-year framing questions. Pragmatist and Devil's Advocate provide reality-check counterweight.

### Topic Cluster 08: Community Identity, Trust, and Whose Voice Is Heard

- **Topic classification:** User-Facing/Community. The cluster is about "our school is our town" as load-bearing infrastructure, missing voices (renters, recent arrivals, parents working two jobs, students themselves), unnamed grief, asymmetric loss, trust deficits from prior unfulfilled promises, and ongoing accountability.
- **Inner Ring Pick 1 — Empath:** "Strong Fit" for User-Facing/Community. Grief that doesn't get named, identity attachment as principled (not transactional) opposition, and the emotional load of "our school is our town" all sit in Empath's register. The cluster explicitly asks for grief to be acknowledged "as a legitimate part of the process rather than treating it as resistance to be overcome."
- **Inner Ring Pick 2 — Audience Advocate:** "Strong Fit" for User-Facing/Community. The "whose voices are missing" question is a stakeholder-mapping question — students, renters, recent arrivals, IEP families, English language learners, parents working two jobs. Audience Advocate's discipline is to redesign engagement around who is least likely to show up rather than who is loudest.
- **Diversity check:** Empath and Audience Advocate are both Human-Centered Triad — 2 from one family, under the 3-cap. The cluster is the strongest human-centered cluster in the session and genuinely warrants this concentration. Pragmatist and Devil's Advocate balance it.

### Topic Cluster 09: What Already Works, and What a Generative Consolidation Could Unlock

- **Topic classification:** Strategic/Vision (novel/generative). This is the strengths-based, forward-looking register — peak moments of cross-district collaboration, identity infrastructure already in place, the design horizon where consolidation produces something celebrated rather than tolerated, and explicit cross-domain analogs (the software-migration "interoperability before integration" parallel).
- **Inner Ring Pick 1 — Visionary:** "Strong Fit" for Strategic/Vision. The cluster contains explicit "what could this become" prompts — a 2032 case study other districts copy, a governance model genuinely trusted by all three communities, a first design principle. Visionary's paradigm-shift register is the right mode.
- **Inner Ring Pick 2 — Connector:** "Good Fit" for Strategic/Vision and explicitly "Strong Fit" for Novel/Ambiguous. Question 7 is itself a structural-parallel prompt importing the software-migration playbook. Connector is the persona whose explicit job is to identify those structural parallels (rural hospitals, post offices, utility mergers, software cutovers) — all of which appear across the QUESTIONS.md as latent parallels worth exploring more deeply. The novel/ambiguous-topic check in the selection algorithm explicitly prefers Analogist or Connector here; Connector is selected because the cluster's emphasis is on identifying parallel domains and existing strengths to build on, not (yet) on importing concrete solutions.
- **Diversity check:** Visionary (Forward-Looking Pair), Connector (External Perspectives Triad), Pragmatist (Analytical Core), Devil's Advocate (Challenge Triad) — four distinct families.
