# Brainstorming Questions from The Analyst: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## Financial Decomposition and Break-Even Analysis

1. **True net savings timeline**: The REQUEST estimates $1.2M in annual administrative savings, but what are the one-time transition costs — legal, severance, IT system integration, boundary realignment — and at what year does cumulative net savings actually turn positive, assuming a realistic 3-to-5-year transition period?

2. **Deferred maintenance triage**: Given the $4.2M in deferred building maintenance spread across seven buildings, what is the marginal cost per student of bringing each building up to code versus the cost of consolidating students into fewer buildings and mothballing or disposing of the rest — and which scenario yields the lower 10-year total cost of ownership?

3. **Structural deficit root causes**: What portion of each district's structural deficit is attributable to fixed overhead (facilities, administration, debt service) versus variable costs that scale with enrollment, and how does that split change the calculus for whether consolidation solves the problem or merely merges three insolvent systems into one larger insolvent system?

4. **Per-pupil funding step change**: The state formula would increase per-pupil allocation by roughly 12% upon consolidation; breaking this down — how does that translate to actual dollar inflows against projected enrollment curves through 2035, and what enrollment floor must the consolidated district maintain to preserve that funding tier?

5. **Property tax base equalization**: The three districts likely have different assessed-value-per-pupil ratios driving different local tax yields; what is the range of local levy rates across districts today, what does equalization imply for tax burdens in the highest-rate district versus the lowest-rate district, and how does that redistribution affect the political feasibility of voter approval in each jurisdiction?

---

## Enrollment Trajectory and Demand-Side Risk

6. **Disaggregated enrollment drivers**: Enrollment has declined 22% over the past decade, but what are the component causes — out-migration of working-age families, declining birth rates, private/charter school growth, or some combination — and does each cause respond differently to policy levers the district could actually control post-consolidation?

7. **Consolidation's effect on enrollment decline**: What evidence exists from comparable rural consolidations that the act of closing or merging schools itself accelerates out-migration (the "school closure = town death" feedback loop), and how should the financial model stress-test for a scenario where consolidation triggers an additional 5–10% enrollment loss beyond the baseline 15% projection?

8. **Break-even enrollment threshold**: What is the minimum student population at which the consolidated district's operating model is financially sustainable under the new state funding formula, and how many years of runway does the projected 2035 enrollment leave before that threshold is breached?

---

## Operational and Facility Decision Framework

9. **Facility scoring matrix**: What criteria — condition, capacity utilization, geographic centrality, transportation cost implication, community function — should be weighted in a systematic facility decision framework, and what does a rigorous application of that framework recommend for each of the seven buildings?

10. **Transportation cost delta**: Consolidating students across a larger geographic footprint will increase transportation costs; what is the estimated cost per route-mile, how many additional route-miles does each plausible facility configuration add, and at what configuration does the transportation cost increase consume a material portion of the projected administrative savings?

11. **Union contract reconciliation cost structure**: Reconciling three separate teachers' union contracts into one typically means the consolidated district defaults to the most favorable terms from each contract across each dimension; what is the estimated cost of that ratchet effect on compensation and benefits, and how does it net against the administrative reduction savings in year-one through year-five?

---

## Decision Criteria and Risk-Adjusted Scenario Modeling

12. **Scenario framework for the board**: Breaking this decision into its key variables — state funding stability, enrollment trajectory, facility disposition proceeds, union contract settlement terms, and transition costs — what is a structured scenario matrix (optimistic / base / downside) that maps each variable to a 10-year fiscal outcome, and what probability-weighted net present value does each scenario yield?

13. **No-consolidation baseline cost**: What is the quantified cost of inaction — specifically, when does the smallest district exhaust its fund balance, what happens to its students and liabilities at that point, and what fiscal contagion risk does that event create for the other two districts — so the board is comparing consolidation against a realistic alternative rather than the status quo?
