# Brainstorming Questions from The Visionary: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## Reimagining the School as a Community Institution

1. **Beyond the classroom building**: What if the consolidated district didn't treat "school" as a fixed building at all — but instead designed an institution that exists as a living network of community spaces, homes, farms, and gathering places, where the concept of a school campus as the center of learning is abandoned entirely in favor of the county itself becoming the learning environment?

2. **The town-school inversion**: Imagine if we completely flipped the premise — instead of asking "which towns lose their school," what if we asked how a single unified district could become the institution through which all three towns gain something they never had individually: a center of civic life, intergenerational gathering, and economic development that no single small district could have funded or imagined on its own?

## Reinventing What Consolidation Produces

3. **A new institutional category**: What if this consolidation were designed not to produce a "larger version of what existed before," but to create a categorically new kind of rural public institution — one that functions simultaneously as a school, a community college, a public health outpost, an economic development hub, and a civic gathering space — and what would need to be true about governance, funding, and facility design for that to be viable?

4. **The enrollment decline as permission**: What if declining enrollment, rather than being treated as a crisis to manage, were treated as the permission structure to completely reinvent what a rural K-12 education looks like — smaller class sizes, radical personalization, project-based learning embedded in the county's agricultural and natural economy — and how would the financial model of the consolidated district need to change to support that vision rather than replicate the cost structure of larger urban districts?

## Coalition and Identity as Design Material

5. **Identity without buildings**: What if "our school is our town" is actually pointing at something real and valuable — not a resistance to overcome, but a design requirement — and the consolidation were structured so that each community's identity is not housed in a building that might close, but is embedded into the curriculum, traditions, governance, and culture of the new institution in ways that are permanent and visible regardless of where classes are held?

## The Long Horizon

6. **Designing for 2045, not 2026**: If enrollment is projected to fall another 15% by 2035, what does the district look like in 2045 — and rather than designing a consolidation optimized for today's enrollment and cost pressures, what would it mean to design the new institution from the end state backward, building only what will still make sense when the county has perhaps 1,200 students, and what does that imply about facilities, staffing models, and governance structure?

7. **The "no-school" thought experiment**: Imagine a world where compulsory place-based schooling hadn't been invented yet, and this rural county were designing a learning institution from scratch today — with current technology, understanding of pedagogy, and knowledge of this specific community's assets and needs — what would it look like, and which elements of that imagined institution could actually be built into the consolidated district as it launches rather than inherited from the three districts being merged?
