# Brainstorming Questions from The First Principles Thinker: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## The Core Function of a School District

1. **What is a school district actually for?**: At its core, a school district exists to deliver educational outcomes to children — so if we strip away administrative history, legal boundaries, and community sentiment, what organizational structure would best deliver those outcomes for 1,800 students spread across this rural county, and how closely does "three districts" or "one district" approximate that ideal?

2. **Why do district boundaries exist here at all?**: The three district boundaries were drawn at some point in history under conditions that no longer hold — what were those original rationales (population distribution, transportation technology, local tax base logic), and do any of them still apply strongly enough to justify preserving separate governance structures?

3. **What problem is consolidation actually solving?**: The feasibility study frames consolidation as the solution, but what is the fundamental problem — is it insufficient revenue, inefficient cost structure, poor educational outcomes, or inability to attract staff — and does consolidation address the root cause or merely the symptom of structural underfunding of rural education?

---

## Rebuilding the Financial Model From Zero

4. **What would the budget look like if designed today?**: If we erased the three existing budgets and designed a single financial structure from scratch for 1,800 students in this geography, what would the essential cost components be — and how do the actual combined budgets of $28M compare to that clean-slate baseline, revealing where legacy structure is generating waste?

5. **Which cost savings are structural and which are illusory?**: The $1.2M administrative savings figure assumes eliminating duplicate leadership roles, but what costs will inevitably re-emerge in a larger unified district (additional coordination layers, longer transportation routes, larger facilities management overhead) — and what is the net savings after accounting for those second-order costs?

6. **What does per-pupil spending need to be to deliver the promised program?**: Rather than starting from current budgets and finding cuts, what is the minimum per-pupil expenditure required to staff and operate the educational program this community expects — and does the post-consolidation funding formula actually close that gap, or does it merely reduce the deficit?

---

## Questioning the "School Is the Town" Assumption

7. **What community functions does the school actually perform?**: The "our school is our town" framing treats school closure as community death, but what specific social, economic, and civic functions does each school building actually serve — gathering space, employer, identity anchor, youth activity hub — and which of those functions could survive or be redesigned to persist under a consolidated structure?

8. **What is the counterfactual community cost of not consolidating?**: If the smallest district reaches insolvency in two years and state receivership follows, what happens to community identity and local control then — and is the community resistance to consolidation actually choosing between "our school" and "no school," rather than between "our school" and "a different school"?

---

## Governance Built for the Actual Problem

9. **What governance structure would a blank-sheet designer choose for this geography?**: Setting aside the standard unified-district model, what governance structures exist (regional service agencies, federated districts, educational cooperatives) that preserve some local identity and decision-making while still achieving financial consolidation — and why has the feasibility study framed the choice as binary rather than exploring hybrid models?
