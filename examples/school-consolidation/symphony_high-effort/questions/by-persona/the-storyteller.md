# Brainstorming Questions from The Storyteller: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## The Human Face of Closure

1. **The last day of school**: Imagine walking through the hallways of Millbrook Elementary on its final day as an independent school — what does that moment feel like for the third-grader who has known only this building, the teacher who has spent twenty years in the same classroom, and the parent who graduated from this school herself, and what does that emotional weight mean for how consolidation leaders should sequence and communicate facility decisions?

2. **The town without its school**: Picture the diner conversation in a small farming town six months after its school closes — the Little League coach who used the gym, the librarian whose reading programs fed into the kindergarten, the realtor who watched home values shift — and what narrative about community futures would have made those residents feel heard rather than discarded during the consolidation process?

3. **The commute that changed everything**: Walk through a morning in the life of a seventh-grader whose bus ride goes from fifteen minutes to forty-five after consolidation — what does that extra hour mean for her participation in after-school clubs, her parents' work schedules, her sense of belonging at a larger school — and how should transportation design account for these ripple effects?

---

## New Beginnings and What They Could Look Like

4. **The first day at Unified**: Imagine the first day of school in the newly consolidated district — what would have to be true about the transition for students from all three communities to feel they belong to something new rather than something lost, and what symbols, traditions, or rituals might a new unified district deliberately design to create that sense of shared identity?

5. **The teacher who stayed**: Picture a veteran teacher who decided to stay through consolidation rather than retire early — what would have made that choice feel worthwhile to her, how would she describe the transition to a friend a year later if it went well, and what does her story reveal about the staffing and culture decisions that determine whether consolidation retains institutional knowledge or accelerates its loss?

---

## Stakeholders at the Crossroads

6. **The union rep in the room**: Imagine a teachers' union representative who has spent years advocating for her members under one contract, now sitting at a table with two other union reps whose contracts protect different terms — what is the story she needs to be able to tell her members about what they gained (not just what they didn't lose) from contract reconciliation, and what does that suggest about the sequencing and substance of labor negotiations?

7. **The fiscal conservative who voted yes**: Picture a property-tax-sensitive voter in the wealthiest of the three districts — someone who had every reason to vote no — who ended up voting yes for consolidation; what story did she hear, what data became personally meaningful to her, and what moment shifted her from skeptic to reluctant supporter?

---

## The 2035 Scenario

8. **Looking back from 2035**: Imagine a county board member in 2035 reflecting on the consolidation vote — in the version of events where it succeeded and the unified district is thriving, what were the two or three pivotal decisions that made the difference, and in the version where it narrowly failed and the smallest district went insolvent, what were the moments when a different choice might have changed the outcome?
