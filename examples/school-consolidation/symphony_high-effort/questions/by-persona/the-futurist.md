# Brainstorming Questions from The Futurist: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## Demographic Trajectories and the Shape of the District in 2035

1. **Enrollment floor or freefall**: Given the documented acceleration of rural outmigration — particularly among working-age families — what enrollment scenario should the consolidated district plan for as its operational baseline by 2035, and how does that projection change which facilities, staffing levels, and transportation routes are financially viable to maintain?

2. **Demographic composition shifts**: Rural districts nationally are experiencing a growing share of students with higher support needs (special education, English-language learners, Title I eligibility) even as overall enrollment declines — if this trend applies here, how should the consolidated district's staffing model, facilities configuration, and per-pupil cost projections account for a smaller but more resource-intensive student population?

3. **Housing and development wildcards**: Given that rural counties near regional employment hubs are experiencing episodic in-migration driven by remote work trends, what triggers or thresholds should the consolidation plan identify now so the district can scale capacity upward if enrollment reverses, rather than locking into an irreversibly downsized infrastructure?

---

## State Funding Formula Shifts and Revenue Vulnerability

4. **Formula change risk window**: State per-pupil funding formulas for rural districts are under active revision in many states, driven by equity litigation and legislative rural caucus pressure — what is the timeline for anticipated formula changes in this state, and how does the consolidation financial model hold up if the 12% per-pupil funding increase materializes more slowly, or is offset by new consolidation-related funding reductions?

5. **Federal rural education funding trajectories**: Federal programs targeting rural schools (REAP, Small Rural School Achievement) are subject to reauthorization cycles and have historically faced uncertain funding — what is the consolidated district's financial exposure if these revenue streams decline over the next five years, and how should the budget model treat them: as reliable baseline or as variable supplemental income?

6. **Property tax base trajectory**: Given the documented decline in rural assessed property values in counties experiencing population loss, what is the realistic trajectory of local property tax revenue over the next decade, and how does a property tax equalization plan across three districts with different current rates and assessed values position the consolidated district when the tax base itself is shrinking?

---

## The Staffing Crisis as a Structural Trend, Not a Temporary Gap

7. **Teacher pipeline trajectory**: The national teacher shortage is accelerating in rural markets — rural districts are losing ground to suburban competitors on compensation, quality-of-life amenities, and career advancement pathways — so what structural advantages does consolidation create for teacher recruitment and retention that go beyond administrative savings, and are those advantages sufficient to close the gap within the 3-5 year window before the current vacancy rate becomes an instructional crisis?

8. **Administrator succession planning**: Within the next five to eight years, a documented wave of Baby Boomer school administrators will retire across rural districts — what does the consolidation timeline need to accomplish before that transition to avoid a situation where the new unified district faces both a governance transition and a leadership vacuum simultaneously?

---

## Facility Decisions as 30-Year Infrastructure Commitments

9. **Deferred maintenance as a strategic inflection point**: The $4.2M in deferred building maintenance represents a forcing function — but given the documented trend toward aging rural school infrastructure becoming an asset liability rather than a community anchor, how should the facility decision framework evaluate whether renovating underutilized buildings creates a 30-year financial obligation that outlasts the district's enrollment base?

10. **Repurposing trajectory for closed school buildings**: Across rural communities nationally, closed school buildings are increasingly being converted to community health hubs, workforce training centers, early childhood programs, and senior services — given that trend, what decision criteria and community engagement process should the consolidation plan use to evaluate repurposing pathways that preserve community anchors while relieving the district of ongoing operational costs?

---

## Governance and Coalition-Building for a Shifting Political Landscape

11. **Rural governance consolidation as a trend**: County-level governance consolidation across school, fire, library, and municipal services is an accelerating trend in states facing rural fiscal stress — how should the new unified board's governance structure be designed now to remain compatible with potential future service-sharing arrangements with other county entities, rather than locking into a structure that would require re-negotiation later?

12. **Voter approval timing and political environment**: Given the documented trend toward declining voter turnout and increasing anti-consolidation sentiment in rural communities facing economic anxiety, what is the optimal window in the next two to three years to place a consolidation vote — balancing the urgency of the smallest district's two-year financial runway against the need for sufficient coalition-building time to overcome identity-based resistance?
