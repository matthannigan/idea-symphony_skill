# Brainstorming Questions from The Questioner: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## Hidden Assumptions About Why Consolidation Is the Answer

1. **What problem are we actually solving**: If the three districts are experiencing enrollment decline because families are leaving the county, how does consolidating them into one district change the underlying demographic trajectory — and what would it mean if consolidation saves the district financially but not educationally?

2. **Who benefits from the status quo**: What interests — administrative, political, contractual, or community-identity — are structurally served by the current three-district arrangement, and how much of the resistance to consolidation is about those interests rather than genuine concern for students?

3. **Why assume bigger is better**: What evidence exists that consolidated rural districts produce better educational outcomes than small ones, and under what conditions does consolidation actually improve teaching quality versus simply reducing administrative headcount?

4. **What if the feasibility study is wrong**: The commissioned study projects $1.2M in annual administrative savings — what assumptions underlie that figure, and what would have to be true for actual savings to be significantly lower or for transition costs to exceed a decade of projected savings?

5. **Why do we assume voter approval in all three districts is achievable**: What precedents exist from comparable rural consolidations, and what does failure in even one district vote imply for the entire effort?

---

## Unstated Trade-offs in the Financial Analysis

6. **What gets counted and what doesn't**: The $4.2M in deferred building maintenance is cited as a liability — but which facilities would the consolidated district close versus maintain, and how does the decision calculus change if closing a building triggers bond obligation acceleration or community litigation?

7. **How does tax base equalization actually work**: If the three districts have different property tax rates and assessed valuations, what does equalization mean for taxpayers in the district with the lowest current rate — and who loses in that arithmetic?

8. **What is the real cost of transition**: Beyond the headline administrative savings, what are the transition costs that typically get underestimated in consolidations — severance, system integration, new transportation routes, contract buyouts — and over what time horizon does consolidation become net-positive?

9. **What does the 12% per-pupil funding increase actually buy**: The state formula improvement sounds significant — but given that enrollment is projected to decline another 15% by 2035, what does the per-pupil gain look like in absolute dollar terms over a decade, and is it enough to close the structural deficit?

---

## The Community Identity Question Beneath the Financial One

10. **What do people mean when they say "our school is our town"**: Is this primarily about attachment to a physical building, fear of losing a community anchor, concern about student travel times, or worry that their town will be seen as subordinate to a larger one — and does the answer differ meaningfully across the three districts?

11. **What has been lost in comparable communities**: In rural areas where school consolidation happened a decade or more ago, what do community members say they actually lost versus what they feared losing, and are those accounts consistent with measurable outcomes like property values, civic participation, or youth retention?

12. **Why do we assume community identity can be preserved through programming**: If the plan involves closing one or more school buildings, what programming, naming conventions, or governance structures have actually worked in other consolidated districts to maintain distinct community identities — and what evidence exists that they work?

---

## Governance and Power in the New Structure

13. **Who controls the new board**: If the consolidated district creates a single elected board, how would board seats be apportioned across the three communities — by population, by geographic area, or by some other formula — and what happens to the two smaller towns' political power when the largest district's voters outnumber them?

14. **What does reconciling three union contracts actually mean in practice**: Beyond the headline of "three contracts becoming one," what specific provisions — seniority, pay scales, prep time, extra-duty assignments — are most likely to generate conflict, and which district's teachers end up with more versus less favorable terms?

15. **Who decides which buildings close**: Is the facility closure decision made by the new board, the county board, state officials, or some combination — and what happens if the communities whose buildings are closed mount a legal or political challenge after the consolidation vote has already passed?

---

## The Enrollment Decline Beneath the Consolidation Decision

16. **What is driving the enrollment decline**: Is the 22% enrollment drop over the past decade caused by outmigration of young families, declining birth rates, growth of private/charter alternatives, or some combination — and does consolidation address any of those root causes?

17. **What if enrollment continues to decline faster than projected**: The feasibility study presumably models specific enrollment scenarios — what does the financial picture look like under the pessimistic scenario, and at what enrollment threshold does even the consolidated district become financially unsustainable?

18. **Have we considered alternatives to consolidation**: What options besides full consolidation — shared services agreements, administrative mergers without district mergers, state receivership for the financially distressed district — have been evaluated, and why have they been judged insufficient compared to full consolidation?
