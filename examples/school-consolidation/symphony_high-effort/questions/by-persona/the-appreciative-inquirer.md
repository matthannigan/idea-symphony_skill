# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Rural School District Consolidation

---

## Discovery: When These Communities and Schools Have Worked at Their Best

1. **Peak moments of cross-district collaboration**: Think back to a time — a regional athletic competition, a shared arts program, an emergency response — when two or all three districts worked together and the result genuinely surprised people with how well it went. What was present in that moment that made collaboration feel natural rather than threatening?

2. **The school as community anchor**: What are the most vivid, specific examples of each district's school serving as the genuine heart of its community — the moments people point to when they say "that's why our school matters"? What structures, traditions, or relationships made those moments possible, and which of those could survive or even thrive in a unified district?

3. **Successful rural consolidations elsewhere**: Where has a rural school district consolidation genuinely worked — where communities say years later that it strengthened rather than diminished their sense of place? What were the conditions and decisions that made those cases succeed, and which of those conditions are present or buildable here?

4. **Talent that found a way**: Despite 14 vacancies, some teachers and administrators chose to stay or come to these districts. What drew them, what has kept them, and what does their presence reveal about real strengths in this community that a consolidated district could amplify rather than lose?

---

## Dream: A Unified District That Communities Could Love

5. **The consolidated district as a model worth emulating**: Imagine it is 2032 and a delegation from another rural county is visiting to learn from this consolidation's success. What do they see, hear, and experience that makes the trip worthwhile? What would parents, students, and teachers in each of the three original towns say has genuinely gotten better?

6. **What "our school IS our town" could become**: The phrase "our school IS our town" captures something real and precious. If a unified district could honor that identity rather than erase it — if students and parents could feel proud of both their home community and the larger district — what would that look and feel like? What new traditions or structures would signal that belonging wasn't lost, only expanded?

7. **Educational possibilities that weren't possible before**: What programs, pathways, or learning experiences could a unified district of 1,800 students offer that none of the three districts could sustain alone — experiences so compelling that families in each community would see consolidation as an expansion of opportunity rather than a loss?

---

## Design: Structures That Reliably Produce the Best Outcomes

8. **Building on what already bridges communities**: The three districts already share a county, likely share roads, emergency services, and some civic institutions. What existing cross-community structures — boards, organizations, events, relationships — have already built trust across town lines, and how could a consolidation process deliberately route through those existing bridges rather than trying to build new ones from scratch?

9. **Governance that makes communities feel genuinely represented**: Given that some communities will fear their voice shrinking in a larger board, what governance design — seat allocation, community advisory structures, reserved roles, decision-rights for local matters — would give residents in each of the three towns a credible reason to believe their community's concerns will be heard and taken seriously after consolidation?

10. **Facility decisions that strengthen rather than wound**: Rather than framing building closures as losses, what repurposing models — community centers, early childhood hubs, adult learning facilities, spaces for local organizations — have kept a school building alive as a community asset even after it stopped housing K-12 classes? Which of those models fits the specific character and needs of each town here?

---

## Destiny: First Steps and Commitments That Create Momentum

11. **The coalition that could win**: Who in each of the three communities already believes — or could come to believe — that this consolidation is genuinely in their town's interest, not just the county's? What do those potential champions need to feel confident and equipped to make the case to their neighbors, and what early wins could demonstrate that consolidation improves rather than diminishes community life?

12. **A first experiment that builds confidence**: Before any vote, what small-scale collaboration between the three districts — a shared program, a joint facility use, a combined extracurricular — could give residents in all three communities a lived experience of consolidation's benefits rather than just a promise? What would need to be true for that experiment to succeed visibly enough to shift the conversation?
