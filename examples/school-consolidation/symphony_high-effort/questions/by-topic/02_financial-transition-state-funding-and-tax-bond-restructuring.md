# Topic Cluster 02: Financial Transition, State Funding, and Tax/Bond Restructuring

## Questions

8. **Break-even timeline including illusory savings**: With $1.2M in projected annual administrative savings, what are the front-loaded one-time transition costs (severance for displaced administrators, contract buyouts, IT system integration, legal fees for union and bond restructuring, governance setup), and which costs will inevitably re-emerge in a larger entity (additional coordination layers, larger facilities-management overhead, longer transportation routes) — at what year does cumulative net savings actually turn positive across a realistic 3-to-5-year transition, and does that horizon land before or after the smallest district's 24-month insolvency cliff?
9. **Deferred-maintenance accountability**: The $4.2M deferred-maintenance liability is unevenly distributed across seven buildings — what prevents a consolidated board from inheriting all of it while accumulating new deferred maintenance as it postpones building decisions, kicking the problem down the road at greater scale, and how does the unified district gain leverage to triage rather than perpetuate the worst-deferred buildings?
10. **Per-pupil funding step-change in absolute dollars**: The state formula is projected to increase per-pupil allocation by ~12% upon consolidation — what does that translate to in actual annual dollar inflows against projected enrollment curves through 2035, what enrollment floor must the consolidated district maintain to preserve that funding tier, and how vulnerable is the projection to anticipated state formula revisions, REAP/Small Rural School Achievement reauthorization risk, or new consolidation-related funding offsets?
11. **Property tax equalization winners and losers**: Given different assessed-value-per-pupil ratios, levy rates, and tax bases across the three districts, what is the redistribution arithmetic of equalization — who pays more, who pays less, by how much — what phase-in mechanisms exist to avoid imposing immediate rate shocks, and how does that redistribution affect the political feasibility of voter approval in each jurisdiction (especially the highest-rate district)?
12. **Bond obligation allocation across the new entity**: Each district carries outstanding bond debt tied to specific facilities and paid by its own tax base — how does consolidation legally restructure those obligations, which taxpayers become responsible for debt that funded buildings they don't use, and what is the total consolidated debt load against a property tax base whose assessed values may be declining alongside population?
