# Topic Cluster 04: Facilities, Transportation, and Operating Economics

## Questions

20. **Facility scoring matrix and irreversibility**: What weighted criteria — condition, capacity utilization, geographic centrality, transportation cost implication, community-anchor function, 30-year financial-obligation horizon — should structure a systematic facility decision framework across the seven buildings, and what monitoring signal would indicate that a closed building should have stayed open before the decision becomes practically irreversible?
21. **Total cost of ownership and bond-acceleration risk**: For each plausible facility configuration, what is the 10-year total cost of ownership (utilities, maintenance, custodial staff, capital improvements at surviving buildings, plus mothballing or disposition of the rest), and which closure decisions trigger bond-obligation acceleration, community litigation, or other contingent liabilities that the financial model has not quantified?
22. **Repurposing as community-anchor preservation and balance-sheet lever**: Rather than framing closure as loss, what repurposing models — community centers, early childhood hubs, adult learning facilities, community health outposts, workforce-training centers, senior services — have kept a school building alive as a community asset after K-12 use ends, what are the realistic market value and rental-income potentials per building in this rural context, and how should proceeds be structured (endowment, capital reserve, one-time debt paydown) to maximize long-term fiscal stability?
23. **Transportation cost delta and student-experience burden**: Consolidating across a larger geographic footprint will increase transportation costs and bus-ride durations — what is the estimated annual cost increase per route-mile under each plausible facility configuration, at what configuration does transportation consume a material portion of the projected administrative savings, and what does a 45-to-60-minute bus ride each way mean for homework completion, extracurricular participation, and fatigue-related learning outcomes that fall unevenly across parts of the county?
24. **Hidden ongoing operating cost structure**: Beyond transportation, what are the second-order operating costs of fewer, larger schools — staffing-ratio shifts at consolidated grade levels that may require backfilling teaching positions, utilities and custodial loads at concentrated facilities, larger facilities-management overhead — and how do these net against the headline administrative savings on a year-one through year-five basis?
25. **Facility closure decision authority**: The politically most explosive single decision — which school buildings to close or repurpose — will land on the new unified board shortly after consolidation, before trust between the three communities has been established; what governance mechanism (independent facilities committee, binding feasibility criteria, community input process with structured weighting) could be designed now and written into the consolidation agreement to depoliticize that decision enough to survive the early years?
26. **The school-as-anchor parallel**: Urban planners have long recognized that anchor institutions — hospitals, universities, post offices — don't just provide services; they organize surrounding community identity and economic activity. When anchors close, surrounding neighborhoods often destabilize in ways that extend far beyond the lost service itself. How does this "anchor institution" framework reframe the facility closure decisions: not as a facilities efficiency problem but as a community economic development problem — and what mitigation strategies (adaptive reuse, community ownership models, alternative anchors) have urban planners deployed when anchors leave that could apply here?
