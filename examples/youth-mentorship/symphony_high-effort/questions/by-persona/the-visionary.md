# Brainstorming Questions from The Visionary: Youth Mentorship Program for At-Risk Teens

---

## Cluster 1: Reimagining the Relationship — Beyond the "Adult Guides Teen" Paradigm

**What if mentorship weren't a one-way transfer?**: The traditional model assumes adults hold wisdom teens lack — but what if we completely reinvented this as a mutual becoming, where both mentor and mentee are explicitly transformed by the relationship? What structures, rituals, or agreements would make reciprocal growth the stated purpose, not a side effect?

**What if the match were the program?**: Imagine a world where all the training curricula, safety protocols, and outcome metrics dissolve into a single obsessive focus: the design of the pairing itself. What would it look like to treat the matching moment as a sacred act of relational architecture — something more akin to a long-term friendship curation than an administrative intake process? How might this reframing change every downstream decision?

## Cluster 2: Dissolving the Institutional Boundaries — Faith Org, School, Juvenile Justice as One System

**What if the three referring institutions became a single relational membrane?**: Right now, schools, juvenile justice, and the faith organization operate as separate systems that occasionally pass teens to each other. Imagine instead a genuinely new entity — not a coalition or MOU, but a shared social organism — where these three institutions co-hold accountability for each young person. What would it take to design a program that treats these boundaries as legacy artifacts rather than fixed walls?

**What would it mean to let teens design the safety architecture?**: Every protection framework in this program — background checks, meeting-location rules, escalation protocols — is designed by adults for teens. What if we completely reimagined this and involved teens themselves as co-designers of the safety system, drawing on their own lived knowledge of which situations feel dangerous and which feel safe? How might a teen-authored safety framework differ from an expert-authored one, and where might it be more protective?

## Cluster 3: Reinventing Trust — What Compliance Frameworks Cannot Achieve

**What if legal compliance were the floor, not the ceiling, of safety?**: Background checks, mandatory reporter training, and liability coverage are all designed to prevent known failures. But the deepest harms in youth-serving programs often come from dynamics no checklist captures. What would a program look like that treated relational safety — attunement, emotional honesty, power awareness — as its primary protection mechanism, with legal compliance as merely the minimum required baseline?

**What if we invented a new kind of transparency that replaced consent forms?**: Parental/guardian consent frameworks are fundamentally an information-transfer ritual inherited from clinical and legal contexts. What if this program created something categorically different — not a better consent form, but a living, ongoing relational transparency practice between families, teens, mentors, and the organization? What would that look like if we were inventing it from scratch, with no legacy of paperwork to replicate?

## Cluster 4: Transformation at Scale — From Individual Pairs to Community Emergence

**What if a hundred successful matches changed the neighborhood's social physics?**: Most mentorship programs measure outcomes teen by teen. Imagine instead that the real output is something that emerges at the community level — a new kind of social fabric, or a shift in how adults and young people relate to each other across the neighborhood, not just within program pairs. What outcomes would you measure if the unit of transformation were the community rather than the individual? How would you design the program differently if that were the goal?
