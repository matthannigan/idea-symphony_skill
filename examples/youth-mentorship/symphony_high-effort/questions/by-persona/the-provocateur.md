# Brainstorming Questions from The Provocateur: Youth Mentorship Program for At-Risk Teens

---

## Cluster 1: What If the Problem Is the Solution?

**What if chronic absenteeism is the correct response**: Thirty-five percent of these teens are chronically absent from school — but what if they are not failing school, school is failing them, and the mentorship program inadvertently reinforces the same institutional logic? What would a mentorship model look like if it started from the premise that the teen's disengagement is accurate information, not a problem to correct?

**What if the match is backward**: Imagine we reversed the pairing — teens select and vet adult mentors, not the other way around. Adults audition. Teens reject candidates. The teen holds the relationship initiation. What does this reveal about whose comfort the current model is actually designed to protect, and what would the program look like if teen agency were architecturally central rather than a stated aspiration?

## Cluster 2: Making the Familiar Strange — Compliance as Design Material

**What if liability protection made mentorship better**: Here's something absurd, but stay with it: what if every safeguard, disclosure form, background check, and mandatory reporting requirement were treated not as legal overhead but as the primary design material for building trust? What if the program was structured so that the visible presence of these constraints was the message teens received — that adults in this program are accountable in ways the teens have never experienced adults being accountable before? What does a compliance-forward program design look like?

**What if we deliberately made mentors easier to remove**: Imagine a program where the default assumption is that any mentor could be removed at any time, for any reason stated by the teen, with no explanation required and no consequence to the teen. No appeals, no mediation, no "working it out." What would this reveal about how current match-termination protocols are actually protecting program continuity and adult dignity rather than teen safety — and what would a genuinely teen-protective exit architecture look like?

## Cluster 3: Collisions Across Scale and Time

**What if a one-year commitment is the wrong unit entirely**: The program asks for a one-year commitment from mentors, but what if the meaningful relational unit for a 15-year-old is a decade — the period from now until they are 25 and have established adult footing? What would program design look like if it was explicitly trying to create relationships that survive the program's end rather than relationships that fulfill the program's duration? What gets built differently when "program success" means the mentor and teen are still in contact in 2036?

**What if juvenile justice referrals are the program's greatest asset, not its most sensitive liability**: The referral pathway from juvenile justice is treated in the request as a data-privacy and consent complexity to be managed. But here's the provocation: what if teens arriving through juvenile justice have already demonstrated more self-determination and confrontation of consequence than any other population in this program — and treating their referral source as a liability signal rather than a strength signal is the founding error of the matching and training approach? How would mentor training differ if it assumed this cohort was the program's most sophisticated participants?

## Cluster 4: Absurd Extremes That Reveal Hidden Assumptions

**What if we measured outcomes by what mentors learned**: Every outcome measurement framework in this program will focus on teens: attendance, behavior, graduation rates. But here's something absurd — what if the program only measured what mentors learned and how mentors changed? What would it mean if the 40 congregation adults became significantly more capable of sitting with discomfort, navigating unfamiliar institutional systems, and tolerating ambiguity as a result of being mentored by teens? What does inverting the measurement direction reveal about who the current outcome framework implicitly assumes is the developed person and who is the developing one?
