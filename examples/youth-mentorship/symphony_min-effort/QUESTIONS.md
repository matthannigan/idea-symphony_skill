# Brainstorming Questions: Youth Mentorship Program for At-Risk Teens

---

## Topic Cluster 01: Safety, Legal Compliance & Institutional Protection

**Cluster focus**: How the organization establishes and maintains the legal, insurance, and safety infrastructure that makes one-on-one mentoring with minors defensible and trustworthy.

1. **Background check thresholds**: What should our minimum disqualifying criteria be for volunteer background checks, and how should we handle borderline cases like old non-violent offenses or records that were later expunged? [User Q]

2. **Meeting location policy**: Should mentor-mentee meetings always be held in public spaces, or are there specific circumstances where private or home settings are appropriate and defensible? [User Q]

3. **Liability coverage scope**: What level of liability insurance is appropriate for a program of our size, and should we carry a separate sexual misconduct policy in addition to general liability? [User Q]

4. **Juvenile justice consent and privacy**: How do we obtain informed consent from teens referred through juvenile justice, given the power dynamics and the privacy stakes of their records? [User Q]

5. **Data governance for sensitive records**: Beyond consent, what data retention policies, access controls, and breach protocols should govern the storage of juvenile justice referral information and incident reports — particularly given FERPA and state juvenile privacy law?

---

## Topic Cluster 02: Volunteer Readiness, Training & Ongoing Support

**Cluster focus**: How the program equips adult volunteers to show up consistently, handle difficult situations competently, and sustain their commitment over a full year or more.

6. **Mandatory reporter internalization**: How do we structure mandatory reporter training so mentors actually internalize and act on it, rather than just checking a box during onboarding? [User Q]

7. **Concern escalation before the threshold**: How should a mentor raise a concern about a mentee when they're unsure whether it rises to the level of a mandatory report? [User Q]

8. **Burnout prevention and check-in cadence**: What ongoing support and check-in cadence do mentors need to prevent burnout, especially during the first six months of a match? [User Q]

9. **Training curriculum scope and sequencing**: Beyond mandatory reporting and safety protocols, what knowledge and skills — trauma-informed communication, adolescent development, cultural humility — should the pre-match training cover, and in what order to maximize retention without overwhelming volunteers before they've met their mentee?

---

## Topic Cluster 03: Matching, Relationship Quality & Youth Experience

**Cluster focus**: How the program selects pairs, structures early relationship-building, and centers the teen's voice and wellbeing throughout the match lifecycle.

10. **Matching criteria that predict success**: What matching criteria produce the strongest mentor-mentee outcomes — demographic similarity, shared interests, personality fit, or something less obvious? [User Q]

11. **Match termination and transition**: What's the right protocol when a mentor-mentee pairing isn't working, and how do we transition without causing harm to the teen? [User Q]

12. **Youth agency in the match process**: How can teens — many of whom have had little control over the systems that referred them — exercise meaningful choice in who they are matched with and what activities they pursue together, and what does that look like practically within a volunteer-driven model?

13. **Building trust with skeptical youth**: Given that many referred teens have reason to distrust institutions and adults in authority, what relationship structures, early activities, or program norms would help a new mentor earn trust rather than demand it?

---

## Topic Cluster 04: Program Design, Sustainability & Community Fit

**Cluster focus**: How the program's operational model, funding strategy, and ties to the congregation and neighborhood create conditions for long-term viability.

14. **Funding diversification after seed grant**: With $15K in seed funding that will eventually run out, what revenue streams — fee-for-service referrals, government contracts, individual donor cultivation, earned income — are realistically available to a faith-based community organization of this size, and which should be pursued first?

15. **Faith-based identity and programmatic inclusivity**: How does the organization navigate its faith identity in ways that welcome teens and families of all backgrounds — including those referred through secular juvenile justice channels — without requiring religious participation as a condition of program access?

16. **School and juvenile justice referral relationships**: How should the program structure its ongoing relationships with school counselors and juvenile justice partners — what information flows, coordination meetings, and feedback loops maintain the referral pipeline without compromising mentor-mentee confidentiality?

---

## Topic Cluster 05: Outcomes, Learning & Continuous Improvement

**Cluster focus**: How the program defines success, collects evidence without harming participants, and uses what it learns to improve over time.

17. **Measuring outcomes without surveillance**: How do we measure program outcomes without overburdening teens with surveys or making mentors feel surveilled? [User Q]

18. **Defining meaningful success metrics**: Beyond attendance rates and match duration, what indicators — school engagement, social connectedness, mentee self-reported wellbeing, mentor growth — actually reflect whether a mentorship relationship is producing the change the program exists to create?

19. **Learning from early matches**: How should the program systematically capture and act on what it learns from the first cohort of matches — including failed or ended matches — before the model ossifies into habit?

20. **Communicating impact to funders and community**: How does the program tell a credible, non-exploitative story about its results to the regional foundation and the congregation, given that the most powerful evidence often involves minors whose privacy must be protected?
