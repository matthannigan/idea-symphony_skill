# Brainstorming Questions from The Devil's Advocate: Youth Mentorship Program for At-Risk Teens

---

## Faith-Based Identity as Asset and Liability

1. **Mission drift vs. mission clarity**: If the organization's faith identity shapes how mentors approach relationships, how will you ensure that teens referred through juvenile justice — who may hold very different beliefs or none at all — don't experience the program as implicitly conditional on religious conformity, and what happens to a match when a mentor's pastoral instincts conflict with a teen's expressed values?

2. **Volunteer motivation screening**: Beyond background checks, have we considered that volunteers motivated primarily by a sense of religious duty may disengage faster when relationships get difficult or messy — and if so, what commitment structures or self-selection filters would surface that risk before a match is formed?

---

## Relationship Dependency and Structural Fragility

3. **The one-coordinator single point of failure**: One potential challenge is that one-on-one matching programs concentrate enormous relationship risk in a single staff coordinator — if that person leaves mid-year, what is the protocol for maintaining 40 active matches without disrupting fragile trust relationships that took months to build?

4. **What happens after year one**: The program commits mentors to one year, but at-risk teens ages 13–17 often need sustained support across multiple years; have we considered what the drop-off rate looks like at the end of year one, and whether ending a mentorship relationship abruptly could cause measurable harm rather than just neutral outcomes?

5. **Volunteer supply fragility**: With ~40 interested adults and a pool drawn primarily from one congregation, how does the program remain viable if a significant portion of that volunteer base is lost to life changes, burnout, or a single damaging incident that chills volunteer participation?

---

## Harm, Liability, and the Limits of Safeguarding

6. **The safeguarding theater problem**: One potential challenge is that background checks, meeting-location policies, and mandatory reporter training create a compliance posture that looks protective without necessarily being protective — have we considered whether the real risk to teens in this population is more likely to come from emotionally harmful but technically-policy-compliant relationships, and how would you detect and respond to that?

7. **Juvenile justice referrals and data exposure**: Teens referred through juvenile justice carry legally sensitive records, and mentors who know about a teen's history may inadvertently communicate that knowledge in ways that damage the teen's reputation in the congregation community; what firewall exists between referral source information and what mentors are told, and who is accountable if that firewall fails?

---

## Outcome Measurement and Accountability

8. **Defining success on whose terms**: Have we considered that the metrics most legible to funders — attendance, recidivism, grades — may not capture what actually matters to the teens themselves, and that optimizing for funder-visible outcomes could gradually reshape the program in ways that make it less genuinely useful to the young people it is meant to serve?

9. **The $15K runway reality**: With $15,000 in seed funding and no committed renewal, what is the honest probability that this program will still be operating at the 18-month mark, and have we designed the program so that a sudden funding loss causes an orderly wind-down rather than abruptly stranding 40 active mentor-mentee pairs mid-relationship?

---

## Matching, Power, and Youth Agency

10. **Whose criteria drive matching**: One potential challenge is that matching criteria developed by adults — school counselors, juvenile justice workers, and the congregation — may reflect what adults think teens need rather than what teens themselves say they want from a mentor; have we considered building a youth-voice component into the matching process, and what do we risk by not doing so?
