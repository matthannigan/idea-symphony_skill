# Brainstorming Questions from The Analyst: Youth Mentorship Program for At-Risk Teens

---

## Program Structure and Unit Economics

1. **Cost per match**: Breaking this down to the unit level — what is the fully-loaded cost per mentor-mentee pair per year, accounting for screening, training, staff oversight, insurance, and administrative overhead, and how does that figure hold against the $15K seed budget across ~40 potential volunteers?

2. **Retention as a multiplier**: The key variable here seems to be match retention — if the program targets one-year commitments but historically community mentorship programs see 30–40% early attrition, what does the effective cost per completed match look like, and what interventions most reliably improve that rate?

3. **Capacity ceiling**: Given 40 interested volunteers and a one-on-one model, what is the realistic active-match ceiling in Year 1 accounting for disqualifications from background checks, training dropout, and poor-fit screening — and at what number of active matches does the program reach sustainability versus being too thin to demonstrate impact to funders?

---

## Risk Architecture and Compliance Variables

4. **Liability exposure mapping**: Breaking this down structurally — there are at least four distinct liability surfaces (volunteer conduct, mandatory reporting failures, data breach of juvenile justice records, and match-related incidents) — which of these poses the highest probability-times-severity risk given this organization's profile, and what minimum insurance and policy infrastructure addresses each?

5. **Mandatory reporting chain**: The key decision point in mandatory reporting design is not whether to train — it is what the escalation sequence looks like when a mentor observes a concern but is uncertain whether it meets the legal threshold; how do programs with similar profiles define that grey zone and route it through staff?

6. **Juvenile justice data isolation**: Teens referred through juvenile justice carry confidentiality protections that likely exceed standard minor privacy rules — what data handling architecture (who sees which fields, retention limits, disclosure triggers) does this program need to be compliant, and how does that affect how referral information flows between school counselors, probation officers, and program staff?

---

## Matching and Screening as a System

7. **Screening as a funnel, not a gate**: If we model volunteer screening as a sequential funnel — application, background check, interview, training completion, matching — where does the data suggest the most significant drop-off occurs in comparable programs, and how should the program design capacity expectations around that funnel rather than the raw volunteer headcount?

8. **Match quality variables**: The key factors in a successful match appear to be proximity (geographic, schedule), interest alignment, and communication style compatibility — which of these is most predictive of 12-month retention based on mentorship program research, and what minimum matching data is worth collecting without overburdening the intake process?

9. **Disqualifying criteria calibration**: Background check disqualification criteria exist on a spectrum from categorical (any felony) to contextual (offense type, recency, relevance to youth work) — what analytical framework should this organization use to set its own thresholds, and how do comparable faith-based programs typically balance inclusivity of the volunteer pool against risk tolerance?

---

## Outcome Measurement Design

10. **Leading versus lagging indicators**: Lagging outcomes like graduation rates or recidivism reduction will not be measurable within a single grant cycle — breaking this down, what leading indicators (school attendance trajectories, mentor check-in consistency, self-reported goal progress) are both measurable within 12 months and predictive of longer-term outcomes, based on established mentorship evaluation frameworks?

11. **Measurement burden trade-off**: There is a real tension between rigorous outcome data and participant burden — what is the minimum viable measurement set that satisfies a regional foundation's reporting expectations while avoiding survey fatigue in a population of teens already stressed by school and justice system demands?

12. **Baseline data collection window**: Outcome measurement requires a baseline — what data points should be captured at match initiation (attendance records, grades, self-efficacy scores), and which of these can be collected through existing school counselor relationships rather than requiring the program to administer its own assessments?

---

## Organizational Sustainability and Scale

13. **Seed-to-sustaining funding bridge**: The $15K seed grant likely does not cover a full program year at meaningful scale — what does the cost structure look like at 15, 25, and 40 active matches, what funding sources do comparable community mentorship programs use to bridge from seed to Year 2, and at what match volume does per-unit cost drop enough to make the program competitively grantable?
