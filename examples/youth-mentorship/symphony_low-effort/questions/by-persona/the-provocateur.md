# Brainstorming Questions from The Provocateur: Youth Mentorship Program for At-Risk Teens

---

## The Relationship Is the Liability

1. **What if safety protocols are the danger**: What if every safeguard you install — the check-in logs, the two-adult rules, the scheduled meeting times — quietly signals to a teenager that they are a threat to be managed rather than a person to be trusted, and what would you redesign if you accepted that the bureaucratic apparatus of protection is actively eroding the thing you're trying to build?

2. **Imagine the opposite were true — what if the teens screened the mentors**: If the program were flipped so that teens interviewed, selected, and could dismiss their mentors at will with no explanation required, what would that reveal about which adult behaviors we currently tolerate because power runs only one direction?

3. **What if the year-long commitment is the wrong unit entirely**: Here's something absurd, but stay with it — what if the one-year commitment is actually a liability masquerading as a feature, and the most transformative mentorship relationships have no calendar at all? What does that crack open about how the program measures whether it worked?

---

## The Institution as Invisible Third Party

4. **What if the church's 20-year presence is doing harm by being present**: Imagine the faith-based organization's deep neighborhood roots — its known faces, its reputation, its physical building — are subtly disqualifying certain teens who will never walk through that door because of what the institution represents to their family. What would the program look like if the organization agreed to become functionally invisible?

5. **What if we made it deliberately worse at record-keeping**: What if, for teens referred through juvenile justice, the program kept no records at all — nothing searchable, nothing shareable, nothing that could ever be subpoenaed — and what does thinking through the actual risks of that reveal about which data we're collecting to protect the teens versus to protect the organization?

---

## The Wrong Problem Entirely

6. **What if chronic absenteeism is the correct response to the environment**: Imagine the 35% absenteeism rate is not a symptom of at-risk behavior but a rational, accurate assessment by teenagers that school is not serving them — and this mentorship program is being designed to make teens more compliant with a system that deserves their skepticism. What would a program look like that took that premise seriously instead of treating presence as inherently good?

7. **What if the 40 volunteers are the ones who need the program**: Here's something absurd — what if the adults who signed up to mentor at-risk teens are doing so because they are working through something unresolved in themselves, and the program's biggest unexamined risk isn't to the teens but to the quality of care those adults are capable of giving? What would you discover if you ran the intake screening primarily as a mental-health and motivation audit of the mentors, not a background-check audit?
