# Brainstorming Questions from The Appreciative Inquirer: Youth Mentorship Program for At-Risk Teens

---

## Discovery: When Mentorship Has Already Worked

1. **Peak moments in mentoring**: Think about the most impactful mentoring relationships you have witnessed or heard about — what was happening in those relationships that made them so transformative, and what did the organization behind them do to create the conditions for that to flourish?

2. **This community's relational assets**: This faith-based organization has been present in this neighborhood for 20 years. What has it already learned about building trust with families who are skeptical of institutions — and how might those hard-won lessons become the foundation of how mentors are trained to show up with teens?

3. **Volunteers who already get it**: Among the ~40 interested adults, some almost certainly have natural gifts for connecting with young people. What do those people already do instinctively — before any training — that creates safety and genuine rapport, and how could the program design amplify and spread those instincts?

---

## Dream: The Future We Are Reaching Toward

4. **The relationship three years from now**: Imagine a mentor and teen who have met weekly for two years and whose relationship has genuinely changed the trajectory of the teen's life. What does that relationship look and feel like? What did the program make possible that would not have happened without intentional structure?

5. **A program other communities want to replicate**: If this became a model that faith communities across the region sent delegations to study, what would visitors find when they arrived — what practices, what culture, what evidence of impact would make them say "we want to bring this home"?

6. **The teen's own account**: Picture a 17-year-old, two years into the program, describing to a school counselor what the mentorship has meant. What are they saying? What has changed in how they see themselves, their options, their future — and what did the program do to make those changes possible?

---

## Design: Structures That Reliably Create What We Want

7. **Training that builds on existing wisdom**: Given that this organization already knows how to accompany people through hard seasons — what existing pastoral, community, or volunteer practices could form the backbone of a mentor training curriculum, rather than importing a generic program from outside?

8. **Safety systems that strengthen rather than chill relationships**: Every protective protocol — background checks, mandatory reporting, boundary guidelines — could either create bureaucratic distance or build the kind of structured safety that actually makes deeper connection possible. What does it look like when safety systems are designed to support the relationship rather than just to limit liability?

9. **Matching as a relational craft**: The matching process for mentor-mentee pairs could be purely logistical or it could be a thoughtful, relationship-centered practice in its own right. What would a matching process look like that takes seriously both the teen's strengths and aspirations and the mentor's particular gifts, rather than just availability and demographics?

---

## Destiny: First Steps and Sustained Commitments

10. **The volunteer already ready to lead**: Among the 40 interested adults, who already has the skills, the relational credibility in the community, and the passion to help shape this program from the inside — and how could involving them early change the quality of what gets built?

11. **What could happen in the first 90 days**: If the program launched with even five strong mentor-mentee matches in the first 90 days and invested deeply in those relationships, what would you hope those five pairs would experience, learn, and demonstrate — and how would that inform how the program grows from there?
