# Topic Cluster 07: Teen Voice, Family Engagement & Cultural Inclusion

## Questions

1. **The arrival moment and the dignity of being matched**: What does a teen experience in the first 15 minutes of their first mentor meeting — what are they thinking, feeling, and silently assessing — and what does the matching process feel like from the teen's perspective: are they an object being assigned, or a person being heard? What would it mean to design intake as an experience that itself communicates respect?
2. **Parent partnership when home is complicated and communication that doesn't backfire**: Beyond obtaining consent signatures, how might parents and guardians be meaningfully included as participants in the program's success, given barriers like work schedules, language, and distrust of institutions — and for teens whose home environment is part of what makes them "at-risk" (unstable housing, incarcerated parents, family conflict), how does program design account for the reality that parental involvement may be fraught, while structuring guardian communication so it builds trust rather than creating a surveillance dynamic that causes teens to shut down?
3. **Cultural-linguistic fit, the stigma of "at-risk," and faith neutrality**: From the perspective of teens from communities that may historically distrust faith-based institutions, authority figures, or the juvenile justice system — what signals in the program's design, language, and environment either invite them in or quietly tell them this isn't for them; how do teens experience the label that got them into this program; and without explicit policy clarity on religious neutrality in program delivery, does the organization's faith identity (an asset for congregation trust) function as a deterrent for secular families, LGBTQ+ teens, or families from different religious traditions, potentially raising civil rights questions about publicly-referred participants placed in a faith-directed program?
4. **The invisible logistics problem**: What practical barriers — bus routes, after-school jobs, childcare for younger siblings, lack of a phone — might cause a teen to miss meetings without ever explaining why, and how does the program design catch and address these before they read as "disengagement"?
5. **The crossing-the-threshold moment**: Imagine a 15-year-old, referred by her school counselor, walking into her first meeting with a mentor she has never met — what does that room look like, who is in it, and what happens in the first ten minutes that makes her decide whether this is worth her time?
6. **Strip the church entirely**: Imagine the program existed with exactly the same teens, mentors, and funding — but with no faith-based organizational home. What would change? What would the teens who were referred through juvenile justice gain or lose? Which elements of the faith-based context are genuine assets versus implicit barriers to entry for specific youth?
