# Topic Cluster 09: Sustainability, Funding & Institutional Continuity

## Questions

1. **Cost-per-match unit economics, the $15K gap, and behavioral lock-in**: With $15K in seed funding plus in-kind support, what does the estimated cost per active mentor-mentee pair look like across the first year — broken down into screening, training, coordination, and overhead — at what funding level does the program become self-sustaining versus dependent on grant renewal, and which organizational behaviors become load-bearing on that grant such that they collapse if it isn't renewed (behavioral lock-in), versus which the organization has built capacity to sustain independently? If $15K falls short at month six, what contingency keeps already-matched pairs intact rather than abruptly terminating relationships?
2. **Single-coordinator single-point-of-failure and compliance-by-design**: If the administrative coordinator who manages consent records, match files, and mandatory-report logs becomes unavailable, does the program have documented procedures and backup access to continue without compliance exposure — and rather than treating legal compliance as a checklist to complete before launch, what standing governance mechanisms (designated compliance officer, periodic mandatory-reporter refreshers tied to re-enrollment, formal policy review cycle, board-level reporting) should be built into the operating structure from inception so legal obligations evolve with the program?
3. **Caseload supervisor ratio and the mentor-attrition reputation feedback**: How many active mentor-mentee pairs can a single staff coordinator effectively supervise while maintaining quality check-ins, responding to escalations, and supporting struggling mentors — what is the staffing implication if the program scales beyond 40 pairs, and if mentor burnout causes visible departures in the first cohort, how does word-of-mouth from burned-out mentors reshape the volunteer pipeline and reduce future recruitment?
