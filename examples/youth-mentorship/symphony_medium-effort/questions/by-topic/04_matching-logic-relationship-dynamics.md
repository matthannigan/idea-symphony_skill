# Topic Cluster 04: Matching Logic & Relationship Dynamics

## Questions

1. **Matching variable weighting, the relationship-as-outcome assumption, and one-size-fit**: What are the three to five variables — demographic similarity, shared interests, personality fit, geographic proximity, gender, cultural background, referral source, or something less obvious — that carry the most predictive weight for relationship longevity, and behind that question: why do we assume the relationship itself is the primary outcome that moves the needle (rather than evidence-based mechanisms like skill transfer, advocacy, or network access), and that a single one-on-one weekly meeting model is right for every teen — rather than a flexible model that adapts to each teen's stability, school schedule, and family situation? [User Q]
2. **Match quality as a compounding factor with early-warning signals**: When a match is strong, the teen shows up, the mentor stays engaged, and both sides invest more — a self-amplifying pattern; when a match is weak, disengagement feeds more disengagement. What measurable intermediate checkpoints — at 30, 60, 90 days — distinguish a relationship on track from one at risk of early termination, and what intervention protocol activates at each checkpoint before the dynamic becomes irreversible?
3. **Congregation homogeneity and cross-cultural preparation**: A faith-based volunteer pool may skew toward a particular demographic (age, race, class, worldview) that doesn't align with teens referred through juvenile justice — how will the program recruit beyond the congregation, and when mentors and mentees come from different racial, class, or cultural backgrounds, how does the program prepare both parties to navigate that difference with care rather than papering over it?
4. **Matching as ecological fit, not resume alignment**: Conservation biologists match reintroduced species to habitats based on environmental conditions, not just genetic similarity — there's a structural parallel to mentor-mentee matching here: what ecological conditions (neighborhood proximity, shared commute, availability patterns, cultural context) might predict relationship durability better than surface-level interest alignment alone?
5. **Flip the referral**: What if the teens chose and vetted the mentors — not the other way around? If a 15-year-old from the juvenile justice system had full veto power over who entered their life, what would the selection criteria actually look like, and what does that reveal about whose comfort the current process is really designed to protect?
6. **The quiet Tuesday that changes everything**: What kinds of ordinary, low-stakes moments — a shared meal, a car ride, watching someone fix something — does the program design space for, and how do we build a program structure that trusts these unscripted interactions rather than filling every hour with agenda?
