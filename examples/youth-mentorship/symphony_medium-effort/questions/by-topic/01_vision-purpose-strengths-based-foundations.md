# Topic Cluster 01: Vision, Purpose & Strengths-Based Foundations

## Questions

1. **Peak-moment trajectory shift**: Think about the most powerful youth mentorship story you've encountered — what was it about that single relationship that made the difference, and what conditions in the program made that relationship possible? Which conditions can be deliberately reproduced and which were accidents of fit?
2. **The congregation as irreplaceable lifeline and asset base**: This organization has sustained a 20-year presence in the neighborhood — what relational practices, cultural knowledge, and community trust already exist here that no outside program could replicate, and which of those assets (existing relationships, congregation networks, school-counselor knowledge, the foundation grant itself) can the program mobilize immediately, before a single formal policy is written?
3. **Start with five pairs as discovery data**: Rather than designing the whole program in advance, what would it look like to begin with a small cohort of three to five carefully supported pairs, treat their experience as discovery data, and let what works in those relationships shape the program design for everyone who follows?
4. **The 2029 model-program vision**: Imagine it's three years from now and delegations from other cities are visiting to learn from this program. What do they notice first when they walk in the door, what do they hear teens and mentors saying to each other, what metrics are on the wall, and what stories are being shared?
5. **Co-inquiry instead of guidance**: What if we completely reimagined the mentor-mentee relationship so that it isn't a structured adult-guiding-youth dynamic at all — but rather a genuine co-inquiry where both parties explore meaning together, and the power gradient simply doesn't exist in the way we currently assume it must?
6. **A congregation as a new kind of civic institution**: Imagine a world where a faith-based organization with a 20-year neighborhood presence becomes the prototype for an entirely new category of institution — not a church, not a nonprofit, not a social service agency, but something that doesn't have a name yet. What capabilities does this organization already have that no traditional youth-serving institution possesses, and how could a mentorship program be the seed of that new category?
