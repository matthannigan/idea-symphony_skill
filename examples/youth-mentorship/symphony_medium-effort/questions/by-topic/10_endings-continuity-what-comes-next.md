# Topic Cluster 10: Endings, Continuity & What Comes Next

## Questions

1. **The one-year commitment cliff and phased commitment alternatives**: What happens at the one-year mark when teens and mentors who've built real relationships face an abrupt structural ending — what does the off-ramp look like, what signals tell us a relationship should continue versus conclude, and is there a phased commitment structure (rather than the binary one-year minimum) that preserves quality without filtering out volunteers who might thrive with a shorter initial trial? Relatedly, when a mentor must withdraw at month four, is the program prepared to absorb that rupture without re-traumatizing youth who may already have unstable adult relationships?
2. **Match-termination protocol when a pairing isn't working**: What's the right protocol when a mentor-mentee pairing isn't working, and how do we transition without causing harm to the teen — particularly given that from the teen's perspective, requesting a change can feel like another failure on a long list, so does the program make it genuinely safe and easy for a teen to say so? [User Q]
3. **Cross-system coherence when the adults disagree**: What if a teen's school counselor, juvenile justice case manager, parent or guardian, and mentor are all working from different frameworks and giving conflicting guidance — who resolves that, and is the program designed to handle it?
4. **The graduation that doesn't feel like abandonment**: Picture a teen who has just turned 18, aged out of the program, and whose mentor is the most consistent adult relationship in her life — what does the program's closing chapter look like so that this ending feels like a launching rather than another loss, and what, if anything, carries forward?
5. **The mentorship program as a community memory project**: What if the program was designed not just to support individual teens but to create a living archive of neighborhood wisdom, story, and identity — where each mentor-mentee pair becomes a vessel for intergenerational knowledge transfer that the community has never had a structure for before? How would that purpose reshape every element, from matching to meetings to what happens after the formal year ends?
