# Topic Cluster 08: Outcome Measurement & Learning Loops

## Questions

1. **Outcome variable selection, baseline acquisition, and the teens' own definitions**: Given the program's goals, what are the two or three quantifiable proxies — school attendance delta, disciplinary incident rate, mentor-reported relationship quality score — that are both meaningful and obtainable without burdening participants or requiring data-sharing agreements the organization cannot easily execute, how do we measure outcomes without making mentors feel surveilled or teens feel over-surveyed, what is the realistic pre-program baseline-collection window (with which indicators sourced from school counselors or juvenile justice referrers rather than self-report), and how does the measurement framework include a 15-year-old's definition of "a good year with a mentor" alongside the funder's metrics — treating data collection as something done with participants rather than to them? [User Q]
2. **Attrition exit data as a feedback signal**: When a match ends before the one-year commitment, what structured exit data — reason category, duration, initiating party, antecedent events — should be collected, and how does that data feed back into refining the matching process and identifying systemic program weaknesses?
3. **Short-cycle versus long-cycle feedback and the measurement-burden chilling effect**: Many meaningful outcomes — educational attainment, justice system involvement, economic stability — manifest over years, while surveys, check-ins, and reporting requirements add friction for both mentors and teens. At what point does the measurement apparatus start degrading the participation it's trying to study, and what shorter-cycle signals function as reliable proxies for long-term outcomes without optimizing for proxies in ways that miss the deeper changes the program actually cares about?
4. **Measure failure, not success**: What if instead of tracking program outcomes — attendance, graduation rates, "relationship quality" — you tracked every moment a mentor or teen wanted to quit but didn't? What would that data tell you that success metrics never could? Resilience and durability in relationships are invisible to outcome measurement but may be the actual product being built.
5. **What if outcomes were designed to be felt, not counted**: What if we completely abandoned the standard outcome measurement frameworks — attendance, grades, recidivism — and designed a program whose primary evidence of success was qualitative transformation visible to the teens themselves and the community around them? What entirely new forms of witnessing and documentation would we need to invent?

**Question count**: 5
**Cluster focus**: Measuring what matters — to teens, mentors, funders, and the program itself — without turning measurement into surveillance or optimizing for the wrong proxies.
