# Brainstorming Questions from The Storyteller: Youth Mentorship Program for At-Risk Teens

---

## The First Meeting: How Does a Relationship Begin?

1. **The crossing-the-threshold moment**: Imagine a 15-year-old, referred by her school counselor, walking into her first meeting with a mentor she has never met — what does that room look like, who is in it, and what happens in the first ten minutes that makes her decide whether this is worth her time?

2. **The mentor's leap of faith**: Picture a 42-year-old congregation member who signed up excited but now sits in the parking lot before that same first meeting, uncertain whether he has anything real to offer — what story does the program need to have already told him about himself and his role that gets him out of the car with confidence rather than performance anxiety?

---

## The Long Middle: Sustaining a Relationship Through Real Life

3. **The test of a hard week**: Walk through the moment six months in when a teen ghosts two scheduled meetings after a crisis at home — what structures, relationships, and rituals does the program have in place so the mentor knows what to do, feels supported rather than abandoned, and the teen has a clear path back without shame?

4. **The quiet Tuesday that changes everything**: What kinds of ordinary, low-stakes moments — a shared meal, a car ride, watching someone fix something — does the program design space for, and how do we build a program structure that trusts these unscripted interactions rather than filling every hour with agenda?

5. **The year-two question**: Let me walk you through Marcus, who has been matched for 14 months, is doing better in school, and whose mentor is quietly burning out from carrying the weight of Marcus's family instability — what does the program's ongoing support system look like in his mentor's life that makes continuing feel sustainable rather than heroic?

---

## The Rupture and the Repair: When Things Go Wrong

6. **The moment trust breaks**: Imagine a mentor who, trying to be helpful, shares something a teen told her in confidence with the teen's parent — and the teen finds out — what policies, training moments, and repair conversations does the program have ready so this breach doesn't end the relationship and teaches both people something true about trust?

---

## Endings and What They Leave Behind

7. **The graduation that doesn't feel like abandonment**: Picture a teen who has just turned 18, aged out of the program, and whose mentor is the most consistent adult relationship in her life — what does the program's closing chapter look like so that this ending feels like a launching rather than another loss, and what, if anything, carries forward?
