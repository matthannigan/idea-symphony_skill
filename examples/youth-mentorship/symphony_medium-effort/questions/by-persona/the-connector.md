# Brainstorming Questions from The Connector: Youth Mentorship Program for At-Risk Teens

---

## Bridging Referral Systems and Relationship Entry Points

1. **Cross-system intake as a warm handoff**: School counselors and juvenile justice coordinators each have distinct relational authority with a teen — how might the program design its intake process to borrow from the "warm handoff" model used in healthcare transitions, where the referring professional personally introduces the mentee to the program rather than passing paperwork alone?

2. **Referral as a trust-transfer mechanism**: In franchise models, brand trust transfers from the franchisor to a new location the moment a customer walks in; there's a structural parallel here with referral — how might the program design the mentor-matching step so that the trust a teen has in their school counselor or parole officer actually transfers forward into their first interaction with their mentor?

---

## Designing Safety Infrastructure Across Institutional Seams

3. **Mandatory reporting as a shared protocol layer**: Air traffic control works across dozens of airlines and airports through a single shared protocol layer that all parties implement independently — what would it look like to design mandatory reporting obligations and escalation procedures as a shared protocol that the faith org, school, and juvenile justice system each implement in their own language, rather than training mentors to navigate each institution's separate reporting chain?

4. **Data privacy as a firewall architecture**: Network security architects distinguish between what must be isolated, what can be shared with authentication, and what is public — how might the program apply this "firewall" logic to the data it holds on teens (especially those with juvenile records), defining explicit tiers of who can access what information and under what conditions, rather than defaulting to blanket confidentiality that could create coordination failures?

---

## Mentorship Relationship Design and the Matching Problem

5. **Matching as ecological fit, not resume alignment**: Conservation biologists match reintroduced species to habitats based on environmental conditions, not just genetic similarity — there's a structural parallel to mentor-mentee matching here: what ecological conditions (neighborhood proximity, shared commute, availability patterns, cultural context) might predict relationship durability better than surface-level interest alignment alone?

---

## Sustaining the Volunteer Pipeline Across Institutional Cycles

6. **Volunteer retention and the rhythm problem**: Faith congregations, schools, and juvenile justice systems each operate on different annual rhythms (liturgical calendar, academic year, fiscal/court cycles) — how might the program design its volunteer onboarding and renewal cadence to account for the fact that mentor availability and organizational attention will peak and trough on different schedules depending on which institution a volunteer comes from?

7. **Cross-system recognition as a retention mechanism**: Professional associations sustain member engagement partly through peer recognition that travels across employers — what would it look like to create a recognition system for mentors that is meaningful across all three institutional contexts (congregation, school partner, community at large), so that a mentor's contribution is visible and valued whether they're known primarily as a church member, a professional, or a community volunteer?
