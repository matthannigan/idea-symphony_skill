# Brainstorming Questions from The Visionary: Youth Mentorship Program for At-Risk Teens

---

## Reimagining the Relationship Itself

1. **Beyond mentorship as we know it**: What if we completely reimagined the mentor-mentee relationship so that it isn't a structured adult-guiding-youth dynamic at all — but rather a genuine co-inquiry where both parties explore meaning together, and the power gradient simply doesn't exist in the way we currently assume it must?

2. **The relationship as a living ecosystem**: Imagine if a mentorship pair wasn't a bounded dyad but a node in a living network — where each pair's growth, conflicts, and breakthroughs become shared resources for the whole community. What would a program look like if it treated every mentor-mentee relationship as generative infrastructure for the neighborhood, not a private intervention?

---

## Designing for Transformation, Not Compliance

3. **Inventing a new kind of safety**: What if "safety" in a youth mentorship program didn't mean protocols, waivers, and rules — but was instead designed from scratch as an emergent property of the relationships themselves? What would a program look like where safety arises from trust architecture rather than legal architecture?

4. **Rethinking what "at-risk" means as a category**: What if we invented a program that begins from the premise that the teens are not the ones who need to change — and the entire intervention energy was redirected toward transforming the systems, environments, and adults surrounding them? How would that invert the design of every phase, from intake to outcomes?

---

## The Faith Community as a Paradigm Shift

5. **A congregation as a new kind of civic institution**: Imagine a world where a faith-based organization with a 20-year neighborhood presence becomes the prototype for an entirely new category of institution — not a church, not a nonprofit, not a social service agency, but something that doesn't have a name yet. What capabilities does this organization already have that no traditional youth-serving institution possesses, and how could a mentorship program be the seed of that new category?

---

## Outcomes Beyond Measurement

6. **What if outcomes were designed to be felt, not counted**: What if we completely abandoned the standard outcome measurement frameworks — attendance, grades, recidivism — and designed a program whose primary evidence of success was qualitative transformation visible to the teens themselves and the community around them? What entirely new forms of witnessing and documentation would we need to invent?

---

## Identity and Legacy

7. **The mentorship program as a community memory project**: What if the program was designed not just to support individual teens but to create a living archive of neighborhood wisdom, story, and identity — where each mentor-mentee pair becomes a vessel for intergenerational knowledge transfer that the community has never had a structure for before? How would that purpose reshape every element, from matching to meetings to what happens after the formal year ends?
