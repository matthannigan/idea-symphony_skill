# Brainstorming Questions from The Analyst: Youth Mentorship Program for At-Risk Teens

---

## Volunteer Pipeline and Screening Architecture

1. **Screening funnel attrition**: Breaking down the volunteer intake process into its component stages — expression of interest, application, background check, interview, training, matching — at what rate do you expect candidates to drop out at each stage, and how does that affect your ability to sustain a pool of 40 active mentors?

2. **Disqualification criteria tiering**: What framework should govern which background check findings are automatic disqualifiers, which require case-by-case review, and which are irrelevant to working with at-risk teens — and who has decision authority at each tier?

3. **Renewal and re-screening cadence**: What is the right interval for re-running background checks on active volunteers, and what events (arrests, address changes, role changes) should trigger an off-cycle review rather than waiting for the standard renewal?

---

## Program Structure and Matching Logic

4. **Matching variable weighting**: The program needs to pair ~40 mentors with a similar number of teens — what are the three to five variables (shared interests, geographic proximity, gender, cultural background, referral source) that carry the most predictive weight for relationship longevity, and how should those be ranked in the matching algorithm?

5. **Commitment structure enforcement**: If the one-year weekly commitment is the baseline, what are the measurable intermediate checkpoints — at 30, 60, 90 days — that distinguish a relationship on track from one at risk of early termination, and what intervention protocol activates at each checkpoint?

6. **Caseload and supervisor ratio**: How many active mentor-mentee pairs can a single staff coordinator effectively supervise while maintaining quality check-ins, responding to escalations, and supporting struggling mentors — and what is the staffing implication if the program scales beyond 40 pairs?

---

## Compliance and Liability Risk Decomposition

7. **Mandatory reporting training gap analysis**: Mandatory reporter obligations vary in specificity — what are the distinct knowledge components (recognition of abuse indicators, reporting thresholds, chain of notification, documentation requirements) that a mentor must demonstrate competency in before being cleared, and how is that competency verified rather than assumed from attendance?

8. **One-on-one meeting location risk profile**: The program model centers on one-on-one interaction with minors, which creates a distinct liability surface — what is the matrix of acceptable meeting settings (public vs. private, observed vs. unobserved), and which combinations are categorically prohibited regardless of mentor tenure or relationship quality?

9. **Juvenile justice data handling architecture**: Teens referred through juvenile justice carry records subject to stricter privacy protections than school referrals — what information actually needs to flow to program staff to run effective matches and flag safety concerns, and what information must never leave the referring agency?

---

## Outcome Measurement and Program Evaluation

10. **Outcome variable selection**: Given the program's goals of safe, effective mentorship and meaningful relationships, what are the two or three quantifiable proxies — school attendance delta, disciplinary incident rate, mentor-reported relationship quality score — that are both meaningful and obtainable without burdening participants or requiring data-sharing agreements the organization cannot easily execute?

11. **Baseline data acquisition**: For any outcome measure to be interpretable, a pre-program baseline is needed — what is the realistic data-collection window before match activation, and which baseline indicators can be sourced from school counselors or juvenile justice referrers rather than requiring the teen to self-report?

12. **Attrition as a data point**: When a match ends before the one-year commitment, what structured exit data — reason category, duration, initiating party, antecedent events — should be collected, and how does that data feed back into refining the matching process and identifying systemic program weaknesses?

---

## Financial Sustainability and Resource Allocation

13. **Cost-per-match unit economics**: With $15K in seed funding and in-kind space and administrative support, what does the estimated cost per active mentor-mentee pair look like across the program's first year — broken down into screening, training, coordination, and overhead — and at what funding level does the program become self-sustaining versus dependent on grant renewal?
