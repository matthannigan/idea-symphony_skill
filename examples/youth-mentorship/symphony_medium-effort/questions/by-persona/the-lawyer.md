# Brainstorming Questions from The Lawyer: Youth Mentorship Program for At-Risk Teens

---

## Mandatory Reporting Architecture

1. **Mandatory Reporter Scope**: Under your state's mandatory reporter statute, are volunteer mentors—as individuals receiving training from a faith-based nonprofit—classified as mandatory reporters, and if so, what specific training certification, documentation, and reporting-chain design does the organization need to build compliance into the program from day one rather than retrofitting it after an incident?

2. **Incident Escalation and Documentation**: What formal incident-documentation protocol—covering initial disclosure, chain of report notification, regulatory filing timelines, and record retention—must the organization design to ensure mandatory reporting obligations are met without creating conflicting instructions between program staff, church leadership, and external agency mandates?

---

## Minor Consent and Juvenile-Record Privacy

3. **Parental Consent Architecture**: Given that participants are minors ages 13–17, what layered consent framework—distinguishing between initial program enrollment consent, photo/media release, data collection authorization, and ongoing participation acknowledgment—must the organization obtain from parents or legal guardians, and what happens when guardianship is contested, shared, or held by the state through juvenile justice involvement?

4. **Juvenile-Record Confidentiality**: When teens are referred through juvenile justice channels, what restrictions do state juvenile-record confidentiality statutes place on what information school counselors and juvenile justice officers may legally share with program staff, and how should the intake and matching process be designed to operate effectively within those constraints rather than inadvertently soliciting protected information?

5. **Teen Assent and Data Minimization**: What is the minimum data the program must collect to operate effectively, and how should data collection and retention policies be structured so that sensitive information about a minor's justice involvement does not migrate outside its lawful disclosure boundary or create a private record that outlasts the legal protections governing the official one?

---

## Volunteer Background Check Criteria and Liability Design

6. **Background Check Statutory Floor**: Does your state impose a statutory background check requirement on volunteers who have unsupervised access to minors in a nonprofit setting, and if so, what specific disqualifying offense categories are mandated—distinguishing between absolute bars (e.g., registered sex offenders) and discretionary review categories—so the organization can design a written screening policy that meets the legal floor without creating arbitrary exclusions that expose the organization to discrimination claims?

7. **Negligent Selection and Retention Exposure**: If a mentor causes harm to a youth participant, the organization's liability will be assessed partly on whether it exercised reasonable care in selection and supervision—what documented screening standard, ongoing supervision protocol, and mentor-conduct policy does the organization need to demonstrate that standard of care, and at what point does the organization's legal obligation to remove a mentor arise even absent a criminal conviction?

---

## Insurance and Organizational Legal Protections

8. **Insurance Coverage Architecture**: What combination of general liability, directors-and-officers, volunteer accident, and sexual-abuse-and-molestation (SAM) coverage does a one-on-one youth mentorship program require, and is the faith-based organization's existing church liability policy likely to extend to program volunteers and off-site mentoring activities, or does a separate endorsement or standalone policy need to be procured before the first mentor-mentee meeting occurs?

---

## Governance and Compliance-by-Design

9. **Compliance-by-Design Governance Structure**: Rather than treating legal compliance as a checklist to complete before launch, what standing governance mechanisms—such as a designated compliance officer role, periodic mandatory reporter refreshers tied to re-enrollment, a formal policy review cycle, and a board-level reporting requirement—should be built into the program's operating structure from inception to ensure that legal obligations evolve with the program rather than lagging behind it?
